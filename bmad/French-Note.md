## Overview & How JAMB Tests French

In the UTME, French is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into five main domains:
1. **Written Comprehension (~8 Questions):** Reading unseen contemporary narrative, descriptive, and journalistic passages in French, extracting factual information, and drawing inferences.
2. **Grammar and Sentence Structures (~18 Questions):** Articles, determiners, adjective agreement and placement, object pronouns (COD, COI, *y, en*), relative pronouns, verbal tenses (*Présent, Passé composé, Imparfait, Futur, Conditionnel, Subjonctif*), agreement of the past participle, and negative structures.
3. **Vocabulary, Idioms and False Friends (~6 Questions):** Everyday lexical fields, idiomatic expressions with *avoir* and *faire*, proverbs, and avoiding treacherous false friends (*faux amis*).
4. **Phonetics and Sound Discrimination (~4 Questions):** Nasal vowels, silent final consonants, liaisons, elisions, and rhyme matching.
5. **Francophone Culture and Civilization (~4 Questions):** Geography, national symbols, and culture of France and Francophone African countries bordering Nigeria (Benin, Niger, Cameroon, Chad, Togo, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Distinctions in JAMB French:**
> - Agreement of Past Participle with *Avoir*: The past participle conjugated with *avoir* **NEVER agrees with the subject**, but **MUST agree in gender and number with a Preceding Direct Object (COD)** placed before the verb.
> - Passé Composé vs. Imparfait: **Passé Composé** expresses completed, specific, punctual actions in the past; **Imparfait** expresses ongoing, continuous past actions, habits, atmospheric descriptions, and emotional states.
> - Negative Transformation of Partitive Articles: In negative sentences, *du, de la, de l', des* change to simply **de / d'** (e.g., *Je mange du pain* $\to$ *Je ne mange pas de pain*).

# SECTION 1: GRAMMAR AND SENTENCE STRUCTURES

## 1. Articles and Adjective Rules
- **Articles & Partitives:**
  - *Definite:* **le** (masc.), **la** (fem.), **l'** (before vowel/silent h), **les** (plural).
  - *Indefinite:* **un** (masc.), **une** (fem.), **des** (plural).
  - *Partitive Articles (Expressing unspecified quantities):* **du** (masc.), **de la** (fem.), **de l'** (vowel), **des** (plural).
    $$\text{Positive: } \text{Elle boit du lait, de la bière et de l'eau.}$$
    $$\text{Negative Rule: } \mathbf{du, de\ la, de\ l', des} \xrightarrow{\text{negation}} \mathbf{de / d'}$$
    $$\text{Negative: } \text{Elle ne boit pas } \mathbf{de}\text{ lait, pas } \mathbf{de}\text{ bière, pas } \mathbf{d'}\text{eau.}$$
- **Placement of Adjectives (The BANGS Rule):**
  - Most French adjectives are placed **AFTER the noun** (especially colors, shapes, nationalities, and participles).
  - However, **BANGS adjectives precede the noun**:
    - **B = Beauty:** *beau, joli*
    - **A = Age:** *jeune, vieux, nouveau*
    - **N = Number / Rank:** *premier, deuxième, dernier*
    - **G = Goodness:** *bon, mauvais, gentil*
    - **S = Size:** *grand, petit, gros, long, haut*
    $$\text{Example: } \text{Un } \mathbf{grand}\text{ homme } (\text{before}) \quad \text{vs.} \quad \text{Une voiture } \mathbf{noire}\text{ et } \mathbf{ronde}\text{ (after)}$$
  - *Special Forms before Masculine Nouns starting with a vowel:*
    - *Beau* becomes **bel** (e.g., *un bel homme*).
    - *Vieux* becomes **vieil** (e.g., *un vieil arbre*).
    - *Nouveau* becomes **nouvel** (e.g., *un nouvel appartement*).

## 2. Personal, Adverbial, and Relative Pronouns
- **Direct Object Pronouns (COD — Answers "Whom?" or "What?"):**
  - *me, te, le (him/it), la (her/it), nous, vous, les (them)*.
  - Placed **before the conjugated verb**:
    $$\text{Tu regardes la télévision? } \to \text{Oui, je } \mathbf{la}\text{ regarde.}$$
- **Indirect Object Pronouns (COI — Answers "To whom?"):**
  - Replaces *à + person*: *me, te, lui (to him/to her), nous, vous, leur (to them)*.
    $$\text{Il parle à sa mère? } \to \text{Oui, il } \mathbf{lui}\text{ parle.}$$
    $$\text{Il écrit à ses parents? } \to \text{Oui, il } \mathbf{leur}\text{ écrit.}$$
- **Adverbial Pronouns (Y and EN):**
  - **Y:** Replaces **à / dans / sur / en + place or inanimate object**:
    $$\text{Tu vas à Paris? } \to \text{Oui, j'}\mathbf{y}\text{ vais.}$$
  - **EN:** Replaces **de + noun**, a partitive article, or an expression of quantity:
    $$\text{Tu veux du café? } \to \text{Oui, j'}\mathbf{en}\text{ veux.}$$
    $$\text{Tu as trois frères? } \to \text{Oui, j'}\mathbf{en}\text{ ai trois.}$$
- **Relative Pronouns (Qui, Que, Dont, Où):**
  - **QUI:** Subject of the relative clause; **always followed by a VERB**:
    $$\text{L'homme } \mathbf{qui}\text{ parle est mon père.}$$
  - **QUE (QU'):** Direct object; **always followed by a SUBJECT + VERB**:
    $$\text{Le livre } \mathbf{que}\text{ tu lis est intéressant.}$$
  - **DONT:** Replaces a noun preceded by **de** (verbs like *parler de, avoir besoin de, avoir peur de*):
    $$\text{Voici la fille } \mathbf{dont}\text{ je t'ai parlé.}$$
  - **OÙ:** Indicates physical place or point in time:
    $$\text{La ville } \mathbf{où}\text{ j'habite...} \quad / \quad \text{Le jour } \mathbf{où}\text{ il est né...}$$

## 3. Verbal Tenses and Agreement Rules
- **Passé Composé vs. Imparfait:**
  - *Passé Composé:* Expresses completed, punctual, historical events that occurred at a specific moment:
    $$\text{Hier, j'ai fini mes devoirs.}$$
  - *Imparfait:* Expresses habits, repeated past routines, physical/emotional descriptions, weather, and ongoing background actions:
    $$\text{Quand j'étais petit, je jouais au football tous les jours.}$$
- **Verbs Taking Être in the Passé Composé (DR MRS VANDERTRAMP):**
  - *Devenir, Revenir, Monter, Rester, Sortir, Venir, Aller, Naître, Descendre, Entrer, Rentrer, Tomber, Retourner, Arriver, Mourir, Partir* + **ALL Reflexive / Pronominal Verbs** (*se laver, se lever*).
  - Past participle **agrees with the subject in gender and number**:
    $$\text{Elle est } \mathbf{allée}\text{ à l'école. } (\text{feminine } +e)$$
    $$\text{Ils sont } \mathbf{partis}\text{ hier. } (\text{masculine plural } +s)$$
    $$\text{Elles sont } \mathbf{venues}\text{. } (\text{feminine plural } +es)$$
- **Agreement of Past Participle with Avoir (*L'accord avec Avoir*):**
  - The past participle conjugated with *avoir* agrees with the **PRECEDING DIRECT OBJECT (COD)** if placed before the auxiliary:
    $$\text{J'ai acheté les mangues. } \to \text{Les mangues que j'ai } \mathbf{achetées}\text{ sont sucrées.}$$
    $$\text{Voici ma nouvelle montre; mon père me l'a } \mathbf{donnée}\text{.}$$
- **The Subjunctive Mood (*Le Subjonctif*):**
  - Expresses subjectivity, doubt, emotion, obligation, and desire; introduced by conjunction **que**:
    - *Necessity:* **Il faut que** tu viennes.
    - *Desire:* **Je veux que** vous fassiez vos devoirs.
    - *Emotion:* **Je suis content que** tu sois là.

# SECTION 2: VOCABULARY, IDIOMS & FALSE FRIENDS

## 4. Essential Idiomatic Expressions with Avoir and Faire
- **Expressions with Avoir:**
  - *Avoir faim* (To be hungry) | *Avoir soif* (To be thirsty)
  - *Avoir chaud* (To be warm/hot) | *Avoir froid* (To be cold)
  - *Avoir peur (de)* (To be afraid of)
  - *Avoir sommeil* (To be sleepy)
  - *Avoir raison* (To be right) | *Avoir tort* (To be wrong)
  - *Avoir l'air (de)* (To look/seem like)
  - *Avoir besoin de* (To need / to have need of)
  - *Avoir envie de* (To feel like / fancy doing something)
  - *Avoir ... ans* (To be ... years old — e.g., *J'ai 16 ans*, NOT *Je suis 16 ans!*)
- **Expressions with Faire:**
  - *Faire beau / mauvais* (To be good/bad weather)
  - *Faire chaud / froid* (To be hot/cold weather)
  - *Faire attention* (To pay attention / be careful)
  - *Faire la queue* (To stand in a queue / line up)

## 5. False Friends (Les Faux Amis)
French words that resemble English words but possess completely different meanings:
| French Word | Common Mistaken Meaning | **Actual True English Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Actuellement** | Actually | **Currently / At present** |
| **Attendre** | To attend | **To wait for** (*Assister à* = to attend) |
| **Blesser** | To bless | **To injure / wound** (*Bénir* = to bless) |
| **Demander** | To demand | **To ask / request** (*Exiger* = to demand) |
| **Librairie** | Library | **Bookshop / Bookstore** (*Bibliothèque* = library) |
| **Monnaie** | Money | **Loose change / coins** (*Argent* = money) |
| **Rester** | To rest | **To stay / remain** (*Se reposer* = to rest) |
| **Journée** | Journey | **Full day / daytime** (*Voyage* = journey) |
| **Formidable** | Formidable / threatening | **Wonderful / Great / Terrific** |

# SECTION 3: PHONETICS, PRONUNCIATION & CULTURE

## 6. Phonetics and Silent Letters
- **Nasal Vowels:**
  - [*ã*] spelled: *an, am, en, em* (e.g., *enfant, champ, temps*).
  - [*õ*] spelled: *on, om* (e.g., *bonbon, ombre*).
  - [*ɛ̃*] spelled: *in, im, ain, aim, ein* (e.g., *matin, pain, faim, plein*).
- **Silent Final Consonants (The "Careful" Rule):**
  - In French, final consonants (*-d, -p, -s, -t, -x, -z*) are **SILENT** (e.g., *chaud, trop, français, petit, nez*).
  - **Exceptions (The CRFL rule):** Final consonants **C, R, F, L** are usually **PRONOUNCED** (e.g., *sa**c**, ave**c**, me**r**, acti**f**, chefa**l**, bébi**l**).
- **Liaison:** Linking the normally silent final consonant of a word to the initial vowel of the following word:
  - *Les amis* is pronounced as **/le-za-mi/** (the silent 's' sounds like 'z').
  - *Un grand homme* is pronounced as **/un gran-to-me/** (final 'd' sounds like 't').

## 7. Francophone Culture and Civilization
- **France (La République Française):**
  - Capital: **Paris**; Currency: **Euro (€)**; Motto: **Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité**.
  - National Anthem: **La Marseillaise**; National Day: **14th of July (Bastille Day)**; Symbol: **Marianne** / The Gallic Rooster.
  - River Seine flows through Paris; River Loire is the longest river in France.
- **Francophone Africa (Neighboring Countries of Nigeria):**
  - Nigeria is surrounded exclusively by Francophone African nations:
    - West: **Republic of Benin** (Capital: Porto-Novo).
    - North: **Niger Republic** (Capital: Niamey).
    - East: **Chad** (Capital: N'Djamena) and **Cameroon** (Capital: Yaoundé).
  - Currency used by Francophone West and Central African nations: **Franc CFA** (issued by BCEAO and BEAC).
  - **OIF (Organisation Internationale de la Francophonie):** Global organization representing countries where French is the official or customary language.

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB FRENCH

1. **Agreement with Preceding COD (*Avoir*):** Look before the verb! In *Les lettres qu'il a écrites*, *lettres* is feminine plural placed before *avoir*, so the past participle must end with **-es**.
2. **Negative Partitive Transformation:** After *ne... pas*, change *du, de la, des* to **de / d'**. E.g., *Je ne veux pas **de** sucre*.
3. **Age Idiom Uses Avoir:** In French, you "have" years, you are not years old. The correct formulation is **J'ai 16 ans**, never *Je suis 16 ans*.
4. **False Friend *Actuellement*:** *Actuellement* means **currently / at present**, NOT "actually".
5. **False Friend *Librairie*:** A *librairie* is a **bookshop** where you purchase books; a *bibliothèque* is a public **library** where you borrow books.
6. **Relative Pronoun Shortcut:** If the relative pronoun is immediately followed by a **VERB**, the answer is **QUI**. If followed by a **SUBJECT NOUN/PRONOUN**, the answer is **QUE (QU')**.
7. **Weather Idiom Uses Faire:** For weather conditions, always use the verb *faire* (e.g., **Il fait beau**, **Il fait chaud**).
8. **BANGS Adjectives Position:** Adjectives of Beauty, Age, Number, Goodness, and Size precede the noun (e.g., *un **beau** garçon*, *une **petite** maison*).
9. **DR MRS VANDERTRAMP Verbs Take Être:** All movement verbs in this acronym use auxiliary **être** in the Passé Composé and their past participles **must agree with the subject**.
10. **Pronouncing Liaison "S":** When a silent plural '-s' links to a vowel in liaison (*les enfants*), it is pronounced phonetically as a **[z]** sound.

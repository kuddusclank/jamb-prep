## Overview & How JAMB Tests Yoruba

In the UTME, Yoruba is examined via **40 multiple-choice questions** based on standard written Yoruba (*Yoruba Ajumolo*). The examination assesses three foundational areas:
1. **Ede ati Girama (Language, Phonology & Grammar — ~16 Questions):**
   - Reading comprehension (*Akaye*), essay structure and formal writing (*Aroko*), standard orthography (*Akotọ Ede Yoruba*), 25-letter alphabet, oral and nasal vowels (*Faweli Airanmupe ati Faweli Aranmupe*), consonants (*Konsonanti*), tone system and tone marking (*Ami Ohun* — Do, Re, Mi), vowel elision (*Iparesi Faweli*), vowel contraction (*Isunki Faweli*), parts of speech (*Isori Ọrọ*), word morphology/derivation (*Ihun Ọrọ*), sentence structures (*Gbolohun*), and translation (*Itumo Ede*).
2. **Litireso (Oral & Written Literature — ~14 Questions):**
   - Oral literature (*Litireso Alohun*): Folktales (*Alo Apamo* — riddles, *Alo Apagbe* — moral stories with songs), praise poetry (*Oriki*), occupational and cult chants (*Ijala* hunters' chants, *Esa/Iwi Egungun* masquerade chants, *Iyere Ifa* divination chants, *Rara* praise songs), proverbs (*Owe*), and idiomatic expressions (*Agbegbọ Ọrọ*).
   - Written literature (*Litireso Apilẹkọ*): Critical analysis of prescribed texts in drama (*Ere Onise*), prose/novels (*Itan Aroso*), and modern poetry (*Ewi*), examining plot (*Idite*), setting (*Ibudo Itan*), characterization (*Iwa ati Eda Itan*), themes (*Koko Ọrọ*), and literary devices (*Ona Ede*).
3. **Asa ati Isese (Culture, Philosophy & Institutions — ~10 Questions):**
   - Life cycle and rites of passage (*Igbese Aye Eniyan*): pregnancy and birth rituals, naming ceremonies (*Isomọlorukọ*), traditional marriage (*Igbeyawo*), chieftaincy titles (*Oye Jije*), and funeral ceremonies (*Isinku*).
   - Governance and social structures: kingship (*Eto Ọba Alaye*), kingmakers (*Ọyọmesi, Iwarefa*), town councils, and age grades (*Egbe*).
   - Religion and metaphysics: Olodumare (Supreme God), major divinities (*Orisa* — Ògún, Sàngó, Ọya, Ọbàtálá, Ẹ̀ṣù, Ọ̀rúnmìlà), ancestral veneration (*Egungun*), reincarnation beliefs (*Akudaaya, Abiku, Emere*), secret cults (*Oro, Ogboni*), traditional occupations, attire (*Aso Ibile*), and cuisine.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Distinction in JAMB Yoruba:**
> - Standard Yoruba alphabet consists of **25 letters**:
>   - **7 Oral Vowels (*Faweli Airanmupe*):** `a, e, ẹ, i, o, ọ, u`
>   - **5 Nasal Vowels (*Faweli Aranmupe*):** `an, ẹn, in, ọn, un` (Note: `en` and `on` do not exist as nasal vowels in standard Yoruba orthography; their corresponding nasal forms use sub-dots: `ẹn` and `ọn`!).
> - **The Three Tones (*Ami Ohun Mẹta*):**
>   - **Ami Oke (High Tone):** `/` (*Mí*)
>   - **Ami Aarin (Mid Tone):** Unmarked (*Re*)
>   - **Ami Isale (Low Tone):** `\` (*Dò*)
>   - **Tone transforms meaning completely:** e.g., `ọwọ́` (hand) vs `ọ̀wọ̀` (respect/honor) vs `ọwọ̀` (broom) vs `owó` (money).

## Detailed Topic Summaries

### 1. Ede ati Fonoloji (Language & Phonology)

#### A. Alifabeeti Ede Yoruba (The Yoruba Alphabet)
The standard modern Yoruba orthography comprises **25 letters**:
- **18 Consonants (*Konsonanti*):** `b, d, f, g, gb, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, ṣ, t, w, y`.
  - Notice the digraph `gb` counts as a single consonant letter.
  - The letter `ṣ` carries a sub-dot and represents the voiceless postalveolar fricative `/ʃ/`.
  - The letter `p` is phonetically a voiceless labial-velar plosive `/k͡p/`.
- **7 Vowels (*Faweli*):** `a, e, ẹ, i, o, ọ, u`.
  - Half-close vowels: `e, o`.
  - Half-open vowels (with sub-dots): `ẹ, ọ`.
  - Open vowel: `a`.
  - Close vowels: `i, u`.

#### B. Ami Ohun (Tone System)
Yoruba is a strict tonal language with three register tones:
1. **Ami Oke (High Tone - *Mi*):** Represented by an acute accent (`´`). Examples: *wá* (come), *bá* (meet), *tó* (enough).
2. **Ami Aarin (Mid Tone - *Re*):** Represented by **no mark** on the vowel. Examples: *lo* (go), *je* (eat), *ba* (perch).
3. **Ami Isale (Low Tone - *Do*):** Represented by a grave accent (`` ` ``). Examples: *rò* (think), *kà* (read), *wọ̀* (wear).
- **Tone changes in nouns vs verbs:** Verbs never begin with a vowel in Yoruba. Nouns derived from verbs typically take a vowel prefix with a low or mid tone (e.g., *kọ* [teach] -> *olùkọ́* [teacher]; *sùn* [sleep] -> *oorun* / *orun* [sleep]).

#### C. Iparesi ati Isunki (Elision & Contraction)
- **Iparesi Faweli (Vowel Elision):** When two vowels meet at word boundaries (usually verb + noun), one vowel is dropped to ensure fluid pronunciation:
  - *Ta + ẹja* -> *Tẹja* (vowel `a` is elided).
  - *Ra + iwe* -> *Rawe* (vowel `i` is elided).
- **Isunki (Vowel Contraction):** When two vowels blend together into a single lengthened or modified vowel bearing a compound tone (e.g., *Ko + iwe* -> *Kọ̀wé*; *Mu + ẹkọ* -> *Mọ́kọ*).

#### D. Isori Ọrọ (Parts of Speech)
1. **Ọrọ-Orukọ (Noun):** Refers to persons, places, animals, or things. Every indigenous Yoruba noun begins with a vowel (except those starting with the syllabic nasal `n` or loan words).
2. **Ọrọ-Aropo-Orukọ (Pronoun):**
   - First person: *Èmi / Mo* (I), *Àwa / A* (We).
   - Second person: *Ìwọ / O* (You singular), *Ẹ̀yin / Ẹ* (You plural / honorific).
   - Third person: *Òun / Ó* (He/She/It), *Àwọn / Wọ́n* (They).
3. **Ọrọ-Iṣe (Verb):** Action or state words. Always begin with a consonant. Types: *Ọrọ-iṣe alakọbẹrẹ* (transitive) and *alaikọbẹrẹ* (intransitive).
4. **Ọrọ-Aponle (Adverb):** Modifies verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs (e.g., *kia-kia*, *fẹẹrẹ*, *dara-dara*).
5. **Ọrọ-Apejuwe (Adjective):** Qualifies nouns; placed after the noun (e.g., *aṣọ pupa*, *ọmọ rere*).
6. **Ọrọ-Asopọ (Conjunction):** Connects clauses (e.g., *ati*, *sugbọn*, *tabi*, *nitori*).
7. **Ọrọ-Atokun (Preposition):** Standard prepositions include *ni*, *si*, *lati*, *fun*.

#### E. Gbolohun Ede Yoruba (Sentence Structure)
- Basic word order: **SVO (Oluwa + Ọrọ-iṣe + Abo — Subject + Verb + Object)**.
- Structural classifications:
  - *Gbolohun Eleeyọ* (Simple sentence — one finite verb).
  - *Gbolohun Olopo* (Compound sentence — joined by coordinating conjunctions like *ati*, *sugbọn*).
  - *Gbolohun Alakanpọ* (Complex sentence — principal clause + subordinate clause introduced by *ti*, *bi*, etc.).

### 2. Litireso (Oral & Written Literature)

#### A. Litireso Alohun (Oral Literature)
- **Alo Apamo (Riddles):** Word puzzles testing intellect and metaphorical association. Opening formula: *"Àlọ́ o!"* Response: *"Àlọ̀!"*
- **Alo Apagbe (Folktales):** Didactic narratives punctuated with songs (*orin àlọ́*). The central protagonist is usually **Ìjàpá (The Tortoise)** and his long-suffering wife **Yánníbo**. Tortoise represents cunning tricks, selfish greed, and inevitable downfall, teaching moral righteousness (*Iwa Rere*).
- **Ewi Alohun (Oral Poetry & Chants):**
  - **Oriki:** Praises of lineage (*Oriki Idile*), towns (*Oriki Orilẹ*), kings, and divinities. It celebrates bravery, noble lineage, and historical achievements.
  - **Ijala:** Chants of hunters (*Ọdẹ*) and iron-workers dedicated to **Ògún**. Themes feature wild animal lore, hunting exploits, and forest courage.
  - **Esa / Iwi Egungun:** Melodious, strained-voice chants of ancestral masqueraders (*Egungun*), rich in social critique, ancestral eulogies, and moral counsel.
  - **Iyere Ifa:** Chants recited by the Babalawo during Ifa divination sessions, reflecting ancient metaphysical philosophy and herbal wisdom.
  - **Rara:** Secular chants by professional bards celebrating patrons, social events, and weddings.
- **Owe ati Agbegbọ Ọrọ (Proverbs & Idioms):** Proverbs are the horses of speech (*"Owe lẹṣin ọrọ, bi ọrọ ba sọnu, owe la fi n wa a"*). They resolve conflicts, convey moral philosophy, and add cultural depth.

#### B. Litireso Apilẹkọ (Written Literature)
- **Ere Onise (Drama):** Divided into acts (*Ipele*) and scenes (*Eseran*). Types include *Ere Idanilaraya / Apanilerin* (Comedy), *Ere Banininuje* (Tragedy), and *Ere Afojudi* (Satire).
- **Itan Aroso (Prose / Novels):** Narrative fiction pioneered by D.O. Fagunwa (*Ogboju Ọdẹ Ninu Igbo Irunmale*, 1938), characterized by magical realism, moral allegories, rich vocabulary, and mythical quests.
- **Ewi Apilẹkọ (Modern Written Poetry):** Structured in stanzas (*Ese Ewi*) and lines (*Ila Ewi*).
- **Ona Ede (Literary & Figurative Devices):**
  - *Afiwe Taara (Simile):* Direct comparison using *bi*, *dabi*, *gẹgẹ bi*.
  - *Afiwe Afarawe (Metaphor):* Implicit comparison without comparative markers.
  - *Fiyeniyan (Personification):* Giving human attributes to animals or inanimate objects.
  - *Asọdun / Asọle (Hyperbole / Understatement).*
  - *Iforogbale / Afojudi (Irony / Sarcasm).*
  - *Iro-Ninu-Ọrọ (Onomatopoeia):* Words that echo natural sounds (e.g., *gbàù*, *yọ̀rọ̀-yọ̀rọ̀*).

### 3. Asa ati Isese (Culture, Institutions & Worldview)

#### A. Igbese Aye Eniyan (Rites of Passage)
- **Oyún Nini ati Ibimo (Pregnancy and Childbirth):** Protected with herbal remedies and taboos. Twins (*Ìbejì*) are revered with special rites; the firstborn is **Táyéwò** (come to taste the world) and the second is **Kẹ́hìndé** (the senior twin who arrived last). Special children include **Àbíkú** (born to die repeatedly).
- **Isomọlorukọ (Naming Ceremony):** Traditionally held on the **8th day** for all children (in some lineages, 7th for females, 9th for males). Elements used:
  - *Oyiri / Oyin (Honey):* For a sweet, peaceful life.
  - *Iyo (Salt):* To add taste, preservation, and prevent misfortune.
  - *Ataare (Alligator pepper):* For resilience and fruitful endeavors.
  - *Oti (Schnapps/Wine):* For societal favor and standing.
  - *Epo Pupa (Palm oil):* To smooth the storms of life.
  - *Omi (Water):* As water has no enemies (*"Omi o lota"*), the child will live without hostility.
  - *Owo (Money/Cowries):* For wealth and financial prosperity.
- **Igbeyawo (Marriage):**
  1. *Ijọsọ / Ifẹ (Courtship).*
  2. *Itọrọ / Isihun (Formal proposal and asking for consent).*
  3. *Idana (Engagement / bride price presentation):* Includes yam tubers (*Isu Idana*), honey, salt, bitter kola (*Orogbo*), kolanut (*Obi abata*), and traditional clothing fabrics.
  4. *Igbeyawo (Wedding ceremony):* Groom and friends prostrate (*Idoobale*) before the bride's parents. The bride's feet are washed (*Iwe-ẹsẹ*) before stepping into her husband's home.
- **Isinku (Funeral Ceremonies):** Natural death in ripe old age (*Oku Agba*) is celebrated with dancing, feasts, and animal sacrifices. Accidental or abominable deaths (*Oku Ofo / Oku Agbako* — by lightning, drowning, or suicide) are buried without festivities outside town by specialized priests.

#### B. Eto Ijoba Ibile (Traditional Political Systems)
- **Ọba Alaye (The Monarch):** Spiritual and political head (*Alase Ekeji Orisa*). Rules through royal decrees and holds sacred authority.
- **Igbimo Awọn Oloye (Council of Chiefs):**
  - **Ọyọmesi (In Oyo Kingdom):** Supreme council of seven non-royal chiefs headed by the **Baṣọrun**. They possess the constitutional power to check the Alaafin; if the king becomes tyrannical, the Baṣọrun presents him with an empty calabash (*Igba Iku*), compelling him to commit suicide.
  - **Iwarefa / Ogboni:** Judicial and religious council resolving land and chieftaincy disputes.
- **Egbe (Age Grades):** Mobilize community labor, defend territorial boundaries, and execute communal development projects.

#### C. Esin ati Igbagbo (Religion & Metaphysics)
- **Olodumare / Olorun:** The Supreme Creator, sovereign, omnipotent, and eternal.
- **Awọn Orisa (Divinities):**
  - **Ògún:** Deity of iron, technology, hunting, and war. Welcomes vows sworn on iron implements.
  - **Sàngó:** Deity of thunder, lightning, and justice. Represented by the double-bladed axe (*Oṣe Sango*).
  - **Ọya:** Goddess of the River Niger, tempest winds, and hurricane; loyal wife of Sango.
  - **Ọbàtálá / Òrìṣà-Ńlá:** Deity of creation, mold of human physical forms, custodian of purity, associated with the color white (*Aṣọ funfun*).
  - **Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ifá:** Deity of wisdom, divination, and destiny; communicated through the 16 primary *Odu Ifa*.
  - **Ẹ̀ṣù:** Deity of crossroads, choice, and divine mediation; conveys sacrifices from mortals to Olodumare.
- **Egungun (Ancestral Masquerade):** Physical manifestation of the departed ancestors visiting earth to bless, heal, and enforce communal moral purity.

## 10 Golden Exam Strategies for JAMB Yoruba

1. **Master the 3-Tone System (*Do, Re, Mi*):** Always identify the tone marks (`\`, unmarked, `/`). JAMB frequently asks for the accurate tonal pattern of words (e.g., *Ìwé* = Low-High / *Do-Mi*; *Ọlọ́pàá* = Mid-High-High / *Re-Mi-Mi*).
2. **Distinguish Oral vs Nasal Vowels:** Remember that `ẹ` and `ọ` take the nasal `n` (`ẹn`, `ọn`), whereas `e` and `o` do not form standard nasal pairs.
3. **Know the Traditional Elements of Isomọlorukọ:** Memorize the symbolic meanings of *oyin* (sweetness), *iyọ̀* (preservation), *ataare* (resilience), *orógbó* (longevity), and *omi* (peace).
4. **Learn the Names and Birth Order of Twins:** *Táyéwò* is the first to arrive; *Kẹ́hìndé* is the second. The child born immediately after twins is **Ìdòwú**, followed by **Àlàbá**, and then **Ìdògbé**.
5. **Differentiate Oral Poetic Chants by Audience/Profession:**
   - *Ijala* = Hunters / Ogun devotees.
   - *Esa / Iwi* = Egungun masquerade cult.
   - *Iyere Ifa* = Babalawo / Ifa divination.
   - *Oriki* = Lineage praise poetry.
6. **Understand the Constitutional Check of the Ọyọmesi:** The Baṣọrun heads the Ọyọmesi and presents the calabash (*Igba*) to an oppressive Alaafin.
7. **Spot Vowel Elision Rules in Compound Words:** Be ready to identify which vowel drops when two words combine (e.g., *kẹ́ + ẹrù* -> *kẹ́rù*).
8. **Analyze Prescribed Literary Texts Early:** Know the main characters (*Ẹdá Ìtàn*), themes (*Kókó Ọ̀rọ̀*), and climax in the prescribed JAMB drama, poetry, and prose texts.
9. **Recognize Idiomatic Expressions (*Agbegbọ Ọrọ*):** Expressions like *"Gbá igi dánù"* (die), *"Tẹ orí ba"* (surrender/bow), *"Je gbi"* (suffer severe consequences) are tested regularly.
10. **Allocate 25 Minutes Maximum:** Answer all 40 questions systematically. Read questions first before tackling the comprehension passage (*Akaye*) to locate key details quickly.

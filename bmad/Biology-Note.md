## Overview & How JAMB Tests Biology

In the UTME, Biology is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** spanning four core thematic areas:
1. **Variety of Organisms & Cell Biology (~6 Questions):** Characteristics of life, levels of organization, diagnostic features of kingdoms/phyla, cell structure and organelles, plant vs animal cells, diffusion, osmosis, plasmolysis, mitosis, and meiosis.
2. **Form and Function / Physiology (~16 Questions):** Plant and mammalian nutrition, digestion, circulatory and transport systems, respiration and gaseous exchange, excretion and osmoregulation, skeletal support and locomotion, nervous and hormonal coordination, sense organs (eye/ear), plant tropisms/hormones, and reproductive biology.
3. **Ecology & Environmental Biology (~10 Questions):** Ecosystems, energy flow, food chains/webs, ecological pyramids, nutrient cycles (Nitrogen, Carbon, Water), ecological associations, succession, Nigerian biomes, pollution, and conservation.
4. **Heredity, Genetics, and Evolution (~8 Questions):** Mendelian crosses, monohybrid/dihybrid ratios, sex determination, sex-linked traits, blood group inheritance, morphological vs physiological variation, theories of evolution (Lamarck vs Darwin), and evidence of evolution.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Characteristics for JAMB Biology:**
> - High frequency of diagrammatic questions (identifying structures and functions of the mammalian nephron, eye, reflex arc, heart, flower parts, and seed anatomy).
> - Frequent questions on experimental controls (e.g., destarching leaves before photosynthesis experiments, using potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) to absorb $\text{CO}_2$, and boiling in ethanol to extract chlorophyll).
> - Strict testing on precise biological terminology (e.g., distinguishing between homologous vs analogous organs, and endosperm ($3n$) vs embryo ($2n$)).

# SECTION 1: LIVING ORGANISMS AND CELL BIOLOGY

## 1. Classification & Diagnostic Features of Kingdoms
- **Levels of Biological Organization:** Organelle $\to$ Cell $\to$ Tissue $\to$ Organ $\to$ Organ System $\to$ Organism.
- **Five Kingdom Classification System:**
  1. **Monera (Prokaryotes):** Unicellular, no true membrane-bound nucleus or organelles, circular naked DNA, peptidoglycan cell wall. Examples: Bacteria, Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae).
  2. **Protista (Protoctista):** Unicellular or colonial eukaryotes, true nucleus and organelles. Examples: *Amoeba* (pseudopodia), *Paramecium* (cilia, dual nuclei), *Euglena* (flagellum, chloroplasts, eyespot/stigma — exhibits both plant and animal characteristics).
  3. **Fungi:** Non-photosynthetic, heterotrophic (saprophytic or parasitic), chitinous cell walls, body composed of thread-like hyphae forming a mycelium. Store carbohydrate as **glycogen** (not starch). Examples: *Rhizopus* (bread mould), *Yeast* (unicellular), *Mushroom*.
  4. **Plantae:** Multicellular autotrophs, cellulose cell walls, contain chlorophyll, store carbohydrate as **starch**.
     - *Thallophyta (Algae):* Simple aquatic plants, no vascular tissue (e.g., *Spirogyra* with spiral chloroplast).
     - *Bryophyta:* Non-vascular terrestrial/amphibious plants, lack true roots/stems/leaves, reproduce by spores, show alternation of generations with dominant gametophyte. Examples: Mosses (*Funaria*), Liverworts.
     - *Pteridophyta:* Seedless vascular plants (have xylem and phloem), true roots/stems/leaves, dominant sporophyte. Example: Ferns (*Dryopteris*).
     - *Spermatophyta (Seed Plants):*
       - *Gymnosperms:* Naked seeds borne on cones, non-flowering (e.g., *Pinus*, Cycads).
       - *Angiosperms:* Flowering plants, enclosed seeds inside fruit. Divided into:
         - **Monocotyledons:** One cotyledon, parallel leaf venation, fibrous root system, vascular bundles scattered in stem, trimerous flowers (multiples of 3). Examples: Maize, rice, palm, grasses.
         - **Dicotyledons:** Two cotyledons, net/reticulate venation, taproot system, vascular bundles arranged in a distinct ring with cambium (secondary growth), tetramerous/pentamerous flowers (multiples of 4 or 5). Examples: Beans, mango, cowpea.
  5. **Animalia:** Multicellular heterotrophs, lack cell walls and chlorophyll, exhibit locomotion.
     - *Invertebrates:* Porifera (sponges, porous), Coelenterata/Cnidaria (hydra, sea anemone, stinging nematocysts, radial symmetry), Platyhelminthes (flatworms, bilateral, acoelomate, e.g., *Taenia*/tapeworm, *Planaria*), Nematoda (roundworms, pseudocoelomate, e.g., *Ascaris*), Annelida (segmented worms, true coelom, e.g., Earthworm with clitellum), Mollusca (soft-bodied, muscular foot, calcareous shell, e.g., Snail, Squid), Arthropoda (jointed appendages, chitinous exoskeleton, segmented body).
     - *Classes of Arthropoda:*
       - **Insecta:** 3 body parts (head, thorax, abdomen), 3 pairs of walking legs on thorax, 1 pair of antennae, compound eyes, breathe via tracheae. Examples: Housefly, Mosquito, Cockroach.
       - **Arachnida:** 2 body parts (cephalothorax, abdomen), 4 pairs of legs, no antennae, simple eyes, breathe via book lungs. Examples: Spider, Scorpion, Tick.
       - **Crustacea:** Cephalothorax and abdomen, 5 or more pairs of appendages, 2 pairs of antennae, breathe via gills. Examples: Crab, Prawn, Crayfish.
       - **Myriapoda:** Many segments with legs (Centipede: 1 pair per segment, carnivorous, venomous; Millipede: 2 pairs per segment, herbivorous, rolls into spiral when touched).
     - *Vertebrates (Phylum Chordata):*
       - **Pisces (Fish):** Cold-blooded (poikilothermic), 2-chambered heart (1 atrium, 1 ventricle), breathe by gills with operculum, scales, fins.
       - **Amphibia (Frogs, Toads):** Poikilothermic, 3-chambered heart (2 atria, 1 ventricle), metamorphosis, breathe by gills (tadpole), skin/lungs/mouth (adult).
       - **Reptilia (Lizards, Snakes):** Poikilothermic, 3-chambered heart (ventricle partially divided; crocodile has 4 chambers), dry horny scales, lay cleidoic (shelled) eggs.
       - **Aves (Birds):** Warm-blooded (homoiothermic), 4-chambered heart, feathers, toothless beak, forelimbs modified into wings, pneumatic (hollow) bones.
       - **Mammalia:** Homoiothermic, 4-chambered heart, possess hair/fur, mammary glands for suckling young, external ear pinnae, heterodont dentition, diaphragm.

## 2. Cell Organelles, Movement of Molecules, and Cell Division
- **Organelles & Functions:**
  - *Mitochondria:* Site of cellular respiration and ATP generation ("powerhouse of the cell"). Inner membrane folded into cristae.
  - *Ribosomes:* Sites of protein synthesis.
  - *Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):* Rough ER (studded with ribosomes, transports proteins); Smooth ER (synthesizes lipids and detoxifies chemicals).
  - *Golgi Apparatus:* Packages, modifies, and secretes cellular products and enzymes.
  - *Chloroplasts:* Double-membraned organelle containing chlorophyll in thylakoids/grana for photosynthesis.
  - *Vacuole:* Large and central in plant cells (maintains turgidity); small, temporary, or contractile (osmoregulation) in animals/protozoa.
  - *Nucleus:* Contains chromosomes/DNA, controls all metabolic activities.
  - *Lysosomes:* Contain hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion and autolysis ("suicide bags").
- **Plant Cell vs Animal Cell Differences:**
  - Plant cells have: Cellulose cell wall, large permanent central vacuole, chloroplasts, rigid shape, starch grains.
  - Animal cells have: Only flexible cell membrane, small temporary vacuoles, centrosomes/centrioles (form spindle fibers during division), glycogen granules.
- **Transport Across Membranes:**
  - *Diffusion:* Passive movement of solute molecules from a region of higher to lower concentration down a concentration gradient.
  - *Osmosis:* Movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (hypotonic/dilute solution) to lower water potential (hypertonic/concentrated solution) through a **semi-permeable membrane**.
  - *Effects on Cells:*
    - Plant cell in hypotonic solution $\to$ gains water, becomes **turgid** (cell wall prevents bursting).
    - Plant cell in hypertonic solution $\to$ loses water, protoplasm shrinks away from cell wall = **plasmolysis**.
    - Red blood cell in hypotonic solution $\to$ absorbs water and bursts = **hemolysis**.
    - Red blood cell in hypertonic solution $\to$ shrinks and crinkles = **crenation**.
  - *Active Transport:* Movement of substances against a concentration gradient requiring metabolic energy (**ATP**) and carrier proteins (e.g., mineral ion uptake by plant roots).
- **Cell Division:**
  - *Mitosis:* Division of somatic (body) cells yielding **two genetically identical diploid ($2n$) daughter cells**. Essential for growth, tissue repair, and asexual reproduction. Four phases: **Prophase, Metaphase** (chromosomes line up at equatorial plate), **Anaphase** (sister chromatids separate to opposite poles), **Telophase**.
  - *Meiosis:* Reduction division occurring in germ cells during gametogenesis yielding **four genetically distinct haploid ($n$) daughter cells**. Involves two successive divisions (Meiosis I & II). **Crossing over** (exchange of genetic material between non-sister chromatids) occurs in Prophase I, introducing genetic variation.

# SECTION 2: PLANT AND ANIMAL PHYSIOLOGY

## 3. Nutrition and Digestion
- **Autotrophic Nutrition (Photosynthesis):**
  $$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{chlorophyll}]{\text{sunlight}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$
  - *Light Reaction (in Grana/Thylakoids):* Photolysis of water ($2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^- + \text{O}_2$), generating ATP and NADPH.
  - *Dark Reaction / Calvin Cycle (in Stroma):* Reduction of $\text{CO}_2$ using ATP and NADPH to form glucose. Independent of light.
  - *Essential Mineral Elements:*
    - Nitrogen ($\text{N}$): Protein synthesis; deficiency causes stunted growth and severe **chlorosis** (yellowing of leaves).
    - Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$): Central atom in chlorophyll molecule; deficiency causes chlorosis.
    - Phosphorus ($\text{P}$): ATP and nucleic acids; deficiency causes poor root growth and purplish leaves.
    - Iron ($\text{Fe}$): Chlorophyll synthesis catalyst.
- **Mammalian Digestion & Enzymes:**
  - *Mouth:* Chewing, saliva containing **Ptyalin (Salivary Amylase)** breaks starch into maltose at neutral/slightly alkaline pH ($6.8-7.2$).
  - *Stomach:* Gastric juice contains **Hydrochloric Acid ($\text{HCl}$)** (kills germs, provides acidic pH $\approx 2$), **Pepsin** (converts proteins to peptones), and **Rennin** (curdles milk protein caseinogen in infants).
  - *Duodenum (Small Intestine):* Receives:
    - **Bile** (produced by liver, stored in gall bladder): Contains no enzymes; neutralizes acidic chyme and **emulsifies fats** into small droplets (increasing surface area).
    - **Pancreatic Juice:** Pancreatic Amylase (starch $\to$ maltose), **Trypsin** (proteins/peptones $\to$ polypeptides), and **Pancreatic Lipase** (emulsified fats $\to$ fatty acids and glycerol).
  - *Ileum:* Succus entericus (intestinal juice) completes digestion: Erepsin/Peptidases (peptides $\to$ amino acids), Maltase (maltose $\to 2$ glucose), Sucrase (sucrose $\to$ glucose + fructose), Lactase (lactose $\to$ glucose + galactose).
  - *Absorption:* Villi in ileum. Glucose, amino acids, vitamins, and minerals enter **blood capillaries** leading to hepatic portal vein. Fatty acids and glycerol enter **lacteals** leading to the lymphatic system.

## 4. Transport and Circulatory Systems
- **Plant Transport:**
  - **Xylem:** Dead tissue (vessels and tracheids) that transports water and dissolved mineral salts upward from roots to leaves. Driven by **transpiration pull**, root pressure, and capillarity.
  - **Phloem:** Living tissue (sieve tube elements and companion cells) that transports manufactured sucrose/organic solutes downward and upward by **translocation**.
  - **Transpiration:** Evaporative loss of water vapor from aerial parts of plants, predominantly through **stomata** (controlled by guard cells) and lenticels. Measured using a **potometer**. Factors increasing transpiration: High temperature, high wind speed, high light intensity, low atmospheric humidity.
- **Mammalian Circulatory System:**
  - *Blood Components:*
    - Plasma (55%): Liquid matrix transporting nutrients, urea, hormones, carbon dioxide (as bicarbonate ions $\text{HCO}_3^-$), and fibrinogen.
    - Erythrocytes (RBCs): Biconcave discs, no nucleus at maturity, contain **hemoglobin** ($4\text{Fe}$ atoms) which binds reversibly with oxygen to form **oxyhemoglobin**.
    - Leucocytes (WBCs): Phagocytes (engulf pathogens) and Lymphocytes (produce antibodies).
    - Thrombocytes (Platelets): Cell fragments initiating blood clotting:
      $$\text{Thromboplastin} + \text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{Prothrombin} \to \text{Thrombin}$$
      $$\text{Thrombin} + \text{Fibrinogen (soluble)} \to \text{Fibrin (insoluble mesh)}$$
  - *Blood Groups (ABO System):*
    - Group A: A-antigen on RBCs, anti-B antibody in plasma.
    - Group B: B-antigen on RBCs, anti-A antibody in plasma.
    - Group AB: Both A & B antigens, no antibodies $\implies$ **Universal Recipient**.
    - Group O: No antigens, both anti-A & anti-B antibodies $\implies$ **Universal Donor**.
    - Rhesus factor: $\text{Rh}^+$ has Rhesus antigen; $\text{Rh}^-$ mother carrying $\text{Rh}^+$ fetus can develop antibodies causing **erythroblastosis fetalis** in second pregnancy.
  - *Heart & Circulation:* 4 chambers. Deoxygenated blood returns from body via vena cava $\to$ right atrium $\to$ tricuspid valve $\to$ right ventricle $\to$ pulmonary artery $\to$ lungs. Oxygenated blood returns via pulmonary vein $\to$ left atrium $\to$ bicuspid (mitral) valve $\to$ left ventricle (thickest muscular wall) $\to$ aorta $\to$ systemic body circulation.

## 5. Respiration and Gaseous Exchange
- **Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration:**
  - *Aerobic:* Requires oxygen; completely oxidizes glucose:
    $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \to 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + 38\text{ ATP} \quad (\approx 2880\text{ kJ})$$
    - Glycolysis (in cytoplasm, anaerobic, net $2\text{ ATP}$) $\to$ Krebs Cycle (in mitochondrial matrix, $2\text{ ATP}$) $\to$ Electron Transport Chain (on cristae, $34\text{ ATP}$).
  - *Anaerobic Respiration:* Incomplete breakdown without oxygen:
    - In mammalian muscle cells during strenuous exercise: Glucose $\to$ Lactic acid + $2\text{ ATP}$ (accumulated lactic acid causes muscle fatigue and **oxygen debt**).
    - In yeast (Alcoholic fermentation): Glucose $\to 2\text{ Ethanol } (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}) + 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{ ATP}$.
- **Gaseous Exchange Surfaces in Organisms:**
  - Earthworm: Moist vascularized skin.
  - Fish: Gills with gill filaments and gill rakers; **counter-current exchange mechanism** maximizes oxygen extraction from water.
  - Insects: Tracheal system opening via **spiracles** on thorax and abdomen (no blood involvement in gas transport).
  - Amphibians: Tadpole (gills), adult frog (moist cutaneous skin, buccopharyngeal cavity, and lungs).
  - Mammals: Alveoli of lungs (one-cell thick, moist, extensive capillary network). Inhalation: Diaphragm contracts and flattens, external intercostal muscles contract, rib cage moves upward and outward, thoracic volume increases, pressure drops, air rushes in.

## 6. Excretion and Osmoregulation
- **Excretory Products Across Taxa:**
  - Ammonia: Highly toxic, requires vast amounts of water; excreted by aquatic invertebrates and bony fish.
  - Urea: Moderately toxic, water-soluble; excreted by mammals, adult amphibians, and cartilaginous fish.
  - Uric Acid: Insoluble non-toxic paste, conserves water; excreted by birds, reptiles, and terrestrial insects.
- **Excretory Organs in Lower Animals:**
  - *Amoeba / Paramecium:* Contractile vacuole (osmoregulation).
  - *Flatworms (Planaria, Tapeworm):* Flame cells / protonephridia.
  - *Earthworm:* Nephridia.
  - *Insects:* Malpighian tubules (absorb uric acid from hemolymph and empty into hindgut).
- **Mammalian Kidney Structure & Nephron Function:**
  1. **Ultrafiltration (at Malpighian Corpuscle):** High hydrostatic pressure in glomerulus (due to afferent arteriole being wider than efferent arteriole) forces water, glucose, amino acids, urea, and mineral salts through Bowman's capsule into the nephron, forming the **glomerular filtrate**. Blood cells and plasma proteins remain in blood.
  2. **Selective Reabsorption (at Proximal Convoluted Tubule):** 100% of glucose and amino acids, and ~80% of water and sodium ions are reabsorbed back into peritubular capillaries by active transport and osmosis.
  3. **Loop of Henle:** Counter-current multiplier creating high osmotic concentration in renal medulla to facilitate water reabsorption.
  4. **Hormonal Osmoregulation (Distal Tubule & Collecting Duct):**
     - **Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin):** Secreted by posterior pituitary when blood is concentrated (dehydration). Increases permeability of collecting duct to water $\to$ more water reabsorbed into blood $\to$ small volume of concentrated urine produced.
     - **Aldosterone:** Regulates reabsorption of sodium ions ($\text{Na}^+$).

## 7. Support, Locomotion, and Skeletal Systems
- **Skeletal Types:**
  - Hydrostatic skeleton: Fluid-filled coelom under pressure (earthworm, sea anemone).
  - Exoskeleton: Outer cuticle made of **chitin**, strengthened with $\text{CaCO}_3$ in crustaceans; shed periodically via **ecdysis (moulting)** to permit growth (arthropods).
  - Endoskeleton: Internal framework of bones and cartilage (vertebrates).
- **Mammalian Skeleton Structure:**
  - *Axial Skeleton:* Skull, vertebral column, ribs (12 pairs: 7 true, 3 false, 2 floating), sternum.
    - Vertebral Column:
      - **Cervical (7):** Neck region. Atlas (1st vertebra, ring-shaped, articulates with skull for nodding "yes" movements) and Axis (2nd vertebra, has **odontoid process** acting as pivot for rotational "no" movements).
      - **Thoracic (12):** Long neural spine, have articulating surfaces for ribs.
      - **Lumbar (5):** Large centrum, prominent extra processes (metapophyses/anapophyses) for strong abdominal muscle attachment.
      - **Sacral (5 fused):** Fused with pelvic girdle to form sacrum.
      - **Caudal (4 fused):** Reduced to form coccyx (tailbone).
  - *Appendicular Skeleton:* Pectoral girdle (scapula, clavicle) with arms; Pelvic girdle (ilium, ischium, pubis) with legs.
- **Joints:**
  - Ball and Socket Joint: Movement in all planes ($360^\circ$ rotation). Found at shoulder (glenoid cavity) and hip (acetabulum).
  - Hinge Joint: Movement in one plane only ($180^\circ$). Found at elbow and knee.
  - Suture Joint: Immovable fibrous joint (skull bones).

## 8. Coordination, Nervous System, and Sense Organs
- **Nervous vs Endocrine Coordination:**
  - Nervous: Electrical impulses along neurons, rapid, short-lived, localized response.
  - Endocrine: Chemical messengers (hormones) transported via bloodstream, slower, long-lasting, widespread target organs.
- **The Reflex Arc:**
  $$\text{Receptor (Sense Organ)} \to \text{Sensory Neuron} \to \text{Relay / Intermediate Neuron (Spinal Cord)} \to \text{Motor Neuron} \to \text{Effector (Muscle / Gland)}$$
- **The Human Brain:**
  - *Cerebrum:* Largest part; responsible for intelligence, memory, voluntary actions, thought, consciousness, and interpretation of sensations.
  - *Cerebellum:* Coordinates voluntary muscular movements, posture, and balance.
  - *Medulla Oblongata:* Controls vital involuntary reflexes (heartbeat, breathing rate, blood pressure, peristalsis, swallowing).
- **The Eye & Common Vision Defects:**
  - Photoreceptors on Retina: **Rods** (sensitive to dim light, peripheral vision, contain rhodopsin) and **Cones** (sensitive to bright light and color vision; concentrated at **fovea centralis / yellow spot** where vision is sharpest). **Blind spot** has no photoreceptors (where optic nerve exits).
  - *Myopia (Short-sightedness):* Eyeball too long or lens too convex; distant images form in front of retina. Corrected with a **biconcave (diverging) lens**.
  - *Hypermetropia (Long-sightedness):* Eyeball too short; near images form behind retina. Corrected with a **biconvex (converging) lens**.
  - *Cataract:* Lens becomes opaque/cloudy; treated by surgical replacement.
  - *Glaucoma:* Increased intraocular pressure due to blocked aqueous humor drainage; leads to optic nerve damage and blindness.
- **The Ear:**
  - Outer ear: Pinna collects sound waves into external auditory meatus.
  - Middle ear: Tympanic membrane (eardrum) transmits vibrations to 3 ossicles: **Malleus (hammer) $\to$ Incus (anvil) $\to$ Stapes (stirrup)**, which amplify pressure onto the oval window. **Eustachian tube** equalizes air pressure between middle ear cavity and atmosphere.
  - Inner ear: **Cochlea** (contains Organ of Corti with sensory hair cells for hearing) and **Semicircular canals** (contain endolymph and ampullae for maintaining dynamic balance).
- **Plant Tropisms and Hormones:**
  - *Tropisms:* Directional growth movements in response to unilateral stimuli:
    - Phototropism: Shoot is positively phototropic (bends toward light because **auxins** migrate to shaded side and stimulate cell elongation); Root is negatively phototropic.
    - Geotropism: Shoot is negatively geotropic; Root is positively geotropic (auxins accumulate on lower side and inhibit cell elongation in roots).
  - *Plant Hormones:*
    - **Auxins (IAA):** Promote cell elongation, apical dominance, and root initiation.
    - **Gibberellins:** Promote stem elongation, break seed dormancy, trigger flowering.
    - **Cytokinins:** Stimulate cell division and delay leaf senescence.
    - **Abscisic Acid (ABA):** Stress hormone, causes stomatal closure during drought, induces seed dormancy.
    - **Ethene (Ethylene):** Gas promoting **fruit ripening** and leaf/fruit abscission.

## 9. Reproduction in Plants and Animals
- **Flower Anatomy & Pollination:**
  - Floral whorls: Calyx (sepals), Corolla (petals), Androecium (stamens: anther with pollen grains + filament), Gynoecium (carpels/pistil: stigma, style, ovary with ovules).
  - *Insect-Pollinated (Entomophilous) Flowers:* Large, brightly colored petals, sweet scent, nectar present, sticky/spiky pollen grains, small sticky stigma enclosed inside flower.
  - *Wind-Pollinated (Anemophilous) Flowers:* Small, dull petals or absent, no scent or nectar, light/smooth/copious pollen grains, large feathery pendulous stigmas hanging outside flower.
- **Double Fertilization in Angiosperms (Unique to flowering plants):**
  1. 1st male gamete ($n$) + egg cell ($n$) $\to$ **Diploid Zygote ($2n$)** $\to$ develops into the **Embryo**.
  2. 2nd male gamete ($n$) + 2 polar nuclei ($n + n$) $\to$ **Triploid Endosperm Nucleus ($3n$)** $\to$ develops into food-storing **Endosperm**.
  - Post-fertilization transformations: Ovary $\to$ Fruit; Ovary wall $\to$ Pericarp (epicarp, mesocarp, endocarp); Ovule $\to$ Seed; Integuments $\to$ Testa (seed coat).
- **Fruit Types & Dispersal:**
  - True fruit: Develops solely from fertilized ovary (e.g., Mango, Tomato).
  - False fruit: Other floral parts like receptacle contribute to fruit (e.g., Apple, Cashew, Pineapple).
  - Dispersal mechanisms: Wind (parachutes in *Tridax*, wings in *Combretum*), Animals (succulent edible pericarp in mango, hooks/spines in *Desmodium*), Water (fibrous buoyant mesocarp in Coconut), Explosive mechanism (pods split suddenly along sutures in Cowpea, *Pride of Barbados*).
- **Mammalian Reproductive Endocrinology:**
  - **FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone):** Stimulates development of Graafian follicles in ovary and spermatogenesis in testes.
  - **Estrogen:** Secreted by growing follicle; repairs and thickens uterine endometrium following menstruation.
  - **LH (Luteinizing Hormone):** Triggers **ovulation** (release of mature ovum on Day 14 of cycle) and transforms empty follicle into the **corpus luteum**.
  - **Progesterone:** Secreted by corpus luteum; maintains vascularized uterine endometrium for blastocyst implantation and inhibits further ovulation during pregnancy. If fertilization does not occur, corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone plunges, and **menstruation** begins.

# SECTION 3: ECOLOGY AND ENVIRONMENTAL BIOLOGY

## 10. Ecosystems, Trophic Dynamics, and Biogeochemical Cycles
- **Ecological Terminology:**
  - Habitat: Natural dwelling place of an organism.
  - Ecological Niche: Functional role and physical space occupied by an organism within its community.
  - Carrying Capacity ($K$): Maximum population size an environment can sustainably support without environmental degradation.
- **Energy Flow in Ecosystems:**
  $$\text{Sunlight} \xrightarrow{1-2\%} \text{Producers (Plants)} \xrightarrow{10\%} \text{Primary Consumers} \xrightarrow{10\%} \text{Secondary Consumers} \xrightarrow{10\%} \text{Tertiary Consumers}$$
  - *10% Energy Transfer Law (Lindeman):* Only approximately $10\%$ of energy is transferred to the next trophic level; $90\%$ is lost as heat, respiration, and excretion. This explains why food chains rarely exceed 4 or 5 trophic levels.
  - *Ecological Pyramids:* Pyramid of Numbers and Pyramid of Biomass can occasionally be inverted (e.g., one oak tree supporting thousands of insects). **The Pyramid of Energy is ALWAYS upright** because energy is irrevocably lost as heat at each step.
- **The Nitrogen Cycle:**
  1. *Nitrogen Fixation:* Conversion of atmospheric $\text{N}_2$ into nitrates:
     - Biological: Symbiotic bacteria (*Rhizobium* in root nodules of leguminous plants) and free-living bacteria (*Azotobacter*, *Clostridium*).
     - Atmospheric: Lightning discharges.
  2. *Nitrification:*
     $$\text{Ammonia } (\text{NH}_3) \xrightarrow{\mathbf{Nitrosomonas}} \text{Nitrite } (\text{NO}_2^-)$$
     $$\text{Nitrite } (\text{NO}_2^-) \xrightarrow{\mathbf{Nitrobacter}} \text{Nitrate } (\text{NO}_3^-) \quad (\text{Absorbable by plant roots})$$
  3. *Denitrification:* Conversion of soil nitrates back into atmospheric $\text{N}_2$ gas by anaerobic denitrifying bacteria (e.g., *Pseudomonas denitrificans*), depleting soil fertility in waterlogged soils.
- **Symbiotic and Ecological Associations:**
  - *Mutualism (+/+):* Both benefit (e.g., *Rhizobium* and legumes, Lichen = alga + fungus, Termite and cellulose-digesting flagellate in gut).
  - *Commensalism (+/0):* One benefits, other unaffected (e.g., Remora fish attaching to Shark for transport/scraps; Epiphytes growing on tree trunks).
  - *Parasitism (+/-):* One benefits at host's expense (e.g., *Plasmodium* in human RBCs, *Taenia* tapeworm in intestines, *Striga* on maize).

## 11. Ecological Succession and Nigerian Biomes
- **Succession:** Predictable, orderly sequence of community replacements over time leading to a stable, self-sustaining **climax community**.
  - *Primary Succession:* Begins on completely barren, lifeless substrate (bare rock, sand dune, cooled lava). Pioneer species are typically **lichens and mosses**.
  - *Secondary Succession:* Occurs in an area where an existing community was disturbed or cleared (abandoned farmland, burned forest). Begins with grasses and weeds, proceeds much faster due to pre-existing soil and seed banks.
- **Vegetation Zones (Biomes) in Nigeria (South to North):**
  1. *Mangrove Swamp:* Brackish coastal water, high rainfall ($>2500\text{ mm}$), stilt roots, pneumatophores (breathing roots). Dominant plants: Red mangrove (*Rhizophora*), White mangrove (*Avicennia*).
  2. *Tropical Rainforest:* High humidity, stratified canopy (understory, middle layer, emergents), epiphytes, lianas, buttress roots. High biodiversity. Dominant trees: Iroko, Mahogany, Obeche.
  3. *Southern Guinea Savanna:* Tall grasses, fire-resistant trees with thick corky bark.
  4. *Northern Guinea Savanna:* Shorter grasses, scattered trees.
  5. *Sudan Savanna:* Short feathery grasses, thorny acacia, baobab trees with water-storing trunks.
  6. *Sahel Savanna:* Semi-arid, sparse drought-resistant shrubs, date palms, desert encroachment.

# SECTION 4: HEREDITY, GENETICS, AND EVOLUTION

## 12. Mendelian Genetics and Inheritance
- **Mendel's Laws:**
  1. **First Law (Law of Segregation):** Alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, such that each gamete carries only one allele for each gene.
     - *Monohybrid Cross (Heterozygous $Tt \times Tt$):*
       - Phenotypic Ratio $= 3\text{ Tall} : 1\text{ Short } (3:1)$.
       - Genotypic Ratio $= 1\text{ } TT : 2\text{ } Tt : 1\text{ } tt \ (1:2:1)$.
  2. **Second Law (Law of Independent Assortment):** Alleles of two or more different genes sort independently of one another during gamete formation.
     - *Dihybrid Cross (Heterozygous $RrYy \times RrYy$):*
       - Phenotypic Ratio $= 9\text{ Round Yellow} : 3\text{ Round Green} : 3\text{ Wrinkled Yellow} : 1\text{ Wrinkled Green } (9:3:3:1)$.
- **Non-Mendelian Inheritance:**
  - *Incomplete Dominance:* Neither allele is completely dominant; heterozygote displays an intermediate phenotype (e.g., 4 O'clock plant / Snapdragon: Red $\times$ White $\to$ Pink). Phenotypic ratio $= 1:2:1$.
  - *Codominance:* Both alleles are equally expressed in heterozygote (e.g., Human ABO blood group: Alleles $I^A$ and $I^B$ produce blood group $\text{AB}$).
  - *Multiple Alleles:* More than two alleles exist for a gene in the population (e.g., $I^A, I^B, I^O$ for human blood groups).

## 13. Human Genetics and Sex-Linked Traits
- **Sex Determination:** Humans have 46 chromosomes (22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes).
  - Females are homogametic ($XX$); Males are heterogametic ($XY$).
  - Probability of giving birth to a male or female child is always $50\%$ ($\frac{1}{2}$) at every pregnancy.
- **Sex-Linked Traits (Carried on non-homologous portion of X-chromosome):**
  - Examples: **Hemophilia** (failure of blood to clot) and **Red-Green Color Blindness**.
  - Recessive alleles: $X^h$ and $X^c$.
  - Females can be normal ($X^H X^H$), carriers ($X^H X^h$), or sufferers ($X^h X^h$).
  - Males cannot be carriers because they possess only one X chromosome ($X^H Y =$ normal; $X^h Y =$ sufferer). Consequently, sex-linked recessive traits occur far more frequently in males.
  - Transmission: Father passes his affected X-chromosome to **all his daughters** (making them carriers or affected) and **never to his sons**.
- **Sickle Cell Anemia:**
  - Autosomal recessive condition caused by a point mutation substituting valine for glutamic acid in hemoglobin $\beta$-chain.
  - Normal: $Hb^A Hb^A$; Sickle cell trait (carrier): $Hb^A Hb^S$; Sickle cell disease: $Hb^S Hb^S$.
  - Heterozygous individuals ($Hb^A Hb^S$) have selective survival advantage in malaria-endemic regions because the malaria parasite (*Plasmodium falciparum*) cannot thrive in partially sickled RBCs.

## 14. Variation and Theories of Evolution
- **Types of Variation:**
  - *Morphological Variation:* Observable physical traits. Mostly **continuous variation** (controlled by multiple genes/polygenic, influenced by environment, shows normal distribution curve). Examples: Height, weight, skin color, leaf length.
  - *Physiological Variation:* Functional traits. Mostly **discontinuous variation** (distinct categories, no intermediates, controlled by single genes, unaffected by environment). Examples: ABO blood groups, ability to roll tongue, presence of PTC tasting, fingerprints.
- **Theories of Evolution:**
  - *Jean-Baptiste Lamarck (1809):* Theory of Use and Disuse, and Inheritance of Acquired Characteristics. E.g., ancestral short-necked giraffes stretched their necks to reach high tree leaves, and this elongated neck was passed to offspring. (Disproved because somatic modifications do not alter DNA in germ cells).
  - *Charles Darwin (1859):* Theory of Natural Selection ("Survival of the Fittest"). Observations: Overproduction of offspring $\to$ constancy of population numbers $\to$ struggle for existence $\to$ variation exists $\to$ individuals with favorable adaptations survive and reproduce $\to$ favorable traits accumulate over generations leading to new species.
- **Evidence of Evolution:**
  - *Fossil Record (Paleontology):* Direct evidence preserved in sedimentary rocks.
  - *Comparative Anatomy:*
    - **Homologous Structures:** Similar embryonic origin and fundamental anatomical plan, adapted for different functions. Demonstrates **divergent evolution**. Example: Pentadactyl limb in vertebrates (human arm, bird wing, whale flipper, bat wing).
    - **Analogous Structures:** Different evolutionary origins and anatomical structure, performing similar functions due to similar environmental pressures. Demonstrates **convergent evolution**. Example: Wings of a bird (bones) and wings of a butterfly (chitin).
  - *Vestigial Structures:* Reduced, functionless remnants of organs once useful in ancestors. Examples: Human appendix, coccyx (tailbone), nictitating membrane in eye.

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB BIOLOGY

1. **Watch the Negative Wording:** JAMB loves "Which of the following is **NOT**...", "**EXCEPT**", and "All of the following are true **LEAST**". Underline these words immediately to avoid picking the opposite option.
2. **Master the Mitosis vs Meiosis Distinction:** Mitosis $= 2$ identical diploid daughter cells (somatic). Meiosis $= 4$ genetically varied haploid daughter cells (gametes). Crossing over happens ONLY in Meiosis (Prophase I).
3. **Endosperm vs Embryo Ploidy Trap:** In angiosperms, the embryo is diploid ($2n$), but the endosperm is triploid ($3n$) due to double fertilization with two polar nuclei.
4. **Enzyme Action at pH & Temperature Extremes:** High temperatures ($>45^\circ\text{C}$) permanently **denature** enzymes (alter their active site shape). Low temperatures ($0^\circ\text{C}$) only **inactivate** them (they regain full activity when rewarmed).
5. **Remember the 10% Ecological Energy Rule:** If producers capture $10,000\text{ kJ}$ of energy, primary consumers get $1,000\text{ kJ}$, secondary consumers get $100\text{ kJ}$, and tertiary consumers get only $10\text{ kJ}$.
6. **Nitrosomonas vs Nitrobacter:** Alphabetical order trick: **M** comes before **B** $\to$ *Nitrosomonas* converts Ammonia to Nitrite first; *Nitrobacter* converts Nitrite to Nitrate second.
7. **Homologous vs Analogous Organs:** Same Structure $=$ Homologous (Divergent). Same Function $=$ Analogous (Convergent).
8. **Blood Transfusion Safety:** Group O is universal donor because it has no A or B antigens on its RBCs to trigger agglutination in the recipient's antibodies. Group AB is universal recipient because it has no antibodies in its plasma to attack incoming donor RBCs.
9. **Auxin Shoot vs Root Response:** In shoots, high auxin concentration **stimulates** growth (causing bending toward light). In roots, high auxin concentration **inhibits** growth (causing bending downward with gravity).
10. **Sex-Linked Inheritance Shortcut:** If a mother is color-blind ($X^c X^c$), **100% of her sons** will inevitably be color-blind because they receive their single X-chromosome from her.

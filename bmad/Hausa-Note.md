# JAMB Hausa — Comprehensive Study Note for Candidates (Jambites)

**Subject Code:** `HAU`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Hausa  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Hausa

In the UTME, Hausa is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into three canonical areas:
1. **Harshe da Nahawu (Language, Phonology & Grammar) (~16 Questions):** Karatun fahimta (reading comprehension), qa'idojin rubutu (orthography and punctuation), tsarin sauti (vowels, special hook consonants, tone system), nahawu (parts of speech, gender, pluralization, affixation), and translation.
2. **Adabi (Oral & Written Literature) (~14 Questions):** Adabin baka (tatsuniya, karin magana, habaici, ka-ci-ci-ka-ci, wake-wake) and rubutaccen adabi (analysis of classic and prescribed novels like *Magana Jari Ce*, *Shehu Umar*, *Ruwan Bagaja*, drama, and poetry).
3. **Al'adu da Gargajiya (Culture & Traditional Customs) (~10 Questions):** Life cycle ceremonies (haihuwa da rada suna, aure, jana'iza), sarautun gargajiya (the Emirate administrative hierarchy: Sarki, Waziri, Madawaki, Galadima), sana'o'in gargajiya (traditional crafts: rini, kira, dukanci, noma), and cultural festivals (Hawan Daba, Kalankuwa).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Characteristics for JAMB Hausa:**
> - Special Hook Consonants (*Haruffan baki masu lankwasa*): **ɓ (implosive bilabial), ɗ (implosive alveolar), ƙ (ejective velar)**, alongside **ts (ejective alveolar affricate)** and **'y (glottalized palatal)**.
> - Prescribed Classic Literature Milestones: The 1933 Translation Bureau competition: *Ruwan Bagaja* (Abubakar Imam), *Shehu Umar* (Abubakar Tafawa Balewa), *Gandoki* (Muhammadu Bello Kagara), and *Magana Jari Ce*.
> - Traditional Occupations: **Rini** (dyeing indigo cloth in dye-pits e.g. Kofar Mata in Kano); **Dukanci** (leatherworking); **Kira** (blacksmithing); **Fawa** (butchery).

# SECTION 1: HARSHE DA NAHAWU (LANGUAGE & GRAMMAR)

## 1. Tsarin Sauti (Phonology and Tone System)
- **Wasula (Vowels in Hausa):**
  - *Wasula Gajiere (5 Short Vowels):* **a, e, i, o, u** (e.g., *fari* = white).
  - *Wasula Masu Tsawo (5 Long Vowels):* Written double: **aa, ee, ii, oo, uu** (e.g., *faari* = drought).
  - *Tagwayen Wasula (Diphthongs):* Formed by glides: **ai** (e.g., *kai*) and **au** (e.g., *kazaure, sauro*).
- **Haruffan Baki (Consonants):**
  - Standard Hausa has 29 consonants.
  - *Haruffa Masu Kebantacciyar Siffa (Special Glottalized Letters):*
    1. **ɓ (Implosive bilabial):** e.g., *ɓarawo* (thief), *ɓauna* (buffalo).
    2. **ɗ (Implosive alveolar):** e.g., *ɗalibi* (student), *ɗaki* (room).
    3. **ƙ (Ejective velar):** e.g., *ƙofa* (door), *ƙasa* (earth/country).
    4. **ts (Ejective alveolar affricate):** e.g., *tsuntsu* (bird), *tsawo* (height).
    5. **'y (Glottalized palatal glide):** e.g., *'ya'ya* (children/fruits).
- **Karin Sauti (Tone System):**
  - Hausa is a **tonal language**; changes in tone change the meaning of identically spelled words:
    - *Karin Sauti Mai Girma (High Tone):* Unmarked or marked with acute accent ($/$).
    - *Karin Sauti Mai Kasa (Low Tone):* Marked with grave accent ($\backslash$).
    - *Karin Sauti Mai Faduwa (Falling Tone):* Marked with circumflex ($\wedge$).
    $$\text{Misali: } \text{fā̀fā́ (wide/broad) } \quad \text{vs.} \quad \text{fáfà (to tell a secret)}$$

## 2. Rabe-raben Kalmomi da Ginin Jimla (Grammar and Syntax)
- **Rabe-raben Kalmomi (Parts of Speech):**
  - **Suna (Noun):** Sunan mutum, dabba, guri, ko abu (e.g., *Musa, doki, Kano, tebur*).
  - **Wakilin Suna (Pronoun):** Kalmar da ke maye gurbin suna (e.g., *shi, ita, su, mu, ni*).
  - **Aikatau (Verb):** Kalmar da ke nuna aiki ko yanayi (e.g., *karanta, ci, tafi, rubuta*).
  - **Sifa (Adjective):** Kalmar da ke bayyana sunan abu ko yanayinsa (e.g., *babba, fari, gajere, danye*).
  - **Bayanau (Adverb):** Kalmar da ke bayyana yadda aiki ya gudana (e.g., *da sauri, a hankali, yanzu*).
  - **Harafofi (Particles / Prepositions):** Kalmomin da ke hada kalmomi (e.g., *da, a, cikin, karkashin, amma*).
- **Jinsi (Gender in Hausa):**
  - *Jinsin Maza (Masculine):* Most nouns ending in consonants or vowels other than *-a* (e.g., *doki, yaro, kudi*).
  - *Jinsin Mata (Feminine):* Most nouns ending in *-a* (e.g., *yarinya, kaza, kujera, mota*).
- **Jam'i (Pluralization Patterns):**
  - Hausa has rich, irregular pluralization structures:
    - *Yaro* (boy) $\to$ **Yara** (boys)
    - *Kujera* (chair) $\to$ **Kujeru** (chairs)
    - *Littafi* (book) $\to$ **Littattafai** (books)
    - *Gida* (house) $\to$ **Gidaje** (houses)
    - *Doki* (horse) $\to$ **Dawaki** (horses)
- **Ginin Jimla (Sentence Structure):**
  - *Jimla Mai Sauki (Simple Sentence):* Contains one independent clause:
    $$\text{Musa ya tafi makaranta.}$$
  - *Jimla Mai Sarƙaƙiya (Complex Sentence):* Main clause with subordinate clauses.
  - *Jimla Mai 'Ya'ya (Compound Sentence):* Two independent clauses joined by conjunctions (*kuma, amma, ko*).

# SECTION 2: ADABI (ORAL & WRITTEN LITERATURE)

## 3. Adabin Baka (Oral Literature)
- **Tatsuniya (Folktales):**
  - Fictional moral oral stories told in the evening around the fireside; forbidden to be told during the day (myth that horns will grow on the narrator's head).
  - Opens with formula: *"Ga ta ga ta nan"* (Here it comes); Audience responds: *"Ta zo mu ji ta"* (Let it come so we can hear).
  - Ends with formula: *"Kunkurus kan kusu"*.
  - Central archetype character: **Gizo** (the cunning, gluttonous, deceitful spider) and his sensible wife **Koki**, alongside **Zaki** (the King of Beasts / Lion) and **Dila** (the sly jackal).
- **Karin Magana (Proverbs):**
  - Concentrated philosophical wisdom passed down through generations:
    - *"Kowa ya tuna bara, bai ji dadin bana ba"* (Whoever remembers last year's suffering cannot appreciate this year's relief).
    - *"Komi ya lalace, mai rabo yana nan"* (No matter how bad things become, what is destined for one will reach him).
    - *"Zaman gida ya fi zama a daji"* (Peace at home is better than isolation in the forest).
    - *"Kukan kurciya jawabi ne"* (The cry of the dove is a meaningful message).
- **Ka-ci-ci-ka-ci (Riddles):** Intellectual word games testing memory and wit (e.g., *"Na tafi na bar shi, na dawo yana nan"* $\to$ *Tafarki / Hanya* - The road).
- **Wake-Waken Gargajiya (Traditional Praise Singing):**
  - **Maroka / Mawaka:** Oral bards and praise singers:
    - **Dr. Mamman Shata Katsina:** Foremost modern oral poet of Hausaland; accompanied by the *Kalangu* drum.
    - **Alhaji Dan Kwairo Maradun:** Royal court praise singer for the Sardauna of Sokoto (Sir Ahmadu Bello).
    - **Dan Maraya Jos:** Celebrated solo wandering bard who played the **Kuntigi** (one-stringed lute).

---

## 4. Rubutaccen Adabi (Written Literature)
- **Evolution of Written Hausa:**
  - *Ajami:* Hausa language written using adapted Arabic script, flourishing from the 17th century (used by Usman Dan Fodio, Nana Asma'u, and Abdullahi Dan Fodio).
  - *Boko:* Modern Latin-based orthography introduced by the British colonial administration in the early 1900s.
- **The Historic 1933 Translation Bureau Literature Competition:**
  - Organized by Dr. Rupert East in Zaria to create indigenous secular reading materials. Produced the foundational classics of modern Hausa prose:
    1. **Ruwan Bagaja (The Water of Cure):** Masterpiece picaresque novel written by **Alhaji Abubakar Imam**; follows the humorous adventures of Abubakar on his quest to find the curative water.
    2. **Shehu Umar:** Tragic historical novel written by **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**; depicts the trans-Saharan slave trade, filial piety, Islamic devotion, and forgiveness.
    3. **Gandoki:** Adventure novel by **Muhammadu Bello Kagara**; narrates heroic supernatural battles against colonial British forces and jinns.
    4. **Idon Matambayi:** Written by **Muhammadu Gwarzo**.
    5. **Jiki Magayi:** Written by **John Tafida and Dr. Rupert East**.
- **Magana Jari Ce (Wisdom is an Asset):**
  - Three-volume epic storybook compiled by **Abubakar Imam** (1937); uses the framing story of a wise parrot (**Aku**) who tells gripping continuous stories every night to prevent a young prince from going to war foolishly until his father returns.
- **Modern Hausa Poetry:**
  - **Sa'adu Zungur:** Progressive political reform poet (*Arewa Jamhuriyya ko Mulukiya*).
  - **Mu'azu Hadejia:** Celebrated Islamic and social morality poet.

# SECTION 3: AL'ADU DA GARGAJIYA (CULTURE & TRADITIONS)

## 5. Bukukuwan Rayuwa (Life Cycle Customs)
- **Haihuwa da Rada Suna (Childbirth and Naming Ceremony):**
  - When a child is born, elderly women bathe the infant and whisper the Call to Prayer (*Kiran Sallah*) into the right ear and *Iqamah* into the left ear.
  - Mother undergoes hot herbal baths (**Wankan Jego**) for 40 days.
  - **Rada Suna:** Held on the **7th day** after birth; the Islamic cleric (*Malam*) announces the name; a ram is slaughtered according to Sunnah; the child's head is shaved (**Aski**) and traditional facial marks (*Shaushawa*) may be made.
- **Bikin Aure (Marriage Customs):**
  - *Baiko (Engagement):* Formal consent given by bride's family; gifts presented (*Kudin Gaisuwa*).
  - *Sadaki (Dowry):* The mandatory Islamic dowry paid directly to the bride's father before the wedding.
  - *Daurin Aure (Wedding Solemnization):* Conducted in the mosque or family courtyard by the Imam in the presence of representatives (**Wakili**) of both families and witnesses.
  - *Kulle (Purdah / Seclusion):* Traditional practice of married women remaining within the family compound.
- **Jana'iza (Funeral and Burial Rites):**
  - Conducted strictly according to Islamic Sharia: Ritual washing of corpse (*Wankan Gawa*), shrouding in white unstitched cloths (*Likitani*), funeral prayer (*Salatul Janazah* with 4 Takbirs), and prompt burial facing the Qiblah without a coffin.

---

## 6. Sarautun Gargajiya da Sana'o'i (Chieftaincy Hierarchy & Crafts)
- **Tsarin Sarautar Gargajiya (Emirate Governance Hierarchy):**
  - **Sarki (The Emir):** Supreme executive, judicial, and spiritual head of the Emirate.
  - **Waziri:** Prime Minister and closest administrative adviser to the Emir.
  - **Madawaki:** Commander-in-Chief of the cavalry and army; second most powerful official.
  - **Galadima:** Administrator and governor of the capital city when the Emir is away.
  - **Sarkin Fada:** Chief of palace officials, royal protocol, and chamberlains.
  - **Hakimi:** District Head overseeing a territory of villages.
  - **Dagaci:** Village Head.
  - **Mai Unguwa:** Ward Head (grassroots level administrator).
- **Sana'o'in Gargajiya (Traditional Occupations and Guilds):**
  - **Noma (Farming):** Chief occupation of the Hausa (*"Noma na gaba, ba don noma ba a ba da me za a ci"*); staples: Guinea corn (*dawa*), millet (*gero*), groundnuts (*gyada*).
  - **Kira (Blacksmithing):** Forging iron tools (*fartanya* - hoe, *lauje* - sickle, *gatari* - axe); led by *Sarkin Kira*.
  - **Rini (Cloth Dyeing):** Indigo dyeing of cotton cloths in ancient earthen pits (**Kafofi**); world-famous **Kofar Mata Dye Pits** in Kano (established since 1498).
  - **Dukanci (Leatherworking):** Tanning and crafting goat and sheep hides into world-renowned "Moroccan leather", cushions, bags, and saddles.
  - **Fawa (Butchery):** Headed by *Sarkin Pawa*.
- **Bukukuwan Gargajiya (Traditional Festivals):**
  - **Hawan Daba (Durbar):** Grand equestrian festival held during Eid al-Fitr (*Karamar Sallah*) and Eid al-Adha (*Babbar Sallah*); thousands of horsemen in ornate ceremonial armor charge at gallop, halting abruptly in the royal salute (**Jafi**) before the seated Emir.
  - **Kalankuwa:** Post-harvest youth festival celebrated in rural villages with wrestling, feasting, and roleplay after crops are stored in granaries (*rumbu*).

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB HAUSA

1. **Identify the 5 Hook Letters:** Remember the special Hausa hook consonants: **ɓ, ɗ, ƙ**, plus **ts** and **'y**.
2. **Magana Jari Ce Author:** The monumental three-volume storybook *Magana Jari Ce* and *Ruwan Bagaja* were written by **Alhaji Abubakar Imam**.
3. **Shehu Umar Author:** The classic novel *Shehu Umar* was authored by Nigeria's first Prime Minister, **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**.
4. **Dan Maraya Jos Instrument:** The celebrated bard Dan Maraya Jos performed exclusively with the **Kuntigi** (one-stringed lute), NOT a drum.
5. **Kofar Mata Pits Location:** The historic ancient indigo dye pits (*Rini*) celebrated across West Africa are located at **Kofar Mata in Kano**.
6. **Naming Ceremony Day:** The traditional naming ceremony (*Rada Suna*) in Hausa custom is observed on the **7th day** after birth.
7. **Emirate Army Commander Title:** The Commander-in-Chief of the cavalry and armed forces in the Hausa Emirate hierarchy is the **Madawaki**.
8. **Tatsuniya Formula Openings:** Every Hausa folktale opens with *"Ga ta ga ta nan"* and closes with *"Kunkurus kan kusu"*.
9. **Central Character in Tatsuniya:** The master trickster and cunning character in Hausa folktales is **Gizo** (the spider).
10. **Hawan Daba Royal Salute:** The dramatic military charge of horsemen halting before the Emir to pledge allegiance with raised fists is called the **Jafi**.

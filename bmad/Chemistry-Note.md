## Overview & How JAMB Tests Chemistry

In the UTME, Chemistry is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major domains:
1. **Physical and Theoretical Chemistry (~14 Questions):** Separation techniques, mole concept, stoichiometry, atomic structure, chemical bonding, gas laws, thermochemistry, rates of reaction, and dynamic equilibrium.
2. **Inorganic Chemistry (~10 Questions):** Periodic table trends, non-metals (Hydrogen, Oxygen, Carbon, Nitrogen, Sulfur, Halogens) and their compounds, extraction of metals (Al, Fe, Cu), and alloys.
3. **Organic Chemistry (~10 Questions):** Hybridization, functional groups, IUPAC nomenclature, hydrocarbons, alkanols, alkanoic acids, esters, fats/oils, soaps, and polymers.
4. **Analytical and Environmental Chemistry (~6 Questions):** Qualitative analysis (cation and anion confirmatory tests), acid-base titrations, water treatment, air pollution, and industrial processes.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Essential Constants Provided by JAMB:**
> - Molar volume of any gas at S.T.P. $= 22.4\text{ dm}^3\text{ (or } 22,400\text{ cm}^3\text{)}$
> - Avogadro's constant ($N_A$) $= 6.02 \times 10^{23}\text{ particles/mol}$
> - Faraday's constant ($F$) $= 96,500\text{ Coulombs/mol of } e^-$
> - Standard Temperature $= 273\text{ K } (0^\circ\text{C})$; Standard Pressure $= 1\text{ atm } (760\text{ mmHg} = 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ N/m}^2)$

# SECTION 1: SEPARATION TECHNIQUES AND PURIFICATION

## 1. Physical vs. Chemical Changes & Separation Methods
- **Physical Change:** Easily reversible, no new substance formed, no significant energy change (e.g., melting ice, dissolving salt in water, magnetization of iron).
- **Chemical Change:** Irreversible, new substance formed with different properties, heat usually absorbed or released (e.g., burning wood, rusting of iron, fermentation, neutralization).
- **Primary Separation Techniques Tested by JAMB:**
  - *Separating Funnel:* Separates two **immiscible liquids** of different densities (e.g., kerosene and water, oil and water).
  - *Fractional Distillation:* Separates **miscible liquids** with close boiling points (difference $< 25^\circ\text{C}$), such as crude oil fractions or ethanol and water.
  - *Simple Distillation:* Recovers solvent from a solution of solute and solvent with wide boiling point differences (e.g., pure water from seawater).
  - *Sublimation:* Separates a substance that changes directly from solid to gas on heating from non-subliming impurities. Common subliming substances: **Ammonium chloride ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$), Iodine ($I_2$), Naphthalene, Dry ice ($\text{CO}_2$)**.
  - *Chromatography:* Separates complex mixtures of pigments, dyes, or amino acids based on differing solubilities in mobile phase and adsorption to stationary phase ($R_f = \frac{\text{distance moved by solute}}{\text{distance moved by solvent front}}$).
  - *Crystallization / Fractional Crystallization:* Purifies solid solutes from solutions based on solubility differences at different temperatures.
  - *Centrifugation:* Accelerates sedimentation of small suspended particles by high-speed spinning (e.g., blood plasma separation).

# SECTION 2: CHEMICAL COMBINATION, MOLE CONCEPT & STOICHIOMETRY

## 2. Laws of Chemical Combination
1. **Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier):** Matter cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. $\text{Mass of Reactants} = \text{Mass of Products}$.
2. **Law of Definite / Constant Proportions (Proust):** All pure samples of a particular chemical compound contain the same elements combined in the same ratio by mass.
3. **Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton):** When two elements combine to form more than one compound, the different masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other are in simple whole-number ratios (e.g., $\text{CO}$ and $\text{CO}_2$; $\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{SO}_3$).
4. **Gay-Lussac's Law of Combining Volumes:** When gases react, they do so in volumes that bear simple whole-number ratios to one another and to the volumes of gaseous products, provided temperature and pressure remain constant.
5. **Avogadro's Law:** Equal volumes of all gases under the same conditions of temperature and pressure contain the same number of molecules.

---

## 3. The Mole Concept & Stoichiometric Calculations
- **Key Mole Formulas:**
  $$\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Mass } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass } (M)}$$
  $$n = \frac{\text{Number of particles}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}}$$
  $$n = \frac{\text{Volume of gas at S.T.P. (in dm}^3)}{22.4\text{ dm}^3} = \frac{\text{Volume in cm}^3}{22,400\text{ cm}^3}$$
  $$\text{For Solutions: } n = \text{Molarity } (M) \times \text{Volume in dm}^3 = \frac{C \times V(\text{cm}^3)}{1,000}$$
- **Empirical and Molecular Formula:**
  - *Empirical Formula (EF):* Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
  - *Molecular Formula (MF):* Actual number of atoms of each element: $\text{MF} = (\text{EF})_n$.
  - Relationship with Vapor Density: $\text{Molar Mass} = 2 \times \text{Vapour Density (V.D.)}$.
- **Volumetric Titration Formula:**
  $$\frac{C_A V_A}{C_B V_B} = \frac{n_A}{n_B}$$
  *(Where $C_A, C_B$ are molar concentrations, $V_A, V_B$ are volumes in $\text{cm}^3$, and $n_A, n_B$ are mole ratios from balanced chemical equation).*

# SECTION 3: ATOMIC STRUCTURE AND THE PERIODIC TABLE

## 4. Subatomic Particles, Isotopes, and Electronic Structure
- **Subatomic Particles:**
  - Proton ($p^+$): Mass $\approx 1\text{ amu}$, Charge $= +1$, located in nucleus.
  - Neutron ($n^0$): Mass $\approx 1\text{ amu}$, Charge $= 0$, located in nucleus.
  - Electron ($e^-$): Mass $\approx \frac{1}{1840}\text{ amu}$, Charge $= -1$, orbits in energy shells.
  - Atomic Number ($Z$) $= p = e$ (in a neutral atom). Mass Number ($A$) $= p + n$.
- **Isotopy:** Atoms of the same element having the same atomic number ($Z$) but different mass numbers ($A$) due to different numbers of neutrons (e.g., $^{35}_{17}\text{Cl}$ and $^{37}_{17}\text{Cl}$).
  $$\text{Relative Atomic Mass (RAM)} = \frac{(A_1 \times \%_1) + (A_2 \times \%_2)}{100}$$
- **Rules of Electronic Configuration:**
  1. **Aufbau Principle:** Orbitals of lowest energy are filled first ($1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d \dots$).
  2. **Pauli Exclusion Principle:** No two electrons in the same atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers; an orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons with opposite spins ($\upharpoonleft\downharpoonright$).
  3. **Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity:** Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly first before pairing occurs.

---

## 5. Periodic Trends
| Periodic Property | Across a Period (Left $\to$ Right) | Down a Group (Top $\to$ Bottom) | Fundamental Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atomic Radius** | **Decreases** | **Increases** | Increased nuclear charge pulls electrons closer across period; extra electron shells added down group. |
| **Ionization Energy** | **Increases** | **Decreases** | Stronger nuclear pull makes valence electrons harder to remove across period; increased shielding makes removal easier down group. |
| **Electron Affinity** | **Increases** | **Decreases** | Higher nuclear attraction across period attracts incoming electrons more readily. |
| **Electronegativity** | **Increases** (Fluorine is highest: 4.0) | **Decreases** | Valence electrons are closer to the positive nucleus across period. |
| **Metallic Character** | **Decreases** | **Increases** | Tendency to lose valence electrons increases down group. |

> [!NOTE]
> **Ionization Energy Anomalies:** 
> - Group 2 (Be: $1s^2 2s^2$) has higher 1st IE than Group 13 (B: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^1$) because $2s$ is completely filled and stable.
> - Group 15 (N: $2p^3$) has higher 1st IE than Group 16 (O: $2p^4$) because half-filled $p$-orbitals are extra stable.

# SECTION 4: CHEMICAL BONDING AND MOLECULAR SHAPES

## 6. Types of Chemical Bonds
1. **Electrovalent (Ionic) Bond:** Complete transfer of electrons from a metal (low IE) to a non-metal (high EA). Form crystalline solids with high melting points, soluble in polar solvents (water), conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state.
2. **Covalent Bond:** Sharing of electron pairs between non-metals.
   - *Non-polar Covalent:* Equal sharing (e.g., $H_2, Cl_2, CH_4$, electronegativity difference $\approx 0$).
   - *Polar Covalent:* Unequal sharing due to electronegativity difference (e.g., $H_2O, HCl, NH_3$).
3. **Co-ordinate (Dative) Covalent Bond:** Both shared electrons are contributed by only one of the participating atoms (donor must have a lone pair). Examples tested by JAMB:
   - Ammonium ion: $\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}^+ \to \text{NH}_4^+$
   - Hydroxonium ion: $\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \to \text{H}_3\text{O}^+$
   - Carbon monoxide: $\text{C} \equiv \text{O}$ (one of the triple bonds is dative)
4. **Metallic Bond:** Electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and sea of delocalized electrons (responsible for electrical conductivity, malleability, and ductility).
5. **Intermolecular Forces:**
   - *Hydrogen Bonding:* Special dipole-dipole attraction when hydrogen is bonded to strongly electronegative atoms: **Fluorine, Oxygen, Nitrogen (F-O-N)**. Explains why $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is liquid at room temperature while $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ is a gas, and why alcohols have higher boiling points than corresponding alkanes.
   - *Van der Waals Forces:* Weak temporary dipole attractions present in all molecules (increases with molecular weight).

## 7. Shapes of Simple Molecules (VSEPR Theory)
- **Linear ($180^\circ$):** $\text{BeCl}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ ($sp$ hybridization).
- **Trigonal Planar ($120^\circ$):** $\text{BF}_3, \text{AlCl}_3, \text{C}_2\text{H}_4$ ($sp^2$ hybridization).
- **Tetrahedral ($109.5^\circ$):** $\text{CH}_4, \text{CCl}_4, \text{NH}_4^+$ ($sp^3$ hybridization, 0 lone pairs).
- **Trigonal Pyramidal ($107^\circ$):** $\text{NH}_3, \text{PCl}_3$ ($sp^3$ hybridization, 1 lone pair repels bond pairs).
- **V-shaped / Bent ($104.5^\circ$):** $\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{H}_2\text{S}, \text{SO}_2$ ($sp^3$ hybridization, 2 lone pairs).

# SECTION 5: STATES OF MATTER, GAS LAWS & DIFFUSION

## 8. Gas Laws & Calculations
- **Boyle's Law:** $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$ ($T =$ constant).
- **Charles's Law:** $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ ($P =$ constant, $T$ in Kelvin).
- **General Gas Law:** $\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$.
- **Ideal Gas Equation:** $PV = nRT = \frac{m}{M}RT \implies M = \frac{\rho RT}{P}$.
- **Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures:** $P_{total} = P_A + P_B + P_C + \dots$
  - Partial pressure: $P_A = x_A \times P_{total}$ (where $x_A = \frac{n_A}{n_{total}}$ is mole fraction).
  - For gas collected over water: $P_{dry\ gas} = P_{total} - \text{Aqueous Tension}$.
- **Graham's Law of Diffusion:**
  $$\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}} = \frac{t_2}{t_1}$$
  *(Lighter gases diffuse faster than denser gases. E.g., $\text{CH}_4\ (M=16)$ diffuses twice as fast as $\text{SO}_2\ (M=64)$).*

# SECTION 6: ENERGETICS, KINETICS & CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM

## 9. Thermochemistry
- **Exothermic Reaction:** Heat released to surroundings, $\Delta H$ is negative ($\Delta H < 0$). Products are more stable than reactants. (e.g., combustion, neutralization, respiration).
- **Endothermic Reaction:** Heat absorbed from surroundings, $\Delta H$ is positive ($\Delta H > 0$). Reactants are more stable than products. (e.g., thermal decomposition of $\text{CaCO}_3$, photosynthesis, dissolution of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$).
- **Enthalpy of Neutralization:** $\Delta H$ for strong acid + strong base is constant $\approx -57.3\text{ kJ/mol}$ because it represents: $\text{H}^+_{(aq)} + \text{OH}^-_{(aq)} \to \text{H}_2\text{O}_{(l)}$.

## 10. Reaction Rates and Chemical Equilibrium
- **Factors Increasing Reaction Rate:** Increasing reactant concentration/pressure, increasing surface area (finely divided solids), increasing temperature (increases fraction of molecules with $E \ge E_a$), adding a positive catalyst (lowers activation energy $E_a$).
- **Dynamic Equilibrium:** Rates of forward and reverse reactions are equal; concentrations of reactants and products remain constant.
- **Le Chatelier's Principle:** When a system in equilibrium is subjected to a constraint (change in temperature, pressure, or concentration), the system shifts in a direction to annul that constraint:
  - *Temperature:* Increasing temperature favors the **endothermic** direction. Decreasing temperature favors the **exothermic** direction.
  - *Pressure:* Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with **fewer moles of gas**. Decreasing pressure favors side with **more moles of gas**. If $\Delta n_{gas} = 0$, pressure has no effect on equilibrium position.
  - *Catalyst:* Speeds up attainment of equilibrium equally in both directions; **does not change equilibrium position or yield**.

# SECTION 7: ACIDS, BASES, SALTS & VOLUMETRIC ANALYSIS

## 11. Acids, Bases, and pH Scale
- **Definitions:**
  - *Arrhenius:* Acid yields $\text{H}^+$ in water; Base yields $\text{OH}^-$ in water.
  - *Brønsted-Lowry:* Acid is a proton ($\text{H}^+$) donor; Base is a proton acceptor. Conjugate acid-base pairs differ by exactly one $\text{H}^+$.
  - *Lewis:* Acid is an electron pair acceptor; Base is an electron pair donor.
- **pH Calculations:**
  $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+] \quad \text{and} \quad \text{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\text{OH}^-]$$
  $$\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14 \quad \text{at } 25^\circ\text{C}$$
  - For $0.01\text{ M } \text{HCl}$: $[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-2} \implies \text{pH} = 2$.
  - For $0.01\text{ M } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: $[\text{H}^+] = 2 \times 10^{-2} \implies \text{pH} = -\log(0.02) = 1.70$.
- **Indicators and Endpoints:**
  | Indicator | Acid Color | Base Color | Suitable Titration |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Methyl Orange** | **Pink / Red** | **Yellow** | Strong Acid vs Weak Base ($\text{HCl}$ vs $\text{NH}_3$) |
  | **Phenolphthalein** | **Colorless** | **Pink** | Weak Acid vs Strong Base ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ vs $\text{NaOH}$) |
  | *Both Suitable* | — | — | Strong Acid vs Strong Base ($\text{HCl}$ vs $\text{NaOH}$) |
  | *Neither Suitable* | — | — | Weak Acid vs Weak Base (No sharp endpoint) |

## 12. Types of Salts & Hydrolysis
- **Normal Salt:** All replaceable hydrogens of acid are replaced by a metal (e.g., $\text{NaCl, Na}_2\text{SO}_4, \text{KNO}_3$). Neutral in water.
- **Acid Salt:** Replaceable hydrogens are partially replaced (e.g., $\text{NaHCO}_3, \text{NaHSO}_4$).
- **Basic Salt:** Contains $\text{OH}^-$ ions (e.g., $\text{Zn(OH)Cl, Mg(OH)Cl}$).
- **Double Salt:** Yields two different cations on ionization (e.g., Alums: $\text{KAl(SO}_4)_2 \cdot 12\text{H}_2\text{O}$; Mohr's salt: $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{Fe(SO}_4)_2 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
- **Salt Hydrolysis:**
  - Salt of Strong Acid + Weak Base ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$) $\implies$ **Acidic solution ($\text{pH} < 7$)**.
  - Salt of Weak Acid + Strong Base ($\text{CH}_3\text{COONa, Na}_2\text{CO}_3$) $\implies$ **Basic solution ($\text{pH} > 7$)**.

# SECTION 8: REDOX REACTIONS AND ELECTROCHEMISTRY

## 13. Oxidation Numbers and Balancing
- **Definitions:**
  - *Oxidation:* Loss of electrons, increase in oxidation number, addition of oxygen, removal of hydrogen.
  - *Reduction:* Gain of electrons, decrease in oxidation number, removal of oxygen, addition of hydrogen.
  - *Oxidizing Agent (Oxidant):* Gets reduced (accepts electrons). Examples: $\text{KMnO}_4, \text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7, \text{HNO}_3, \text{Cl}_2, \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$.
  - *Reducing Agent (Reductant):* Gets oxidized (donates electrons). Examples: $\text{SO}_2, \text{H}_2\text{S}, \text{CO}, \text{C}, \text{Fe}^{2+}$.
- **Common Oxidation States in Exam:**
  - $\text{Mn}$ in $\text{KMnO}_4$: $+1 + x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies x = +7$.
  - $\text{Cr}$ in $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$: $2(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 \implies 2x = 12 \implies x = +6$.
  - $S$ in $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$: $x + 4(-2) = -2 \implies x = +6$.
  - $O$ in peroxides ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$): $-1$. $H$ in metal hydrides ($\text{NaH}$): $-1$.

## 14. Electrolysis and Faraday's Laws
- **Faraday's First Law:** Mass ($m$) of substance deposited is directly proportional to quantity of electricity ($Q = It$):
  $$m = Z \cdot Q = Z \cdot I \cdot t$$
- **Combined General Electrolysis Formula:**
  $$m = \frac{M \cdot I \cdot t}{n \cdot F}$$
  *(Where $M =$ molar mass, $I =$ current in Amperes, $t =$ time in **seconds**, $n =$ number of electrons transferred / valency, $F = 96,500\text{ C/mol}$).*
- **Preferential Discharge of Ions:**
  - *Cations (Cathode, Reduction):* $\text{K}^+ < \text{Na}^+ < \text{Ca}^{2+} < \text{Mg}^{2+} < \text{Al}^{3+} < \text{Zn}^{2+} < \text{Fe}^{2+} < \text{Pb}^{2+} < \mathbf{H^+} < \text{Cu}^{2+} < \text{Ag}^+ < \text{Au}^{3+}$. (Lower ions discharged preferentially).
  - *Anions (Anode, Oxidation):* $\text{SO}_4^{2-} < \text{NO}_3^- < \text{Cl}^- < \text{Br}^- < \text{I}^- < \mathbf{OH^-}$.
  - *Concentration Effect:* Concentrated $\text{NaCl}$ (brine) discharges $\text{Cl}^-$ at anode instead of $\text{OH}^-$, producing $\text{Cl}_2$ gas.

# SECTION 9: INORGANIC CHEMISTRY & INDUSTRIAL PROCESSES

## 15. Key Non-Metals and Compounds
- **Hydrogen:** Prepared by reaction of dilute acid with active metals ($\text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{H}_2$). Tested with a lighted splint $\to$ burns with a **pop sound**.
- **Oxygen:** Prepared by catalytic decomposition of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ using $\text{MnO}_2$. Tested with a glowing splint $\to$ **relights / rekindles**.
- **Carbon:**
  - Allotropes: Diamond (tetrahedral, hardest substance, non-conductor) and Graphite (hexagonal layers, slippery, conducts electricity due to delocalized electrons). Amorphous forms: charcoal, lampblack, coal.
  - $\text{CO}$ vs $\text{CO}_2$: $\text{CO}$ is neutral, highly poisonous (forms carboxyhemoglobin); $\text{CO}_2$ is acidic, turns lime water ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) milky due to $\text{CaCO}_3$ precipitate.
- **Nitrogen & Ammonia:**
  - **Haber Process:** $\text{N}_{2(g)} + 3\text{H}_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_{3(g)}$ ($\Delta H = -92\text{ kJ/mol}$). Conditions: $450^\circ\text{C}$, $200\text{ atm}$, finely divided Iron catalyst.
  - $\text{NH}_3$ is the only alkaline gas; turns damp red litmus blue; gives dense white fumes of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ with concentrated $\text{HCl}$.
- **Sulfur & Sulfuric Acid:**
  - Allotropes: Rhombic sulfur (stable below $95.6^\circ\text{C}$) and Monoclinic sulfur (needle-shaped).
  - **Contact Process:** $2\text{SO}_{2(g)} + \text{O}_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_{3(g)}$ ($\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ catalyst, $450^\circ\text{C}, 1-2\text{ atm}$).
  - $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ acts as strong acid, oxidizing agent, and powerful **dehydrating agent** (chars sugar to pure black carbon foam).
- **Halogens (Group 17):** $\text{F}_2$ (pale yellow gas) $> \text{Cl}_2$ (greenish-yellow gas) $> \text{Br}_2$ (reddish-brown liquid) $> \text{I}_2$ (dark purple/black solid). Reactivity decreases down the group.

## 16. Metals, Extraction & Alloys
- **Aluminium:** Extracted from **Bauxite ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)** by electrolysis (Hall-Héroult process). **Cryolite ($\text{Na}_3\text{AlF}_6$)** is added to lower the melting point of alumina from $2050^\circ\text{C}$ to $950^\circ\text{C}$ and improve electrical conductivity.
- **Iron:** Extracted in **Blast Furnace** from Haematite ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$). Limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) is added as flux to remove silica impurities as molten slag ($\text{CaSiO}_3$).
- **Important Alloys Tested by JAMB:**
  - *Brass:* Copper ($\text{Cu}$) + Zinc ($\text{Zn}$).
  - *Bronze:* Copper ($\text{Cu}$) + Tin ($\text{Sn}$).
  - *Steel:* Iron ($\text{Fe}$) + Carbon ($\text{C}$). Stainless Steel: $\text{Fe} + \text{Cr} + \text{Ni}$.
  - *Duralumin:* Aluminium ($\text{Al}$) + Copper ($\text{Cu}$) + Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$) + Manganese ($\text{Mn}$) (lightweight, used for aircraft bodies).
  - *Solder:* Lead ($\text{Pb}$) + Tin ($\text{Sn}$) (low melting point).

# SECTION 10: ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

## 17. Fundamental Concepts & Nomenclature
- **Tetravalency & Hybridization:**
  - $sp^3$: 4 single bonds, tetrahedral geometry ($109.5^\circ$), alkanes.
  - $sp^2$: 1 double bond, planar triangular geometry ($120^\circ$), alkenes, benzene.
  - $sp$: 1 triple bond, linear geometry ($180^\circ$), alkynes.
- **Isomerism:**
  - *Structural:* Chain (branched vs straight), Positional (position of functional group), Functional (same MF, different functional group, e.g., Ethanol $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$ and Methoxymethane $\text{CH}_3\text{OCH}_3$).
  - *Geometric (Stereoisomerism):* Cis-trans isomerism occurring around restricted $C=C$ double bonds (requires two different groups attached to each double-bonded carbon).

## 18. Hydrocarbons (Alkanes, Alkenes, Alkynes, Aromatics)
- **Alkanes ($\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$):** Saturated, unreactive. Undergo free-radical **substitution** reactions with halogens in presence of UV light.
- **Alkenes ($\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$):** Unsaturated. Undergo **addition** reactions (hydrogenation, halogenation, hydration).
  - *Test for unsaturation:* Decolorizes bromine water (red-brown $\to$ colorless) and acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ (purple $\to$ colorless).
  - *Markovnikov's Rule:* When an unsymmetrical reagent ($\text{HX}$) adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the hydrogen attaches to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms.
- **Alkynes ($\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$):** Terminal alkynes (e.g., Ethyne $\text{CH}\equiv\text{CH}$, Propyne) have acidic hydrogens:
  - Form reddish-brown precipitate with Ammoniacal Copper(I) Chloride.
  - Form white precipitate with Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate (Tollens' reagent). Non-terminal alkynes (But-2-yne) do not react!
- **Benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$):** Aromatic resonance hybrid. Surprisingly undergoes **substitution** rather than addition due to aromatic stability (nitration with $\text{HNO}_3/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, halogenation with $\text{FeCl}_3$).

## 19. Alkanols, Alkanoic Acids, and Esters
- **Alkanols (Alcohols):**
  - Primary ($1^\circ$): $\text{R}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{[O]} \text{Aldehyde (Alkanal)} \xrightarrow{[O]} \text{Carboxylic Acid}$.
  - Secondary ($2^\circ$): $\text{R}_2\text{CHOH} \xrightarrow{[O]} \text{Ketone (Alkanone)}$ (resistant to further oxidation).
  - Tertiary ($3^\circ$): $\text{R}_3\text{COH}$ (cannot be oxidized under mild conditions).
  - *Iodoform Test:* Compounds containing $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH(OH)}-$ or $\text{CH}_3-\text{C=O}$ group react with $\text{I}_2/\text{NaOH}$ to yield a **yellow precipitate of triiodomethane ($\text{CHI}_3$)**. Ethanol tests positive; Methanol tests negative.
- **Esterification:**
  $$\text{Alkanoic Acid} + \text{Alkanol} \xrightleftharpoons{\text{conc. } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{Ester (Alkanoate)} + \text{Water}$$
  - Esters have characteristic **pleasant fruity smells** (used in perfumes and food flavorings).
- **Saponification (Soap Making):**
  $$\text{Fat/Oil (Triglyceride)} + 3\text{NaOH} \to \text{Glycerol (Propane-1,2,3-triol)} + 3\text{Soap (Sodium Salt)}$$
  - Hard water contains $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ or $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions, which precipitate soap as insoluble scum ($(\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_2\text{Ca}$). Synthetic detergents do not form scum with hard water.

## 20. Polymers and Macromolecules
- **Addition Polymerization:** Monomers with double bonds join without loss of any small molecule (e.g., Polyethene, Polychloroethene/PVC, Polystyrene).
- **Condensation Polymerization:** Monomers join with elimination of small molecules like $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{HCl}$ (e.g., Nylon 6,6, Terylene/Dacron).
- **Proteins:** Polymers of $\alpha$-amino acids linked by **peptide bonds ($-\text{CONH}-$ )**. Tested by Biuret test (violet color) and Millions test (red precipitate).

# SECTION 11: QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS & FLAME TESTS

## 21. Cation Confirmatory Tests
- **Flame Tests:**
  - Sodium ($\text{Na}^+$): **Golden Yellow**
  - Potassium ($\text{K}^+$): **Lilac / Violet** (viewed through cobalt glass)
  - Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$): **Brick Red**
  - Copper ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$): **Bluish-Green**
- **Precipitation with Aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ and $\text{NH}_3$:**
  - $\text{Cu}^{2+}$: Blue precipitate with $\text{NaOH}$; dissolves in excess $\text{NH}_3$ to give a **deep blue solution**.
  - $\text{Fe}^{2+}$: **Dirty green** precipitate, insoluble in excess, turns reddish-brown on standing.
  - $\text{Fe}^{3+}$: **Reddish-brown** precipitate, insoluble in excess.
  - $\text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}$: White precipitates that dissolve in excess $\text{NaOH}$ (amphoteric).
  - *Distinguishing White Precipitates with excess $\text{NH}_3$:*
    - $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ dissolves in excess $\text{NH}_3$ (forms soluble complex $[\text{Zn(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$).
    - $\text{Al}^{3+}$ and $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ remain **insoluble** in excess $\text{NH}_3$.
    - $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ is confirmed by adding $\text{KI} \to$ **bright yellow precipitate of $\text{PbI}_2$** (dissolves on boiling, recrystallizes on cooling).

# SECTION 12: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB CHEMISTRY

1. **Convert Volumes to $\text{dm}^3$ Immediately:** In molarity calculations ($n = C \times V$), volume must be in $\text{dm}^3$. Always divide $\text{cm}^3$ by $1,000$.
2. **Remember Molar Volume at STP:** $1\text{ mole of any gas} = 22.4\text{ dm}^3 = 22,400\text{ cm}^3$ at STP. If you have $44.8\text{ cm}^3$ of oxygen at STP, that is $0.002\text{ moles}$.
3. **Equilibrium Pressure Rule:** Count the gas coefficients! $2\text{SO}_{2(g)} + \text{O}_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_{3(g)}$ has 3 gas moles on the left and 2 on the right. Increasing pressure shifts to the right (fewer moles). Solids and liquids count as 0 moles!
4. **Distinguish Ethanol from Methanol:** Use the **iodoform test**. Ethanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$) gives a yellow precipitate of $\text{CHI}_3$; Methanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$) gives no reaction.
5. **Terminal Alkynes vs Non-Terminal Alkynes:** Only terminal alkynes (with a hydrogen directly attached to the triple-bonded carbon) precipitate with ammoniacal $\text{AgNO}_3$ or $\text{CuCl}$.
6. **Faraday Calculations Shortcut:** The charge required to deposit 1 mole of an element is $n \times 96,500\text{ C}$, where $n$ is the ion charge. To deposit 1 mole of $\text{Al}^{3+}$ requires $3\text{ F}$; $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ requires $2\text{ F}$; $\text{Ag}^+$ requires $1\text{ F}$.
7. **Dilution Formula:** $C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2$. Adding water decreases concentration but the total moles of solute remains constant.
8. **Catalysts Do Not Change Equilibrium Position:** A catalyst speeds up the rate at which equilibrium is reached, but it never increases product yield or shifts the equilibrium constant $K_{eq}$.
9. **Strong vs Weak Acid pH Trap:** $0.1\text{ M } \text{HCl}$ ($\text{pH} = 1$) is much more acidic than $0.1\text{ M } \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ ($\text{pH} \approx 2.9$) because ethanoic acid only partially ionizes in water.
10. **Hydride Boiling Point Anomalies:** Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), and Hydrogen Fluoride ($\text{HF}$) have abnormally high boiling points compared to other hydrides of their groups because of intermolecular **hydrogen bonding**.

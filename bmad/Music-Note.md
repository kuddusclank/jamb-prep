## Overview & How JAMB Tests Music

In the UTME, Music is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major segments:
1. **Rudiments & Music Theory (~12 Questions):** Clefs, pitch notation, note/rest time values, simple and compound time signatures, accidentals, major/minor scales, intervals (size and quality), and transposition.
2. **Harmony, Part-Writing & Musical Form (~10 Questions):** Triads (root position, 1st and 2nd inversions), primary and secondary triads, cadences (perfect, plagal, imperfect, interrupted), four-part SATB vocal harmony, voice leading rules, and structural forms (Binary, Ternary, Rondo, Sonata-Allegro).
3. **African Music & Organology (~10 Questions):** Characteristics of African music (polyrhythm, call-and-response, syncopation), the Hornbostel-Sachs classification of African traditional musical instruments (Idiophones, Membranophones, Chordophones, Aerophones), and socio-cultural contexts.
4. **Western & Nigerian Music History (~8 Questions):** Eras of Western art music (Baroque, Classical, Romantic) and master composers (Bach, Handel, Mozart, Beethoven), Nigerian art music pioneers (Fela Sowande, T.K.E. Phillips, Akin Euba), and popular music genres (Highlife, Juju, Afrobeat, Fuji).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Distinctions in JAMB Music:**
> - Organological Classifications: **Agogo, Sekere, Ekwe** are **Idiophones**; **Gangan (talking drum), Bata** are **Membranophones**; **Goje, Kora** are **Chordophones**; **Kakaki, Algaita, Oja** are **Aerophones**.
> - Cadences: $V \to I$ is a **Perfect Cadence** (full close); $IV \to I$ is a **Plagal Cadence** ("Amen" cadence); Any chord $\to V$ is an **Imperfect Cadence** (half close); $V \to vi$ is an **Interrupted (Deceptive) Cadence**.
> - Major Scale Semitones: In every major scale, semitones occur strictly between the **3rd–4th** and **7th–8th** degrees.

# SECTION 1: RUDIMENTS AND THEORY OF MUSIC

## 1. Notation, Clefs, Notes, and Time Signatures
- **The Stave (Staff) & Clefs:**
  - Standard five horizontal lines and four spaces.
  - **Treble Clef (G Clef):** Loops around the 2nd line ($G_4$). Lines: **E, G, B, D, F**; Spaces: **F, A, C, E**.
  - **Bass Clef (F Clef):** Two dots sandwich the 4th line ($F_3$). Lines: **G, B, D, F, A**; Spaces: **A, C, E, G**.
  - **C Clef (Movable Clef):** Central notch marks Middle C ($C_4$). Placed on 3rd line = **Alto Clef** (used by viola); placed on 4th line = **Tenor Clef** (used by cello, bassoon, trombone).
- **Notes, Rests, and Beat Values:**
  | Note Name | British Name | Relative Duration in Beats (in 4/4 Time) | Equivalent Rest Symbol |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Whole Note** | **Semibreve** | $4\text{ Beats}$ | Filled bar hanging below 4th line |
  | **Half Note** | **Minim** | $2\text{ Beats}$ | Filled bar resting on 3rd line |
  | **Quarter Note** | **Crotchet** | $1\text{ Beat}$ | Vertical squiggly rest |
  | **Eighth Note** | **Quaver** | $\frac{1}{2}\text{ Beat}$ (Single tail/flag) | Resembles figure '7' with hook |
  | **Sixteenth Note** | **Semiquaver** | $\frac{1}{4}\text{ Beat}$ (Double tail/flag) | Figure '7' with double hooks |
- **Dotted Notes:** A dot placed after a note increases its original duration by **half its value** (e.g., a dotted minim $= 2 + 1 = 3\text{ beats}$; a dotted crotchet $= 1 + \frac{1}{2} = 1\frac{1}{2}\text{ beats}$).
- **Time Signatures:**
  - *Top Number:* Indicates the number of beats per measure/bar.
  - *Bottom Number:* Indicates the type of note receiving one beat ($4 =$ quarter note/crotchet, $8 =$ eighth note/quaver).
  - *Simple Time (Beat unit divides into 2 equal parts):*
    - **Simple Duple:** $\mathbf{2/4}$ ($2$ crotchet beats per bar).
    - **Simple Triple:** $\mathbf{3/4}$ ($3$ crotchet beats per bar).
    - **Simple Quadruple:** $\mathbf{4/4}$ ($4$ crotchet beats per bar; also symbolized as **Common Time, C**).
  - *Compound Time (Beat unit is a dotted note dividing into 3 equal parts):*
    - **Compound Duple:** $\mathbf{6/8}$ ($2$ dotted crotchet beats per bar, counted as two groups of 3 quavers: **1**-2-3, **4**-5-6).
    - **Compound Triple:** $\mathbf{9/8}$ ($3$ dotted crotchet beats per bar).
    - **Compound Quadruple:** $\mathbf{12/8}$ ($4$ dotted crotchet beats per bar).

## 2. Scales, Key Signatures, and Intervals
- **Scale Formulas:**
  - *Major Scale:* Step pattern of **Tone – Tone – Semitone – Tone – Tone – Tone – Semitone** ($T-T-S-T-T-T-S$). Semitones occur between **3–4** and **7–8**.
  - *Natural Minor Scale:* Formula: $T-S-T-T-S-T-T$ (semitones between 2–3 and 5–6).
  - *Harmonic Minor Scale:* Natural minor with the **7th degree raised by a semitone** both ascending and descending (creates an augmented 2nd interval between 6th and 7th degrees).
  - *Melodic Minor Scale:* Raises both **6th and 7th degrees** ascending, but reverts to the natural minor descending.
- **Key Signatures & Order of Accidentals:**
  - **Order of Sharps:** $\mathbf{F^\sharp, C^\sharp, G^\sharp, D^\sharp, A^\sharp, E^\sharp, B^\sharp}$ (*"Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle"*).
    - *Shortcut for Major Keys with Sharps:* The last sharp in the key signature is the **leading note (7th degree)**; raise it by a semitone to find the keynote (e.g., last sharp is $F^\sharp \implies$ Key of G Major).
  - **Order of Flats:** $\mathbf{B^\flat, E^\flat, A^\flat, D^\flat, G^\flat, C^\flat, F^\flat}$ (*"Battle Ends And Down Goes Charles' Father"*).
    - *Shortcut for Major Keys with Flats:* The **penultimate (second to last) flat** is the keynote (e.g., two flats $B^\flat, E^\flat \implies$ Key of $B^\flat$ Major; one flat $B^\flat = \text{Key of F Major}$).
- **Technical Names of Scale Degrees:**
  - 1st: **Tonic** | 2nd: **Supertonic** | 3rd: **Mediant** | 4th: **Subdominant** | 5th: **Dominant** | 6th: **Submediant** | 7th: **Leading Note** | 8th: **Tonic / Octave**.
- **Intervals and Inversions:**
  - Perfect intervals: **Unison, 4th, 5th, Octave**. (Never major or minor!).
  - Major / Minor intervals: **2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th**.
  - **The Inversion Rule of Nine:**
    $$\text{Original Interval Number} + \text{Inverted Interval Number} = \mathbf{9}$$
    - Perfect becomes **Perfect** (A Perfect 5th inverts to a Perfect 4th).
    - Major becomes **Minor** (A Major 3rd inverts to a Minor 6th).
    - Augmented becomes **Diminished**.

# SECTION 2: HARMONY, PART-WRITING AND MUSICAL FORM

## 3. Triads, Cadences, and Four-Part Harmony
- **Triads and Chord Building:**
  - A triad consists of a **Root, a 3rd, and a 5th**.
  - *Root Position (5/3):* Root is the lowest sounding bass note.
  - *First Inversion (6/3 or 6):* The 3rd of the triad is in the bass.
  - *Second Inversion (6/4):* The 5th of the triad is in the bass.
  - *Primary Triads:* Built on degrees **I (Tonic), IV (Subdominant), and V (Dominant)**; all are major triads in a major key.
- **The Four Musical Cadences (Harmonic Punctuation):**
  1. **Perfect Cadence (Full Close):** Chord $\mathbf{V \to I}$. The ultimate harmonic resolution, sounding like a definitive grammatical period/full stop.
  2. **Plagal Cadence:** Chord $\mathbf{IV \to I}$. Known as the **"Amen" cadence**; traditionally sung at the conclusion of Christian hymns.
  3. **Imperfect Cadence (Half Close):** Any chord ending on the Dominant ($\mathbf{I \to V, ii \to V, IV \to V}$). Sounds unfinished, expectant, like a comma or question mark.
  4. **Interrupted / Deceptive Cadence:** Chord $\mathbf{V \to vi}$. The dominant chord resolves unexpectedly to the minor submediant ($vi$) instead of the expected tonic ($I$), producing a sudden surprise.
- **Part-Writing Rules for SATB (Soprano, Alto, Tenor, Bass):**
  - **Avoid Parallel Fifths and Parallel Octaves:** Two voice parts must never move in parallel motion at the interval of a 5th or an octave.
  - Never allow voice parts to cross (e.g., Tenor singing higher than Alto).

## 4. Musical Terms, Dynamics, and Structural Forms
- **Dynamic Markings:**
  - $pp$ (*Pianissimo* = Very soft) $\to$ $p$ (*Piano* = Soft) $\to$ $mp$ (*Mezzo-piano* = Moderately soft) $\to$ $mf$ (*Mezzo-forte* = Moderately loud) $\to$ $f$ (*Forte* = Loud) $\to$ $ff$ (*Fortissimo* = Very loud).
  - *Crescendo ($<$):* Gradually getting louder; *Decrescendo / Diminuendo ($>$):* Gradually getting softer.
- **Tempo Markings:**
  - *Largo:* Very slow and broad.
  - *Adagio:* Slow, leisurely.
  - *Andante:* At a walking pace.
  - *Allegro:* Quick, lively, fast.
  - *Presto:* Extremely fast.
  - *Accelerando:* Gradually accelerating; *Ritardando / Rallentando:* Gradually slowing down.
- **Structural Musical Forms:**
  - *Binary Form ($AB$):* Two contrasting sections.
  - *Ternary Form ($ABA$):* Three sections (Statement, Contrast, Recapitulation/Restatement).
  - *Rondo Form ($ABACA$):* Principal recurring theme ($A$) alternates with contrasting episodes ($B, C$).
  - *Sonata-Allegro Form:* Three core movements: **Exposition** (introduces subjects in contrasting keys) $\to$ **Development** (themes fragmented and modulated) $\to$ **Recapitulation** (both subjects restated in the tonic key).

# SECTION 3: AFRICAN TRADITIONAL MUSIC & ORGANOLOGY

## 5. Characteristics and Organological Classification
- **Core Elements of African Music:**
  - **Polyrhythm:** The simultaneous performance of two or more conflicting rhythmic meters (e.g., 3 against 2).
  - **Call-and-Response:** Soloist sings an improvised melodic phrase (**Call**), answered by the choral ensemble with a fixed refain (**Response**).
  - **Syncopation:** Deliberately shifting musical accents to the weak or off-beats.
  - **Ostinato:** A persistently repeated rhythmic or melodic phrase forming the foundation of a performance.
- **The Hornbostel-Sachs Classification of African Instruments:**
  1. **Idiophones (Instruments whose solid body vibrates to produce sound without strings or membranes):**
     - Slit Wooden Drum (**Ekwe** of the Igbo).
     - Clapperless Iron Hand Bell (**Agogo** of the Yoruba, **Ogene** of the Igbo).
     - Beaded Gourd Rattle (**Sekere** of the Yoruba, **Ichaka** of the Igbo).
     - Wooden Xylophone (**Ngedegwu** of the Igbo, **Kundung** of the Birom).
     - Lamellophone / Thumb Piano (**Agidigbo** of the Yoruba, **Ubo-aka** of the Igbo, **Mbira**).
  2. **Membranophones (Instruments that produce sound via a tightly stretched vibrating skin/membrane):**
     - Hourglass Talking Drum (**Dundun / Gangan** of the Yoruba, squeezed under the armpit to change pitch, mimicking tonal language).
     - **Bata** Drums (conical, double-headed drums sacred to Sango, the Yoruba god of thunder).
     - **Sakara** Drum (circular clay-framed tambourine-like drum).
  3. **Chordophones (Instruments that produce sound through vibrating stretched strings):**
     - **Goje / Goge:** One-stringed bowed horsehair fiddle popular in Hausaland.
     - **Kora:** 21-string harp-lute played by Mandinka griots in West Africa.
     - Earth Bow / Musical Bow.
  4. **Aerophones (Wind instruments that produce sound through a vibrating column of air):**
     - **Kakaki:** Long, straight metal royal trumpet (up to 3 meters long) blown exclusively for Emirs and Sultans in Northern Nigeria.
     - **Algaita:** Double-reed wooden oboe-like instrument used in Hausa royal courts.
     - **Oja:** Small notched wooden transverse flute of the Igbo, used for praising masquerades and titled men.
     - Elephant Tusk Horns (**Odu** of the Igbo).

# SECTION 4: WESTERN & NIGERIAN MUSIC HISTORY

## 6. Western Music Eras and Composers
- **Baroque Era (1600–1750):** Continuous basso continuo, terraced dynamics, complex polyphony, fugues, cantatas, and oratorios.
  - **Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750):** Master of polyphony and fugue; composed *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, *Brandenburg Concertos*.
  - **George Frideric Handel (1685–1759):** Master of English oratorios; composed ***The Messiah*** (famous for the *"Hallelujah Chorus"*).
- **Classical Era (1750–1820):** Homophonic texture (melody with chordal accompaniment), emotional restraint, balance, perfection of the Symphony and String Quartet.
  - **Franz Joseph Haydn (1732–1809):** "Father of the Symphony" and "Father of the String Quartet".
  - **Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756–1791):** Child prodigy; composed operas (*The Magic Flute, The Marriage of Figaro*), symphonies (*No. 40, 41*), and requiems.
  - **Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827):** Bridge between Classical and Romantic eras; composed 9 epic symphonies despite progressive deafness (*Symphony No. 5, Symphony No. 9 "Choral"*).
- **Romantic Era (1820–1900):** Emotional passion, expressive freedom, virtuosity, programmatic music, nationalism. Master composers: **Frédéric Chopin** (piano), **Franz Schubert** (Lieder), **Pyotr Tchaikovsky** (ballets).

---

## 7. Nigerian Art and Popular Music Traditions
- **Pioneers of Nigerian Art Music:**
  - **T.K.E. Phillips (1884–1969):** Organist at Cathedral Church of Christ, Lagos; universally known as the **"Father of Nigerian Church Music"**; published the foundational treatise *Yoruba Music* (1953), pioneering the harmonization of indigenous pentatonic melodies without violating native tonal inflections.
  - **Fela Sowande (1905–1987):** The most renowned African composer of Western art music; composed the celebrated ***African Suite for String Orchestra***, skillfully weaving indigenous Nigerian folk tunes into classical symphonic structures.
  - **Akin Euba (1935–2020):** Renowned musicologist who formulated the creative theory of **"African Pianism"** (a keyboard performance style treating the European grand piano as an African percussive instrument to replicate the rhythmic complexity of traditional drum batteries).
  - **Laz Ekwueme:** Renowned composer and choral conductor; pioneer of scholarly studies in African choral polyphony.
- **Pioneers of Nigerian Popular Music:**
  - **Highlife Music:** Originated in Ghana, fused with Nigerian palm-wine acoustic music and brass bands. Pioneers: **E.T. Mensah** ("King of Highlife"), **Dr. Victor Olaiya**, **Cardinal Rex Jim Lawson**, and **Chief Osita Osadebe**.
  - **Juju Music:** Originated in Lagos in the 1930s among Yoruba social clubs. Pioneers: **Tunde King**, **I.K. Dairo** (introduced the accordion and talking drum), **King Sunny Ade** (introduced the pedal steel guitar, synthesizers, and vibraphone), and **Chief Commander Ebenezer Obey** (developed the distinctive "Miliki" sound).
  - **Afrobeat:** Created in the late 1960s by **Fela Anikulapo-Kuti**; an explosive, polyrhythmic fusion of highlife, American funk (James Brown), jazz, and traditional Yoruba spiritual drumming, infused with radical anti-corruption socio-political protest lyrics.
  - **Fuji Music:** Developed in the late 1960s by **Dr. Sikiru Ayinde Barrister**; evolved directly from traditional Islamic **"Were" / "Ajiwere"** music (early morning wake-up chanting performed during Ramadan); devoid of Western guitars, driven solely by talking drums, sakara, and vocal improvisation; modernized by Ayinla Kollington and King Wasiu Ayinde Marshal (K1 De Ultimate).

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB MUSIC

1. **Plagal Cadence Recognition:** A cadence moving from **$IV \to I$** is always a **Plagal Cadence**, popularly known as the "Amen Cadence".
2. **Order of Sharps and Flats:** Sharps $=$ **FCGDAEB**; Flats $=$ **BEADGCF**. They are exact mirror reversals of each other!
3. **Organological Classification of Gangan:** The Yoruba talking drum (*Gangan*) is a **Membranophone**, while the iron gong (*Agogo*) and wooden slit drum (*Ekwe*) are **Idiophones**.
4. **Kakaki Instrument Type:** The *Kakaki* is a brass royal trumpet of Northern Nigeria, classified as an **Aerophone**.
5. **Major Scale Semitone Locations:** Semitones in a major scale occur strictly between scale degrees **3 and 4**, and **7 and 8**.
6. **Compound Duple Time:** **$6/8$** time is **Compound Duple**, meaning it has two main dotted-crotchet beats per bar, NOT six individual beats.
7. **Father of Nigerian Church Music:** **T.K.E. Phillips** is the "Father of Nigerian Church Music", while **Fela Sowande** composed the famous *African Suite*.
8. **Origin of Fuji Music:** Fuji music evolved from **Were / Ajiwere** Islamic music, created by **Dr. Sikiru Ayinde Barrister**.
9. **Rule of Nine for Inverting Intervals:** An interval and its inversion always add up to **$9$**. A Major 3rd inverts to a **Minor 6th** ($3 + 6 = 9$; Major becomes Minor).
10. **African Pianism Concept:** The concept of using the European piano as an African percussive drum instrument was pioneered by **Professor Akin Euba**.

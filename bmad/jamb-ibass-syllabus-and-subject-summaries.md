# Step 1: JAMB IBASS Syllabus Inventory & Structural Architecture

## Overview of the IBASS Portal

The **Integrated Brochure and Syllabus System (IBASS)** is the official regulatory and curriculum publication platform deployed by the **Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB)** of Nigeria. Hosted officially at `https://ibass.jamb.gov.ng`, IBASS serves two fundamental mandates for tertiary admissions in Nigeria:
1. **Interactive Brochure:** Outlines the statutory minimum admission criteria, UTME subject combinations, O'Level requirements (WAEC/NECO/NABTEB), Direct Entry (DE) requirements, and accredited degree/diploma programs across all Nigerian universities, polytechnics, monotechnics, and colleges of education.
2. **E-Syllabus:** Houses the official national curricula and assessment frameworks for all **25 approved subjects** tested in the annual Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME).

Each official subject syllabus establishes:
- The broad **Aims and General Objectives** of the curriculum.
- Specific **Topics / Contents** organized into thematic units.
- Explicit **Learning Objectives** ("Candidates should be able to..."), which govern the cognitive level of examination questions.
- A curated **Bibliography / Recommended Textbooks** list evaluated and approved by subject panels consisting of university professors, curriculum specialists from the Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council (NERDC), and chief examiners.

---

## UTME Examination Architecture

Every registered UTME candidate must sit for exactly **four (4) subjects**:
1. **Use of English (Compulsory):** 60 questions.
2. **Three (3) Faculty-Specific Electives:** 40 questions each (Total 120 elective questions).

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        JAMB UTME EXAM STRUCTURE                        │
├─────────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬────────────────────┤
│ Subject Component   │ Questions    │ Time Share   │ Scaled Score Marks │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┼────────────────────┤
│ 1. Use of English   │ 60 questions │ ~40 minutes  │ 100 marks (scaled) │
│ 2. Elective One     │ 40 questions │ ~27 minutes  │ 100 marks (scaled) │
│ 3. Elective Two     │ 40 questions │ ~27 minutes  │ 100 marks (scaled) │
│ 4. Elective Three   │ 40 questions │ ~27 minutes  │ 100 marks (scaled) │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┼────────────────────┤
│ TOTALS              │ 180 questions│ 120 minutes  │ 400 marks max      │
└─────────────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┴────────────────────┘
```

**Key Exam Hall Conditions:**
- **Duration:** Exactly 120 minutes (2 hours) total for all 4 subjects combined.
- **Pacing:** Average of 40 seconds per question across the 180 items.
- **Interface:** Standard JAMB 8-key keyboard navigation (`A`, `B`, `C`, `D` for options; `P` for Previous; `N` for Next; `S` for Submit; `R` for Return/Review) or mouse cursor navigation.
- **Negative Marking:** None. Unanswered or incorrect questions score 0 marks.

# Step 2: Comprehensive Subject-by-Subject Syllabus Summaries

Below is the definitive, structured syllabus summary for each of the 25 subjects tested in the JAMB UTME.

---

### 1. Use of English (`ENG`) — Compulsory

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To assess candidates’ competence in communicating effectively in written English; understanding and synthesizing literary and non-literary prose; analyzing grammatical structures, syntactic patterns, idioms, and figurative expressions; mastering pronunciation, vowel/consonant distinctions, word stress, and emphatic stress patterns.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Comprehension and Summary:** Unseen narrative, descriptive, expository, and argumentative passages. Identifying topic sentences, central themes, implied meanings, author's mood, attitude, and tone.
  2. **Lexis and Structure (Vocabulary & Grammar):** Synonyms (nearest in meaning); Antonyms (opposite in meaning); Sentence interpretation (idioms, proverbs, figurative meanings); Sentence completion (prepositions, conjunctions, tenses, concord, phrasal verbs, clauses).
  3. **Oral Forms (Phonology):** Vowels (monophthongs, diphthongs, triphthongs); Consonants (single consonants, consonant clusters); Rhymes; Syllable stress (primary stress in words of 2, 3, 4+ syllables); Emphatic stress (determining focus of assertion in spoken sentences).
  4. **Prescribed Reading Text:** In-depth study of characterization, themes, plot development, narrative point of view, setting, and sociopolitical relevance of the current JAMB-prescribed reading text (e.g., *The Lekki Headmaster* by Kabir Adeniyi / *Life Changer* by Khadija Abubakar Jalli).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (60 Questions):**
  - Section A: Comprehension & Summary (Passage 1: 5 questions; Cloze Passage: 10 questions) = 15 Qs
  - Section B: Prescribed Reading Text = 10 Qs
  - Section C: Lexis and Structure (Sentence Interpretation: 5 Qs; Antonyms: 5 Qs; Synonyms: 5 Qs; Sentence Completion: 10 Qs) = 25 Qs
  - Section D: Oral Forms (Vowels/Consonants: 4 Qs; Rhymes: 2 Qs; Word Stress: 2 Qs; Emphatic Stress: 2 Qs) = 10 Qs
- **Prescribed Reading:**
  - Standard national reading text announced annually by JAMB Registrar.
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Students often lose marks on Oral Forms (specifically identifying silent letters such as the 'b' in *subtle* or 'p' in *receipt*, and distinguishing between noun/verb stress shifts like *RE-cord* vs. *re-CORD*).
  - Comprehension questions demand contextual inference rather than verbatim sentence matching.

---

### 2. Mathematics (`MTH`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate mathematical reasoning, computational precision, spatial relationships, algebraic manipulation, logical deduction, and ability to apply mathematical concepts to real-world physical and socio-economic situations.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Number and Numeration:** Number bases (conversions, operations in base $n$); Modular arithmetic; Fractions, decimals, percentages, and approximations; Indices, logarithms (log laws, characteristics/mantissa), and surds (rationalization); Sets (Venn diagrams, operations).
  2. **Algebra:** Polynomials (factor and remainder theorems); Linear and quadratic equations/inequalities; Simultaneous equations; Variation (direct, inverse, joint, partial); Progressions (Arithmetic and Geometric Progressions - AP/GP); Binary operations; Matrices and Determinants ($2 \times 2$ and $3 \times 3$).
  3. **Geometry and Trigonometry:** Euclidean plane geometry (angles on lines, triangles, polygons, circles, circle theorems); Mensuration (lengths of arcs, perimeters, areas of plane shapes, surface areas and volumes of solids - spheres, cones, cylinders, pyramids, frustums); Loci in 2D; Coordinate geometry (gradients, distances, midpoints, equations of straight lines, parallel/perpendicular lines); Trigonometric ratios, sine and cosine rules, angles of elevation and depression, bearings and distances.
  4. **Calculus:** Differentiation (limits, first principles, polynomial functions, product, quotient, chain rule); Applications of differentiation (rates of change, tangents/normals, stationary points, maxima/minima); Integration (indefinite and definite integrals, area under curves).
  5. **Statistics and Probability:** Frequency distributions, histograms, cumulative frequency (ogive); Measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode); Measures of dispersion (range, variance, standard deviation, interquartile range); Permutations and combinations; Elementary probability theory (independent, mutually exclusive events).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Number & Numeration: ~8 Qs
  - Algebra: ~12 Qs
  - Geometry & Trigonometry: ~10 Qs
  - Calculus: ~5 Qs
  - Statistics & Probability: ~5 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Candidates struggle most with geometric circle theorems, 3D mensuration of frustums, matrix determinants, and differentiation applications.
  - Calculators are strictly prohibited in the UTME hall (candidates are given a basic digital 4-function onscreen calculator), making mental arithmetic, estimation, and algebraic factoring essential speed skills.

---

### 3. Physics (`PHY`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To develop an understanding of physical principles, scientific inquiries, mathematical formulations of laws of nature, technological applications of physics, and laboratory analysis.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Mechanics and Measurement:** Fundamental and derived units, dimensional analysis; Kinematics (scalars, vectors, motion in 1D and 2D, projectile motion); Newton's laws of motion, momentum and impulse; Work, energy, power, and conservation of mechanical energy; Machines and mechanical advantage; Circular motion, centripetal force, and gravitation; Equilibrium of forces, center of gravity, and stability; Elasticity (Hooke’s law, Young’s modulus); Fluid statics and dynamics (pressure, Archimedes’ principle, flotation, surface tension, viscosity).
  2. **Thermal Physics and Properties of Matter:** Thermometry, temperature scales; Thermal expansion (solids, liquids, gases); Heat capacity, specific latent heat; Gas laws (Boyle’s, Charles’, Gay-Lussac’s, ideal gas equation); Kinetic theory of gases; Heat transfer (conduction, convection, radiation).
  3. **Waves, Sound, and Optics:** Wave motion (transverse and longitudinal, wave equation $v = f\lambda$); Reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference, polarization; Sound waves (velocity, pitch, loudness, timbre, resonance, Doppler effect, vibrations in strings and pipes); Light reflection (plane and curved mirrors); Light refraction (Snell’s law, critical angle, total internal reflection, lenses, prism dispersion); Optical instruments (microscopes, telescopes, eye defects and corrections).
  4. **Electricity and Magnetism:** Electrostatics (Coulomb’s law, electric fields, capacitors); Current electricity (Ohm’s law, series/parallel circuits, EMF, internal resistance, heating effects, electrical power); Magnetism (magnetic fields, terrestrial magnetism); Electromagnetism (magnetic forces on conductors, electric motors, electromagnetic induction, Faraday’s and Lenz’s laws, transformers, AC circuits, reactance, resonance).
  5. **Modern, Atomic, and Nuclear Physics:** Conduction through gases (cathode rays); Thermionic and photoelectric emission; Wave-particle duality, de Broglie wavelength; Atomic structure, energy levels, Bohr model, X-rays; Radioactivity ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ decays, half-life, nuclear fission and fusion); Elementary semiconductor electronics ($p$-$n$ junctions, diodes, rectifiers).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Mechanics & Properties of Matter: ~14 Qs
  - Thermal Physics: ~6 Qs
  - Waves, Sound & Optics: ~8 Qs
  - Electricity & Magnetism: ~8 Qs
  - Modern / Nuclear Physics: ~4 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Heavy concentration of quantitative problems involving unit conversions ($cm^3$ to $m^3$, $kW \cdot h$ to Joules).
  - Frequent conceptual traps occur in AC circuit phase diagrams, lens formula sign conventions, and transformer efficiency equations.

---

### 4. Chemistry (`CHM`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To cultivate knowledge of basic chemical principles, concepts, reactions, calculations, laboratory techniques, industrial chemical processes, and the environmental impacts of chemicals.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Separation of Mixtures and Purification:** Physical and chemical changes; Pure substances vs mixtures; Filtration, distillation, fractional distillation, crystallization, chromatography, sublimation.
  2. **Chemical Combination and Stoichiometry:** Laws of chemical combination; Atomic, molecular, and formula masses; The mole concept, Avogadro's number, molar volume of gases; Empirical and molecular formulas; Chemical equations and stoichiometric calculations.
  3. **Atomic Structure and the Periodic Table:** Subatomic particles (protons, neutrons, electrons), isotopes; Electronic configurations (Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, Hund’s rule); Periodic trends (atomic/ionic radii, ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity).
  4. **Chemical Bonding:** Electrovalent (ionic), covalent, co-ordinate (dative), metallic, and intermolecular bonds (van der Waals, hydrogen bonding); Shapes of molecules.
  5. **States of Matter & Gas Laws:** Kinetic theory; Boyle’s, Charles’, Graham’s law of diffusion, Dalton’s law of partial pressures; Liquids and solids.
  6. **Energetics, Kinetics, and Chemical Equilibrium:** Endothermic and exothermic reactions, enthalpy changes ($\Delta H$); Collision theory, activation energy, catalysts; Dynamic equilibrium, Le Chatelier’s principle.
  7. **Acids, Bases, and Salts:** Arrhenius, Brønsted-Lowry, Lewis concepts; pH scale, neutralization, indicators; Types of salts, hydrolysis of salts, standard solutions, and volumetric analysis calculations.
  8. **Redox Reactions and Electrochemistry:** Oxidation numbers; Balancing redox equations; Electrolysis (Faraday’s laws, preferential discharge of ions); Electrochemical cells (galvanic/voltaic cells, standard electrode potentials), corrosion.
  9. **Chemistry of Elements (Inorganic):** Non-metals: Hydrogen, oxygen, halogens, nitrogen, sulfur, carbon, and their principal compounds (oxides, hydrides, oxoacids); Metals: Alkali and alkaline earth metals, aluminum, iron, copper, extraction of metals; Alloys.
  10. **Organic Chemistry:** Tetravalency of carbon, hybridization ($sp^3, sp^2, sp$); Homologous series, functional groups, IUPAC nomenclature, isomerism; Hydrocarbons: Alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, benzene and aromatic compounds; Alkanols, alkanoic acids, alkanoates (esters), fats and oils, soaps and detergents; Polymers and macromolecules (carbohydrates, proteins, synthetic polymers).
  11. **Environmental Chemistry and Industry:** Atmospheric pollution, acid rain, greenhouse effect, ozone depletion; Water pollution, water treatment; Chemical industries and biotechnology.
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - General Chemistry & Physical Chemistry (Gas laws, Stoichiometry, Energetics, Kinetics, Equilibrium): ~14 Qs
  - Inorganic Chemistry (Periodic trends, Non-metals, Metals): ~10 Qs
  - Organic Chemistry & Polymers: ~10 Qs
  - Analytical, Environmental & Industrial Chemistry: ~6 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - High calculation volume around volumetric titrations ($C_A V_A / C_B V_B = n_A / n_B$), gas laws, and Faraday's electrolysis calculations ($Q = It = n F$).
  - Organic reaction mechanisms, IUPAC rules for polyfunctional groups, and qualitative salt analysis ions tests ($Fe^{2+}, Fe^{3+}, Cu^{2+}, Pb^{2+}, SO_4^{2-}, Cl^-$) appear constantly.

### 5. Biology (`BIO`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate understanding of the diversity, anatomy, physiology, and evolutionary relationships of living organisms, ecological principles, genetics, and the practical application of biological knowledge in medicine, agriculture, and conservation.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Living Organisms and Classification:** Characteristics of life; Levels of biological organization (cell, tissue, organ, system, organism); Classification systems (Kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia); Diagnostic features of major phyla and classes.
  2. **Cell Biology and Cellular Metabolism:** Cell structure and functions (organelles); Plant vs animal cells; Diffusion, osmosis, plasmolysis, and active transport; Cell division (mitosis and meiosis).
  3. **Internal Structure and Physiology of Plants and Animals:**
     - Nutrition: Autotrophic (photosynthesis, mineral nutrition) and heterotrophic (holozoic, parasitic, saprophytic); Digestive systems and enzymes.
     - Transport: Vascular tissues in plants (xylem, phloem), transpiration; Circulatory systems in animals (blood composition, heart structure, double circulation).
     - Respiration: Aerobic and anaerobic respiration; Gaseous exchange surfaces in plants (stomata, lenticels) and animals (skin, gills, tracheae, lungs).
     - Excretion and Osmoregulation: Excretory organs and wastes (kidneys, malpighian tubules, nephridia, skin).
     - Support and Movement: Skeletal systems (hydrostatic, exoskeleton, endoskeleton); Bones of mammalian skeleton; Joints.
     - Coordination and Irritability: Nervous system (brain, spinal cord, reflex actions); Endocrine system (hormones); Sense organs (eye, ear, skin).
     - Reproduction: Asexual reproduction (binary fission, budding, vegetative propagation); Sexual reproduction in plants (flowers, pollination, fertilization, seed/fruit dispersal); Sexual reproduction in animals (reproductive systems, gametogenesis, fertilization, embryonic development).
  4. **Ecology:** Ecosystem components (biotic, abiotic); Energy flow (food chains, food webs, trophic levels); Biogeochemical cycles (carbon, nitrogen, water); Ecological habitats (aquatic: marine, estuarine, freshwater; terrestrial: savanna, rainforest, desert); Ecological succession; Population ecology (density, carrying capacity); Pollution and conservation of natural resources.
  5. **Heredity and Evolution:** Mendel’s laws of inheritance; Monohybrid and dihybrid crosses; Genes, chromosomes, DNA; Blood groups, sex determination, sex-linked traits (hemophilia, color blindness); Morphological and physiological variation; Theories of evolution (Lamarckism, Darwinism); Evidence of evolution (fossils, comparative anatomy, embryology).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Variety of Organisms & Cell Structure: ~6 Qs
  - Form and Function (Physiology of Plants and Animals): ~16 Qs
  - Ecology and Environmental Biology: ~10 Qs
  - Heredity, Genetics, and Evolution: ~8 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - High volume of diagrammatic questions (identifying parts of the nephron, inner ear, mammalian heart, flower parts, and reflex arc).
  - Candidates often confuse genetic inheritance ratios (3:1 phenotypic vs 1:2:1 genotypic) and ecological biomes distribution in Nigeria.

### 6. Economics (`ECN`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate grasp of fundamental economic principles, tools of economic analysis, understanding of the structure and workings of the Nigerian and global economy, and capacity to interpret socio-economic data.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Foundations of Economics & Methodology:** Definition, scope, economics as a social science; Basic concepts: Wants, scarcity, scale of preference, choice, opportunity cost; Production possibility curve (PPC).
  2. **Tools of Economic Analysis:** Tables, graphs, charts; Measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion; Basic percentages and index numbers.
  3. **Price Theory (Demand, Supply, and Market Equilibrium):** Law of demand, demand curve, determinants; Elasticity of demand (price, income, cross); Law of supply, supply curve, determinants; Elasticity of supply; Market equilibrium, price determination, price control (minimum/maximum price legislation).
  4. **Theory of Consumer Behaviour:** Utility concepts (total, marginal utility, law of diminishing marginal utility); Equi-marginal principle; Consumer surplus.
  5. **Theory of Production and Cost:** Factors of production and rewards (land, labour, capital, entrepreneur); Division of labour and specialization; Short-run and long-run production; Law of variable proportions; Returns to scale; Cost concepts ($TC, TFC, TVC, AC, MC$); Revenue concepts ($TR, AR, MR$).
  6. **Market Structures:** Perfect competition; Monopoly; Monopolistic competition; Oligopoly; Price discrimination.
  7. **National Income Accounting:** Concepts ($GDP, GNP, NNP, NI$); Circular flow of income; Methods of measuring national income; Problems of measurement; Uses of national income data.
  8. **Money, Banking, and Inflation:** Evolution and functions of money; Value of money and quantity theory of money; Commercial banks, credit creation; Central Bank and monetary policy; Inflation and deflation (causes, effects, controls).

  3. **Food and Nutrition:** Food nutrients (sources, functions, deficiency diseases); Digestion, absorption, and metabolism; Meal planning for physiological and vulnerable groups (infants, adolescents, manual workers, elderly, convalescents); Cooking methods (moist heat, dry heat, frying); Kitchen safety and hygiene; Food spoilage, preservation, and storage techniques; Flour mixtures (pastries, cakes, yeast doughs, raising agents); Table setting and etiquette.
  4. **Clothing and Textiles:** Textile fibers (natural: cotton, linen, silk, wool; synthetic: nylon, polyester, rayon); Fiber manufacturing, properties, identification tests (burning tests); Fabric construction (weaving, knitting); Sewing tools and machines; Garment construction (stitches, seams, darts, gathers, pleats, facings, sleeves, collars, fasteners); Wardrobe planning, garment repair, laundry, and stain removal.
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Home Management & Family Living: ~12 Qs
  - Food & Nutrition: ~16 Qs
  - Clothing & Textiles: ~12 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Candidates must know the specific chemical tests for textile fibers and exact nutrient requirements for specialized diets (e.g., diabetic vs hypertensive meal design).

### 19. Art / Visual Arts (`ART`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To assess understanding of the visual elements and principles of design, historical development of traditional African and world art traditions, contemporary Nigerian art, and creative workshop practice.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Classification and Design Foundations:** Branches of art (Fine art, Applied/Commercial art, Industrial art); Visual elements of art (line, shape, form, space, texture, value, color); Principles of design (balance, harmony, contrast, rhythm, proportion, dominance/emphasis, unity).
  2. **Art Materials, Tools, and Techniques:** Drawing (life, still life, nature, perspective: 1-point and 2-point); Painting (media: watercolor, oil, gouache, acrylic; techniques: wash, impasto); Sculpture (carving, modelling, casting, construction; materials: clay, wood, metal, plaster); Ceramics (pottery methods: pinch, coil, slab; firing, glazing); Graphics and Design (lettering, calligraphy, posters, book illustration, printmaking: relief, serigraphy/screen printing); Textiles (tie and dye, batik, applique).
  3. **History of Art (World and Africa):** Prehistoric art; Ancient Egyptian art; Classical Greek and Roman art; Traditional African art traditions: Nok terracottas, Igbo-Ukwu bronzes, Ife terracottas and bronzes, Benin court art (cire perdue / lost-wax casting), Owo, Esie soapstones; Traditional masks and masquerade traditions.
  4. **Modern and Contemporary Nigerian Art:** Pioneer Nigerian artists (Aina Onabolu, Akinola Lasekan); Masters of modern Nigerian art (Ben Enwonwu, Bruce Onobrakpeya, Yusuf Grillo, Demas Nwoko, Uche Okeke, Erhabor Emokpae); The Zaria Art Society ("Natural Synthesis"); Major art galleries, museums, and monuments in Nigeria.
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Elements & Principles of Design: ~8 Qs
  - Tools, Materials & Studio Techniques: ~12 Qs
  - Traditional African Art History: ~12 Qs
  - Modern Nigerian & World Art: ~8 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Identifying distinguishing stylistic features of Nok (triangular pierced eyes) vs Ife (naturalistic, striated facial marks) vs Benin (stylized ceremonial regalia) is tested without fail.

### 20. Music (`MUS`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate musicianship, notation literacy, harmonic analysis, knowledge of African musical forms and instruments, Western music history, and prominent Nigerian art and popular musicians.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Rudiments and Theory of Music:** The musical staff, clefs (treble, bass, alto, tenor); Note and rest values; Measure and time signatures (simple, compound, irregular); Pitch notation, accidentals, enharmonics; Scale construction (major scales, natural/harmonic/melodic minor scales, chromatic, pentatonic); Key signatures (up to 5 sharps and flats); Intervals (size, quality, inversion); Transposition.
  2. **Harmony and Part-Writing:** Triads and chord building (root position, first and second inversions); Primary and secondary triads; Cadences (perfect, imperfect, plagal, interrupted); Four-part vocal harmony (SATB voice ranges, voice-leading rules, parallel octaves/fifths avoidance); Modulation to closely related keys.
  3. **Musical Terms, Forms, and Instruments:** Dynamic, tempo, and articulation markings; Western orchestral instruments (strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion); Musical forms (binary, ternary, rondo, sonata-allegro, theme and variations).
  4. **African Music:** Characteristics (polyrhythm, call-and-response, syncopation, ostinato); African traditional instruments and classifications (Idiophones, Membranophones, Chordophones, Aerophones); Social contexts of music in African life (festivals, rites, work).
  5. **History of Western Music:** Medieval, Renaissance, Baroque (J.S. Bach, G.F. Handel), Classical (J. Haydn, W.A. Mozart, L. van Beethoven), Romantic (F. Chopin, F. Schubert, J. Brahms), 20th Century Modernism.
  6. **Nigerian Art and Popular Music:** Pioneer art composers (T.K.E. Phillips, Fela Sowande, Akin Euba, Ayo Bankole, Laz Ekwueme); Popular music genres and pioneers (Highlife: E.T. Mensah, Victor Olaiya, Rex Lawson; Juju: I.K. Dairo, King Sunny Ade, Ebenezer Obey; Afrobeat: Fela Anikulapo-Kuti; Fuji: Sikiru Ayinde Barrister).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Rudiments & Theory: ~12 Qs
  - Harmony & Compositional Analysis: ~10 Qs
  - African Traditional Music & Instruments: ~10 Qs
  - Western & Nigerian Music History: ~8 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Candidates must recognize organological classifications (e.g. *gangan* is a membranophone, *agogo* is an idiophone, *kakaki* is an aerophone).

### 21. Arabic (`ARA`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To assess candidates’ ability to read, comprehend, and translate Modern Standard Arabic (*al-Fusha*); compose standard Arabic essays; analyze grammatical syntax (*an-Nahw*) and morphology (*as-Sarf*); and appreciate prescribed literary texts.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Comprehension and Translation:** Unseen classical and modern Arabic prose passages; Direct translation from Arabic to English and English to Arabic.
  2. **Morphology (*As-Sarf*):** Verb roots (Triliteral - *ath-Thulathi*); Derived verb forms (*Awzan al-Fi'l* - Forms I through X); Active and passive participles (*Ism al-Fa'il*, *Ism al-Maf'ul*); Verbal nouns (*al-Masdar*); Broken plurals (*Jam' at-Taksir*); Diminutive (*at-Tasghir*).
  3. **Grammar and Syntax (*An-Nahw*):** Parts of speech (*al-Ism, al-Fi'l, al-Harf*); The Nominal Sentence (*al-Jumla al-Ismiyyah*: *Mubtada'* and *Khabar*); The Verbal Sentence (*al-Jumla al-Fi'liyyah*: *Fi'l, Fa'il, Maf'ul*); Modifiers: *Kana wa Akhwatuha*, *Inna wa Akhwatuha*; Grammatical cases and declensions (*al-I'rab*: *Marfu', Mansub, Majrur, Majzum*); Pronouns (*Dama'ir*), relative pronouns, prepositions; Number systems and countables (*al-'Adad wa al-Ma'dud*).
  4. **Literature (*Al-Adab*):** Outline of Pre-Islamic (*Jahiliyyah*), Early Islamic, Umayyad, Abbasid, and Modern Arabic literary eras; Analysis of prescribed Arabic poetry and prose texts.
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Comprehension & Translation: ~10 Qs
  - Grammar & Syntax (*Nahw*): ~14 Qs
  - Morphology (*Sarf*): ~8 Qs
  - Literature & Appreciation (*Adab*): ~8 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Case ending rules (*Dammah, Fathah, Kasrah, Sukun*) under the governance of particles like *Inna* and *Kana* are tested on nearly every grammar question.

### 22. French (`FRN`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate candidates' proficiency in reading and interpreting written French, mastering French grammar and vocabulary, sound discrimination and phonetics, and understanding Francophone cultures.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Written Comprehension:** Expository, narrative, and dialogic French texts; Literal, inferred, and thematic comprehension questions.
  2. **Grammar and Sentence Structures:**
     - Nouns and determiners (definite, indefinite, partitive articles); Gender and number agreement.
     - Adjectives (qualitative, demonstrative, possessive, interrogative, numeral); Irregular adjectives and position (before/after nouns).
     - Pronouns (subject, tonic, direct object: *le, la, les*, indirect object: *lui, leur*, adverbial pronouns: *y, en*, relative pronouns: *qui, que, dont, où*).
     - Verbs and tenses: *Présent de l'indicatif, Passé composé, Imparfait, Plus-que-parfait, Futur simple, Futur proche, Conditionnel présent, Subjonctif présent, Impératif*; Pronominal verbs; Active and passive voice.
     - Negation (*ne... pas, ne... jamais, ne... rien, ne... personne*), interrogation, adverbs, prepositions.
  3. **Vocabulary and Idiomatic Usage:** Everyday lexical fields (family, school, transport, health, commerce, hobbies); Synonyms, antonyms, false friends (*faux amis*); Common idioms and proverbs.
  4. **Phonetics and Oral Discrimination:** Identification of French vowels, nasal vowels, consonants, silent final letters; Liaisons and elisions; Rhymes and sound matching.
  5. **Francophone Culture and Civilization:** Social customs, geographical regions of France and Francophone West Africa (Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Benin, Niger, Togo, Cameroon); Francophone institutions (*OIF*).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Written Comprehension: ~8 Qs
  - Grammar & Syntax: ~18 Qs
  - Vocabulary & Idioms: ~6 Qs
  - Phonetics & Sound Matching: ~4 Qs
  - Francophone Culture: ~4 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Agreement of the past participle with preceding direct objects (*accord du participe passé*) and distinctions between *Imparfait* and *Passé composé* are notorious student traps.

### 23. Hausa (`HAU`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate competence in standard written Hausa orthography, grammatical analysis, phonological awareness, understanding of oral and written Hausa literature, and mastery of traditional Hausa cultural institutions.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Language and Grammar (*Harshe*):**
     - Reading Comprehension (*Karatun Fahimta*): Unseen narrative and expository passages.
     - Composition (*Rubutun Maqala*): Formal and informal writing, structure, and expression.
     - Orthography and Mechanics (*Qa'idojin Rubutu*): Standard orthography, capitalization, punctuation, word division.
     - Phonology (*Tsarin Sauti*): Vowels (*Wasula* - short, long, diphthongs); Consonants (*Haruffan baki* - glottalized, implosive, ejectives: *ɓ, ɗ, ƙ, ts, 'y*); Tone system (*Karin sauti* - high, low, falling tones).
     - Morphology and Syntax (*Nahawu*): Parts of speech (*Rabe-raben kalmomi*); Noun classes, gender (*jinsi*), pluralization (*jam'i*); Verb forms, aspect, tenses; Affixation (*dafi* - prefix, infix, suffix); Sentence structures (*Ginin jimla* - simple, compound, complex).
     - Translation (*Fassara*): English to Hausa and Hausa to English.
  2. **Literature (*Adabi*):**
     - Oral Literature (*Adabin Baka*): Folktales (*tatsuniya*), myths, legends; Proverbs (*karin magana*), idioms (*habaici, zaurance*), riddles (*ka-ci-ci-ka-ci*); Traditional songs (*waka*).
     - Written Literature (*Rubutaccen Adabi*): Analysis of prescribed prose novels, drama plays, and poetic anthologies (plot, themes, character development, language).
  3. **Culture and Customs (*Al'adu*):**
     - Social Life and Institutions: Birth and naming ceremonies (*haihuwa da rada suna*), marriage customs (*aure*), burial rites (*jana'iza*).
     - Chieftaincy and Traditional Governance: The Emirate administrative hierarchy (*sarautun gargajiya*).
     - Traditional Occupations and Crafts: Farming, blacksmithing, dyeing, weaving, trading (*sana'o'in gargajiya*).
     - Beliefs, festivals (*Hawan Daba, Kalankuwa*), and contemporary social issues.
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Language, Phonology & Grammar: ~16 Qs
  - Oral & Written Literature: ~14 Qs
  - Culture & Customary Institutions: ~10 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Candidates must be thoroughly familiar with the special Hausa hook letters (*ɓ, ɗ, ƙ*) and prescribed annual literature texts.

### 24. Igbo (`IGB`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To test proficiency in standard written Igbo (*Igbo Izugbe*), orthography, grammatical mechanics, phonology, analysis of oral and written literature, and understanding of Igbo cultural heritage.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Language and Linguistics (*Asusu*):**
     - Reading Comprehension (*Aghota Ederede*): Interpreting seen and unseen texts.
     - Composition and Essay Writing (*Ederede na Nkowa*).
     - Orthography (*Usoro Nsupe Igbo*): 36-letter Igbo alphabet, vowel harmony (*ndakori udaume* - light vs heavy vowels: *A, I, O, U* vs *E, I, O, U* with sub-dots); Tone marking (*Akara uda* - high, low, downstep).
     - Phonology (*Fonoloji*): Vowels (*udaume*), Consonants (*mgbochiume*), Syllabic nasals (*myiriudaume*).
     - Grammar and Syntax (*Grama na Usoro Okwu*): Parts of speech (*nkejiasusu* - nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, prepositions, conjunctions); Verbal affixes (*Nsodupe, Nragide, Nsokwunye*); Sentence patterns (*Ahiriokwu*).
     - Translation (*Ntughari*): English to Igbo and Igbo to English.
  2. **Literature (*Agumagu*):**
     - Oral Literature (*Agumagu Odinala*): Folktales (*akuko ifo*), folk songs (*abu odinala*), proverbs (*ilu*), riddles (*agwugwa*), incantations.
     - Written Literature (*Agumagu Ederede*): Critical review of prescribed drama (*ejije*), prose (*akuko koro*), and poetry (*abu*) texts.
  3. **Culture and Traditions (*Omenala*):**
     - Life Cycle: Birth rites (*omumu nwa*), naming ceremony, marriage rites (*alumdi na nwunye*), burial and funeral ceremonies (*olili ozu na ikwa ozu*).
     - Social and Political Systems: Title taking (*ichi echichi: Ozo, Nze*), age grades (*otu ogbo*), village assemblies (*amala*).
     - Economic and Material Culture: Farming, yam cultivation and the New Yam Festival (*Iri Ji Ohuru*), traditional trades, masquerade cults (*mmanwu*).
     - Belief Systems: God (*Chukwu*), minor deities (*arusi*), ancestors (*ndichie*), taboos and moral codes (*nso ala*).
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Language, Grammar & Phonology: ~16 Qs
  - Oral & Written Literature: ~14 Qs
  - Culture, Beliefs & Customs: ~10 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Vowel harmony rules and correct placement of diacritical sub-dots (*akara okpuru*) in standard *Igbo Izugbe* words are critical testing areas.

### 25. Yoruba (`YOR`)

- **General Aims & Objectives:**  
  To evaluate mastery of standard written Yoruba orthography, grammatical concepts, tone structures, traditional oral and written literature genres, and Yoruba customary life, philosophy, and institutions.
- **Key Syllabus Sections & Themes:**
  1. **Language and Grammar (*Ede*):**
     - Reading Comprehension (*Akaye*): Literary and expository unseen passages.
     - Composition (*Aroko*): Essay writing, letters, speeches, structured reports.
     - Orthography (*Leta ati Akotọ Ede Yoruba*): 25-letter alphabet, standard spelling rules, elision (*iparesi*), vowel contraction (*isunki*).
     - Phonology (*Fonoloji*): Oral vowels (*faweli airanmupe*), nasal vowels (*faweli aranmupe*); Consonants (*konsonanti*); Tones (*ami ohun* - high/oke, mid/aarin, low/isale); Tone changes and semantic impacts.
     - Grammar (*Girama*): Word classes (*Isori oro* - noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction); Morphemes and word derivation (*Ihun oro* - prefixation, reduplication); Sentence structures (*Gbolohun* - simple, compound, complex).
     - Translation (*Itumo Ede*): Translating contemporary passages between English and Yoruba.
  2. **Literature (*Litireso*):**
     - Oral Literature (*Litireso Alohun*): Folktales (*alo apamo, alo apagbe*); Poetry and chants (*oriki, ijala, esa egungun, iyere ifa, rara*); Proverbs (*owe*) and idioms (*agbegbo oro*).
     - Written Literature (*Litireso Apileko*): In-depth literary critique of prescribed plays (*ere onise*), novels (*itan aroso*), and modern poetry collections (*ewi*).
  3. **Culture and Traditions (*Asa ati Isese*):**
     - Rites of Passage (*Igbese Aye Eniyan*): Pregnancy and child care, naming ceremonies (*isomoloruko*), marriage ceremonies (*igbeyawo*), chieftaincy titles, funerals (*isinku*).
     - Traditional Governance and Social Organization: Kingship (*eto oba alaye*), council of chiefs (*oyomesi, iwarefa*), age grades (*egbe*).
     - Religion and Philosophy: Olodumare, Orisa worship (Ogun, Sango, Oya, Obatala), ancestral veneration (*egungun*), reincarnation beliefs (*akudaaya, abiku*).
     - Material Culture and Occupations: Traditional crafts (farming, blacksmithing, cloth weaving, adire dyeing), food, clothing (*agbada, iro, buba*), body adornments.
- **Exam Format & Question Distribution (40 Questions):**
  - Language, Tone System & Grammar: ~16 Qs
  - Oral & Written Literature: ~14 Qs
  - Culture, Religion & Customs: ~10 Qs
- **Pedagogical & Preparation Insights:**
  - Tone mark accuracy (*Do-Re-Mi* / `\` ` ` `/`) is vital; slight variations in tone transform word meanings entirely (e.g., *ọwọ́* - broom, *ọwọ̀* - respect, *ọwọ́* - hand).

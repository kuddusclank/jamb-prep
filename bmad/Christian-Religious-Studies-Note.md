# JAMB Christian Religious Studies (CRS) — Comprehensive Study Note for Candidates (Jambites)

**Subject Code:** `CRK` / `CRS`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Christian Religious Studies  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests CRS

In the UTME, Christian Religious Studies is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into three canonical areas:
1. **Themes from the Old Testament (~14 Questions):** Sovereignty of God, Creation, the Fall, covenants (Abraham, Moses), judges, kings of Israel (Saul, David, Solomon), division of the kingdom, prophetic ministries (Elijah, Amos, Hosea, Isaiah), and the exile and return.
2. **Themes from the Gospels & the Acts of the Apostles (~16 Questions):** Birth and early life of Jesus, baptism and temptations, the Sermon on the Mount, parables and miracles, passion, crucifixion, resurrection, Pentecost, life of the early church, martyrdom of Stephen, Philip's ministry, Paul's conversion, Peter and Cornelius, and the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15).
3. **Themes from Selected Epistles (~10 Questions):** Pauline theology (Justification by Faith in Romans, Law and Grace in Galatians, Spiritual Gifts and Love in 1 Corinthians, Humility in Philippians, Civic submission in Romans 13), Faith and Works (James), and the Parousia (1 Thessalonians).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Characteristics for JAMB CRS:**
> - Questions test **precise biblical names, relationships, and locations** (e.g., Mount Carmel for Elijah's contest; Mount Gilboa where Saul died; Joppa where Peter raised Dorcas and had his vision; Lystra where Paul and Barnabas were called Hermes and Zeus).
> - Specific prophetic analogies must be identified (e.g., Hosea's unfaithful wife **Gomer** as a symbol of Israel's spiritual adultery; Amos' condemnation of the wealthy women of Samaria as **"cows of Bashan"**).

# SECTION 1: THEMES FROM THE OLD TESTAMENT

## 1. Sovereignty of God, Creation, and Covenant
- **The Two Creation Accounts:**
  - *Genesis 1 (Priestly account):* Creation by divine spoken word in six days; God creates man (male and female) on the sixth day in His image; rests on the seventh day (Sabbath). Order: Light $\to$ Firmament $\to$ Seas/Dry land/Plants $\to$ Sun/Moon/Stars $\to$ Fish/Birds $\to$ Land animals/Man.
  - *Genesis 2 (Yahwist account):* Man formed from the dust of the ground, God breathes the breath of life into his nostrils; God plants the Garden of Eden; woman created from man's rib.
- **The Fall of Man (Genesis 3):** Disobedience prompted by the serpent's deceit; eating from the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil. Consequences: Serpent condemned to crawl on belly; woman suffers pain in childbirth and submission to husband; man toils to eat from the cursed ground; spiritual death and expulsion from Eden.
- **The Abrahamic Covenant (Genesis 12 & 17):**
  - God's call at Haran (age 75): promises land, descendants, great nation, and universal blessing.
  - Sign of the Covenant: **Circumcision** of every male child at **8 days old**.
  - Ultimate test of faith: Command to sacrifice Isaac on Mount Moriah; God provides a ram caught in a thicket (**Jehovah-Jireh** — The Lord will provide).
- **Moses and the Exodus:**
  - Divine encounter at the Burning Bush on Mount Horeb (**Yahweh** — "I AM WHO I AM").
  - The 10 Plagues on Egypt (concluding with the death of the firstborn).
  - **The Passover Feast (Pesach):** Unblemished yearling lamb/goat, blood smeared on doorposts/lintels, eaten roasted with bitter herbs and unleavened bread, dressed ready for flight.
  - Crossing the Red Sea; God's wilderness provision of Manna and Quail; Water from the rock at Rephidim (Massah and Meribah).
  - The Sinai Covenant: The Ten Commandments (Decalogue) given on two stone tablets on Mount Sinai.

---

## 2. Leadership and Kingship in Israel
- **The Judges of Israel:**
  - *Deborah:* Prophetess and judge; sat under the Palm of Deborah; commanded **Barak** to lead Israel's army against Jabin's general **Sisera**; Sisera fled and was killed by **Jael** with a tent peg through his temple.
  - *Gideon:* Called while threshing wheat in a winepress; destroyed father's Baal altar; defeated the Midianite army using only **300 men** armed with trumpets, torches, and pitchers.
  - *Samson:* Nazarite from birth (vow: no razor on head, no wine, no touching dead bodies); supernatural strength; betrayed by **Delilah** of the Valley of Sorek who cut his seven locks of hair.
- **Eli and Samuel:**
  - Eli the High Priest at Shiloh; failed to restrain his wicked sons (**Hophni and Phinehas**) who desecrated sacrificial offerings and committed immorality at the tabernacle; judgment pronounced through young boy Samuel; Eli dies falling from his seat on hearing the Ark of God was captured by Philistines.
- **The Monarchy: Saul, David, and Solomon:**
  - *Saul:* First King of Israel; anointed by Samuel. Disobedience: offered premature burnt sacrifice at Gilgal without Samuel; spared King Agag and the best sheep/cattle of the Amalekites. Samuel's rebuke: *"To obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams."*
  - *David:* Anointed at Bethlehem; played harp to soothe Saul's tormenting spirit; killed Goliath with a sling and smooth stone; covenant of friendship with **Jonathan**; committed adultery with **Bathsheba** and orchestrated the murder of her husband **Uriah the Hittite**; confronted by Prophet **Nathan** with the parable of the rich man and the ewe lamb; repented (Psalm 51).
  - *Solomon:* Given wisdom, wealth, and honor after offering 1,000 burnt offerings at Gibeon; built the first Temple in Jerusalem; downfall caused by marrying 700 foreign wives and 300 concubines who turned his heart to idolatry (Ashtoreth, Chemosh, Molech).
  - *Division of the Kingdom (931 BC):* Solomon's son **Rehoboam** rejected the advice of the elders and adopted the harsh advice of young men ("My father scourged you with whips, but I will scourge you with scorpions"); ten northern tribes broke away under **Jeroboam I** to form the Northern Kingdom of Israel (capital: Samaria); southern kingdom became Judah (capital: Jerusalem).

---

## 3. Prophetic Ministries and Religious Reforms
- **Elijah on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18):**
  - Confronted King Ahab and Queen Jezebel over Baal worship.
  - Contest on Mount Carmel: Elijah vs. 450 prophets of Baal. Baal prophets prayed, danced, and cut themselves without answer; Elijah rebuilt the altar of the Lord with 12 stones, poured 12 jars of water, prayed a simple prayer, and fire fell from heaven consuming the sacrifice, wood, stones, and water; people cried: *"The Lord, He is God!"*; Baal prophets slaughtered at the Brook Kishon.
  - Naboth's Vineyard: Jezebel framed Naboth with false witnesses and had him stoned to death so Ahab could seize his vineyard; Elijah pronounced divine judgment on Ahab and Jezebel.
- **Prophetic Messages of Social Justice:**
  - *Amos:* Shepherd from Tekoa in Judah sent to prophesy in Northern Israel (Samaria). Condemned greed, luxury, bribery, cheating with false weights and measures, selling the righteous for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals; denounced wealthy women as *"cows of Bashan"*; declared that external religious rituals are worthless without righteousness: *"Let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."*
  - *Hosea:* Commanded to marry an unfaithful woman (**Gomer**) to illustrate God's unceasing covenant love and mercy toward unfaithful Israel.
  - *Josiah's Reforms (2 Kings 22–23):* King Josiah ordered repairs on the Temple; High Priest **Hilkiah** discovered the **Book of the Law**; verified by Prophetess **Huldah**; Josiah tore his clothes, destroyed all pagan altars and high places, defiled Topheth, and reinstituted the Passover feast.

# SECTION 2: THE GOSPELS AND THE ACTS OF THE APOSTLES

## 4. Life and Ministry of Jesus Christ
- **Birth and Early Life:**
  - Annunciation to Mary by Angel Gabriel; born in Bethlehem; visited by shepherds and Magi (Wise Men bearing gold, frankincense, myrrh); dedication in the temple recognized by **Simeon** (*Nunc Dimittis*) and prophetess **Anna**; escape to Egypt to evade Herod's slaughter of innocents; Jesus at age 12 in the temple debating with Jewish doctors of the law.
- **Baptism and Temptations:**
  - Baptized by John the Baptist in the Jordan River; the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and a voice from heaven declared: *"This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."*
  - Fasted 40 days and nights in the wilderness; tempted by Satan in three areas:
    1. *Stones to bread* (Fleshly appetite) $\to$ "Man shall not live by bread alone..."
    2. *Throwing Himself from temple pinnacle* (Presumption / Testing God) $\to$ "You shall not tempt the Lord your God."
    3. *Worshiping Satan for kingdoms of the world* (Idolatry / Power) $\to$ "You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve."
- **The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7):**
  - The Beatitudes: Spiritual blessings for the poor in spirit, meek, merciful, peacemakers, and persecuted.
  - Metaphors of disciples: "Salt of the earth" and "Light of the world".
  - Moral standards: Anger equated with murder, lustful looking equated with adultery, loving enemies, prayer, fasting in secret, storing treasures in heaven, and the Golden Rule (*"Do unto others as you would have them do unto you"*).
- **Parables and Miracles:**
  - *Parables of the Kingdom:* The Sower (four soils: path, rocky, thorns, good soil); Wheat and Tares; Mustard Seed; Prodigal Son (unconditional fatherly forgiveness); Good Samaritan (defining who is my neighbor: priest and Levite pass by, Samaritan shows mercy).
  - *Categories of Miracles:*
    - Nature: Water turned to wine at Cana (first miracle); calming the storm; feeding the 5,000 with 5 barley loaves and 2 small fish.
    - Healing: The paralytic lowered through the roof; the woman with the issue of blood for 12 years; ten lepers (only the Samaritan returned to give thanks); blind Bartimaeus at Jericho.
    - Resurrection: Jairus' daughter; the widow's son at Nain; Lazarus raised after four days in the tomb.
- **Passion, Crucifixion, and Resurrection:**
  - Triumphal Entry into Jerusalem on a donkey (Palm Sunday); cleansing of the temple.
  - The Last Supper: Institute of Holy Communion (bread = broken body, wine = new covenant in blood).
  - Agony in the Garden of Gethsemane (*"Not my will, but yours be done"*); betrayed with a kiss by **Judas Iscariot** for 30 pieces of silver; arrested; denied three times by Peter before the cock crowed.
  - Trials: Before the Sanhedrin (Caiaphas), Pontius Pilate, and Herod Antipas; crowd demanded the release of **Barabbas** and the crucifixion of Jesus.
  - Crucifixion at Golgotha (The Place of a Skull); Simon of Cyrene compelled to carry the cross; died on Good Friday; buried in the rock-hewn tomb of **Joseph of Arimathea**.
  - Resurrection on Sunday morning; discovered empty by Mary Magdalene; Great Commission on the mountain in Galilee; Ascension into heaven from the Mount of Olives.

## 5. The Early Church and the Acts of the Apostles
- **The Day of Pentecost (Acts 2):**
  - 120 disciples gathered in the Upper Room; sudden sound of a rushing mighty wind; cloven tongues like of fire rested on each; filled with the Holy Spirit and began speaking in other tongues; Peter's powerful sermon explaining Joel's prophecy; **about 3,000 souls converted and baptized**.
- **Life in the Early Christian Community:**
  - Devoted to the apostles' teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayers; had all things in common; sold possessions to distribute to those in need (Barnabas sold a field); judgment on **Ananias and Sapphira** who lied to the Holy Spirit about the sale price of their land and fell dead.
- **Early Persecution and Expansion:**
  - Appointment of seven deacons to oversee welfare (Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, Nicolaus).
  - **Stephen:** Arrested for preaching; delivered a magnificent historical defense before the Sanhedrin; saw heaven open and Jesus standing at God's right hand; stoned to death becoming the **first Christian martyr**; young Saul of Tarsus consented to his death.
  - **Philip:** Preached with signs in Samaria; Simon the sorcerer offered money to buy the power of the Holy Spirit (**Simony**); guided by an angel to the Gaza road to preach to and baptize the **Ethiopian Eunuch** who was reading Isaiah 53.
- **Conversion of Saul and Ministry to Gentiles:**
  - Saul blinded by a brilliant light on the road to Damascus (*"Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?"*); sight restored by **Ananias**; transformed into Apostle Paul.
  - **Peter and Cornelius (Acts 10):** Peter saw a vision at Joppa of a sheet descending with clean and unclean animals, commanded: *"What God has cleansed, you must not call common"*; visited the Roman Centurion Cornelius in Caesarea; the Holy Spirit fell upon Gentile listeners; baptized into the Church.
- **The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15, c. 49 AD):**
  - Convened to resolve whether Gentile converts must be circumcised and obey the Law of Moses to be saved.
  - Peter and Paul testified to God's grace among Gentiles; **James (brother of Jesus)** presided and delivered the apostolic verdict: Gentile Christians were exempt from circumcision, required only to abstain from:
    1. Food sacrificed to idols.
    2. Blood.
    3. Meat of strangled animals.
    4. Sexual immorality.

# SECTION 3: THEMES FROM SELECTED EPISTLES

## 6. Pauline and General Epistles
- **Justification by Faith (Romans 3–5):**
  - All humanity (Jews and Gentiles) has sinned and fallen short of God's glory.
  - Man cannot be justified (declared righteous) before God by the works of the law, but solely by God's free grace through faith in the sacrificial death and resurrection of Jesus Christ.
  - **Abraham** is the ultimate model of justification by faith: he believed God before he was circumcised, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness.
- **Law and Grace (Galatians 3–5):**
  - The Mosaic Law served as a **tutor / schoolmaster** to lead mankind to Christ.
  - Christian liberty: Christians are free from the ceremonial yoke of the law; warn against returning to legalism.
  - Contrast: *Works of the Flesh* (fornication, idolatry, sorcery, anger, drunkenness) vs. *Fruit of the Spirit* (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control).
- **Spiritual Gifts and the Supremacy of Love (1 Corinthians 12–13):**
  - One body with many diverse members; spiritual gifts distributed by the Spirit for the common good (wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, tongues).
  - **Chapter 13 (The Hymn of Love):** Without love, the greatest spiritual gifts, eloquent tongues, and sacrificial martyrdom are completely worthless; love is patient, kind, does not envy, never fails; *"Now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love."*
- **Humility and Christ's Self-Emptying (Philippians 2):**
  - Disciples commanded to have the mind of Christ who, though being in the form of God, emptied Himself (*kenosis*), took the form of a servant, and became obedient unto death on a cross; wherefore God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every other name.
- **Faith and Works (James 1–2):**
  - True faith is active and practical: *"Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead."*
  - Condemnation of partiality/discrimination in the church (honoring the wealthy in fine clothes while dishonoring the poor man in filthy rags).
  - Controlling the tongue: Small like a horse's bit or ship's rudder, but can set a whole forest on fire.
- **The Second Coming of Christ / Parousia (1 Thessalonians 4–5):**
  - Reassurance concerning believers who have died ("fallen asleep"); Christ will descend from heaven with a shout, the voice of the archangel, and the trumpet of God; **the dead in Christ will rise first**, then those who are alive will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air. Day of the Lord will come unexpectedly like a thief in the night.

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB CRS

1. **Jerusalem Council Verdict (Acts 15):** The council decided that Gentile converts do **NOT** need circumcision, but must abstain from idols, blood, strangled meat, and sexual immorality.
2. **Prophetic Marital Metaphors:** **Hosea's** marriage to the adulterous **Gomer** symbolizes God's enduring love for unfaithful Israel.
3. **Amos' Target Audience:** Amos prophesied primarily against the social corruption, economic injustice, and fraudulent scales of **Northern Israel (Samaria)**, even though he was born in Tekoa (Judah).
4. **Mount Carmel vs Mount Sinai:** The contest between Elijah and the 450 prophets of Baal took place on **Mount Carmel**; the 10 Commandments were given to Moses on **Mount Sinai (Horeb)**.
5. **Stephen's Dying Words:** Stephen cried: *"Lord, do not charge them with this sin"* and *"Lord Jesus, receive my spirit"*, reflecting Christ's words on the cross.
6. **Simon the Sorcerer (Simony):** The sin of attempting to buy the power and gifts of the Holy Spirit with money is called **Simony** (from Simon Magus in Acts 8).
7. **Justification Paradigm:** In both Romans and Galatians, Apostle Paul cites **Abraham** as the supreme Old Testament proof that justification is by faith alone, before circumcision.
8. **Parables of Lost Things:** Luke 15 contains three parables of lost things: The Lost Sheep, the Lost Coin, and the Lost (Prodigal) Son.
9. **First Christian Martyr:** **Stephen** was the first Christian martyr, stoned to death under the supervision of Saul of Tarsus.
10. **Saul's Disobedience at Gilgal and Amalek:** King Saul was rejected as king for two specific sins: offering sacrifice without Samuel at Gilgal, and sparing King Agag and the best livestock of Amalek.

## Overview & How JAMB Tests PHE

In the UTME, Physical and Health Education is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into five main domains:
1. **Foundations, History & Physical Fitness (~8 Questions):** Historical development of PHE, Ancient and Modern Olympic Games, Commonwealth movements, national sports policies, and health/skill-related physical fitness components.
2. **Anatomy, Exercise Physiology & Somatotypes (~10 Questions):** The skeletal and muscular systems, postural defects (scoliosis, kyphosis, lordosis), Sheldon's somatotypes, aerobic vs. anaerobic energy systems, and cardiorespiratory adaptations to exercise.
3. **Athletics, Ball Games & Sports Rules (~10 Questions):** Track events (sprints, hurdles, relay exchange zone), field events (shot put, discus, javelin, high jump, long jump), soccer, basketball, volleyball rules, and aquatics.
4. **Health Education, Nutrition & Disease Prevention (~8 Questions):** Personal hygiene, community health, nutritional deficiencies (kwashiorkor, marasmus, scurvy), drug and substance abuse, doping in sports, communicable and non-communicable diseases.
5. **First Aid, Sports Injuries & Family Life (~4 Questions):** Principles of First Aid, management of soft tissue injuries via the **R.I.C.E. protocol**, CPR procedures, sprains vs. strains, fractures, and reproductive health.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Dimensions & Protocols Tested in JAMB PHE:**
> - Relay Baton Exchange Zone: Exactly **$20\text{ meters}$ long**; exchanging the baton outside this zone causes immediate disqualification.
> - Shot Put Throwing Circle Diameter: Exactly **$2.135\text{ meters}$ ($7\text{ feet}$)**; legal landing sector angle is **$34.92^\circ$**.
> - Sprain vs. Strain: A **Sprain** is a tear or stretch of a **ligament** (bone to bone); a **Strain** is an injury to a **muscle or tendon** (muscle to bone).
> - Soft Tissue Injury Treatment: The **R.I.C.E.** protocol (**Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation**).

# SECTION 1: FOUNDATIONS OF PHE AND PHYSICAL FITNESS

## 1. History of Physical Education and Major Sports Movements
- **Ancient Olympic Games (776 BC – 393 AD):**
  - Held in Olympia, Greece, in honor of the god Zeus; strictly for free Greek male citizens; athletes competed nude; prize was a crown of sacred **olive leaves**; held every 4 years (**Olympiad**); wars suspended during the Games (**Olympic Truce**).
- **Modern Olympic Games (Revived 1896):**
  - Revived by French educator **Baron Pierre de Coubertin**; first modern games held in **Athens, Greece (1896)**.
  - *Olympic Motto:* **"Citius, Altius, Fortius"** (Latin for *"Faster, Higher, Stronger"*).
  - *Olympic Flag:* Five interlocking rings (Blue, Yellow, Black, Green, Red) on a white background representing the union of the **five inhabited continents of the world**.
  - *Olympic Flame:* Lit by solar rays at Olympia, Greece, and carried via torch relay to the host city cauldron.
- **National Sports Administration in Nigeria:**
  - Governed by the Federal Ministry of Youth and Sports Development and the **National Sports Commission (NSC)**; organises the biennial **National Sports Festival (NSF)** launched in 1973 after the civil war to promote national unity.

## 2. Physical Fitness Components
- **Health-Related Fitness Components:**
  1. *Cardiorespiratory Endurance:* Ability of the heart and lungs to supply oxygen during prolonged physical activity (tested by the 12-minute Cooper run or Beep test).
  2. *Muscular Strength:* Maximum force a muscle group can exert in a single contraction (tested by 1-rep max bench press, dynamometer).
  3. *Muscular Endurance:* Ability of a muscle to sustain repeated contractions over time without fatigue (tested by sit-up and push-up tests).
  4. *Flexibility:* Range of motion around a joint (tested by Sit-and-Reach test).
  5. *Body Composition:* Relative proportion of fat mass to lean body mass.
- **Skill-Related (Performance) Fitness Components:**
  1. *Agility:* Ability to rapidly change body direction with speed and accuracy (tested by Illinois agility run, shuttle run).
  2. *Balance:* Ability to maintain equilibrium in static or dynamic states (tested by Stork stand).
  3. *Coordination:* Harmonious integration of senses and body movements.
  4. *Power:* Combination of strength and speed; rate of performing explosive work (tested by vertical jump, standing broad jump).
  5. *Reaction Time:* Time elapsed between the presentation of a stimulus and initiation of movement (tested by ruler drop test).
  6. *Speed:* Ability to cover distance in the shortest time (tested by 50m sprint).

# SECTION 2: ANATOMY, EXERCISE PHYSIOLOGY & SOMATOTYPES

## 3. Skeletal System, Posture, and Postural Defects
- **The Skeletal Framework:**
  - 206 bones in adult human body; classified into Long bones (femur, humerus), Short bones (carpals, tarsals), Flat bones (scapula, ribs, sternum), and Irregular bones (vertebrae).
  - Joints: **Synovial (Freely movable)** with synovial fluid and cartilage (Ball and socket at hip/shoulder, Hinge at elbow/knee, Pivot at neck atlas/axis); Cartilaginous (slightly movable, vertebrae); Fibrous (immovable, skull sutures).
- **Postural Deviations:**
  - **Scoliosis:** Abnormal **lateral (sideways)** C-shaped or S-shaped curvature of the spinal column.
  - **Kyphosis ("Hunchback"):** Excessive **outward posterior curvature** of the thoracic spine, leading to rounded shoulders and stooped posture.
  - **Lordosis ("Swayback"):** Excessive **inward anterior curvature** of the lumbar spine, causing an exaggerated forward pelvic tilt.
  - *Lower Limb Defects:* **Genu Valgum** (Knock-knees: knees touch while ankles remain wide apart); **Genu Varum** (Bow-legs: outward bowing of knees); **Pes Planus** (Flat foot: collapse or absence of the longitudinal medial foot arch).

---

## 4. Somatotypes and Energy Systems
- **Sheldon's Body Somatotypes:**
  1. **Endomorph:** Soft, rounded body contours, wide hips, high percentage of body fat, short limbs. Athletic advantage: Heavy strength events requiring high mass (**Shot put, Super-heavyweight weightlifting, Sumo wrestling**).
  2. **Mesomorph:** Muscular, athletic build, broad shoulders, narrow waist, strong bones, low body fat. Athletic advantage: Power, speed, and agility events (**100m/200m sprinting, Decathlon, Gymnastics, Football, Boxing**).
  3. **Ectomorph:** Slender, thin, linear frame, narrow chest, delicate bones, very low muscle mass and body fat. Athletic advantage: Aerobic endurance and height-dominant events (**Marathon running, High jump, Basketball**).
- **Bioenergetics (Energy Pathways in Exercise):**
  - **ATP-PC System (Phosphagen System):** Anaerobic, utilizes stored adenosine triphosphate (ATP) and phosphocreatine (PC); delivers immediate explosive power for **$0 - 10\text{ seconds}$** (e.g., 100m sprint, high jump, shot put).
  - **Lactic Acid System (Anaerobic Glycolysis):** Breaks down glycogen without oxygen; provides energy for high-intensity efforts lasting **$10 - 60\text{ seconds}$** (e.g., 400m sprint, 100m swim); produces **lactic acid** which causes muscle soreness, fatigue, and **oxygen debt**.
  - **Aerobic System (Oxidative System):** Uses oxygen to break down carbohydrates and fats in mitochondria into $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$; sustains exercise beyond 2 minutes; dominant in long-distance running, road cycling, and football.
- **Cardiovascular Adaptations to Aerobic Training:**
  - *Cardiac Hypertrophy (Athlete's Heart):* Increased size and left ventricular wall thickness of the heart.
  - *Increased Stroke Volume (SV):* Heart pumps more blood per beat.
  - *Resting Bradycardia:* Trained athletes exhibit a low resting heart rate ($<60\text{ beats per minute}$) due to improved stroke volume efficiency.

# SECTION 3: ATHLETICS, TEAM GAMES & WATER SAFETY

## 5. Athletics (Track and Field Events)
- **Track Events:**
  - *Sprints ($100\text{m}, 200\text{m}, 400\text{m}$):* Run in assigned lanes; start using **starting blocks**; starting commands: **"On your marks" $\to$ "Set" $\to$ Gun fired**. Crouch start styles: Bunch (bullet), Medium (most common), Elongated. False start leads to immediate disqualification.
  - *Relays ($4 \times 100\text{m}, 4 \times 400\text{m}$):*
    - Non-visual baton exchange (receiver does not look back; used in $4 \times 100\text{m}$).
    - Visual baton exchange (receiver turns and watches handoff; used in $4 \times 400\text{m}$).
    - **Take-over / Baton Exchange Zone:** Exactly **$20\text{ meters}$ long**. The baton must be handed over within this zone; throwing the baton or exchanging outside the lines results in disqualification.
  - *Hurdles:* Men's $110\text{m}$ (hurdle height: $1.067\text{ m}$); Women's $100\text{m}$ (height: $0.838\text{ m}$); $400\text{m}$ hurdles (men $0.914\text{ m}$, women $0.762\text{ m}$).
- **Field Events:**
  - *Shot Put:* Putting (pushing from the shoulder, never thrown); circle diameter $= 2.135\text{ m}$; stop board ($10\text{ cm}$ high); legal landing sector $= 34.92^\circ$; competitor must exit through the rear half of the circle after the shot lands. Techniques: O'Brien Glide, Rotational technique.
  - *Discus Throw:* Throwing circle diameter $= 2.50\text{ m}$; conducted inside a protective safety cage.
  - *Javelin Throw:* Thrown from a runaway behind a curved scratch arc; must land tip first; grip styles: American grip (index finger), Finnish grip (middle finger), Fork / "V" grip.
  - *High Jump:* Styles: Scissors, Western Roll, Straddle, and the modern **Fosbury Flop** (jumping backward over the bar, popularized by Dick Fosbury at the 1968 Mexico Olympics).
  - *Triple Jump:* Sequential three-phase motion: **Hop $\to$ Step $\to$ Jump**.

---

## 6. Ball Games, Racket Sports & Aquatics
- **Football (Soccer):**
  - Regulation match: Two halves of 45 minutes ($90\text{ minutes}$ total); 11 players per team (including goalkeeper); ball circumference: $68 - 70\text{ cm}$.
  - *Offside Rule (Law 11):* A player is in an offside position if they are nearer to the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent when the ball is played to them, unless they are in their own half.
  - Cards: Yellow card (Caution); Red card (Expulsion).
- **Basketball:**
  - Court dimensions: $28\text{m} \times 15\text{m}$; Ring height: **$3.05\text{ meters}$ ($10\text{ feet}$)**; 5 active players on court.
  - Time restrictions: 24-second shot clock; 8 seconds to advance across midcourt; 5 seconds inbound; 3 seconds in the key.
  - Fouls: 5 personal fouls disqualify a player (6 in NBA). Violations: Traveling (walking without dribbling), Double dribbling.
- **Volleyball:**
  - Court: $18\text{m} \times 9\text{m}$; Net height: $2.43\text{m}$ (men), $2.24\text{m}$ (women); 6 players per team; clockwise rotation upon winning serve.
  - Team allowed maximum **3 touches/hits** before returning ball over net; **Libero** is a specialized defensive player wearing a contrasting jersey, restricted from attacking or serving.
- **Aquatics (Swimming):**
  - Standard Olympic pool: $50\text{ meters}$ long, 8 to 10 lanes.
  - Four competitive strokes: **Front Crawl / Freestyle** (fastest), **Butterfly**, **Backstroke** (only stroke started in water), **Breaststroke**.

# SECTION 4: HEALTH EDUCATION, NUTRITION & SPORTS MEDICINE

## 7. Nutrition, Deficiency Diseases & Doping
- **Nutritional Deficiency Disorders:**
  - **Kwashiorkor:** Severe **protein deficiency** in young children; symptoms: edema (swelling of feet and belly), flaky dermatitis, thin reddish/brown hair, lethargy, muscle wasting.
  - **Marasmus:** Severe **total calorie / starvation deficiency** (carbohydrates and proteins); symptoms: severe emaciation ("skin and bones"), sunken eyes, old-man face appearance, no edema.
  - **Scurvy:** Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid) deficiency; bleeding gums, loose teeth, poor wound healing.
  - **Rickets:** Vitamin D and Calcium deficiency in children; soft pliable bones, bowlegs, knock-knees.
  - **Night Blindness:** Vitamin A (Retinol) deficiency.
  - **Goitre:** Iodine deficiency; enlargement of thyroid gland in neck.
  - **Anemia:** Iron deficiency; pale skin, fatigue, reduced hemoglobin oxygen-carrying capacity.
- **Doping in Sports (WADA Prohibitions):**
  - *Anabolic Steroids:* Synthetic testosterone derivatives used to artificially increase muscle mass and power; side effects: liver damage, cardiovascular disease, infertility, testicular atrophy.
  - *Erythropoietin (EPO) & Blood Doping:* Stimulates red blood cell production to boost oxygen uptake; thickens blood, causing heart attacks and strokes.
  - *Diuretics:* Masking agents used to rapidly excrete banned drugs or achieve rapid weight cuts (combat sports).
  - *Beta-Blockers:* Calm nerves, lower heart rate, and prevent hand trembling; prohibited in precision sports (archery, shooting).

## 8. First Aid, Sports Injuries & The R.I.C.E. Protocol
- **Classification of Sports Injuries:**
  - **Sprain:** Stretched or torn **ligament** caused by sudden twisting around a joint (e.g., sprained ankle).
  - **Strain ("Pulled Muscle"):** Stretched or torn **muscle fiber or tendon** caused by over-stretching or violent contraction (e.g., hamstring strain).
  - **Dislocation:** Total displacement of the articulating bones at a joint out of their sockets (shoulder, finger).
  - **Fracture:** Break or crack in a bone:
    - *Closed (Simple) Fracture:* Bone broken internally, skin remains intact.
    - *Open (Compound) Fracture:* Broken bone penetrates through the skin; severe infection risk.
    - *Greenstick Fracture:* Incomplete break where bone bends and cracks (common in flexible bones of young children).
- **The R.I.C.E. Management Protocol for Soft Tissue Injuries:**
  - **R = REST:** Immediately stop activity; immobilize the injured limb to prevent further trauma.
  - **I = ICE:** Apply crushed ice wrapped in a damp towel for **15–20 minutes** every 2–3 hours; causes vasoconstriction, reducing bleeding, swelling, and pain.
  - **C = COMPRESSION:** Apply an elastic crepe bandage firmly (without stopping arterial blood circulation) to limit internal edema.
  - **E = ELEVATION:** Raise the injured limb above heart level to facilitate venous and lymphatic drainage away from the injury site.
- **Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR):**
  - Life-saving procedure for cardiac arrest; cycle of **30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths** ($30:2$) at a rate of 100–120 compressions per minute.

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB PHE

1. **Relay Baton Exchange Zone:** The official exchange zone in athletics is **$20\text{ meters}$**. Exchanging the baton outside this box means immediate disqualification.
2. **Sprain vs Strain Distinction:** Ligament injury $=$ **Sprain**; Muscle/Tendon injury $=$ **Strain**.
3. **Shot Put Dimensions:** Circle diameter is **$2.135\text{ meters}$ ($7\text{ feet}$)**; legal landing sector angle is **$34.92^\circ$**.
4. **Basketball Hoop Height:** The basketball ring is placed exactly **$3.05\text{ meters}$ ($10\text{ feet}$)** above the court floor.
5. **R.I.C.E. Acronym Order:** **R**est, **I**ce, **C**ompression, **E**levation. Never apply heat to an acute injury in the first 48 hours!
6. **Postural Defects Recognition:** Lateral/sideways curvature is **Scoliosis**; upper thoracic hunchback is **Kyphosis**; lower lumbar swayback is **Lordosis**.
7. **Sheldon's Somatotypes:** Shot putters are **Endomorphs**; 100m sprinters and gymnasts are **Mesomorphs**; high jumpers and marathoners are **Ectomorphs**.
8. **Kwashiorkor vs Marasmus:** Kwashiorkor is protein deficiency with **edema (swollen belly/feet)**; Marasmus is total calorie starvation without edema (emaciated "old man" look).
9. **Fosbury Flop Pioneer:** The high jump technique of crossing the bar backward on one's shoulders was invented by **Dick Fosbury** (1968 Olympics).
10. **Modern Olympics Revival:** Revived in **1896 in Athens, Greece**, by the French nobleman **Baron Pierre de Coubertin**.

**Subject Code:** `HEC`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Home Economics  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Home Economics

In the UTME, Home Economics is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into three main domains:
1. **Home Management, Family Living & Child Development (~12 Questions):** Management processes (planning, organizing, implementing, evaluating), family resources, household budgeting, housing, cleaning surfaces, consumer education, family life cycles, courtship, prenatal care, labor stages, child growth, and immunization.
2. **Food and Nutrition (~16 Questions):** Nutrients, chemical tests for food classes, digestion and metabolism, meal planning for special vulnerable groups (infants, adolescents, pregnant women, diabetics, hypertensives), food spoilage, food preservation methods, flour mixtures, raising agents, and pastries.
3. **Clothing and Textiles (~12 Questions):** Classification and identification of textile fibers (burning tests for cotton, wool, silk, nylon), fabric construction (weaving, knitting), sewing machine parts, temporary and permanent stitches, seams (French, Flat felled), fullness control (darts, pleats, gathers), and stain removal.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Distinctions in Home Economics:**
> - Fiber Burning Test: **Cotton** burns with a yellow flame and smells like burning paper (leaves gray ash); **Wool** burns slowly, self-extinguishes, and smells like burning hair/feathers (leaves dark irregular bead); **Nylon** shrinks from flame, melts, and forms a **hard, uncrushable round bead**.
> - Seams: **French Seam** is an enclosed self-finished seam ideal for delicate, sheer, and washable fabrics (baby clothes); **Flat Felled (Run and Fell) Seam** is a very strong flat seam with two rows of stitching, used on heavy fabrics like jeans and shirts.
> - Pastry Ratio: Standard **Shortcrust Pastry** requires **half the weight of fat to flour** ($1:2$ ratio, e.g., $100\text{g}$ fat to $200\text{g}$ flour).

# SECTION 1: HOME MANAGEMENT, FAMILY LIVING & CHILD DEVELOPMENT

## 1. Home Management and Consumer Rights
- **The Management Process:**
  1. *Planning:* Formulating goals, setting priorities, and choosing methods of implementation.
  2. *Organizing:* Assigning tasks, allocating resources, and scheduling sequence of activities.
  3. *Implementing / Controlling:* Putting the plan into actual execution while monitoring progress.
  4. *Evaluating:* Assessing the final outcome against initial goals to learn lessons for future planning.
- **Family Resources:**
  - *Human Resources:* Inherent personal attributes that originate from within individuals (skills, knowledge, energy, intelligence, talents, attitudes, time).
  - *Material (Non-Human) Resources:* Tangible, physical, and financial assets (money, family house, furniture, equipment, land, community facilities like libraries and parks).
- **Household Surfaces and Cleaning Reagents:**
  - *Wood Surfaces:* Polished wood must be wiped along the grain; water causes warping; clean with turpentine/wax polish.
  - *Aluminum:* Soft metal damaged by strong alkalis (soda/lye); clean with mild soap and hot water.
  - *Brass and Copper:* Tarnish into green verdigris ($\text{CuCO}_3$); cleaned with vinegar, lemon, and salt.
  - *Stainless Steel:* Durable, rust-resistant; clean with warm soapy water; avoid abrasive steel wool.
- **Consumer Rights & Protection Agencies:**
  - *J.F. Kennedy's Consumer Bill of Rights:* Right to Safety, Right to be Informed, Right to Choose, Right to be Heard, Right to Redress, Right to Consumer Education.
  - *Regulatory Bodies in Nigeria:*
    - **FCCPC:** Protects consumers against deceptive business practices, false advertising, and substandard services.
    - **NAFDAC:** Regulates safety of food, drugs, packaged water, cosmetics, and medical devices.
    - **SON:** Enforces industrial manufacturing standards (awards the NIS mark of quality).

## 2. Family Living and Child Development
- **Family Life Cycle Stages:**
  1. *Beginning / Founding Stage:* Marriage, establishment of home, adjustment between spouses.
  2. *Expanding / Childbearing Stage:* Birth of children, preschool, elementary, and adolescent years; highest expenditure demands.
  3. *Contracting / Launching Stage:* Children leave home for tertiary education or marriage; parents reach the "empty nest" phase.
- **Prenatal Care, Labor, and Delivery:**
  - *Antenatal Care:* Regular medical monitoring of mother's blood pressure, weight, urine (protein/sugar), and fetal heartbeat.
  - *Three Stages of Labor:*
    1. **Stage 1 (Dilation Stage):** Onset of regular uterine contractions; progressive thinning (effacement) and opening of the cervix to full dilation ($10\text{ cm}$).
    2. **Stage 2 (Expulsion Stage):** Actual delivery of the baby through the birth canal; mother bears down.
    3. **Stage 3 (Placental Stage):** Expulsion of the placenta (afterbirth) and fetal membranes within 5–15 minutes after delivery.
- **Child Health, Immunization & Oral Rehydration:**
  - *National Immunization Schedule (EPI):*
    - **At Birth:** **BCG** (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin for tuberculosis), **OPV 0** (Oral Polio Vaccine), Hepatitis B 0.
    - **6, 10, and 14 Weeks:** **Penta** (Diphtheria, Pertussis/Whooping cough, Tetanus, Hep B, Hib), OPV 1, 2, 3, Rota, PCV.
    - **9 Months:** **Measles** vaccine and Yellow Fever vaccine.
  - *Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT) Solution:* Home preparation to prevent dehydration during infant diarrhea:
    $$\mathbf{1\text{ level teaspoon of Salt}} + \mathbf{5\text{ level teaspoons of Sugar}} + \mathbf{1\text{ clean beer bottle (600 ml) of boiled cooled water}}$$

# SECTION 2: FOOD AND NUTRITION

## 3. Nutrients, Functions, and Chemical Tests
- **Classes of Food Nutrients:**
  - *Carbohydrates:* Primary source of metabolic energy. Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose), Disaccharides (sucrose, maltose, lactose), Polysaccharides (starch, cellulose, glycogen).
    - **Starch Test:** Add a few drops of **Iodine solution** $\to$ turns **blue-black**.
    - **Reducing Sugar Test:** Add **Benedict's (or Fehling's) solution** and boil $\to$ color changes from blue $\to$ green $\to$ yellow $\to$ **brick-red precipitate**.
  - *Proteins:* Body-building, tissue growth, repair, enzyme and hormone synthesis. Composed of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
    - **Biuret Test:** Add dilute $\text{NaOH}$ followed by $1\%$ $\text{CuSO}_4$ $\to$ distinct **violet / purple coloration**.
    - **Million's Test:** Add Million's reagent and heat $\to$ **brick-red precipitate**.
  - *Fats and Oils (Lipids):* Concentrated energy storage ($37\text{ kJ/g}$, double that of carbohydrates), insulation, solvent for fat-soluble vitamins. Saturated fats (solid at room temperature, animal sources) vs. Unsaturated fats (liquid oils, plant sources).
    - **Grease Spot Test:** Rub on white paper $\to$ permanent **translucent grease spot**.
  - *Vitamins:*
    - **Fat-Soluble (Stored in liver):** Vitamins **A, D, E, K**. (Vitamin K essential for blood clotting; Vitamin D for bones; Vitamin A for vision).
    - **Water-Soluble (Excreted in urine daily):** Vitamin **B-Complex** (Thiamine/B1, Riboflavin/B2, Niacin/B3) and Vitamin **C** (Ascorbic acid).

## 4. Meal Planning and Food Preservation
- **Therapeutic and Special Group Diets:**
  - *Diabetic Diet:* Control blood glucose levels; restrict refined simple sugars, sweets, and pastries; high in complex carbohydrates with **low glycemic index (GI)**, high dietary fiber (vegetables, oats, legumes), and moderate lean proteins.
  - *Hypertensive Diet (DASH Diet):* Low sodium / reduced table salt; low saturated fats and cholesterol; rich in potassium, magnesium, and calcium (fruits, leafy vegetables).
  - *Adolescents:* High energy (active metabolism), high protein, and **extra Iron** (especially for adolescent girls to compensate for menstrual blood loss) and Calcium.
  - *Convalescents (Recovering from illness):* Easily digestible, nutrient-dense meals; steamed or poached foods; attractive presentation to stimulate weak appetite.
- **Food Preservation Principles:**
  - *Drying / Dehydration:* Removes moisture to inhibit microbial growth and enzyme activity (sun drying of grains, fish, peppers).
  - *Refrigeration and Freezing:* Low temperatures ($0^\circ\text{C}$ to $-18^\circ\text{C}$) retard microbial multiplication and deactivate enzymes without altering flavor.
  - *Canning / Sterilization:* High temperature thermal processing destroys all vegetative bacteria and bacterial spores in airtight hermetically sealed metal cans.
  - *Pasteurization:* Heat treatment of milk ($72^\circ\text{C}$ for 15 seconds — HTST) destroying pathogenic bacteria (*Mycobacterium tuberculosis*) without boiling or coagulating milk proteins.
  - *Chemical Preservatives:* High concentrations of salt (osmotic dehydration of bacteria in meat/fish), high sugar (in jams/jellies), vinegar/acid (pickling), and wood smoke (phenolic compounds).

## 5. Flour Mixtures, Raising Agents, and Pastries
- **Raising Agents:** Substances that produce gas ($\text{CO}_2$, air, steam) causing flour batters and doughs to expand and rise during baking:
  - *Mechanical:* Trapping air through whisking eggs, creaming fat and sugar, or folding/rolling dough.
  - *Chemical:* **Baking Powder** (mixture of Sodium Bicarbonate $\text{NaHCO}_3$ as alkali + Cream of Tartar as acid salt + cornstarch filler). Reacts with moisture and heat to release $\text{CO}_2$:
    $$\text{Acid} + \text{Alkali} \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} \text{Carbon Dioxide } (\text{CO}_2) + \text{Water} + \text{Neutral Salt}$$
  - *Biological:* **Baker's Yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*)**: Living single-celled fungus fermenting sugars into $\text{CO}_2$ gas and ethanol in warm conditions ($25^\circ - 30^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Pastry Making Principles:**
  - *Shortcrust Pastry:* Standard ratio is **half fat to flour ($1:2$)** (e.g., $100\text{g}$ margarine to $200\text{g}$ plain flour).
  - Rules: Keep all ingredients, utensils, and hands **cold**; incorporate fat by the **rubbing-in method** using fingertips until mixture resembles fine breadcrumbs; mix with minimal ice-cold water to avoid overworking the gluten (which makes pastry tough rather than crumbly and short).
  - *Flaky and Puff Pastry:* Requires higher fat-to-flour ratios ($2:3$ or $1:1$); fat is rolled and folded in layers to trap air and steam.

# SECTION 3: CLOTHING AND TEXTILES

## 6. Textile Fibers & Fiber Identification Burning Tests
- **Classification of Textile Fibers:**
  - *Natural Fibers:*
    - **Vegetable / Cellulosic:** **Cotton** (from cotton seed bolls, breathable, absorbs moisture, strong, withstands boiling), **Linen** (from flax stem, crisp, cool, wrinkles easily).
    - **Animal / Protein:** **Wool** (from sheep fleece, warm, crimped, resilient, shrinks easily in hot water), **Silk** (from cocoons of *Bombyx mori* silkworm, smooth, lustrous, high tensile strength).
  - *Synthetic / Man-Made Fibers:*
    - **Regenerated:** **Viscose Rayon** (chemically processed wood pulp/cellulose; resembles silk/cotton).
    - **Pure Chemical Polymers:** **Nylon** (polyamide, non-absorbent, ultra-strong, quick-drying), **Polyester** (Terylene, wrinkle-resistant), **Acrylic** (synthetic wool substitute).
- **The Systematic Burning Test for Fibers:**
  | Fiber Class | Behavior Near Flame | Burning Characteristics & Odor | Ash / Residue Characteristics |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Cotton / Linen** | Ignites readily, does not melt | Burns rapidly with a **bright yellow flame**; smells like **burning paper/wood** | Light, feathery, soft **gray ash** |
  | **Wool / Silk** | Curls away from flame | Burns slowly, self-extinguishes; smells like **burning hair or feathers** | Dark, irregular, hollow, **crushable black bead** |
  | **Nylon** | Shrinks and melts before touching flame | Melts and burns slowly; distinct **chemical / celery-like odor** | Extremely **hard, tough, uncrushable round bead** |
  | **Polyester** | Melts and curls | Burns with black sooty smoke; sweet aromatic odor | Hard, dark, round bead |

## 7. Garment Construction, Stitches, Seams & Fullness
- **Classification of Hand Stitches:**
  - *Temporary Stitches:* Removed after permanent sewing: **Even tacking, Uneven tacking, Basting, Tailor's tacks** (transferring pattern markings).
  - *Permanent Joining Stitches:* **Running stitch, Back stitch** (strongest hand stitch, looks like machine stitching on top), **Hemming stitch, Oversewing**.
- **Seams in Garment Construction:**
  - *Plain Seam:* Most common; two fabric pieces placed right sides together and stitched $1.5\text{ cm}$ from the raw edge; edges must be neatened (pinked, zigzagged).
  - *French Seam:* An enclosed **double seam** finished on both sides without exposed raw edges. Stitched first on the **wrong side**, trimmed, turned, and stitched on the **right side**. Best for **sheer, lightweight fabrics, fine silk, and baby garments**.
  - *Flat Felled (Run-and-Fell) Seam:* Extremely durable flat seam with two parallel rows of visible machine stitching; completely encloses raw edges. Used on **men's shirts, pyjamas, and denim jeans**.
- **Methods of Controlling Fullness:**
  - *Darts:* Triangular folds stitched on the wrong side tapering to a sharp point, used to shape flat fabric to the curved contours of the body (bust, waist, hips).
  - *Gathers:* Pulling two parallel rows of loose running stitches to draw fabric into small even puckers.
  - *Pleats:* Folds of fabric pressed flat: **Knife pleats** (all folded in one direction), **Box pleats** (two knife pleats folded away from each other), **Inverted pleats** (two knife pleats folded toward each other meeting in the center).

---

## 8. Stain Removal Principles
- Treat stains **immediately** before they oxidize and set permanently.
- Identify the fiber type before applying chemical reagents (acids weaken cotton/linen; alkalis destroy wool/silk).
- **Specific Stain Removal Protocols:**
  - *Blood Stain:* Soak immediately in **cold salt water** (hot water coagulates the protein, setting the stain permanently).
  - *Grease / Oil Stain:* Apply absorbent powder (talcum powder/chalk) to draw out oil, then wash with warm soapy water or dab with grease solvents (turpentine, benzene).
  - *Tea / Coffee Stain:* Pour boiling water directly through the stretched stain from a height; apply borax solution if persistent.
  - *Rust / Iron Mould:* Apply **lemon juice and salt**, hold over steam, or treat with dilute oxalic acid.
  - *Ballpoint Pen Ink:* Dab with methylated spirit or alcohol.

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB HOME ECONOMICS

1. **Fiber Burning Test Shortcut:** Burning paper smell $=$ **Cotton**; Burning hair smell $=$ **Wool**; Hard uncrushable plastic bead $=$ **Nylon**.
2. **French Seam Application:** When a question specifies baby garments, delicate sheer fabrics, or chiffon, the appropriate seam is always the **French Seam**.
3. **Flat Felled Seam Application:** Jeans, workwear, and denim require the strong, double-stitched **Flat Felled (Run and Fell) Seam**.
4. **Shortcrust Pastry Fat-to-Flour Ratio:** The fat-to-flour ratio for shortcrust pastry is strictly **$1:2$** (half the weight of fat to flour).
5. **Cold Water for Blood Stains:** Never use warm or hot water on a fresh blood stain because heat permanently coagulates protein into the textile weave. Always use **cold water and salt**.
6. **Kwashiorkor vs Marasmus Identification:** Protein deficiency with edema and potbelly $=$ **Kwashiorkor**; Starvation/calorie deficiency without edema (emaciated "old man" face) $=$ **Marasmus**.
7. **Stages of Labor Sequence:** 1. Dilation of cervix $\to$ 2. Expulsion/Delivery of baby $\to$ 3. Delivery of Placenta (afterbirth).
8. **Diabetic Meal Planning:** High complex carbohydrates, low glycemic index, high dietary fiber, zero refined sugars.
9. **Oral Rehydration Therapy Formulation:** 1 level teaspoon of salt + 5 level teaspoons of sugar in 1 beer bottle (600 ml) of clean boiled water.
10. **Vitamin C Chemical Name and Source:** Vitamin C is **Ascorbic Acid**, found in citrus fruits (oranges, lemons, guavas); deficiency causes **Scurvy** (bleeding gums).

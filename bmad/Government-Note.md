# JAMB Government — Comprehensive Study Note for Candidates (Jambites)

**Subject Code:** `GOV`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Government  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Government

In the UTME, Government is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** systematically divided into three main sections:
1. **Elements & Principles of Government (~15 Questions):** Basic political concepts (State, Power, Authority, Legitimacy, Sovereignty), political ideologies, forms and systems of government (Unitary, Federal, Confederal, Presidential, Parliamentary), organs of government, separation of powers, rule of law, citizenship, electoral systems, political parties, pressure groups, and public administration.
2. **Pre-Colonial Systems, Colonial Rule & Constitutional History (~15 Questions):** Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba (Old Oyo), and Igbo pre-colonial political structures; British Indirect Rule and why it succeeded or failed; Constitutional progression from Clifford (1922) through Richards (1946), Macpherson (1951), Lyttleton (1954), Independence (1960), Republican (1963) to Presidential Constitutions (1979 and 1999).
3. **Post-Independence Politics, Military Rule & Foreign Policy (~10 Questions):** Major political crises (1962–1965), military interventions and decrees, the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970), the 1976 Local Government Reforms, Afrocentric foreign policy, and membership in international bodies (UN, AU/OAU, ECOWAS, Commonwealth, OPEC).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Traps in JAMB Government:**
> - Mixing up constitutional dates and landmarks (e.g., Clifford 1922 introduced the elective principle; Lyttleton 1954 established true federalism and regional premiers; 1963 Republican constitution replaced the British Privy Council with the Supreme Court).
> - Confusing the checks and balances of pre-colonial empires (e.g., Bashorun led the Oyomesi in Oyo, while the Waziri was the prime minister in the Sokoto Caliphate).
> - Distinguishing between Pressure Groups (seek to influence government policy without contesting elections) and Political Parties (contest elections to capture state power).

# SECTION 1: ELEMENTS AND PRINCIPLES OF GOVERNMENT

## 1. Basic Political Concepts & Ideologies
- **State vs. Nation vs. Society:**
  - *State:* A politically organized body of people occupying a definite geographic territory with an organized government, independence from external control, and possessing monopoly of legitimate force (**Sovereignty**). Four essential elements: **Population, Territory, Government, Sovereignty**.
  - *Nation:* A distinct group of people bound together by common historical, cultural, linguistic, or ethnic identity, with or without a sovereign state (e.g., the Kurdish nation, the Yoruba nation).
- **Power, Authority, and Legitimacy:**
  - *Power:* The capacity to compel or influence obedience through physical coercion, sanctions, or incentives.
  - *Authority:* The legitimate, institutionalized right to exercise power and command voluntary obedience. Max Weber's 3 types of authority:
    1. **Traditional Authority:** Based on custom, ancestral heritage, and long-standing belief (e.g., Obas, Emirs, Obis).
    2. **Charismatic Authority:** Based on extraordinary personal qualities, magnetism, or heroism of a leader (e.g., Nelson Mandela, Kwame Nkrumah).
    3. **Legal-Rational / Constitutional Authority:** Derived from formally enacted written laws, official offices, and constitutional procedures.
  - *Legitimacy:* The general recognition and acceptance by citizens of the moral and legal right of a government to rule. Conferred by free and fair elections, rule of law, good governance, economic performance, and ideological appeal.
- **Sovereignty (Jean Bodin, 1576):**
  - The supreme, final, absolute, and unlimited power of a state to make and enforce laws within its territory without external interference.
  - Types: *Internal Sovereignty* (absolute authority over all citizens and associations within borders); *External Sovereignty* (independence from foreign control); *De Jure* (legal right to rule based on law); *De Facto* (actual physical control of power, e.g., following a military coup).
  - *Limitations on Sovereignty:* International treaties and obligations (UN, AU, ECOWAS), constitutional supremacy, fundamental human rights, public opinion, and military strength of superpowers.
- **Political Ideologies:**
  - *Capitalism (Free Market / Laissez-Faire):* Private ownership of means of production, profit motive, market price mechanism, minimal state intervention (Adam Smith).
  - *Socialism:* Public/state ownership of major means of production, production for social welfare rather than private profit, central economic planning (Karl Marx).
  - *Communism:* Advanced, theoretical stage of socialism; complete abolition of private property, classless and stateless society ("From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs").
  - *Fascism / Nazism:* Totalitarian dictatorship, violent suppression of opposition, state supremacy over individuals, extreme nationalism and militarism (Benito Mussolini in Italy, Adolf Hitler in Germany).
  - *Feudalism:* Medieval system where land is owned by lords/nobles and worked by tenant serfs in exchange for military protection and tribute.

## 2. Forms and Systems of Government
- **Unitary vs. Federal vs. Confederal Systems:**
  - **Unitary System:** All constitutional powers are concentrated in a single central government. Component regional/local units derive power strictly by devolution from the center (e.g., United Kingdom, France, Ghana). Merits: Strong central authority, uniform laws, reduced administrative cost. Demerits: Risk of autocracy, neglect of remote/minority areas.
  - **Federal System:** Constitutional division of powers between a central (federal) government and autonomous sub-national units (states/regions/provinces) (e.g., Nigeria, USA, Canada). Requires: Written and rigid constitution, supreme court for constitutional disputes, and bicameral legislature.
    - *Exclusive Legislative List:* Powers reserved strictly for the federal government (Defense, Foreign Affairs, Currency, Customs, Aviation, Mining).
    - *Concurrent Legislative List:* Powers shared by both federal and state governments (Education, Health, Agriculture, Roads). In case of conflict, federal law prevails.
    - *Residual Legislative List:* Unspecified matters left exclusively to the states (Local government affairs, chieftaincy titles, customary land tenure).
  - **Confederal System (Confederation):** A loose league or union of sovereign independent states that delegate limited, specific powers to a weak central authority (e.g., Senegambia Confederation, early USA). Member states retain full sovereignty and reserve the constitutional right to **secede**.
- **Presidential vs. Parliamentary (Cabinet) Systems:**
  | Feature | Presidential System (USA, Nigeria 1979/1999) | Parliamentary System (Britain, Nigeria 1960/1963) |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Executive Structure** | **Single Executive:** President is both Head of State (ceremonial) and Head of Government (executive). | **Dual Executive:** Ceremonial Head of State (Monarch/President) + Real Executive Head of Government (Prime Minister). |
  | **Relationship of Organs** | **Strict Separation of Powers:** President and ministers cannot be members of the legislature. | **Fusion of Powers:** Prime Minister and cabinet ministers MUST be elected members of parliament. |
  | **Tenure of Office** | Fixed term (e.g., 4 years); removed only by cumbersome legislative **impeachment**. | Flexible tenure; government falls whenever it loses a **Vote of No Confidence** in parliament. |
  | **Cabinet Responsibility** | Individual responsibility to the President (President hires and fires ministers). | **Collective Responsibility:** Cabinet stands or falls together; public dissent requires resignation. |

## 3. Machinery and Organs of Government
- **The Legislature:**
  - *Unicameralism:* Single legislative chamber (e.g., Ghana, Kenya, Israeli Knesset). Advantages: Cheaper, faster law-making. Disadvantages: Risk of hasty, ill-considered legislation without check.
  - *Bicameralism:* Two legislative chambers — Upper and Lower House (e.g., Nigeria: Senate + House of Representatives; USA: Senate + House of Representatives; UK: House of Lords + House of Commons). Advantages: Prevents legislative tyranny, allows thorough review, balances representation of states (Senate represents states equally; House of Reps by population).
  - *Stages in Bill Passage:* First Reading (introduction & title) $\to$ Second Reading (debate on general principles) $\to$ Committee Stage (clause-by-clause detailed scrutiny by Standing/Special Committee) $\to$ Report Stage (committee reports back amendments) $\to$ Third Reading (final vote) $\to$ Presidential Assent (or Veto; veto can be overridden by a two-thirds majority in both houses).
- **The Executive:** Formulates and implements public policies, enforces laws, maintains law and order, conducts foreign diplomacy, and grants pardons (prerogative of mercy).
- **The Judiciary:**
  - Interprets the constitution and laws, settles disputes, and protects fundamental human rights.
  - *Independence of the Judiciary:* Essential to prevent executive/legislative tyranny. Protected by: Security of tenure (judges cannot be arbitrarily dismissed), non-reduction of salaries charged directly on the **Consolidated Revenue Fund**, appointment via independent judicial councils (National Judicial Council - NJC), and judicial immunity for official rulings.
  - *Judicial Review:* The constitutional power of superior courts to declare acts of the legislature or actions of the executive null, void, and unconstitutional if they conflict with the constitution (established in *Marbury v. Madison*, 1803).
  - *Prerogative Writs:*
    - **Habeas Corpus:** Demands an unlawfully detained person be brought to court to determine the legality of detention.
    - **Mandamus:** Compels a public official or body to perform a statutory duty.
    - **Certiorari:** Orders a lower court/tribunal to transmit records of a proceeding to a higher court for review.
    - **Injunction:** Restrains an individual or authority from performing an unlawful act.
- **Separation of Powers & Checks and Balances (Baron de Montesquieu, 1748):**
  - To preserve political liberty, the three governmental powers (legislative, executive, judicial) must be held by separate persons and bodies.
  - Checks and Balances: Each branch possesses constitutional powers to check and balance the excesses of the other two (e.g., Executive can veto legislative bills; Legislature can impeach the President and approve ministerial/judicial appointments; Judiciary can declare laws unconstitutional).

## 4. Political Participation, Franchise, and Institutions
- **Citizenship & Human Rights:**
  - *Acquisition:* By Birth (jus sanguinis / jus soli), by Descent, by Registration (foreign women married to citizens), by Naturalization (long residence, good character, oath of allegiance).
  - *Loss:* Renunciation, treason, criminal conviction, false declaration during naturalization.
  - *Fundamental Human Rights:* Right to life, dignity of human person, personal liberty, fair hearing, private and family life, freedom of thought/conscience/religion, freedom of expression and the press, peaceful assembly, freedom of movement, and freedom from discrimination.
- **The Rule of Law (A.V. Dicey, 1885):**
  1. **Supremacy of Regular Law:** Absolute predominance of regular law over arbitrary power; no one can be punished except for a distinct breach of an established law proven in an ordinary court.
  2. **Equality Before the Law:** All persons, regardless of rank or wealth, are equally subject to the ordinary law of the land.
  3. **Protection of Individual Liberties:** Fundamental rights are guaranteed by ordinary constitutional law and judicial enforcement.
- **Electoral Systems:**
  - *First-Past-The-Post (Simple Majority / Plurality):* Candidate with the highest number of votes wins the single-member constituency, even if they do not achieve an absolute majority ($>50\%$). Used in Nigeria and UK. Tends to produce two-party dominance.
  - *Proportional Representation (PR):* Seats in parliament are awarded to political parties in direct proportion to the percentage of total popular votes won nationwide. Prevents wasted votes and guarantees fair representation for minority parties.
- **Political Parties vs. Pressure Groups:**
  - *Political Parties:* Organized associations of citizens seeking to gain and exercise political power by contesting elections, forming government, and implementing political party manifestos.
  - *Pressure Groups (Interest Groups):* Organized bodies of individuals seeking to **influence government policies and legislation** in favor of their members' specific interests, **without seeking to capture political power or contest elections**. Types: Sectional/Protective (NLC, ASUU, NBA) and Promotional/Cause (Amnesty International, Environmental groups). Tactics: Lobbying, petitions, media campaigns, peaceful strikes, and demonstrations.
- **Public Corporations vs. The Civil Service:**
  - *Civil Service:* Government ministries executing general administrative policies; funded from national budget; staff (civil servants) governed by Max Weber's bureaucratic principles: **Hierarchy, Political Neutrality, Anonymity, and Permanence of Tenure**.
  - *Public Corporations (Parastatals):* State-owned commercial or statutory enterprises established by special Acts of Parliament to provide essential social utilities (e.g., water, electricity, railways) at affordable rates with operational autonomy.
  - *Ombudsman (Public Complaints Commission):* Independent statutory official established to investigate citizens' complaints of administrative injustice, corruption, abuse of power, or maltreatment by government officials.

# SECTION 2: PRE-COLONIAL SYSTEMS & CONSTITUTIONAL HISTORY

## 5. Pre-Colonial Political Systems in Nigeria
- **The Hausa-Fulani Emirate System (Sokoto Caliphate):**
  - Highly centralized, autocratic, Islamic theocracy established following the 1804 Usman Dan Fodio Jihad.
  - Headed by the **Emir**, who was both political and spiritual leader, ruling under **Sharia law**.
  - Key Caliphate Officials:
    - **Waziri:** Prime Minister and chief administrative adviser to the Emir.
    - **Madawaki:** Commander of the cavalry and armed forces.
    - **Galadima:** Administrator and governor of the capital city.
    - **Sarkin Fada:** Head of palace officials and master of royal protocol.
    - **Sarkin Ruwa:** Commissioner of waterways, rivers, and fishing.
    - **Sarkin Pawa:** Chief of butchers and slaughterhouses.
    - **Alkali:** Professional Islamic magistrate administering justice according to the Maliki school of Sharia law.
  - Taxes collected: **Zakat** (Islamic poor-due tax on wealth), **Jizya** (poll tax on non-Muslim subjects), **Kharaj** (land tax), **Jangali** (cattle tax).
- **The Yoruba Political System (Old Oyo Empire):**
  - Decentralized constitutional monarchy characterized by sophisticated system of **checks and balances**.
  - **The Alaafin:** The supreme traditional ruler (King) residing in Oyo-Ile; regarded as divine companion of the gods (*Iku Baba Yeye*), but his powers were strictly checked.
  - **The Oyomesi (Council of 7 Kingmakers):** Headed by the **Bashorun** (Prime Minister). They elected the Alaafin and could check royal tyranny by presenting the Alaafin with an empty calabash or parrot egg, carrying the mandate: *"The gods reject you, the earth rejects you, the people reject you"* — obligating the Alaafin to commit suicide.
  - **The Ogboni Secret Society:** Mediated between the Alaafin and the Oyomesi; worshiped the earth goddess (*Ile*); checked the Bashorun and the Oyomesi to prevent arbitrary rejection of an Alaafin.
  - **The Are-Ona-Kakanfo:** Generalissimo of the imperial army. Forbidden from suffering military defeat; if defeated in battle, he was expected to commit suicide or go into self-exile.
- **The Igbo Political System:**
  - Segmentary, acephalous ("headless"), stateless, egalitarian, direct consensus democracy. Power was completely decentralized across the village:
    - **Village Assembly (Oha-na-Eze / Amala):** General democratic meeting of all adult male citizens in the village square to make vital community decisions.
    - **Council of Elders (Ndichie):** Composed of family and lineage heads holding the sacred ancestral staff of authority (**Ofo**); mediated disputes.
    - **Title Holders (Ozo Society):** Men of proven wealth, character, and integrity who purchased prestigious titles; commanded immense moral and judicial authority.
    - **Age Grades (Uke):** Cohorts of youth responsible for public sanitation, road maintenance, defense, and executing decisions of the village assembly.
    - **Umuada / Umuokpu:** Association of married and unmarried daughters of the lineage; acted as powerful peacemakers in marital and family disputes.

## 6. Colonial Administration & The Policy of Indirect Rule
- **Indirect Rule:** British administrative policy of ruling colonized populations through their **existing traditional rulers and institutions**, supervised by British Colonial Officers (Residents and District Officers).
  - *Pioneered by:* Sir Frederick Lugard in Northern Nigeria (formulated in his book *The Dual Mandate in British Tropical Africa*).
  - *Reasons for Adoption:* Acute shortage of British personnel, extreme financial constraints (Britain wanted colonies to be financially self-sustaining), preservation of traditional structures, language and cultural barriers, and harsh tropical climate/diseases.
- **Application Across Nigerian Regions:**
  - *Northern Nigeria (Complete Success):* Highly successful because of the pre-existing centralized, autocratic Hausa-Fulani emirate system, literate bureaucracy, established direct tax system (**Jangali**), and unquestioning culture of obedience to the Emir.
  - *Western Nigeria (Partial / Moderate Success):* Moderate success. Hindered because the constitutional checks on the Oba prevented autocratic rule, educated elites opposed the system, and attempts to introduce direct taxation provoked severe riots (e.g., **Abeokuta Women's Revolt** led by Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti, and the 1918 Iseyin-Okeiho riots).
  - *Eastern Nigeria (Complete Failure):* Completely failed. Reasons: Total absence of centralized chiefs among the egalitarian Igbo; the British arbitrarily created artificial **"Warrant Chiefs"** who were corrupt, oppressive, and lacked ancestral legitimacy; and the introduction of direct taxation triggered the widespread **Aba Women's War of 1929**.

## 7. Constitutional Evolution in Nigeria (1922 – 1999)
- **Clifford Constitution (1922):**
  - Governor Sir Hugh Clifford replaced the Nigerian Council.
  - **Introduced the Elective Principle for the first time in Nigeria**, allowing 4 elected Africans into the 46-member Legislative Council (3 for Lagos, 1 for Calabar) with franchise restricted by income/property (£100 per annum).
  - Sparked modern Nigerian political party activity; Herbert Macaulay established the first Nigerian political party: the **Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP)** in 1923 ("Father of Nigerian Nationalism").
  - *Limitation:* Northern Nigeria was excluded from the Legislative Council and ruled by proclamations of the Governor.
- **Richards Constitution (1946):**
  - Aimed to promote unity of Nigeria while recognizing regional diversity.
  - Created **Regional Houses of Assembly** for the North, West, and East (introducing **Regionalism**).
  - Brought Northern Nigeria into the central Legislative Council for the first time.
  - Retained the elective principle (4 seats); unofficial majority in the Legislative Council.
- **Macpherson Constitution (1951):**
  - Drafted after unprecedented grassroots nation-wide consultation from village to provincial and regional levels.
  - Created a **Quasi-Federal** constitution with a central House of Representatives (148 members: 50% from North, 50% from South) and a 12-member central **Council of Ministers** (4 from each region).
  - Enhanced regional autonomy by empowering Regional Assemblies to pass actual laws on specified matters, subject to central veto.
- **Lyttleton Constitution (1954):**
  - **Formally established TRUE FEDERALISM in Nigeria**.
  - Established 3 autonomous Regions (Northern, Western, Eastern) and the Federal Capital Territory of Lagos; Southern Cameroons separated from Eastern Region as a quasi-federal territory.
  - Created the official post of **Regional Premier**; regionalized the Civil Service and the Judiciary.
  - Divided powers into **Exclusive Legislative List** (Federal) and **Concurrent Legislative List** (Shared).
- **Independence Constitution (1960):**
  - Granted Nigeria sovereign national independence on October 1, 1960.
  - Retained the British Westminster parliamentary system: Queen Elizabeth II remained ceremonial Head of State represented by Governor-General **Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe**; **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa** was Prime Minister (Head of Government).
  - Retained the **Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London** as Nigeria's final court of appeal.
- **Republican Constitution (1963):**
  - Severed all formal constitutional ties with the British Crown; Nigeria became a Federal Republic on October 1, 1963.
  - Queen was replaced by an indigenous constitutional President (Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe) as ceremonial Head of State.
  - **The Supreme Court of Nigeria replaced the British Privy Council** as the highest appellate court in Nigeria.
  - Created a fourth region: the **Mid-Western Region** (carved out of Western Region via plebiscite).
- **The 1979 Presidential Constitution (Second Republic):**
  - Prepared by the Constitution Drafting Committee (CDC - 49 "Wise Men" chaired by Chief Rotimi Williams) and the Constituent Assembly.
  - **Discarded the British parliamentary system and introduced the American-style Presidential System** (Executive President directly elected by the whole nation as both Head of State and Government).
  - Introduced the **Federal Character Principle** into the constitution to ensure equitable geographical representation in public appointments.
  - Created a 19-state federal structure and established the Code of Conduct Bureau.
- **The 1999 Constitution (Fourth Republic):**
  - Retained the presidential system with separation of powers, bicameral National Assembly (109 Senators: 3 per state + 1 FCT; 360 House of Reps members), 36 states, and 774 Local Government Areas.

# SECTION 3: POST-INDEPENDENCE POLITICS, MILITARY RULE & FOREIGN POLICY

## 8. Post-Independence Crises and Military Rule
- **Major Early Post-Independence Crises:**
  - *Action Group (Western Region) Crisis of 1962:* Factional power struggle between Chief Obafemi Awolowo and Premier Ladoke Akintola; led to violent uproar in the Western House of Assembly, declaration of a state of emergency, and appointment of Dr. M.A. Majekodunmi as Administrator.
  - *Census Crisis of 1962/1963:* Accusations of gross demographic inflation by regional governments to claim higher federal revenue allocations and parliamentary seats.
  - *Federal Election of 1964 and Western Election of 1965:* Massive rigging, violence, and breakdown of law and order ("Operation Wetie").
- **Military Coups and Civil War in Nigeria:**
  - *First Military Coup (January 15, 1966):* Led by Major Chukwuma Kaduna Nzeogwu. Assassinated Balewa, Ahmadu Bello, Akintola, Festus Okotie-Eboh. Major-General J.T.U. Aguiyi-Ironsi took over power.
  - *Decree No. 34 (Unification Decree of May 1966):* Promulgated by Ironsi, abolishing the federal structure and establishing a unitary system; sparked massive anti-Ironsi riots in the North.
  - *Counter-Coup (July 29, 1966):* Northern soldiers assassinated Ironsi; Lt. Col. Yakubu Gowon became Head of State; restored federalism.
  - *Creation of 12 States (May 27, 1967):* Gowon broke the 4 regions into 12 states to weaken regional secessionist movements.
  - *The Nigerian Civil War (July 6, 1967 – January 15, 1970):* Lt. Col. Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu declared the Republic of Biafra on May 30, 1967 following pogroms against Easterners. War ended with the unconditional surrender of Biafran forces led by Philip Effiong ("No victor, no vanquished").
- **1976 Local Government Reforms:**
  - Undertaken under the Murtala/Obasanjo military regime.
  - Formally established local government as the **autonomous third tier of government** in Nigeria.
  - Introduced a **uniform, single-tier system of local governments** throughout Nigeria; granted local governments statutory revenue allocation directly from the Federation Account.

## 9. Nigerian Foreign Policy & International Organizations
- **Foundations of Nigerian Foreign Policy:**
  - Basic principles: Preservation of sovereign territorial integrity; peaceful co-existence; Non-Alignment with Western or Eastern power blocs during the Cold War.
  - **Afrocentric Policy (Africa as the Centerpiece):** Total liberation of Africa from colonial domination, eradication of Apartheid in South Africa, promotion of African unity, and active support for freedom fighters in Angola (MPLA), Namibia (SWAPO), Zimbabwe, and South Africa (ANC).
- **Nigeria's Role in International Organizations:**
  - **United Nations (UN):** Nigeria joined as the 99th member on October 7, 1960. Contributed heavily to UN Peacekeeping operations in Congo (ONUC), Yugoslavia, Lebanon, and Sudan.
  - **African Union (AU / formerly Organization of African Unity - OAU, 1963):** Established in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Transformed into the AU in Durban, South Africa (2002). Objectives: Defend sovereignty, accelerate economic integration, and promote peace.
  - **Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS):** Founded on May 28, 1975 via the **Treaty of Lagos**, spearheaded by General Yakubu Gowon of Nigeria and President Gnassingbé Eyadéma of Togo. Headquartered in Abuja. Established **ECOMOG** (ECOWAS Ceasefire Monitoring Group) which brought peace to civil wars in Liberia and Sierra Leone.
  - **The Commonwealth of Nations:** Voluntary association of independent sovereign countries that were formerly British colonies, recognizing the British Monarch as the symbolic head. Provides educational scholarships, technical assistance, and sports competitions (Commonwealth Games).
  - **OPEC:** Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries; Nigeria joined in 1971.

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB GOVERNMENT

1. **Constitutional Elective Principle Milestone:** Whenever you see "Elective Principle introduced in Nigeria", the answer is **Clifford Constitution (1922)**. Franchise was limited to Lagos (3) and Calabar (1).
2. **True Federalism Milestone:** True federalism, the office of Premier, and regionalization of the public service were introduced by the **Lyttleton Constitution (1954)**.
3. **Privy Council Abolition Date:** Nigeria abolished appeals to the British Judicial Committee of the Privy Council under the **1963 Republican Constitution**, establishing the Supreme Court of Nigeria as the final arbiter.
4. **Recall the Pre-Colonial Officials:**
   - Sokoto: **Waziri** (Prime Minister), **Madawaki** (Army), **Alkali** (Judge).
   - Oyo: **Alaafin** (King), **Oyomesi** (7 Kingmakers), **Bashorun** (Head of Oyomesi), **Ogboni** (Mediators), **Kakanfo** (Army).
   - Igbo: **Oha-na-eze / Amala** (Assembly), **Ndichie** (Elders), **Ozo** (Title-holders), **Uke** (Age grades).
5. **Why Indirect Rule Failed in the East:** Remember the 3 root causes: Absence of centralized chiefs, corruption of British-appointed **"Warrant Chiefs"**, and arbitrary imposition of direct taxation sparking the **1929 Aba Women's War**.
6. **Pressure Groups vs Political Parties:** Pressure groups **do not** nominate candidates or seek to control the apparatus of state power; they merely lobby and pressure government to protect specific group interests.
7. **Presidential vs Parliamentary Distinctions:** Presidential $=$ Single executive, strict separation of powers, fixed tenure. Parliamentary $=$ Dual executive (Ceremonial President/Queen + Executive Prime Minister), fusion of powers, collective responsibility.
8. **Rule of Law Dicey Triad:** Supremacy of regular law, equality before the law, and individual human rights. If an option mentions "absolute discretion of the executive", it violates Dicey's rule of law!
9. **Exclusive List Belongs to Federal Only:** Defense, Aviation, Currency, External Affairs, Customs, and Mining are strictly on the **Exclusive Legislative List**.
10. **ECOWAS Genesis:** Established on **May 28, 1975** by the **Treaty of Lagos**, championed by Nigeria's **Yakubu Gowon** and Togo's **Gnassingbé Eyadéma**.

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Economics

In the UTME, Economics is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major areas:
1. **Basic Concepts & Tools of Analysis (~6 Questions):** Scarcity, choice, scale of preference, opportunity cost, Production Possibility Curve (PPC), tables, graphs, arithmetic mean, median, mode, range, and percentage index numbers.
2. **Microeconomics (~16 Questions):** Price theory (laws and determinants of demand/supply, elasticity of demand/supply, market equilibrium, price ceilings/floors), consumer utility theory, theory of production, cost and revenue curves, and market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly).
3. **Macroeconomics (~12 Questions):** National income accounting ($GDP, GNP, NNP, NI$), circular flow of income, consumption, savings, investment multiplier, money and banking, credit creation, central bank monetary policy, inflation, and public finance (taxation and government budgets).
4. **International Economics & Nigerian Economic Development (~6 Questions):** International trade (absolute and comparative advantage), terms of trade, balance of payments, trade restrictions, population theories (Malthusian theory, demographic transition), agriculture, petroleum, industrialization, and regional organizations (ECOWAS, OPEC, AfDB).

# SECTION 1: BASIC CONCEPTS AND TOOLS OF ECONOMIC ANALYSIS

## 1. Fundamental Economic Concepts
- **Definition of Economics:** According to Professor Lionel Robbins (1932): *"Economics is the social science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses."*
- **Basic Economic Concepts:**
  - *Wants:* Insatiable human desires for goods and services.
  - *Scarcity:* The fundamental economic problem — resources (land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship) are limited relative to unlimited human wants.
  - *Choice:* The act of selecting from alternatives arising as a direct consequence of scarcity.
  - *Scale of Preference:* A list of unsatisfied wants arranged in order of relative importance or priority, with the most pressing want placed at the top. Helps in making rational choices.
  - *Opportunity Cost (Real / True Cost):* The alternative forgone or sacrificed when a choice is made. E.g., if a student chooses to buy a textbook instead of a shirt with ₦5,000, the opportunity cost of the textbook is the forgone shirt.
- **Production Possibility Curve (PPC / Frontier):**
  - A graph showing all possible combinations of two commodities that an economy can produce using all available resources and technology efficiently.
  - *Points on the curve:* Efficient resource allocation (full employment).
  - *Points inside the curve:* Inefficiency, underemployment, or idle resources.
  - *Points outside the curve:* Currently unattainable given existing resources and technology (can only be reached via economic growth, technological progress, or increased resource endowment).
  - *Slope of PPC:* Marginal Rate of Transformation (MRT) or Opportunity Cost. Concave to origin due to increasing opportunity costs.

---

## 2. Tools of Economic Analysis
- **Measures of Central Tendency:**
  - *Arithmetic Mean ($\bar{x}$):* $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$. Affected by extreme values (outliers).
  - *Median:* The middle item when data is arranged in ascending or descending order. For odd $n$: position $= \frac{n+1}{2}$. Unaffected by extreme values; best for skewed income distributions.
  - *Mode:* The most frequently occurring score/value. Bimodal if two scores tie.
- **Measures of Dispersion:**
  - *Range:* $\text{Highest value} - \text{Lowest value}$.
  - *Variance ($\sigma^2$):* $\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n}$.
  - *Standard Deviation ($\sigma$):* $\sqrt{\text{Variance}}$.

# SECTION 2: MICROECONOMICS & PRICE THEORY

## 3. Theory of Demand
- **Law of Demand:** Other things being equal (*ceteris paribus*), as the price of a commodity increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa. Demand curves slope downward from left to right (negative slope).
- **Movement Along vs. Shift of Demand Curve:**
  - *Change in Quantity Demanded:* Movement along the same demand curve caused **solely by a change in the commodity's own price**.
  - *Change in Demand:* Complete shift of the demand curve (to the right for an increase, to the left for a decrease) caused by **non-price determinants** (consumer income, tastes/fashion, population size, price of substitutes or complements, government taxes/subsidies).
- **Types of Demand:**
  1. *Derived Demand:* Demand for a commodity not for its own sake, but because it is needed to produce another good (e.g., demand for labour, cement, land, flour).
  2. *Joint / Complementary Demand:* Demand for two or more commodities that are used together to satisfy a single want (e.g., car and petrol, pen and ink, bread and butter).
  3. *Competitive Demand:* Demand for commodities that serve as substitutes for each other; an increase in the price of one increases the demand for the other (e.g., Milo and Ovaltine, tea and coffee, beef and fish).
  4. *Composite Demand:* Demand for a commodity that has multiple uses (e.g., electricity used for lighting, cooking, heating; timber used for furniture, building, paper).

---

## 4. Elasticity of Demand
- **Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_d$):**
  $$E_d = \frac{\%\text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\%\text{ Change in Price}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P_1}{Q_1}$$
  - *Degrees of Elasticity:*
    - $E_d = 0$: **Perfectfully Inelastic** (Vertical line; quantity demanded does not respond to price changes, e.g., essential life-saving medication).
    - $E_d < 1$: **Inelastic** (Steep curve; percentage change in quantity is smaller than percentage change in price, e.g., salt, staple food).
    - $E_d = 1$: **Unitary Elastic** (Rectangular hyperbola; percentage change in quantity equals percentage change in price).
    - $E_d > 1$: **Elastic** (Gentle slope; percentage change in quantity is greater than percentage change in price, e.g., luxuries).
    - $E_d = \infty$: **Perfectfully Elastic** (Horizontal line; infinite change in quantity at a given price).
  - *Total Revenue (TR) Test:*
    - If demand is **elastic** ($E_d > 1$): A price cut **increases** Total Revenue.
    - If demand is **inelastic** ($E_d < 1$): A price cut **decreases** Total Revenue; a price increase **increases** Total Revenue.
- **Income Elasticity of Demand ($YED$):**
  $$YED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta Y}$$
  - $YED > 0$: **Normal Good** (demand rises as income rises). If $YED > 1$, it is a **Luxury Good**.
  - $YED < 0$: **Inferior Good** (demand falls as income rises, e.g., low-grade staples).
- **Cross Elasticity of Demand ($XED$):**
  $$XED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_{dA}}{\%\Delta P_B}$$
  - $XED > 0$ (Positive): Goods $A$ and $B$ are **Substitutes** (e.g., butter and margarine).
  - $XED < 0$ (Negative): Goods $A$ and $B$ are **Complements** (e.g., cars and petrol).
  - $XED = 0$: Goods are completely unrelated.

## 5. Theory of Supply & Market Equilibrium
- **Law of Supply:** *Ceteris paribus*, as price rises, quantity supplied increases, and vice versa. Supply curve slopes upward from left to right (positive slope).
- **Types of Supply:**
  - *Joint / Complementary Supply:* Two or more commodities produced from the same source (e.g., beef and hide from cattle, crude oil producing petrol, diesel, and kerosene).
  - *Competitive Supply:* Commodities competing for the same resources (e.g., cultivating cassava or yam on the same plot of land).
  - *Composite Supply:* Supply of a commodity from several different sources to satisfy a common demand (e.g., light supplied from sun, candle, generator, NEPA).
- **Market Equilibrium:**
  - Occurs where Quantity Demanded equals Quantity Supplied ($Q_d = Q_s$).
  - *Excess Demand (Shortage):* When market price is set below equilibrium price ($Q_d > Q_s$).
  - *Excess Supply (Surplus):* When market price is set above equilibrium price ($Q_s > Q_d$).
- **Price Legislation (Price Control):**
  - **Maximum Price (Price Ceiling):** Set by government **below** the equilibrium price to protect consumers from exploitation during shortages. Results in **excess demand (shortages), hoarding, rationing, and black markets**.
  - **Minimum Price (Price Floor):** Set by government **above** the equilibrium price to protect producers/farmers (agricultural guarantee) or workers (minimum wage legislation). Results in **excess supply (surplus/unemployment)**.

## 6. Theory of Consumer Behaviour
- **Utility:** The satisfaction derived from consuming a good or service.
  - *Total Utility ($TU$):* Cumulative satisfaction derived from consuming a given quantity of a commodity.
  - *Marginal Utility ($MU$):* The additional satisfaction derived from consuming one extra unit:
    $$MU = \frac{\Delta TU}{\Delta Q}$$
  - **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility:** As more and more units of a commodity are consumed consecutively, the marginal utility derived from each additional unit decreases.
  - When $MU = 0$, $TU$ is at its maximum (**Point of Satiety**). When $MU$ becomes negative, $TU$ begins to decline.
- **Consumer Equilibrium:**
  - Single Commodity: $MU_x = P_x$.
  - Two or More Commodities (**Equi-Marginal Principle**):
    $$\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = \frac{MU_y}{P_y} = \dots = \frac{MU_n}{P_n}$$
- **Consumer Surplus:** The difference between the maximum amount a consumer is willing to pay and the actual price paid in the market.

## 7. Theory of Production and Costs
- **Factors of Production & Rewards:**
  1. *Land:* Natural gifts; immobile, fixed supply, geographically varied. Reward = **Rent**.
  2. *Labour:* Human mental and physical effort; mobile, perishable. Reward = **Wages/Salaries**.
  3. *Capital:* Man-made wealth used to produce further wealth; reproducible, subject to depreciation. Reward = **Interest**.
  4. *Entrepreneur:* The coordinator, decision-maker, and risk-bearer. Reward = **Profit**.
- **Short-Run Production & Law of Variable Proportions (Diminishing Returns):**
  - As successive units of a variable factor (labour) are combined with fixed factors (land/capital), a point is reached where the marginal product of the variable factor diminishes.
  - Three Stages of Production:
    - *Stage I (Increasing Returns):* $AP$ is rising to its maximum; $MP > AP$.
    - *Stage II (Diminishing Returns):* $MP$ is falling but positive; $MP < AP$. **Rational production stage** where the rational producer operates.
    - *Stage III (Negative Returns):* $MP$ becomes negative; $TP$ declines. Irrational.
  - When $MP = AP$, Average Product ($AP$) is at its **maximum**.
- **Cost Concepts & Calculations:**
  - $TC = TFC + TVC$
  - Average Fixed Cost: $AFC = \frac{TFC}{Q}$ (continually falls as output expands, forming a rectangular hyperbola).
  - Average Variable Cost: $AVC = \frac{TVC}{Q}$.
  - Average Total Cost: $ATC = \frac{TC}{Q} = AFC + AVC$ (U-shaped due to the law of diminishing returns in short run).
  - Marginal Cost: $MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q} = \frac{\Delta TVC}{\Delta Q}$.
  - The $MC$ curve cuts both $AVC$ and $ATC$ at their **lowest points from below**.
- **Revenues & Profit Maximization:**
  - $TR = P \times Q$
  - Average Revenue: $AR = \frac{TR}{Q} = P$ (The $AR$ curve is the firm's demand curve).
  - Marginal Revenue: $MR = \frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta Q}$.
  - **Profit Maximization Condition for ALL Firms:**
    $$MC = MR \quad \text{and the } MC \text{ curve must cut the } MR \text{ curve from below}$$

## 8. Market Structures
| Feature | Perfect Competition | Monopoly | Monopolistic Competition | Oligopoly |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Sellers** | Very large number | Single seller (Sole supplier) | Many sellers | Few dominant sellers |
| **Nature of Product** | Homogeneous (identical) | Unique, no close substitutes | Differentiated (branding, packaging) | Homogeneous or differentiated |
| **Entry / Exit Barriers** | Free entry and exit | Extremely high / Blocked | Free entry and exit | High barriers |
| **Price Control** | **Price Taker** ($P = AR = MR$) | **Price Maker** ($AR > MR$) | Slight price discretion | Mutual interdependence (Collusion/Cartels) |
| **Demand Curve** | Perfectly elastic (horizontal) | Downward sloping (inelastic) | Downward sloping (elastic) | **Kinked Demand Curve** (Price rigidity) |
| **Long-Run Profit** | Normal profit only | Supernormal (abnormal) profit | Normal profit only | Supernormal profit |

- **Price Discrimination by a Monopolist:** Charging different prices to different buyers for the same commodity for reasons not related to cost differences. Conditions required: Monopoly power, market separation (no resale between markets), and differing price elasticities of demand between markets.

# SECTION 3: MACROECONOMICS

## 9. National Income Accounting
- **Core National Income Concepts:**
  - **Gross Domestic Product (GDP):** Total market value of all final goods and services produced within the geographic borders of a country in a year, regardless of the nationality of producers.
  - **Gross National Product (GNP):**
    $$GNP = GDP + \text{Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)}$$
    *(Where $\text{NFIA} = \text{Income earned by citizens abroad} - \text{Income earned by foreigners locally}$).*
  - **Net National Product (NNP):**
    $$NNP = GNP - \text{Depreciation (Capital Consumption Allowance)}$$
  - **National Income (NI at Factor Cost):**
    $$NI = NNP - \text{Indirect Taxes} + \text{Subsidies}$$
  - **Personal Disposable Income ($DI$):** Income remaining for spending and saving after deducting direct personal income taxes:
    $$DI = PI - \text{Personal Direct Taxes} = C + S$$
- **Methods of Measuring National Income:**
  1. *Output / Net Product Method:* Sums the value added by all industries at each production stage (avoids **double counting**).
  2. *Income Method:* Sums all payments received by factors of production: $\text{Wages} + \text{Rent} + \text{Interest} + \text{Profits}$ (excludes transfer payments like pensions, student grants, gifts).
  3. *Expenditure Method:* Sums total spending on final goods and services:
    $$Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$$
    *(Where $C =$ Consumer spending, $I =$ Investment, $G =$ Government expenditure, $X - M =$ Net exports).*
- **The Multiplier Effect:**
  - Marginal Propensity to Consume ($MPC$) $= \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}$.
  - Marginal Propensity to Save ($MPS$) $= \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y}$.
  - $MPC + MPS = 1 \implies MPS = 1 - MPC$.
  - **Investment / National Income Multiplier ($k$):**
    $$k = \frac{1}{MPS} = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta I}$$

## 10. Money, Banking, and Inflation
- **Money & Functions:** Anything generally acceptable as a medium of exchange and settlement of debts.
  - Primary functions: Medium of exchange, Unit of account / Measure of value.
  - Secondary functions: Store of value, Standard of deferred payment.
- **Quantity Theory of Money (Fisher's Equation of Exchange):**
  $$MV = PT$$
  *(Where $M =$ money supply, $V =$ velocity of circulation, $P =$ general price level, $T =$ total volume of transactions).*
  - Under classical assumptions ($V$ and $T$ are constant at full employment), an increase in money supply ($M$) leads to an equal proportionate increase in the price level ($P$).
- **Commercial Banks & Credit Creation:**
  - Commercial banks create money by lending out excess deposits.
  - **Credit Multiplier:**
    $$\text{Total Deposits Created} = \text{Initial Cash Deposit} \times \frac{1}{\text{Cash Reserve Ratio}}$$
- **Central Bank Monetary Policy Instruments:**
  - *To Fight Inflation (Contractionary Monetary Policy):*
    - Raise Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) and Liquidity Ratio.
    - Raise the Monetary Policy Rate (MPR / Bank Rate) to make borrowing expensive.
    - **Sell Government Securities** in Open Market Operations (OMO) to mop up excess liquidity.
    - Issue directives for credit contraction.
  - *To Fight Recession (Expansionary Monetary Policy):* Lower reserve ratios, lower MPR, and buy securities in OMO.
- **Inflation & Deflation:**
  - *Inflation:* Persistent and perceptible rise in the general price level leading to a fall in the purchasing power of money.
  - *Types:* Demand-pull (too much money chasing too few goods, $AD > AS$), Cost-push (rising production costs, wages, imported raw materials), Hyperinflation.
  - *Winners & Losers of Inflation:*
    - **Winners / Gainers:** Debtors (borrowers pay back in depreciated currency), speculators, businessmen/entrepreneurs.
    - **Losers:** Creditors (lenders receive depreciated money), fixed-income earners (salaried workers, pensioners).

## 11. Public Finance and Fiscal Policy
- **Taxation Principles & Classification:**
  - Adam Smith's Canons of Taxation: Equity, Certainty, Convenience, Economy.
  - *Direct Taxes:* Levied directly on the income or wealth of individuals and firms; burden cannot be shifted (e.g., Personal Income Tax/PAYE, Companies Income Tax/CIT, Capital Gains Tax).
  - *Indirect Taxes:* Levied on goods and services; burden can be shifted from manufacturer to consumer (e.g., Excise duties, Customs/Import tariffs, Value Added Tax/VAT).
  - *Tax Systems:*
    - **Progressive Tax:** Tax rate increases as income increases (reduces income inequality, e.g., PAYE).
    - **Regressive Tax:** Tax rate decreases as income increases (places heavier relative burden on the poor, e.g., flat indirect sales taxes).
    - **Proportional Tax:** Tax rate remains constant regardless of income level.
- **Government Budgets:**
  - Balanced Budget: Government Revenue $=$ Government Expenditure.
  - Surplus Budget: Government Revenue $>$ Government Expenditure (used to control inflation).
  - Deficit Budget: Government Expenditure $>$ Government Revenue (financed by borrowing or printing money; used to stimulate an economy out of recession).

# SECTION 4: INTERNATIONAL TRADE & NIGERIAN ECONOMY

## 12. International Trade & Balance of Payments
- **Theories of International Trade:**
  - *Absolute Advantage (Adam Smith):* A country should specialize in and export commodities that it can produce at a lower absolute cost than other nations.
  - *Comparative Advantage (David Ricardo):* A country should specialize in the production and export of goods in which it has the **lowest opportunity cost**, even if it has an absolute disadvantage in both.
- **Terms of Trade (TOT):**
  $$\text{Terms of Trade} = \frac{\text{Index of Export Prices}}{\text{Index of Import Prices}} \times 100$$
  - If $> 100$: Favourable Terms of Trade; If $< 100$: Unfavourable Terms of Trade.
- **Balance of Trade (BOT) vs. Balance of Payments (BOP):**
  - *Balance of Trade:* Visible Exports $-$ Visible Imports (goods only).
  - *Balance of Payments:* Comprehensive accounting statement of all economic and financial transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world over a year.
    - **Current Account:** Visible trade (goods) + Invisible trade (services like shipping, insurance, tourism) + Unilateral transfers (remittances).
    - **Capital and Financial Account:** Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), portfolio investment, government foreign loans and reserves.
- **Correcting Balance of Payments Deficits:** Devaluation / depreciation of domestic currency (makes exports cheaper and imports dearer), import tariffs, quotas, exchange controls, and export promotion.

---

## 13. Population and the Nigerian Economy
- **Malthusian Population Theory (Thomas Malthus, 1798):**
  - Population tends to grow at a **geometric progression** ($1, 2, 4, 8, 16 \dots$).
  - Food supply increases only at an **arithmetic progression** ($1, 2, 3, 4, 5 \dots$).
  - Unless checked, population will outstrip food supply, resulting in famine, poverty, disease, and war.
  - Checks: Preventive checks (moral restraint, delayed marriage, celibacy) and Positive checks (war, disease, pestilence, starvation).
- **Demographic Transition Theory:**
  - *Stage 1:* High birth rate and high death rate $\to$ slow population growth.
  - *Stage 2:* High birth rate and rapidly falling death rate (improved medicine/sanitation) $\to$ **population explosion**. (Developing countries like Nigeria).
  - *Stage 3:* Low birth rate and low death rate $\to$ stable or slow population growth (Developed nations).
- **Structure of the Nigerian Economy:**
  - *Petroleum Sector:* Dominates foreign exchange earnings (>90%) and government revenue, making Nigeria vulnerable to global crude oil price shocks (Dutch Disease).
  - *Agriculture:* Employs the largest percentage of the informal rural labor force; challenges include primitive tools, poor infrastructure, lack of credit, and post-harvest storage losses.
  - *Economic Organizations:*
    - **OPEC:** Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (coordinates petroleum production quotas to stabilize global prices).
    - **ECOWAS:** Economic Community of West African States (promotes regional economic integration, free movement of persons, and common external tariffs).
    - **AfDB:** African Development Bank (finances infrastructural development across Africa).

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB ECONOMICS

1. **Watch the Price Elasticity Signs:** When calculating $E_d$, the mathematical answer is negative due to the inverse price-quantity relationship, but economists express $E_d$ as an **absolute value** $|E_d|$. Ignore the negative sign when ranking elasticity.
2. **Memorize Cross Elasticity Signs:** Positive $XED$ means Substitutes ($+$ like butter and margarine). Negative $XED$ means Complements ($-$ like car and petrol). Zero means completely unrelated.
3. **PPC Opportunity Cost Shortcut:** Movement along the PPC curve from point A to point B represents opportunity cost. Point inside $=$ Underemployment/Inefficiency; Point outside $=$ Unattainable with current resources.
4. **Identify Profit Maximization Instantaneously:** Wherever $MC = MR$, profit is maximized. In perfect competition, because $P = MR$, this becomes $P = MC$.
5. **Differentiate Movement vs Shift:** A change in the commodity's **own price** causes a *movement along* the curve (change in quantity demanded). Any other factor causes a *complete shift* of the curve (change in demand).
6. **Price Ceiling vs Price Floor Placement:** Maximum Price (Ceiling) is placed **below** equilibrium (causes shortages and black markets). Minimum Price (Floor) is placed **above** equilibrium (causes surpluses).
7. **National Income Component Inclusions:** When calculating GDP by the expenditure method ($C + I + G + X - M$), never include transfer payments, second-hand goods, or intermediate goods (to avoid double counting).
8. **The Multiplier Formula:** $k = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}$. If $MPC = 0.8$, then $MPS = 0.2$ and $k = \frac{1}{0.2} = 5$. If investment increases by ₦10m, national income increases by $5 \times 10\text{m} = \text{₦50m}$.
9. **Kinked Demand Curve belongs to Oligopoly:** When you see "kinked demand curve" or "price rigidity", the answer is **Oligopoly**. Firms anticipate that competitors will match price cuts but ignore price hikes.
10. **Debtors Win in Inflation:** In high inflation periods, borrowers (debtors) gain because they repay in depreciated purchasing power, while lenders (creditors) lose.

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Geography

In the UTME, Geography is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major areas:
1. **Practical Geography & Cartography (~8 Questions):** Map scales, scale reduction and enlargement, distance and area measurement, bearing, contour interpretation, slope and gradient calculation, cross-sections, intervisibility, and drainage patterns.
2. **Physical Geography & Geomorphology (~16 Questions):** Earth's rotation and revolution, longitudinal local time calculations, earth's interior structure, rock classification, landform development (fluvial, desert, karst, coastal, glacial), vulcanicity, planetary winds, and climatic zones.
3. **Human & Economic Geography (~8 Questions):** Population distribution, demographic factors, rural/urban settlements, world agricultural systems, mineral and energy resources, transportation, and industrialization.
4. **Regional Geography of Nigeria (~8 Questions):** Physical background (relief, drainage, river systems), climate (monsoon vs. harmattan air masses), vegetation zones, agriculture, mineral distribution (petroleum, coal, tin, iron ore), and major environmental hazards.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Guaranteed Calculations in JAMB Geography:**
> - Local Time & Longitude: The earth rotates $360^\circ$ in 24 hours $\implies 15^\circ = 1\text{ hour} \implies 1^\circ = 4\text{ minutes}$. Moving East $\to$ **Add time (+)**; Moving West $\to$ **Subtract time (-)**.
> - Scale Reduction/Enlargement: When a map is reduced by half, the Representative Fraction (RF) denominator is **doubled** ($1:50,000 \to 1:100,000$). When enlarged by 2, the denominator is **halved** ($1:50,000 \to 1:25,000$).
> - Gradient Calculation: $\text{Gradient} = \frac{\text{Vertical Interval (VI)}}{\text{Horizontal Equivalent (HE)}}$.

# SECTION 1: PRACTICAL GEOGRAPHY AND MAPWORK

## 1. Map Scales and Measurements
- **Types of Scales:**
  - *Statement Scale:* Expressed in words (e.g., "$1\text{ cm}$ represents $1\text{ km}$").
  - *Representative Fraction (RF):* Ratio of map distance to ground distance in the same unit (e.g., $1:50,000$, meaning $1\text{ cm}$ on the map represents $50,000\text{ cm} = 500\text{ m} = 0.5\text{ km}$ on the ground).
  - *Linear (Graphic) Scale:* A divided bar drawn at the bottom of the map.
- **Conversion Example:**
  - Convert $1:50,000$ to a statement scale:
    $$50,000\text{ cm} \div 100,000\text{ cm/km} = 0.5\text{ km} \implies 2\text{ cm represents } 1\text{ km}$$
- **Scale Reduction & Enlargement Rules:**
  - *Reduction:* When a map is reduced by a factor of $n$, multiply the old RF denominator by $n$.
    $$\text{Example: Reduce a } 1:50,000 \text{ map by half } (2) \implies 1:(50,000 \times 2) = 1:100,000$$
    *(Map area becomes } \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4} \text{ of the original area)*.
  - *Enlargement:* When a map is enlarged by a factor of $n$, divide the old RF denominator by $n$.
    $$\text{Example: Enlarge a } 1:50,000 \text{ map by } 2 \implies 1:(50,000 \div 2) = 1:25,000$$
- **Bearings & Direction:**
  - *Fore Bearing:* The clockwise angle measured from True North ($000^\circ - 360^\circ$).
  - *Back Bearing:*
    - If Fore Bearing $< 180^\circ \implies \text{Back Bearing} = \text{Fore Bearing} + 180^\circ$.
    - If Fore Bearing $> 180^\circ \implies \text{Back Bearing} = \text{Fore Bearing} - 180^\circ$.

## 2. Relief Representation and Map Interpretation
- **Contours:** Lines on a map connecting points of equal height above mean sea level. The vertical distance between two successive contour lines is the **Vertical Interval (VI)**.
  - *Steep Slope:* Contours are drawn **very close together**.
  - *Gentle Slope:* Contours are drawn **widely spaced apart**.
  - *Uniform Slope:* Contours are **evenly spaced**.
  - *Concave Slope:* Closely packed contours at the top, widely spaced at the bottom.
  - *Convex Slope:* Widely spaced contours at the top, closely packed at the bottom.
  - *Cliff:* Contours merge or touch one another.
  - *Spur vs. Valley:*
    - **Spur:** Contour V-shapes point towards **lower ground** (downhill).
    - **Valley:** Contour V-shapes point towards **higher ground** (uphill / upstream).
- **Gradient Calculation:**
  $$\text{Gradient} = \frac{\text{Vertical Interval (VI)}}{\text{Horizontal Equivalent (HE)}}$$
  *(Ensure VI and HE are converted into the SAME unit before dividing! Express as a ratio, e.g., $1\text{ in } 25$)*.
- **Intervisibility:** Two points are intervisible if an observer at point A can see an observer at point B without an intervening hill or ridge blocking the line of sight. Checked by constructing a relief cross-section.
- **Drainage Patterns:**
  - *Dendritic:* Resembles the branches of a tree; develops on rocks of uniform resistance.
  - *Trellis:* Tributaries join the main river at approximately **right angles ($90^\circ$)**; develops in areas of alternating parallel bands of hard and soft rocks.
  - *Radial:* Rivers flow outward in all directions from a central elevated dome or volcanic cone (e.g., Jos Plateau).
  - *Centripetal:* Streams flow inward from surrounding highlands into a central depression or inland drainage basin (e.g., Lake Chad basin).

# SECTION 2: PHYSICAL GEOGRAPHY AND GEOMORPHOLOGY

## 3. Earth Movements, Longitudes and Time
- **Earth's Rotation (Every 24 Hours on its axis, tilted at $23.5^\circ$):**
  - Causes Day and Night, creates deflection of winds and ocean currents (Coriolis effect), causes sunrise and sunset, and produces the daily cycle of tides.
- **Earth's Revolution (Every $365\frac{1}{4}$ days around the Sun):**
  - Causes the Four Seasons (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter), varying lengths of day and night throughout the year, and changes in the altitude of the midday sun.
  - **Equinoxes (March 21 & September 23):** Midday sun is overhead at the Equator; equal day and night ($12\text{ hours}$ each) worldwide.
  - **Summer Solstice (June 21):** Sun is overhead at the Tropic of Cancer ($23.5^\circ\text{N}$); longest day in Northern Hemisphere.
  - **Winter Solstice (December 22):** Sun is overhead at the Tropic of Capricorn ($23.5^\circ\text{S}$); longest night in Northern Hemisphere.
- **Local Time & Longitude Calculations:**
  - Rate: $360^\circ = 24\text{ hours} \implies 15^\circ = 1\text{ hour} \implies 1^\circ = 4\text{ minutes}$.
  - Rule: **East Gain Add (EGA), West Lose Subtract (WLS)**.
  - *Worked Example:* If local time at Greenwich ($0^\circ$) is 12:00 noon, what is the local time in a town on Longitude $45^\circ\text{E}$?
    $$\text{Difference in longitude} = 45^\circ - 0^\circ = 45^\circ$$
    $$\text{Time difference} = \frac{45^\circ}{15^\circ/\text{hr}} = 3\text{ hours}$$
    $$\text{Since town is East of Greenwich } \to 12:00 + 3\text{ hours} = \mathbf{3:00\text{ PM}}$$

## 4. Earth's Interior and Rock Classification
- **Internal Layers of the Earth:**
  - *Crust (Lithosphere):* Solid outer layer ($0-40\text{ km}$). Continental crust (**Sial:** Silica + Aluminium, lighter, granite) and Oceanic crust (**Sima:** Silica + Magnesium, denser, basalt). Separated from mantle by the **Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho)**.
  - *Mantle (Mesosphere):* Semi-molten plastic rock ($40-2,900\text{ km}$), rich in olivine. Separated from core by the **Gutenberg Discontinuity**.
  - *Core (Barysphere / Centrosphere):* Deepest layer ($2,900-6,370\text{ km}$); composed of **Nife** (Nickel + Iron). Outer core is liquid; inner core is solid due to immense pressure.
- **Classification of Rocks:**
  1. **Igneous Rocks:** Formed by the cooling and solidification of molten magma/lava. Crystalline, non-stratified, contain no fossils.
     - *Plutonic (Intrusive):* Cooled slowly deep underground; coarse-grained crystals (e.g., **Granite, Gabbro, Diorite**).
     - *Volcanic (Extrusive):* Cooled rapidly on earth's surface; fine-grained crystals (e.g., **Basalt, Obsidian, Pumice**).
  2. **Sedimentary Rocks:** Formed from accumulation and lithification of sediments in layers (strata); contain fossils.
     - *Mechanically formed:* Sandstone, Shale, Clay, Conglomerate.
     - *Organically formed:* Formed from remains of living organisms (Limestone, Chalk, Coral, Coal).
     - *Chemically formed:* Formed by precipitation from solutions (Rock salt, Gypsum, Potash).
  3. **Metamorphic Rocks:** Pre-existing rocks completely altered by immense heat and pressure:
     - Limestone $\to$ **Marble**
     - Sandstone $\to$ **Quartzite**
     - Shale $\to$ **Slate**
     - Granite $\to$ **Gneiss**
     - Coal $\to$ **Graphite / Diamond**

## 5. Landform Development and Geomorphic Processes
- **Fluvial (River) Landforms Across 3 Stages:**
  - *Youth / Upper Course (Dominant process: Vertical erosion):* V-shaped valleys, interlocking spurs, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, rapids, and river capture.
  - *Mature / Middle Course (Dominant process: Lateral erosion & transportation):* U-shaped valleys, meanders, river cliffs, and slip-off slopes.
  - *Old Age / Lower Course (Dominant process: Deposition):* Ox-bow lakes, natural levees, deferred tributaries, floodplains, braided channels, and **deltas** (Arcuate e.g. Niger/Nile; Bird's foot e.g. Mississippi; Estuarine e.g. Congo/Rhein).
- **Desert (Wind & Water) Landforms:**
  - *Wind Erosion Landforms:* **Zeugen** (ridge of alternating horizontal hard/soft rock), **Yardang** (vertically striped rocks), **Rock Pedestal / Mushroom Rock**, **Inselberg** (steep-sided isolated residual hill), **Deflation Hollow** (creates oases when reaching water table).
  - *Wind Deposition Landforms:* **Barchan** (crescent-shaped sand dune with horns pointing downwind), **Seif Dunes** (longitudinal ridges parallel to wind), **Loess** (fine wind-blown dust).
  - *Water Action in Deserts:* **Wadis** (dry desert gullies), **Bajada**, **Playa** (ephemeral salt lake).
- **Karst (Limestone) Landforms:**
  - Formed by chemical dissolution of calcium carbonate by carbonated rainwater:
    $$\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2 \quad (\text{Soluble Calcium Bicarbonate})$$
  - Surface features: Grikes and Clints (limestone pavements), Sinkholes / Swallow holes, Dolines, Uvalas, Poljes.
  - Underground features: Caves/caverns; **Stalactites** (calcite deposits hanging down from cave roof; "C" for ceiling); **Stalagmites** (calcite pillars growing up from cave floor; "G" for ground); **Pillar** (stalactite and stalagmite joining).
- **Coastal Landforms:**
  - *Erosional Features:* Headlands and bays, sea cliffs, wave-cut platforms, sea caves, **arches, stacks, and stumps** (sequential erosion of headland).
  - *Depositional Features:* Beaches, sand spits, baymouth bars, **tombolos** (sand ridge connecting an island to the mainland).

---

## 6. Weather, Climate, and Planetary Winds
- **Weather Instruments & The Stevenson Screen:**
  - *Stevenson Screen:* White-painted, double-louvered wooden box on four legs, $1.21\text{ meters}$ above ground; houses **Maximum and Minimum Thermometer (Six's)** and **Hygrometer (Wet and Dry bulb thermometer)** to protect from direct sun and precipitation while allowing free air circulation.
  - *Other Instruments:* Rain Gauge (rainfall, sited in open ground away from trees), Mercury Barometer (atmospheric pressure), Anemometer (wind speed in knots), Wind Vane (wind direction).
- **Lapse Rate:** The rate of temperature decrease with altitude. Normal environmental lapse rate $= \mathbf{6.5^\circ\text{C}\text{ per } 1,000\text{ meters}}$ (or $1^\circ\text{C}$ per $160\text{ meters}$).
- **Planetary Winds & Pressure Belts:**
  - Doldrums (Equatorial Low Pressure Belt: $0^\circ - 5^\circ\text{N/S}$).
  - Horse Latitudes (Sub-Tropical High Pressure Belts: $30^\circ\text{N/S}$).
  - ITCZ (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone): Oscillating low-pressure trough where trade winds converge.

# SECTION 3: REGIONAL GEOGRAPHY OF NIGERIA

## 7. Physical Geography of Nigeria
- **Location & Extent:** Located entirely within the tropics in West Africa between Latitudes **$4^\circ\text{N}$ and $14^\circ\text{N}$**, and Longitudes **$3^\circ\text{E}$ and $15^\circ\text{E}$**.
- **Relief Regions of Nigeria:**
  - *Highlands ($>300\text{ meters}$):*
    - **Jos Plateau:** North-central granitic plateau; radial drainage hub; highest point is Shere Hills ($1,829\text{ m}$).
    - **Northern High Plains:** Undulating landscape of crystalline basement rocks (Hausaland).
    - **Western Highlands:** Inselberg-studded granite ranges of Yoruba uplands (Idanre hills).
    - **Eastern Highlands:** Mountainous border with Cameroon; contains **Chappal Waddi ($2,419\text{ meters}$)** in Taraba State — the **highest mountain peak in Nigeria**.
  - *Lowlands ($<300\text{ meters}$):* Sokoto plains, Chad basin, Niger-Benue trough, Coastal plains, and Niger Delta.
- **Drainage of Nigeria:**
  - Dominated by River Niger (enters Nigeria from Niger Republic, third longest river in Africa) and River Benue (enters from Cameroon), joining at the **Lokoja Confluence** to form a single trunk river emptying into the Atlantic via the Niger Delta.
  - **Radial Drainage Hub:** The **Jos Plateau** acts as the primary hydrographic center from which rivers flow in all directions (River Kaduna and Gongola to Niger/Benue; Rivers Hadejia and Jama'are to Lake Chad).

---

## 8. Climate, Vegetation, and Resources of Nigeria
- **The Two Air Masses Influencing Nigerian Weather:**
  1. **Tropical Maritime Air Mass (mT / South-West Monsoon Wind):** Originates over the South Atlantic Ocean; warm and moisture-laden; brings the **Rainy Season** across Nigeria (heavy rainfall in the south, decreasing northward).
  2. **Tropical Continental Air Mass (cT / North-East Trade Wind):** Originates over the Sahara Desert; dry, cold, and dust-laden; brings the **Harmattan / Dry Season** across Nigeria (peaks from November to February).
- **Vegetation Belts of Nigeria (South to North):**
  1. *Mangrove Swamp Forest:* Tidal coastal creeks, brackish water; red mangrove (*Rhizophora*) with stilt roots and white mangrove (*Avicennia*) with breathing roots (pneumatophores).
  2. *Freshwater Swamp Forest:* Permanent freshwater swamps in the delta; raffia palms.
  3. *Tropical Rainforest:* High rainfall ($>2,000\text{ mm}$), tall hardwood timber trees with layered canopies, buttress roots, lianas, and epiphytes (Iroko, Mahogany, Obeche).
  4. *Southern Guinea Savanna:* Tall grasses, fire-resistant trees; broad transition zone.
  5. *Northern Guinea Savanna:* Moderately tall grasses, open woodland (Isoberlinia).
  6. *Sudan Savanna:* Short feathery grasses, thorny acacia, baobab trees with bulbous water-storing trunks.
  7. *Sahel Savanna:* Semi-arid desert border, sparse drought-resistant tussock grasses and date palms.
- **Mineral Resources Distribution in Nigeria:**
  - **Petroleum & Natural Gas:** Niger Delta (Rivers, Bayelsa, Delta, Akwa Ibom, Ondo, Imo, Abia).
  - **Coal:** Sub-bituminous coal at **Enugu** (Udi hills) and Kogi.
  - **Tin & Columbite:** Alluvial deposits on the **Jos Plateau** (Bukuru).
  - **Iron Ore:** **Itakpe** and Agbaja in Kogi State (supplies Ajaokuta and Aladja steel complexes).
  - **Limestone:** Used for cement manufacturing in **Ewekoro/Sagamu** (Ogun), **Gboko** (Benue), **Nkalagu** (Ebonyi), **Ashaka** (Gombe), **Sokoto**.
  - **Lead and Zinc:** Found together at **Abakaliki** (Ebonyi) and Zurak (Plateau).

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB GEOGRAPHY

1. **Local Time Golden Rule (EGA / WLS):** **East Gain Add; West Lose Subtract**. Find the degree difference first ($15^\circ = 1\text{ hr}, 1^\circ = 4\text{ mins}$). If destination is East of reference meridian, ADD time; if West, SUBTRACT.
2. **Back Bearing Shortcut:** If forward bearing is $< 180^\circ$, **ADD $180^\circ$**. If forward bearing is $> 180^\circ$, **SUBTRACT $180^\circ$**. E.g., Back bearing of $045^\circ = 45 + 180 = 225^\circ$.
3. **Scale Reduction/Enlargement Area Rule:** If a map scale is reduced by half ($2$), its area becomes $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}$ of the original. If enlarged by $3$, area becomes $3^2 = 9$ times larger!
4. **Stalactite vs Stalagmite Mnemonic:** Stala**C**tite has **C** for Ceiling (hangs down); Stala**G**mite has **G** for Ground (grows up from floor).
5. **V-Contour Rule for Rivers:** Contour V-lines point **UPSTREAM (uphill)** towards the river source. A river always flows in the direction the V opens (downhill).
6. **Highest Mountain Peak in Nigeria:** The highest point in Nigeria is **Chappal Waddi ($2,419\text{ m}$)** on the Shebshi Mountains in Taraba State, NOT Shere Hills in Jos ($1,829\text{ m}$).
7. **Lapse Rate Calculation:** For every $1,000\text{ meters}$ rise, temperature drops by $6.5^\circ\text{C}$. If sea level is $30^\circ\text{C}$, at $2,000\text{ meters}$ altitude temperature will be $30 - (2 \times 6.5) = 17^\circ\text{C}$.
8. **Radial Drainage on Jos Plateau:** Rivers radiating outward in all directions from the center indicate a **Radial Drainage Pattern** (characteristic of the Jos Plateau).
9. **Metamorphic Rock Equivalents:** Limestone becomes **Marble**; Sandstone becomes **Quartzite**; Shale becomes **Slate**; Granite becomes **Gneiss**.
10. **Trellis vs Dendritic Patterns:** Dendritic is tree-like on uniform rock. Trellis has tributaries joining the main river at **$90^\circ$ right angles** on alternating hard and soft rocks.

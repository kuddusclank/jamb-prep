## Overview & How JAMB Tests Agricultural Science

In the UTME, Agricultural Science is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major areas:
1. **General Agriculture, Land Tenure & Soil Science (~10 Questions):** Branches of agriculture, land tenure systems, environmental factors, weathering, soil profile, soil chemistry/physics, plant nutrient deficiencies, soil fertility, manures, and erosion control.
2. **Crop Production & Crop Protection (~12 Questions):** Agronomy of major crops (cereals, legumes, roots/tubers, cash crops), cultural practices, propagation methods, weed biology, causal organisms of crop diseases, pests (biting, sucking, boring), and forestry.
3. **Animal Science, Nutrition & Veterinary Health (~10 Questions):** Anatomy/physiology of ruminants and monogastrics, poultry digestion, maintenance vs. production rations, livestock breeds and management, parasitic life cycles (tapeworm, liver fluke), and major viral/bacterial/protozoan diseases.
4. **Agricultural Economics, Farm Records & Mechanization (~8 Questions):** Farm budgeting, production functions, marketing channels, farm survey instruments (chains, arrows, ranging poles), tillage implements (ploughs, harrows, ridgers), and mechanization limitations in West Africa.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Distinctions in Agricultural Science:**
> - Ruminant stomach compartments: **Rumen** (fermentation), **Reticulum** (honeycomb/regurgitation), **Omasum** (water reabsorption), **Abomasum** (true enzymatic stomach).
> - Vectors of livestock diseases: **Tsetse fly (*Glossina*)** transmits Trypanosomiasis (Nagana); **Tick** transmits Babesiosis (Redwater fever) and Heartwater; **Water snail (*Lymnaea*)** is intermediate host for Liver fluke (*Fasciola*).
> - Nutrient deficiency symptoms: **Nitrogen** deficiency causes chlorosis of older leaves; **Phosphorus** causes purple discoloration of leaves and poor root growth; **Potassium** causes marginal leaf scorch.

# SECTION 1: GENERAL AGRICULTURE, LAND TENURE & SOIL SCIENCE

## 1. Scope, Importance & Land Tenure Systems
- **Branches of Agriculture:** Agronomy (crop science and soil management), Animal Science (husbandry and nutrition), Soil Science, Agricultural Economics, Agricultural Extension, Agricultural Engineering, Forestry, Horticulture, Aquaculture, Apiculture.
- **Land Tenure Systems in Nigeria:**
  - *Communal Land Tenure:* Land owned by the entire community, village, or clan; allocated by the chief/head. Promotes large-scale farming but prevents individual investment; cannot be used as collateral for bank loans.
  - *Freehold / Individual Ownership:* Land acquired by direct purchase; complete private title; can be mortgaged or sold. High cost limits acquisition by peasant farmers.
  - *Inheritance / Family Tenure:* Land passed from parents to children; leads to severe **fragmentation of land holdings** into small, scattered, uneconomic plots.
  - *Leasehold / Tenancy:* Farmer pays rent for temporary usage rights over an agreed period. Tenant cannot plant permanent perennial cash crops (e.g., cocoa, oil palm).
  - *The Land Use Act of 1978:* Vests all land in each state in the **State Governor**, held in trust for the public; allocates land via a **Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)**. Aims to make land easily accessible for commercial agriculture.

## 2. Soil Science and Soil Fertility
- **Soil Formation (Weathering):**
  - *Physical Weathering:* Temperature fluctuations (exfoliation), running water, wind, frost action, glacial movement.
  - *Chemical Weathering:* Hydration, Hydrolysis, Carbonation (rainwater + $\text{CO}_2 \to$ carbonic acid dissolving limestone), Oxidation, and Reduction.
  - *Biological Weathering:* Tree roots penetrating crevices, burrowing animals (earthworms, rodents), microbial decomposition.
- **Soil Profile Horizons:**
  - **Horizon O (Organic Layer):** Fresh and decaying plant litter (humus).
  - **Horizon A (Topsoil):** Darkest layer, rich in organic matter and nutrients, highest microbial activity. Maximum biological activity.
  - **Horizon B (Subsoil):** Zone of accumulation (**Illuviation**); minerals leached from topsoil (**Eluviation**) deposit here. Clayey, less aerated.
  - **Horizon C (Parent Material):** Partially weathered rock fragments.
  - **Horizon D (Bedrock):** Unweathered solid bedrock.
- **Soil Components:** Mineral matter (45%), Organic matter (5%), Soil air (25%), Soil water (25%).
  - Soil water types: *Gravitational water* (drains away under gravity), *Capillary water* (held in soil pore spaces; available to plant roots), *Hygroscopic water* (tightly bound to soil particles; unavailable to plants).
- **Soil Texture vs. Soil Structure:**
  - *Texture:* Relative proportions of sand ($0.02 - 2.0\text{ mm}$), silt ($0.002 - 0.02\text{ mm}$), and clay ($< 0.002\text{ mm}$). Loam (mix of sand, silt, clay, and humus) is ideal for agriculture.
  - *Structure:* Physical arrangement of soil particles into aggregates or peds (crumb, granular, platy, prismatic, blocky). Crumb structure provides optimal water retention and aeration.
- **Essential Plant Nutrients:**
  - *Macro-Nutrients:* Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), Calcium (Ca), Magnesium (Mg), Sulfur (S).
    - **Nitrogen (N):** Vegetative leaf growth, chlorophyll formation; deficiency: stunted growth, general **chlorosis (yellowing)** starting from older lower leaves.
    - **Phosphorus (P):** Root development, flower/seed formation, energy transfer (ATP); deficiency: **purplish coloration** of leaves/stems, poor root growth, delayed ripening.
    - **Potassium (K):** Stomatal regulation, enzyme activation, disease resistance; deficiency: **marginal scorching / browning** of leaf tips and margins.
    - **Magnesium (Mg):** Central metallic atom in chlorophyll; deficiency: **interveinal chlorosis** (veins remain green while leaf turns yellow).
  - *Micro-Nutrients (Trace Elements):* Iron (Fe), Manganese (Mn), Zinc (Zn), Copper (Cu), Boron (B), Molybdenum (Mo), Chlorine (Cl).
- **Organic Manures vs. Inorganic Fertilizers:**
  - *Farmyard Manure (FYM):* Decomposed animal dung, urine, and bedding straw.
  - *Compost:* Decomposed plant residues, kitchen waste, and organic matter in pits or heaps.
  - *Green Manure:* Fast-growing leguminous crops (*Mucuna, Pueraria*) ploughed into the soil while still green and succulent.
  - *Inorganic Fertilizers:* Concentrated chemical nutrients: NPK (complete), Urea ($46\%\text{ N}$), Single Superphosphate (SSP).

# SECTION 2: CROP PRODUCTION AND PLANT PROTECTION

## 3. Agronomy of Major Crops
- **Classification by Uses:**
  - Cereals: Maize (*Zea mays*), Rice (*Oryza sativa*), Guinea corn/Sorghum (*Sorghum bicolor*), Millet (*Pennisetum glaucum*). High carbohydrate content.
  - Legumes: Cowpea (*Vigna unguiculata*), Groundnut (*Arachis hypogaea*), Soybean (*Glycine max*). High protein, fix atmospheric nitrogen via root nodules (*Rhizobium*).
  - Roots & Tubers: Cassava (*Manihot esculenta*), Yam (*Dioscorea* spp.), Sweet potato (*Ipomoea batatas*). Propagated vegetatively.
  - Beverage & Cash Crops: Cocoa (*Theobroma cacao*), Oil Palm (*Elaeis guineensis*), Rubber (*Hevea brasiliensis*), Cotton (*Gossypium* spp.).
- **Cultural Practices in Crop Production:**
  - *Pre-Planting:* Site selection, clearing, stumping, plowing, harrowing, ridging, seed treatment (dressing with fungicides/insecticides).
  - *Planting:* Direct seeding vs. nursery bed transplanting (cocoa, oil palm, tomato, pepper, citrus); seed rate, planting depth, plant spacing.
  - *Post-Planting:*
    - **Thinning:** Removing excess weak seedlings from a stand to leave the vigorous ones.
    - **Supplying:** Replanting empty stands where seeds failed to germinate.
    - **Mulching:** Covering soil surface with dry plant materials to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, and regulate temperature.
    - **Staking:** Providing bamboo/wooden sticks to support climbing crops (yam, tomato) for sunlight exposure.
    - **Pruning:** Selective removal of unproductive branches or diseased shoots.

---

## 4. Weeds, Crop Pests, and Diseases
- **Weeds:** Any plant growing where it is not desired, competing with crops for light, nutrients, water, and space. Examples: Spear grass (*Imperata cylindrica*), Siam weed (*Chromolaena odorata*), Guinea grass (*Panicum maximum*), Witchweed (*Striga hermonthica* - parasitic on cereals).
- **Crop Pests:**
  - *Biting and Chewing:* Locusts, grasshoppers, armyworms, variegated grasshopper (*Zonocerus variegatus*), beetles. Destroy leaf foliage. Controlled by stomach poisons.
  - *Piercing and Sucking:* Aphids, cotton stainer (*Dysdercus* spp.), cocoa mirids/capsids (*Sahlbergella singularis*), green spider mite. Pierce tissues, suck sap, and transmit viral diseases. Controlled by **systemic insecticides**.
  - *Boring Pests:* Maize stem borer (*Busseola fusca*), bean weevil (*Callosobruchus maculatus*), rice weevil (*Sitophilus oryzae*). Bore into stems and stored grains. Controlled by fumigants.
- **Important Crop Diseases:**
  | Crop Disease | Causal Organism Type | Specific Pathogen / Vector | Diagnostic Symptoms & Control |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Black Pod of Cocoa** | Fungus | *Phytophthora palmivora* | Brown/black water-soaked lesions on pods; controlled by spraying **copper sulfate (Bordeaux mixture)** and frequent harvesting. |
  | **Cassava Mosaic Disease (CMD)** | Virus | Cassava Mosaic Virus (Vector: **Whitefly, *Bemisia tabaci*)** | Mottled, chlorotic, distorted leaves, stunted plants; controlled by planting resistant cuttings and rogueing infected plants. |
  | **Cocoa Swollen Shoot** | Virus | Cocoa Swollen Shoot Virus (Vector: **Mealybug, *Pseudococcus*)** | Swelling of twigs and roots, red vein-banding on leaves; controlled by cutting out and burning infected trees. |
  | **Maize Smut** | Fungus | *Ustilago maydis* | Large black gall-like fungal growths filled with dark sooty spores on cobs and tassels. |
  | **Groundnut Rosette** | Virus | Groundnut Rosette Virus (Vector: **Aphid, *Aphis craccivora*)** | Chlorotic, stunted, bushy rosette foliage; controlled by early planting at close spacing to deter aphids. |

# SECTION 3: ANIMAL SCIENCE, NUTRITION & VETERINARY HEALTH

## 5. Anatomy and Digestive Physiology of Farm Animals
- **Ruminants (Polygastric: Cattle, Sheep, Goats):**
  - Stomach divided into **4 distinct chambers**:
    1. **Rumen (Paunch):** First and largest compartment; lined with papillae; contains billions of symbiotic micro-organisms (bacteria and protozoa) that produce **cellulase** to digest cellulose/fiber; synthesizes Vitamin B-complex and amino acids. Food is fermented and later regurgitated.
    2. **Reticulum (Honeycomb):** Second chamber with honeycomb-like mucosal folds; collects heavy foreign objects (wire, nails) to prevent hardware disease; aids regurgitation of cud into mouth for chewing.
    3. **Omasum (Manyplies / Bible):** Third chamber filled with leaf-like muscular leaves; absorbs water, volatile fatty acids, and bicarbonate from the digesta.
    4. **Abomasum (True Stomach):** Fourth chamber; only compartment with glandular mucosal lining; secretes **gastric juice (pepsin and $\text{HCl}$)** for true chemical and enzymatic digestion of proteins and microbes.
- **Non-Ruminants (Monogastric: Pigs, Rabbits):** Single simple stomach. Rabbits practice **Coprophagy / Caecotrophy** (ingesting soft nighttime fecal pellets directly from anus to absorb microbial vitamins produced in the large caecum).
- **Poultry Digestive System:**
  - *Beak:* Captures feed; no teeth (cannot chew).
  - *Crop:* Muscular pouch at base of esophagus for temporary storage, softening, and lubrication of feed.
  - *Proventriculus (Glandular Stomach):* Secretes digestive enzymes (pepsin) and $\text{HCl}$.
  - *Gizzard (Ventriculus):* Thick, heavily muscled organ lined with a tough koilin membrane; contains swallowed **grit and small stones** that grind hard seeds and grains into a fine paste.
  - *Cloaca:* Common chamber for digestive, urinary, and reproductive tracts opening to the vent.

---

## 6. Animal Nutrition and Feeding Practices
- **Types of Rations:**
  - **Maintenance Ration:** Minimum quantity of balanced feed required to sustain the animal's basal metabolic functions and keep body weight constant without performing work or yielding production.
  - **Production Ration:** Extra balanced feed supplied **over and above maintenance requirements** to support economic output: growth/fattening, milk production (lactation), egg laying, wool growth, or foetal development during late pregnancy (**steaming up**).
- **Feed Classifications:**
  - *Roughages:* High fiber ($>18\%$), low Total Digestible Nutrients (TDN); e.g., pasture grasses (*Elephant grass, Guinea grass*), legumes (*Centrosema, Stylosanthes*), **Hay** (dried forage grass, moisture $<15\%$), **Silage** (succulent forage preserved by anaerobic fermentation in a silo).
  - *Concentrates:* Low fiber ($<18\%$), high TDN; e.g., Basal/Energy feeds (maize, sorghum, cassava meal) and Protein concentrates (soybean meal, groundnut cake - GNC, fishmeal, bloodmeal).
  - *Additives:* Bone meal (calcium/phosphorus), oyster shell, salt licks, vitamins, antibiotics.

---

## 7. Livestock Diseases and Parasites
- **Viral Diseases:**
  - **Rinderpest (Cattle Plague):** Severe fever, bloody diarrhea, necrotic sores on mucous membranes; controlled by rinderpest vaccination.
  - **Newcastle Disease (Poultry):** Viral infection characterized by respiratory distress, nervous symptoms (**twisted neck / torticollis**), paralysis, and **greenish diarrhea**; high mortality; controlled by Newcastle vaccine (NDV-i2 / Lasota).
  - **Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD):** Blisters on tongue, lips, and hooves in cloven-hoofed cattle/swine; severe salivation and lameness.
  - **Gumboro (Infectious Bursal Disease):** Attacks the bursa of Fabricius in young chicks, destroying their immune system.
- **Bacterial Diseases:**
  - **Anthrax (*Bacillus anthracis*):** Peracute zoonotic disease; causes sudden death with **dark, tarry, uncoagulated blood oozing from all natural body orifices (mouth, nose, anus)**; rigor mortis is incomplete; **carcass must never be opened/autopsied** (spores are extremely resistant and fatal to humans); bury deeply in quicklime.
  - **Brucellosis (Contagious Abortion - *Brucella abortus*):** Causes late-term abortion (5th–8th month) in pregnant cows, retained placenta, and infertility; zoonotic (causes Undulant fever in humans via raw unpasteurized milk).
- **Protozoan Diseases & Parasites:**
  - **Trypanosomiasis (Nagana in cattle):** Caused by *Trypanosoma* spp., transmitted by the **Tsetse fly (*Glossina*)**; causes intermittent fever, progressive emaciation, dullness, anemia, and death. Muturu and N'Dama cattle are trypano-tolerant.
  - **Coccidiosis (Poultry & Calves):** Caused by *Eimeria* spp.; causes droopiness, ruffled feathers, and **bloody diarrhea**; controlled by anticoccidial drugs (Amprolium, Sulfa drugs) and dry litter.
  - **Liver Fluke (*Fasciola hepatica*):** Flatworm parasite inhabiting bile ducts of sheep and cattle, causing liver rot; requires the freshwater **mud snail (*Lymnaea*)** as its essential intermediate host.
  - **Tapeworm (*Taenia solium* in pigs, *Taenia saginata* in cattle):** Primary host is human (intestines); intermediate host is pig/cow (bladder worms/cysticercus in muscle meat).

# SECTION 4: AGRICULTURAL ECONOMICS, RECORDS & MECHANIZATION

## 8. Agricultural Economics & Farm Records
- **Farm Records & Accounts:**
  - *Farm Inventory:* A complete list of all assets on the farm with their physical descriptions and monetary values at a specific date.
  - *Production Record:* Details daily agricultural output (liters of milk, crates of eggs, bags of grain harvested).
  - *Farm Budget:* Forward-looking estimate of total expected farm revenue and expenses for a future production period (Complete budget vs. Partial budget).
  - *Balance Sheet / Net Worth Statement:*
    $$\text{Net Worth (Owner's Equity)} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$$
- **Agricultural Marketing:**
  - Marketing channels: Producer $\to$ Commission Agent $\to$ Wholesaler $\to$ Retailer $\to$ Consumer.
  - Functions: Assembling, processing, grading/standardization, packaging, storage, transportation, financing, and risk-bearing.

---

## 9. Farm Mechanization & Surveying
- **Primary vs. Secondary Tillage Implements:**
  - *Primary Tillage (Ploughing):* Deep inversion, cutting, and shattering of hard soil.
    - **Disc Plough:** Fitted with rotating concave steel discs; ideal for rough, hard, stony, root-infested tropical soils; rolls over obstacles without breaking.
    - **Mouldboard Plough:** Inverts and turns over soil completely, burying weeds; suitable for loose, weed-free, stone-free soils.
  - *Secondary Tillage (Harrowing & Ridging):* Breaking down clods, leveling seedbeds, and shaping soil.
    - **Disc Harrow:** Pulverizes soil clods into fine tilth after ploughing.
    - **Ridger:** Gathers loose soil into ridges and furrows for root crop planting (cassava, yam, sweet potato).
- **Farm Surveying Instruments:**
  - *Gunter's Chain:* Measuring distances; exactly **66 feet long (20.12 meters)**, composed of **100 wire links** connected by rings (each link $= 7.92\text{ inches} = 0.66\text{ ft}$).
  - *Arrows (Survey Pins):* Steel pins used to mark the end of each chain length measured.
  - *Ranging Poles:* Wooden or metal poles painted in alternating red and white bands, used for marking stations and aligning straight survey lines.
  - *Prismatic Compass:* Measures magnetic bearings and angles.

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB AGRICULTURAL SCIENCE

1. **Ruminant Compartment Sequence:** Remember the sequence: **Rumen $\to$ Reticulum $\to$ Omasum $\to$ Abomasum**. The **Abomasum** is the only chamber that secretes true digestive enzymes ($\text{HCl}$ and pepsin).
2. **Anthrax Carcass Rule:** When a question mentions "dark uncoagulated blood oozing from anus/mouth and incomplete rigor mortis", the disease is **Anthrax**. The carcass must **NEVER be cut open** to prevent releasing lethal bacterial spores.
3. **Trypanosomiasis Tolerant Breeds:** The indigenous dwarf humpless cattle breeds of Southern Nigeria—**N'Dama** and **Muturu**—are naturally resistant (trypano-tolerant) to trypanosomiasis transmitted by the tsetse fly.
4. **Intermediate Host Triggers:** The intermediate host for Liver Fluke (*Fasciola*) is the freshwater mud snail (**Lymnaea**). If you see "bladder worm (cysticercus) in pork meat", the parasite is the pork tapeworm (**Taenia solium**).
5. **Disc Plough vs Mouldboard Plough:** Disc ploughs are designed for hard, stony, root-infested soils. Mouldboard ploughs are for soft, stone-free soils.
6. **Identify Gunter's Chain Numbers:** Gunter's chain is **66 feet long** and contains **100 links**. 10 square chains $= 1\text{ acre}$.
7. **Nutrient Deficiency Shortcuts:**
   - Nitrogen $=$ Yellowing of leaves (Chlorosis).
   - Phosphorus $=$ Purple/bronze discoloration and poor roots.
   - Potassium $=$ Leaf margins scorched and brown.
   - Magnesium $=$ Interveinal chlorosis (veins stay green).
8. **Gizzard Function in Birds:** Birds swallow small stones/grit that lodge in the muscular **gizzard** to mechanically crush and grind whole grains, substituting for teeth.
9. **Coprophagy in Rabbits:** Rabbits eat their own soft caecal night droppings directly from the anus to re-digest and absorb Vitamin B produced by caecal bacteria.
10. **Maintenance vs Production Ration:** Feed needed just to keep the animal alive and healthy without losing or gaining weight is **Maintenance Ration**. Any feed beyond this for work, milk, meat, or eggs is **Production Ration**.

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Commerce

In the UTME, Commerce is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into five main domains:
1. **Foundations of Commerce & Production (~6 Questions):** Nature, scope, and evolution of commerce, the barter system, divisions of production (primary, secondary, tertiary), and division of labour.
2. **Trade (~10 Questions):** Home trade (retail, wholesale, middleman functions, trade documents, terms of payment, and discounts) and Foreign trade (import, export, entrepôt, tariffs, trade restrictions, and balance of trade/payments).
3. **Aids to Trade (~12 Questions):** Banking and financial instruments, transport modes, warehousing, fundamental principles and types of insurance, communication, and advertising.
4. **Business Units and Business Finance (~8 Questions):** Sole proprietorships, partnerships, joint-stock companies (Private Ltd vs. Public Plc), public corporations, cooperatives, capital structure, stock exchange operations (bulls, bears, stags), shares, and debentures.
5. **Commercial Law, Consumer Protection & ICT (~4 Questions):** Law of contract, Sale of Goods Act, regulatory agencies (NAFDAC, SON, FCCPC), and e-commerce/electronic banking.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Distinctions in Commerce:**
> - Insurance principles: The principle of **indemnity** (restoring the insured to the exact financial position before the loss) applies strictly to property and fire insurance; it **NEVER applies to Life Assurance** (human life cannot be financially quantified).
> - Capital definitions: Working Capital $= \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$; Liquid Capital (Quick Ratio) $= (\text{Current Assets} - \text{Stock}) / \text{Current Liabilities}$.
> - Stock exchange speculators: **Bulls** buy expecting prices to rise; **Bears** sell expecting prices to fall; **Stags** apply for new share issues hoping to sell at a quick premium.

# SECTION 1: NATURE OF COMMERCE, PRODUCTION & TRADE

## 1. Scope of Commerce and Production
- **Definition of Commerce:** The study of all activities concerned with the distribution and exchange of goods and services, and all activities that facilitate trade (**Aids to Trade**).
  $$\text{Commerce} = \text{Trade} + \text{Aids to Trade}$$
- **The Barter System & Its Deficiencies:** Direct exchange of goods for goods without the use of money.
  - Flaws: Lack of double coincidence of wants, lack of a common measure of value, difficulty in deferred payments, indivisibility of bulky goods, and storage/perishability problems.
- **Production & Its Branches:**
  - *Primary Production (Extractive):* Extracting raw materials from nature (e.g., agriculture, mining, fishing, quarrying, forestry).
  - *Secondary Production (Manufacturing & Construction):* Transforming raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods (e.g., textile mills, oil refineries, car assembly, civil engineering).
  - *Tertiary Production (Commercial & Personal Services):* Distribution of goods and provision of direct/indirect services:
    - Commercial Services: Wholesalers, retailers, transporters, bankers, insurers, advertising agents.
    - Direct Services: Rendered directly to consumers by individuals (e.g., doctors, teachers, barbers, lawyers, domestic servants).

---

## 2. Home Trade (Retail and Wholesale)
- **Retail Trade:** Selling goods in small units directly to the final consumer.
  - *Small-Scale Retailing:* Hawkers, mobile vans, roadside kiosks, unit stores, market stalls.
  - *Large-Scale Retailing:*
    - **Departmental Stores:** Large single-building stores organized into separate departments (e.g., clothing, electronics), each headed by a departmental buyer.
    - **Multiple Shops (Chain Stores):** Networks of branches under centralized ownership and uniform branding, selling identical goods at uniform prices.
    - **Supermarkets:** Large, self-service retail shops selling food and household items.
    - **Hypermarkets:** Giant out-of-town shopping centers combining department store and supermarket inventories with expansive parking facilities.
    - **Mail-Order Business:** Retailing conducted entirely through postal delivery, catalogs, and online orders.
- **Wholesale Trade:** Buying goods in bulk from manufacturers and selling in smaller quantities to retailers.
  - *Functions to Manufacturer:* Provides prompt payment, finances production, provides market feedback, and stores bulk inventory (relieving manufacturers of warehousing costs).
  - *Functions to Retailer:* Breaks bulk, provides credit facilities, delivers goods, and stocks diverse product lines.
- **Documents Used in Home Trade:**
  - *Letter of Inquiry:* Sent by buyer asking about price, terms, and availability.
  - *Quotation / Price List:* Sent by seller stating prices and delivery terms.
  - *Order:* Formal purchase request sent by buyer.
  - *Invoice:* Bill of sale sent by seller detailing quantity, unit prices, total cost, and terms of discount.
  - *Pro-Forma Invoice:* An invoice sent in advance when prepayment is required, for customs declaration, or when goods are sent on approval.
  - *Debit Note:* Sent by seller to correct an **undercharge** on the original invoice ("debit note increases the debt").
  - *Credit Note:* Sent by seller to correct an **overcharge**, or when damaged/faulty goods are returned ("credit note reduces the debt"). Printed in red ink.
  - *Statement of Account:* Summary of transactions sent at month-end showing total balance outstanding.
- **Terms of Trade & Discounts:**
  - *Trade Discount:* Percentage deduction allowed off the catalog price by a manufacturer to a trader/wholesaler to enable them to make a profit.
  - *Cash Discount:* Incentive deduction allowed to encourage prompt payment within a stipulated time limit (e.g., "5% 10 days, net 30").

---

## 3. Foreign Trade (International Trade)
- **Divisions of Foreign Trade:**
  - *Import Trade:* Buying goods and services from other countries (Visible imports = physical goods; Invisible imports = foreign services like shipping, tourism).
  - *Export Trade:* Selling domestic goods and services to other countries.
  - *Entrepôt Trade:* Re-exporting imported goods to another country without significant alteration (handled in free ports or bonded warehouses).
- **Documents in Foreign Trade:**
  - *Indent:* An order sent to an overseas buying agent (Open Indent = agent can choose manufacturer; Closed Indent = exact brand/manufacturer specified).
  - *Bill of Lading:* The document of title to goods shipped by sea; acts as a receipt from the shipmaster, an agreement of carriage, and proof of ownership.
  - *Air Waybill:* Equivalent document for air freight (does not confer title to goods).
  - *Consular Invoice:* Certified by the importing country's consul in the exporter's country to verify prices and prevent under-invoicing for customs duty.
  - *Certificate of Origin:* Certifies the country where goods were manufactured to claim preferential tariff rates.
  - *Letter of Credit (L/C):* A financial guarantee issued by the buyer's bank promising payment to the seller upon presentation of specified shipping documents.
- **Trade Protectionism & Barriers:**
  - *Tariffs (Customs Duties):* Ad Valorem duty (percentage of product value) vs. Specific duty (fixed charge per unit/weight).
  - *Import Quotas:* Legal limit on the physical volume of specific goods that can be imported over a period.
  - *Embargo:* Total legal prohibition on the import or export of certain goods (or with specific nations).
  - *Subsidies:* Financial grants to domestic producers to make their prices competitive against foreign imports.

# SECTION 2: AIDS TO TRADE

## 4. Banking & Financial Instruments
- **Types of Banks:**
  - *Central Bank:* Apex regulatory institution (Central Bank of Nigeria - CBN); sole authority to issue legal tender, banker to the government, banker to commercial banks, lender of last resort, and manager of national foreign exchange reserves.
  - *Commercial Banks:* Profit-making financial intermediaries accepting deposits, granting loans and advances, and providing financial agency services.
  - *Merchant / Investment Banks:* Provide wholesale banking, underwriting of share issues, and long-term corporate financing.
  - *Development Banks:* Non-profit government banks providing subsidized long-term loans for strategic national sectors (e.g., Bank of Industry - BOI, Bank of Agriculture - BOA).
- **Types of Bank Accounts:**
  - *Current Account:* Allows withdrawals on demand via cheques; earns no interest; subject to maintenance charges (Commission on Turnover / Account Maintenance Fees); permits **overdraft facilities** (withdrawing more than the account balance).
  - *Savings Account:* For small savers; earns interest; withdrawals via debit cards/passbooks; no overdraft.
  - *Fixed Deposit (Time Deposit) Account:* Funds locked for a specified tenure (e.g., 90, 180, 365 days) attracting higher interest rates; premature withdrawal attracts penalty.
- **Cheques & Payment Mechanisms:**
  - *Parties to a Cheque:* **Drawer** (account holder writing the cheque), **Drawee** (the bank ordered to pay), **Payee** (person to whom payment is made).
  - *Open Cheque:* Can be cashed across the counter by anyone bearing it.
  - *Crossed Cheque:* Carries two parallel transverse lines across its face; cannot be cashed across the counter; **must be paid into a bank account**. Crossing restricts negotiability and prevents fraud.
  - *Dishonoured Cheque:* A cheque rejected by the paying bank due to: Insufficient funds (R/D - Refer to Drawer), signature mismatch, stale cheque (dated more than 6 months previously), post-dated cheque (dated ahead of current date), words and figures differing, or death/bankruptcy/insanity of drawer.

## 5. Transport and Warehousing
- **Modes of Transport & Comparative Advantages:**
  - *Road Transport:* Door-to-door delivery, flexible schedules, best for short distances and perishable agricultural products.
  - *Rail Transport:* Cheapest for bulky, heavy, non-perishable goods over long overland distances; fixed routes, high initial capital.
  - *Water Transport:* Cheapest mode for international bulk cargo (crude oil, machinery, grains). Tramp vessels (no fixed route or timetable; hired on charter party) vs. Ocean Liners (regular scheduled routes and published tariffs).
  - *Air Transport:* Fastest, most expensive; ideal for emergency supplies, perishables, and high-value lightweight luxury goods (jewelry, medicine).
  - *Pipeline Transport:* Dedicated subterranean transport of liquids and gases (crude oil, natural gas, refined petroleum); unaffected by traffic, but vulnerable to vandalism and leaks.
  - *Containerization:* Packaging cargo in standardized metal containers for seamless intermodal transfer between ships, trains, and trucks without unpacking.
- **Warehousing:**
  - Storage facility providing protection against damage, theft, and weather, stabilizing market prices by evening out seasonal supply fluctuations.
  - *Types of Warehouses:*
    - **Bonded Warehouse:** Supervised by customs authorities for storing **uncustomed/dutiable imported goods** until customs duties are paid. Goods can be processed, repackaged, or re-exported without paying duty.
    - **Public Warehouse:** Commercial storage space rented out to any business or trader on a fee basis.
    - **Private Warehouse:** Owned and operated exclusively by a single manufacturing or retail firm.

## 6. Insurance Principles and Policies
- **Core Principles of Insurance:**
  1. **Insurable Interest:** The insured must stand to suffer a direct financial loss if the insured event occurs, and benefit from its preservation. You cannot insure your neighbor's house or car!
  2. **Indemnity:** The insured is restored to their exact financial position immediately prior to the loss — **no profit can be made from insurance**.
     > [!WARNING]
     > Indemnity applies ONLY to property, fire, and marine insurance. It **never applies to Life Assurance or Personal Accident Insurance** because human life is priceless.
  3. **Utmost Good Faith (*Uberrimae Fidei*):** Both parties must disclose all material facts fully and honestly without concealment. Non-disclosure makes the policy void.
  4. **Proximate Cause:** The direct, dominant, and immediate cause of the loss must be an insured peril under the contract for compensation to be paid.
  5. **Subrogation:** Once the insurer has paid a total loss claim, they take over all legal rights, salvage, and claims against third parties responsible for the damage.
  6. **Contribution:** If property is insured against the same peril with multiple insurers, each insurer contributes only their proportionate share of the loss.
- **Types of Insurance:**
  - *Life Assurance:* Covers an event that is **certain to happen** (death). Types: **Whole Life Policy** (sum assured payable only upon death to beneficiaries); **Endowment Policy** (sum assured payable upon reaching a specified age OR upon death, whichever occurs first; serves as both protection and savings).
  - *Marine Insurance:* Hull (ship structure), Cargo (goods carried), Freight (shipping charge).
    - *General Average Loss:* Deliberate sacrifice of part of the ship or cargo (jettison) to save the entire venture in an emergency; loss is shared proportionately by all cargo owners and the shipowner.
    - *Particular Average Loss:* Accidental partial damage suffered by a specific party, borne solely by that party's insurer.

# SECTION 3: BUSINESS ORGANIZATIONS & BUSINESS FINANCE

## 7. Forms of Business Ownership
| Business Organization | Ownership & Membership | Liability of Owners | Sources of Capital | Legal Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sole Proprietorship** | 1 person | **Unlimited** (personal assets can be seized to settle business debts) | Personal savings, loans from friends/banks, trade credit | Unincorporated (No separate legal personality) |
| **Partnership** | 2 to 20 persons (up to 50 for banking/law firms) | **Unlimited** for Ordinary partners; at least 1 partner must have unlimited liability in a Limited Partnership | Contributions from partners, retained profits | Unincorporated |
| **Private Limited Company (Ltd)** | Minimum: 2, Maximum: 50 | **Limited** to amount invested or unpaid on shares | Private sale of shares (public invitation prohibited) | **Incorporated** (Separate legal entity; perpetual succession) |
| **Public Limited Company (Plc)** | Minimum: 7, Maximum: Unlimited | **Limited** | Public issue of shares and debentures via Prospectus on the Stock Exchange | Incorporated |
| **Public Corporation** | Government (Federal or State) | Limited to state funds | Government budgetary allocations, treasury grants | Incorporated by special Act of Parliament |
| **Co-operative Society** | Minimum: 10 members | Limited | Member shares, monthly dues, entrance fees | Incorporated under Co-operative Laws (Democratic: 1 man, 1 vote) |

- **Partnership Deed (Articles of Partnership):** Written agreement governing: profit/loss sharing ratio, interest on capital, partners' salaries, drawings, and procedures for admitting or dissolving partners. If no deed exists, the **Partnership Act of 1890** dictates:
  - Profits and losses shared **equally**.
  - No interest allowed on capital.
  - No partner is entitled to a salary.
  - 5% interest per annum allowed on loans advanced by partners beyond capital.
- **Key Corporate Documents:**
  - *Memorandum of Association:* Governs the company's relationship with the **outside world** (Name clause, Registered Office clause, Objects clause, Liability clause, Capital clause).
  - *Articles of Association:* Governs the **internal management** and administration of the company (voting rights, directors' powers, dividend declaration, share transfer rules).
  - *Prospectus:* Formal invitation issued by a Public Plc inviting the general public to subscribe to its shares or debentures.

## 8. Business Capital & The Stock Exchange
- **Capital Terminology:**
  - *Authorized / Nominal Capital:* Maximum share capital a company is authorized to raise by its Memorandum of Association.
  - *Issued Capital:* Portion of authorized capital actually offered to shareholders for subscription.
  - *Paid-up Capital:* Actual amount paid by shareholders on the shares issued to them.
  - *Working Capital:* Circulating capital required for day-to-day operations:
    $$\text{Working Capital} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$$
  - *Capital Employed:* Total funds actively invested in generating revenue:
    $$\text{Capital Employed} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities} = \text{Fixed Assets} + \text{Working Capital}$$
- **Shares vs. Debentures:**
  - *Ordinary Shares (Equity):* Owners of the company; carry voting rights; receive dividends only after preference shareholders; variable dividend depending on profits; paid last on winding up (risk-bearers).
  - *Preference Shares:* Fixed dividend rate; paid before ordinary shareholders; no voting rights; can be **Cumulative** (unpaid dividends accumulate and must be paid in future profitable years) or **Participating** (shares in surplus profit after ordinary dividend).
  - *Debentures:* Long-term loans (creditors, not owners); carry **fixed interest payable whether the company makes a profit or not**; secured against company assets (mortgage debenture) or unsecured (naked debenture); paid before all shareholders on liquidation.
- **The Stock Exchange (Capital Market):**
  - Market for buying and selling existing long-term securities (shares, bonds, debentures).
  - *Speculators on the Exchange:*
    - **Bull:** Speculator who buys shares expecting prices to rise, hoping to sell them at a higher price later.
    - **Bear:** Speculator who sells shares expecting prices to fall, hoping to buy them back cheaper later.
    - **Stag:** Speculator who subscribes to new public issues (IPOs) hoping that the shares will open at a premium, enabling an instant sale for profit.
    - **Broker:** Agent who executes buy and sell orders on behalf of the public for a commission (**brokerage**).
    - **Jobber:** Independent dealer who buys and sells securities on his own account, quoting two prices (bid price and offer price; difference is **jobber's turn**).

# SECTION 4: COMMERCIAL LAW, CONSUMER PROTECTION & ICT

## 9. Law of Contract & Consumer Rights
- **Essential Elements of a Valid Contract:**
  1. *Offer:* Clear, definite proposal by one party (offeror) to another (offeree).
  2. *Acceptance:* Unconditional agreement to all terms of the offer.
  3. *Consideration:* The price, value, or detriment exchanged between the parties (must be sufficient, but need not be adequate).
  4. *Intention to Create Legal Relations:* Commercial agreements presume legal intent; social/domestic agreements do not.
  5. *Capacity to Contract:* Parties must be of legal age (18+), sound mind, and not under duress or disqualification.
  6. *Legality of Object:* The purpose of the contract must not violate statutory laws or public policy.
- **Sale of Goods Act & Consumer Protection:**
  - *Caveat Emptor:* Traditional common law doctrine meaning **"Let the buyer beware"** (buyer inspects goods before purchase).
  - Modern statutory protections imply that goods sold must:
    - Match their description.
    - Be of merchantable quality.
    - Be fit for the specific purpose for which they were purchased.
  - *Consumer Regulatory Agencies in Nigeria:*
    - **FCCPC (Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission):** Prevents monopolies, investigates consumer complaints, and eliminates unfair trade practices.
    - **NAFDAC:** Regulates and controls the manufacture, importation, advertisement, distribution, and sale of food, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices, and packaged water.
    - **SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria):** Formulates and enforces quality standards for industrial, manufactured, and imported goods (awards the NIS mark of quality).

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB COMMERCE

1. **Indemnity Never Applies to Life Assurance:** When a question asks which principle does NOT apply to life assurance, the answer is **Indemnity**. You cannot place a monetary value on human life.
2. **Working Capital Calculation:** Memorize $\text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$. If Cash is ₦5,000, Stock is ₦10,000, Debtors is ₦5,000, and Creditors is ₦8,000, then Working Capital $= (5,000 + 10,000 + 5,000) - 8,000 = \text{₦12,000}$.
3. **Debit Note Increases Debt; Credit Note Reduces Debt:** If an invoice was undercharged by ₦2,000, the seller sends a **Debit Note**. If overcharged or goods are returned, the seller sends a **Credit Note**.
4. **Bulls vs Bears Mnemonic:** **Bulls** toss prices **UP** with their horns (optimists expecting price rises); **Bears** swipe prices **DOWN** with their paws (pessimists expecting price drops).
5. **Differentiate Shares from Debentures:** Shareholders are **owners** of the company and receive **dividends** (if profit is made); Debenture-holders are **creditors** of the company and receive **fixed interest** (regardless of profit or loss).
6. **Crossed Cheque vs Open Cheque:** An open cheque is paid over the counter in cash; a crossed cheque **must be lodged into a bank account** (protects against theft).
7. **Bonded Warehouse Identifier:** When you see "uncustomed goods", "dutiable goods awaiting customs clearance", or "re-export without duty", the answer is **Bonded Warehouse**.
8. **Plc vs Ltd Membership Rules:** Private Limited (Ltd) has a minimum of 2 and maximum of 50 members. Public Limited (Plc) has a minimum of 7 and **no maximum limit**.
9. **Bill of Lading is a Document of Title:** In foreign shipping, the **Bill of Lading** is the legal document proving ownership of goods aboard the ship.
10. **Pro-Forma Invoice Triggers:** A pro-forma invoice is sent when goods are sent on approval, when prepayment is demanded before shipping, or for customs valuation.

# JAMB Use of English — Comprehensive Study Note for Candidates (Jambites)

**Subject Code:** `ENG`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 60 Questions  
**Time Target:** 35 – 40 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Use of English  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Use of English

In the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME), **Use of English is compulsory for every single candidate**, regardless of their intended discipline (Medicine, Engineering, Law, Commercials, Humanities, or Education).

The examination assesses four core competencies across 60 multiple-choice questions:
1. **Comprehension and Summary (15 Questions):** Reading comprehension passages and a 10-gap register-based cloze test.
2. **Prescribed Reading Text (10 Questions):** Direct questions based on the prescribed novel/prose text announced for the examination year.
3. **Lexis and Structure (25 Questions):** Synonyms, antonyms, sentence interpretation, idiomatic expressions, grammatical concord, prepositions, and syntax.
4. **Oral Forms (10 Questions):** Vowel and consonant sound discrimination, rhymes, word stress placement, and emphatic stress.

# SECTION A: COMPREHENSION AND SUMMARY

## Topic 1: Reading Comprehension Mastery

### 1. The Anatomy of a JAMB Passage
JAMB comprehension passages are typically 250 to 450 words in length, drawn from diverse fields: science and technology, environment, ethics, governance, health, literature, or sociology. Questions evaluate:
- **Direct/Literal facts:** Information explicitly stated in the text.
- **Inference/Deduction:** Logical conclusions drawn from facts, implied rather than stated.
- **Author's Tone, Mood, and Attitude:** The writer's feelings (e.g., *critical, cynical, objective, sarcastic, laudatory, neutral, nostalgic, alarmed*).
- **Topic Sentence & Main Idea:** Identifying the core thesis of a paragraph or the entire passage.
- **Figures of Speech in Context:** Identifying metaphors, similes, irony, hyperbole, personification, and rhetorical questions within the passage.
- **Vocabulary in Context:** Explaining words or phrases as used specifically in the passage, not their generic dictionary meanings.

### 2. Strategic Step-by-Step Technique for Candidates
1. **Pre-Read the Questions (30 Seconds):** Skim the 5 question stems *before* reading the passage. This primes your brain to spot key nouns, dates, terms, and the focus of the questions.
2. **First Fast Read:** Read through the entire passage at a steady pace to grasp the main topic, the author’s stance, and paragraph progression. Do not get stuck on unfamiliar words.
3. **Locate the Evidence:** Return to the passage for each question. Highlight the sentence containing the clue.
4. **Eliminate Traps:**
   - **The "True but Irrelevant" Trap:** An option is factually correct in real life, but not mentioned or supported by the passage.
   - **The "Extreme Word" Trap:** Options containing absolute words like *always, never, completely, solely, undeniably* are almost always incorrect in inference questions.
   - **The "Opposite Meaning" Trap:** Subtle negations (*unlikely, scarcely, barely*) that reverse the meaning.

### 3. Decoding Author's Tone & Attitude
| Tone / Attitude | Characteristics & Keywords in Passage |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Objective / Neutral** | Presents balanced facts, data, and counter-arguments without emotional adjectives. |
| **Critical / Sceptical** | Questions claims, exposes weaknesses, uses words like *questionable, dubious, flawed*. |
| **Sarcastic / Ironical** | States the opposite of what is meant, uses mockery or veiled ridicule. |
| **Laudatory / Eulogistic** | Praises, commends, uses admiring language (*visionary, exemplary, remarkable*). |
| **Alarmist / Urgent** | Warns of impending danger, calls for immediate intervention (*dire, catastrophe, critical*). |

## Topic 2: The Cloze Passage (Register & Summary)

### 1. What is a Cloze Test?
The Cloze test is a continuous prose passage (usually 10 numbered blanks from 6 to 15) assessing **vocabulary register**, **contextual cohesion**, and **grammatical collocation**. A "register" refers to the specific technical vocabulary belonging to a particular human activity or field (e.g., medicine, law, aviation, maritime, journalism, parliament, agriculture, economics).

### 2. Golden Rules for Solving the Cloze Test
- **Rule 1: Read the passage completely with the blanks unfilled first.** Understanding the overarching theme (e.g., a court trial, a medical surgical procedure, or a banking audit) immediately narrows the acceptable vocabulary field.
- **Rule 2: Look forward and backward around the blank.** The clues often lie in the words immediately preceding or following the blank (collocations and prepositions).
- **Rule 3: Check Grammatical Form.** Ensure the selected word matches the tense, number, and word class of the sentence:
  - If preceded by *has/have/had*, look for a past participle.
  - If preceded by a modal auxiliary (*can, must, will*), look for a base verb.
  - If preceded by a transitive verb, look for an appropriate object.
- **Rule 4: Collocation Awareness.** Certain words habitually pair together in formal English:
  - You *commit* a crime / suicide (not *do* or *make* a crime).
  - You *make* a decision / mistake (not *take* a decision).
  - A judge *adjourns* a case; a committee *adjourns* a meeting.
  - A doctor *diagnoses* a disease and *prescribes* medication.
  - A lawyer *pleads* a case or *cross-examines* a witness.

## Topic 3: The Prescribed Reading Text (The UTME Novel)

### 1. Core Examination Requirements (10 Questions)
JAMB dedicates 10 full questions exclusively to the current officially prescribed literary prose text (e.g., *The Lekki Headmaster* by Kabir Adeniyi, or *The Life Changer* by Khadija Abubakar Jalli).

### 2. What Candidates Must Master for the Prescribed Novel:
1. **Character Profiles & Relationships:**
   - Full names, aliases, occupations, social status, and personal philosophies of major, minor, and flat characters.
   - Blood relationships, marital alliances, rivalries, and mentorships.
2. **Plot Chronology & Milestones:**
   - The inciting incident that sets the story in motion.
   - Major turning points, climaxes, and resolutions.
   - Chapter-by-chapter progression: Which event occurred in which specific setting (city, school, hospital, village)?
3. **Central Themes & Social Commentary:**
   - Societal vices tackled (corruption, drug abuse, examination malpractice, greed, tribalism).
   - Moral lessons (perseverance, integrity, forgiveness, value of education, consequences of indiscretion).
4. **Literary Techniques & Narrative Style:**
   - Narrative point of view (First person "I", Third person omniscient, or Third person limited).
   - Use of flashback (*analepsis*), foreshadowing, symbolism, proverbs, and vernacular code-switching.
5. **Exact Quotes & Dialogue Identification:**
   - Identifying *who said what to whom*, under what specific circumstance, and the immediate consequence of the statement.

## Topic 5: Antonyms (Words Opposite in Meaning)

### 1. Core Concept
Antonym questions require candidates to pick the word that conveys the **exact opposite** of the underlined word in context.

### 2. Candidate Trap Alert: The "Synonym Distractor"
JAMB examiners deliberately place the *synonym* as Option A or B to trap hasty students who forget they are looking for the *opposite*. **Always re-read the instruction header:** *In each of the following sentences, choose the option that is most nearly opposite in meaning to the underlined word.*

### 3. Key High-Frequency Antonyms Pairs in UTME
- **Alacrity** (eagerness, readiness) $\leftrightarrow$ **Reluctance / Hesitation / Apathy**
- **Candid** (frank, outspoken, truthful) $\leftrightarrow$ **Deceitful / Evasive / Guarded**
- **Dearth** (scarcity, lack) $\leftrightarrow$ **Abundance / Plethora / Glut / Surfeit**
- **Exonerate** (absolve from blame) $\leftrightarrow$ **Incriminate / Indict / Convict**
- **Furtive** (secretive, stealthy) $\leftrightarrow$ **Overt / Open / Blatant**
- **Impecunious** (poor, having little money) $\leftrightarrow$ **Affluent / Wealthy / Opulent**
- **Mitigate** (make less severe) $\leftrightarrow$ **Exacerbate / Aggravate / Intensify**
- **Parsimonious** (stingy, miserly) $\leftrightarrow$ **Extravagant / Generous / Prodigal**
- **Prodigal** (recklessly extravagant) $\leftrightarrow$ **Thrifty / Frugal / Economical**
- **Spurious** (false, fake, bogus) $\leftrightarrow$ **Genuine / Authentic / Legitimate**
- **Turbulent** (chaotic, violent) $\leftrightarrow$ **Placid / Serene / Tranquil**
- **Venerate** (regard with great respect) $\leftrightarrow$ **Despise / Scorn / Disdain**

## Topic 6: Sentence Interpretation & Idioms

### 1. Types of Sentence Interpretation Questions
These questions present a full sentence with an underlined idiom, proverb, or structural nuance, and ask you to select the interpretation that best captures its real meaning.

### 2. Essential Idioms & Figurative Expressions for UTME
- **To burn the candle at both ends:** To overwork oneself by going to bed late and waking up early.
- **To take with a grain/pinch of salt:** To view a statement with skepticism and doubt.
- **To throw in the towel:** To admit defeat or surrender.
- **To cross the Rubicon:** To take an irrevocable step that commits one to a specific course of action.
- **To beat about the bush:** To avoid the main issue by talking about irrelevant details.
- **To be in the red:** To be in debt or running a financial deficit (*in the black* means operating at a profit).
- **A flash in the pan:** A sudden, brief success that is not repeated.
- **To leave no stone unturned:** To do everything possible to achieve a goal.
- **To bury the hatchet:** To make peace and end an old feud.
- **To be born with a silver spoon in one's mouth:** To be born into a rich, privileged family.
- **To bell the cat:** To take the lead in undertaking a hazardous or difficult task.
- **To wash one's dirty linen in public:** To discuss private family/personal disputes before outsiders.

### 3. Modals and Conditionals in Sentence Interpretation
- **"He *needn't have come*":** He came, but his coming was completely unnecessary. (Action actually took place).
- **"He *didn't need to come*":** It was unnecessary for him to come, so he probably did not come.
- **"You *should have told* me":** You did not tell me, and that was a mistake or omission.
- **"If he *had studied*, he *would have passed*":** He did not study, therefore he did not pass (Third Conditional — expressing impossibility regarding a past event).

## Topic 7: Lexical and Grammatical Concord

### 1. What is Concord?
Concord is the formal agreement between the subject and the verb, or between words and their modifying pronouns/determiners in gender, number, and person.

### 2. The 12 Mandatory Rules of Concord for UTME

#### Rule 1: Basic Subject-Verb Agreement
A singular subject takes a singular verb (ending in `-s` or `-es` in the simple present tense); a plural subject takes a plural verb:
- *The boy plays.* (Singular)
- *The boys play.* (Plural)

#### Rule 2: Compound Subjects with "And"
When two or more subjects are joined by *and*, they generally take a plural verb:
- *Chidi and Aisha are attending the lecture.*
- **Exception (Singular Unity):** When two nouns joined by *and* represent a single entity, food dish, or concept, the verb is singular:
  - *Bread and butter is his favourite breakfast.*
  - *Slow and steady wins the race.*
  - *The founder and chief executive has arrived.* (One person holding both titles).

#### Rule 3: The Principle of Proximity (Alternative Subjects)
When subjects are connected by *or, nor, either... or, neither... nor, not only... but also*, the verb agrees with the subject **closest to it**:
- *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.*
- *Neither the students nor the teacher **was** present.*
- *Either you or he **has** the key.*

#### Rule 4: Quasi-Conjunctions (Accompaniment Rule)
When a subject is separated from its verb by parenthetical phrases such as *as well as, along with, together with, in conjunction with, including, accompanied by, no less than*, the verb agrees strictly with the **first subject**:
- *The principal, as well as the teachers, **is** in the auditorium.* (Singular subject: *principal*).
- *The players, together with their coach, **are** on the field.* (Plural subject: *players*).

#### Rule 5: Indefinite Pronouns
- The following indefinite pronouns are **strictly singular** and take singular verbs: *each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, no one, nobody, either, neither, one*:
  - *Each of the participants **has** submitted a report.* (NOT *have*).
  - *Everybody **wants** to succeed in the UTME.*
- Pronouns that are **strictly plural**: *both, few, several, many*:
  - *Few **were** chosen for the national scholarship.*
- Pronouns that depend on the noun in the prepositional phrase (*SANAM*: *Some, All, None, Any, Most*):
  - *Some of the milk **has** spilled.* (Uncountable $\rightarrow$ Singular).
  - *Some of the eggs **have** cracked.* (Countable plural $\rightarrow$ Plural).

#### Rule 6: Collective Nouns
Collective nouns (*jury, committee, family, audience, team, choir, police*) take a singular verb when operating as a single unit, but a plural verb when members are acting individually:
- *The committee **has** submitted its report.* (Acting as one unit).
- *The committee **are** divided on the resolution.* (Disagreement among individual members).
- **Special Case:** The word *police* is ALWAYS plural in UTME syntax:
  - *The police **are** investigating the theft.* (NOT *is*).

#### Rule 7: Expressions of Quantity, Money, Time, and Distance
Sums of money, periods of time, distances, and weights are regarded as single units of measurement and take singular verbs:
- *Ten thousand naira **is** too expensive for that book.*
- *Five miles **is** a long distance to walk daily.*
- *Three years **is** enough time to complete the diploma.*

#### Rule 8: Mathematical Operations
- *Two and two **is** (or makes) four.*
- *Ten times five **is** fifty.*

#### Rule 9: One of Those Who... (Relative Pronoun Concord)
When a relative pronoun (*who, which, that*) refers to a plural antecedent, it takes a plural verb:
- *Kalu is one of the candidates who **have** scored 300+ in the mock.* (Antecedent is *candidates*, plural).
- *Kalu is the **only one** of the candidates who **has** scored 300+.* (The phrase *the only one* restricts the subject to singular).

#### Rule 10: "More than One" Rule
The phrase *more than one* takes a singular noun and a singular verb:
- *More than one candidate **has** registered for the exam.*
- But: *More candidates than one **have** registered.*

#### Rule 11: Nouns Plural in Form but Singular in Meaning
Names of diseases (*measles, mumps, rickets*), academic subjects (*mathematics, physics, economics, statistics*), games (*billiards, darts*), and the word *news* take singular verbs:
- *Physics **is** a fascinating science.*
- *The news **was** received with great jubilation.*

#### Rule 12: Paired Nouns (Tools and Garments)
Nouns that consist of two symmetrical parts (*trousers, scissors, pliers, spectacles, shears, binoculars*) take a plural verb unless preceded by the phrase *a pair of*:
- *My scissors **are** sharp.*
- *A pair of scissors **is** on the table.*

## Topic 8: Tenses, Aspects, and Mood

### 1. The Sequence of Tenses
If the verb in the main clause is in the past tense, the verb in the subordinate clause must also be in a past tense:
- *He said that he **was** tired.* (NOT *is*).
- **Exception (Universal Truths):** When the subordinate clause expresses a permanent fact, scientific reality, or universal truth, the present tense is retained:
  - *The teacher taught that the earth **revolves** around the sun.* (NOT *revolved*).

### 2. The Subjunctive Mood (Crucial UTME Trap)
The subjunctive mood expresses a demand, wish, suggestion, or hypothetical condition. In the present subjunctive, the verb remains in its **base form (infinitive without 'to')**, regardless of the subject:
- *The committee recommended that the principal **step** down.* (NOT *steps*).
- *I insist that he **be** present at the meeting.* (NOT *is*).
- *It is imperative that she **arrive** on time.* (NOT *arrives*).

### 3. Past Subjunctive (Unreal Conditions / Wishes)
Used with *wish, as if, as though, would rather, if only*:
- *I wish I **were** a king.* (NOT *was*).
- *She talks as if she **knew** everything.*
- *I would rather you **stayed** at home today.* (Subject change after *would rather* requires past tense).

## Topic 9: Prepositions, Phrasal Verbs, and Collocations

### 1. Tricky Dependent Prepositions
- **Abide by** (a rule / decision)
- **Accuse someone of** (a crime) — but **Charge someone with** (an offense)
- **Acquitted of** (a charge)
- **Agree to** (a proposal) — but **Agree with** (a person)
- **Angry at** (a situation/thing) — but **Angry with** (a person)
- **Congratulate someone on** (an achievement) — (NOT *congratulate for*)
- **Different from** — (Standard British English; avoid *different than* or *different to*)
- **Disabuse someone of** (an illusion/misconception)
- **Dispense with** (something)
- **Divest of** (powers/property)
- **Exempt from** (tax/duty)
- **Infer from** — but **Imply by**
- **Prefer... to...** — *I prefer reading **to** watching television.* (NOT *prefer than* or *prefer more than*)
- **Prior to** (an event)
- **Reminisce about** (the past)
- **Subscribe to** (a journal / an idea)

### 2. Redundancy Traps in Preposition Usage (Words Used Without Prepositions)
Examiners love inserting prepositions where none should exist:
- **Comprise:** *The class comprises forty students.* (NEVER say *comprises of*).
- **Discuss:** *We discussed the matter.* (NEVER say *discussed about*).
- **Emphasize:** *The lecturer emphasized the point.* (NEVER say *emphasized on*).
- **Advocate:** *They advocated peace.* (NEVER say *advocated for*).
- **Demand:** *He demanded an explanation.* (NEVER say *demanded for*).
- **Request:** *She requested a form.* (NEVER say *requested for*).
- **Seek:** *They are seeking admission.* (NEVER say *seeking for*).

### 3. High-Frequency Phrasal Verbs in UTME
- **Call off:** To cancel an event (*The union called off the strike*).
- **Call on/upon:** To visit someone briefly, or formally invite someone to speak.
- **Carry out:** To execute, perform, or conduct an investigation or duty.
- **Come across:** To meet or find by chance (*I came across an old classmate*).
- **Come by:** To acquire or obtain something rare.
- **Do away with:** To abolish, eliminate, or discard.
- **Fall out:** To quarrel or disagree with someone.
- **Give in:** To surrender or yield to pressure.
- **Give up:** To abandon hope or stop trying.
- **Look forward to:** Must be followed by a **gerund (`-ing` form)** or noun: *I look forward to **meeting** you.* (NOT *to meet you*).
- **Put off:** To postpone or delay (*The match was put off due to rain*).
- **Put out:** To extinguish a flame or fire.
- **Put up with:** To tolerate or endure someone/something unpleasant.
- **Stand down:** To resign or withdraw from a position/candidacy.
- **Take after:** To resemble a parent or relative in appearance or behavior.

## Topic 10: Clauses and Sentence Architecture

### 1. Grammatical Hierarchy
$$\text{Morpheme} \rightarrow \text{Word} \rightarrow \text{Phrase} \rightarrow \text{Clause} \rightarrow \text{Sentence}$$

### 2. Identifying Grammatical Names and Functions
JAMB regularly underlines a portion of a sentence and asks for its **grammatical name** and **grammatical function**.

#### A. Noun Clause / Noun Phrase
- **Identification:** Usually introduced by *that, what, whoever, how, why, whether* or functions as an argument of a verb/preposition.
- **Functions:**
  1. *Subject of the verb:* "**What she said** shocked the audience."
  2. *Object of the verb:* "The police discovered **where the suspects hid**."
  3. *Subject complement:* "The truth is **that he failed the examination**."
  4. *Complement of a preposition:* "We are worried about **how he will survive**."
  5. *Appositive to a noun:* "The rumor, **that the university was closed**, is false."

#### B. Adjectival Clause / Adjectival Phrase
- **Identification:** Introduced by relative pronouns (*who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when*); directly describes, modifies, or qualifies a preceding noun or pronoun.
- **Function:** "Qualifies the noun..." or "Modifies the pronoun..."
  - *The boy **who won the math olympiad** received a scholarship.* $\rightarrow$ Grammatical name: *Adjectival clause*; Function: *Qualifies the noun 'boy'*.

#### C. Adverbial Clause / Adverbial Phrase
- **Identification:** Modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb by answering *When? Where? Why? How? Under what condition? To what extent?*
- **Types & Functions:**
  1. *Adverbial clause of time:* Introduced by *when, while, before, after, as soon as, since, until*. Function: *Modifies the verb...*
  2. *Adverbial clause of place:* Introduced by *where, wherever*.
  3. *Adverbial clause of reason/cause:* Introduced by *because, since, as*.
  4. *Adverbial clause of concession/contrast:* Introduced by *although, though, even though, whereas, while*.
  5. *Adverbial clause of condition:* Introduced by *if, unless, provided that, as long as*.
  6. *Adverbial clause of manner:* Introduced by *as, as if, as though*.
  7. *Adverbial clause of purpose:* Introduced by *so that, in order that, lest*.

## Topic 11: Punctuation, Capitalization & Spelling Pitfalls

### 1. Punctuation Marks
- **The Apostrophe (`'`):**
  - Shows possession: *the student's book* (singular), *the students' books* (plural), *children's toys* (irregular plural).
  - Shows contraction: *don't, it's* (it is / it has).
  - **Trap:** The possessive pronoun **its** has NO apostrophe (*The cat licked its paws*). **It's** always means *it is* or *it has*.
- **The Semicolon (`;`):**
  - Separates closely related independent clauses not joined by a coordinating conjunction: *I studied diligently for the mock; my score reflected the effort.*
- **The Colon (`:`):**
  - Introduces a formal list, an explanation, or an elaboration: *He needs three items: a pen, a ruler, and an eraser.*

## Topic 12: Vowel Sounds (Monophthongs and Diphthongs)

English has **20 vowel sounds**: 12 Pure Vowels (Monophthongs) and 8 Diphthongs.

### 1. The 12 Pure Vowels (Monophthongs)

#### Short Vowels (7)
1. **/ɪ/** — *p**i**t, b**i**d, b**u**sy, w**o**men, m**y**th*

### 1. Classification by Voicing
- **Voiced Consonants (vocal cords vibrate):** /b, d, g, v, ð, z, ʒ, dʒ, m, n, ŋ, l, r, w, j/

### 2. Tricky Consonant Identifications
- **/θ/** (Voiceless dental fricative — "thin"): *think, path, cloth, breath, author, theme*.

## Topic 14: Rhymes and Homophones

### 1. Rhyme Patterns
Words rhyme when their final vowel and following consonant sounds are **phonetically identical**, regardless of how they are spelled:
- *Bait* rhymes with *gate, straight, weight, late*.
- *Tough* /tʌf/ rhymes with *rough, cuff, stuff* (NOT with *cough* /kɒf/ or *plough* /plaʊ/).
- *Through* /θruː/ rhymes with *threw, blue, shoe, flew*.
- *Corps* /kɔː/ rhymes with *core, tore, boar, shore* (The 'ps' in military *corps* is completely silent!).
- *Bury* /ˈberi/ rhymes with *berry, marry, ferry*.

### 2. Homophones
Homophones are words that sound exactly the same but have different spellings and meanings:
- *Altar* (place of sacrifice) $\leftrightarrow$ *Alter* (to change)
- *Bare* (uncovered) $\leftrightarrow$ *Bear* (animal / to carry)
- *Cereal* (grain) $\leftrightarrow$ *Serial* (sequential)
- *Cite* (quote) $\leftrightarrow$ *Site* (location) $\leftrightarrow$ *Sight* (vision)
- *Compliment* (praise) $\leftrightarrow$ *Complement* (complete something)
- *Council* (administrative group) $\leftrightarrow$ *Counsel* (advice / legal advocate)
- *Discreet* (tactful, careful) $\leftrightarrow$ *Discrete* (distinct, separate)
- *Principal* (school head / main) $\leftrightarrow$ *Principle* (fundamental truth / moral rule)
- *Stationary* (motionless) $\leftrightarrow$ *Stationery* (writing materials: pens, paper)

## Topic 15: Word Stress (Syllabic Accentuation)

Stress is the relative emphasis or prominence given to a certain syllable in a word by saying it louder, higher, and longer.

### 1. Noun-Verb Stress Shift (Disyllabic Words)
In words of two syllables that function as both nouns/adjectives and verbs:
- **The NOUN / ADJECTIVE takes stress on the FIRST syllable:**
  - `CON-duct` (noun)
  - `EX-port` (noun)
  - `OB-ject` (noun)
  - `PRO-duce` (noun)
  - `RE-cord` (noun)
  - `SUB-ject` (noun)
- **The VERB takes stress on the SECOND syllable:**
  - `con-DUCT` (verb)
  - `ex-PORT` (verb)
  - `ob-JECT` (verb)
  - `pro-DUCE` (verb)
  - `re-CORD` (verb)
  - `sub-JECT` (verb)

### 2. Suffix Rules Governing Word Stress

#### Rule A: Suffixes that Pull Stress to the Penultimate Syllable (Second to the last syllable)
Words ending in the following suffixes always place primary stress on the syllable **immediately before the suffix**:
- **`-tion` / `-sion`:** *edu-`CA`-tion, prepa-`RA`-tion, com-`PLE`-xion, divi-`SI`-on*
- **`-ic` / `-ical`:** *fan-`TAS`-tic, eco-`NO`-mic, demo-`CRA`-tic, his-`TO`-rical*
- **`-ity`:** *elec-`TRI`-city, sim-`PLI`-city, abili-`TY`*
- **`-ian`:** *musi-`CI`-an, politi-`CI`-an, electri-`CI`-an*
- **`-ious` / `-eous`:** *cou-`RA`-geous, infec-`TI`-ous, deli-`CI`-ous*

#### Rule B: Suffixes that Pull Stress to the Antepenultimate Syllable (Third from the last syllable)
Words of three or more syllables ending in these suffixes take stress on the **third syllable from the end**:
- **`-ate`:** *com-`MU`-nicate, `CON`-template, in-`VES`-tigate*
- **`-ify`:** *`SPE`-cify, `CLAR`-ify, i-`DEN`-tify*
- **`-ise` / `-ize`:** *`CRIT`-icise, `APOL`-ogize, `MOD`-ernize*
- **`-ous` (polysyllabic):** *`MON`-strous, a-`NON`-ymous, con-`TIN`-uous*
- **`-cy`, `-phy`, `-gy`:** *de-`MOC`-racy, pho-`TO`-graphy, bi-`OL`-ogy*

#### Rule C: Stress-Absorbing Suffixes (Suffix carries the primary stress itself)
- **`-ee`:** *refu-`GEE`, employ-`EE`, trai-`NEE`*
- **`-eer`:** *engi-`NEER`, volun-`TEER`*
- **`-esque`:** *pictur-`ESQUE`*
- **`-ique`:** *tech-`NIQUE`, cri-`TIQUE`*

#### Rule D: Stress-Neutral Suffixes (Suffix does NOT change the root word stress)
Suffixes like `-able, -al, -ful, -less, -ness, -ment, -ly, -ish, -hood` leave the stress on the base word:
- `HAP`-py $\rightarrow$ `HAP`-pi-ness
- `GOV`-ern $\rightarrow$ `GOV`-ern-ment

## Topic 16: Emphatic Stress & Intonation

### 1. How Emphatic Stress is Tested in JAMB
JAMB gives you a sentence where **ONE WORD IS CAPITALIZED**. You are asked to choose the question that the given sentence answers.

### 2. The Golden Inversion Law for Emphatic Stress
> **Rule:** The correct question **MUST CONTRADICT** the capitalized word, while keeping all other information in the sentence identical!

#### Example 1:
- *Given Statement:* "Musa bought a **RED** car yesterday."
- *Analysis:* The capitalized word is **RED**. The correct question must question the color *red* with an alternative color.
- *Option A:* Did Musa sell a red car yesterday? (Contradicts *bought* — Wrong).
- *Option B:* Did John buy a red car yesterday? (Contradicts *Musa* — Wrong).
- *Option C:* Did Musa buy a **blue** car yesterday? (**CORRECT** — Contradicts the capitalized word *red*).
- *Option D:* Did Musa buy a red car today? (Contradicts *yesterday* — Wrong).

#### Example 2:
- *Given Statement:* "The TEACHER punished the student."
- *Correct Question:* "Did the **principal** punish the student?" (Contradicts the capitalized word *TEACHER*).

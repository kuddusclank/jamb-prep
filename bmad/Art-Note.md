## Overview & How JAMB Tests Visual Arts

In the UTME, Art is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major segments:
1. **Elements & Principles of Design (~8 Questions):** The visual elements (line, shape, form, space, color theory, value, texture) and foundational design principles (balance, harmony, contrast, rhythm, proportion, dominance/emphasis, unity).
2. **Tools, Materials & Studio Techniques (~12 Questions):** Drawing and linear/aerial perspective, painting media and techniques (watercolor, oil, impasto), sculpture processes (carving, modeling, cire-perdue casting), ceramics and pottery stages, printmaking, graphics, and textile techniques (batik, adire, weaving).
3. **Traditional African Art Traditions (~12 Questions):** Nok terracottas, Igbo-Ukwu bronzes, Ife portrait heads, Benin court brasses, Esie stone carvings, Owo art, and masquerade traditions.
4. **Modern & Contemporary Nigerian Art (~8 Questions):** Pioneer Nigerian artists (Aina Onabolu, Akinola Lasekan), the **Zaria Art Society ("Zaria Rebels")** and the philosophy of **Natural Synthesis**, renowned contemporary masters (Ben Enwonwu, Bruce Onobrakpeya, Yusuf Grillo, Demas Nwoko), and cultural institutions.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Visual Identifiers in Traditional Nigerian Art:**
> - **Nok Art:** Distinct **triangular or oval-shaped pierced/perforated eyes and pupils**, flared nostrils, open lips, and elaborate hairdos.
> - **Ife Art:** **Realistic, naturalistic** human portrait heads, serene expressions, with delicate **vertical facial striations (grooves)**.
> - **Benin Art:** Stylized royal court brasses, heavy ceremonial coral bead collars reaching to the mouth, and the **Queen Mother Idia Ivory Mask** (FESTAC '77 symbol).
> - **Igbo-Ukwu Art:** Intricate leaded bronze castings decorated with tiny insects, frogs, and **filigree roped motifs** (e.g., the roped bronze pot).

# SECTION 1: ELEMENTS AND PRINCIPLES OF DESIGN

## 1. The Visual Elements of Art
- **Line:** A mark made by a moving point; defines contours, outlines, and creates direction, texture, and movement. (Horizontal = rest; Vertical = dignity/stability; Diagonal = dynamic action).
- **Shape (2D) vs. Form (3D):**
  - *Shape:* Two-dimensional area defined by boundaries (height and width only); geometric (circles, squares) or organic/free-form.
  - *Form:* Three-dimensional object having volume and mass (**height, width, and depth**).
- **Space:** The area within, around, and between objects. **Positive Space** is the main subject/object; **Negative Space** is the unoccupied surrounding background.
- **Value (Tone):** The lightness or darkness of a color. A **Tint** is created by adding white to a color; a **Shade** is created by adding black. **Chiaroscuro** is the dramatic contrast between light and dark to create 3D volume.
- **Texture:** The surface quality of an object. *Tactile Texture* (felt by physical touch) vs. *Visual / Implied Texture* (illusion of texture depicted on a 2D surface).
- **Color Theory:**
  - *Primary Colors (Pigment):* Cannot be produced by mixing other colors: **Red, Yellow, Blue**.
  - *Secondary Colors:* Produced by mixing two primary colors in equal proportions:
    $$\text{Red} + \text{Yellow} = \mathbf{Orange}$$
    $$\text{Blue} + \text{Yellow} = \mathbf{Green}$$
    $$\text{Red} + \text{Blue} = \mathbf{Violet / Purple}$$
  - *Tertiary / Intermediate Colors:* Formed by mixing an adjacent primary and secondary color (e.g., Red-Orange, Yellow-Green).
  - *Complementary Colors:* Colors directly opposite each other on the color wheel; create intense contrast when placed side-by-side:
    - **Red & Green**
    - **Blue & Orange**
    - **Yellow & Violet**
  - *Color Temperature:* **Warm Colors** (Red, Orange, Yellow — advance toward the viewer); **Cool Colors** (Blue, Green, Violet — recede into the distance).
  - *Monochromatic Scheme:* Variations of a single hue using different tints, shades, and tones.

## 2. Principles of Design
- **Balance:** Distribution of visual weight in an artwork:
  - *Symmetrical (Formal) Balance:* Identical elements mirrored on both sides of a central axis.
  - *Asymmetrical (Informal) Balance:* Different visual elements on either side that achieve visual equilibrium without mirroring.
  - *Radial Balance:* Elements radiate outward from a central focal point like bicycle wheel spokes.
- **Harmony & Unity:** The pleasing combination of related visual elements creating a cohesive, complete sense of oneness in an artwork.
- **Contrast:** The juxtaposition of strongly differing elements (light vs. dark, rough vs. smooth, large vs. small) to prevent monotony.
- **Dominance / Emphasis:** Highlighting a specific focal point in a composition to command the viewer's primary attention.
- **Rhythm & Movement:** The regular repetition of visual elements to lead the viewer's eye smoothly across the composition.
- **Proportion:** The comparative scale and size relationship of parts to each other and to the whole (e.g., the human head is approximately $\frac{1}{7}$th or $\frac{1}{8}$th of total adult body height).

# SECTION 2: STUDIO TECHNIQUES, MEDIA AND CRAFTS

## 3. Drawing, Painting, and Perspective
- **Linear Perspective (Filippo Brunelleschi):**
  - Mathematical system creating the illusion of 3D depth on a flat 2D surface.
  - Key components: **Horizon Line** (eye level of the observer) and **Vanishing Point** (the point on the horizon where parallel lines appear to converge).
  - *One-Point Perspective:* Parallel lines converge at a single vanishing point (e.g., looking down a straight railway track).
  - *Two-Point Perspective:* Objects viewed from an angle; lines converge at two separate vanishing points on the horizon.
- **Aerial / Atmospheric Perspective:** Objects situated in the far distance appear hazier, lighter in tone, less detailed, and bluer due to atmospheric scattering of light.
- **Painting Media & Techniques:**
  - *Watercolor:* Transparent water-soluble pigment bound with **gum arabic**; washed quickly on textured paper; light tones achieved by diluting with water, not white paint.
  - *Gouache:* Opaque watercolor containing chalk or precipitated barium sulfate; dries with a flat, matte, non-reflective finish.
  - *Oil Paint:* Pigments bound with **linseed oil**; thinned with **turpentine**; dries slowly; allows blending.
  - *Acrylic Paint:* Synthetic acrylic polymer resin emulsion; water-soluble when wet, but dries rapidly into a waterproof, flexible plastic film.
  - *Impasto Technique:* Applying thick, heavy, textured paint onto canvas with a palette knife or stiff bristle brush, leaving visible sculptural tool marks.

## 4. Sculpture and Ceramics
- **Sculpture Processes:**
  - *Subtractive Process:* Carving away excess material from a solid block of wood, stone, marble, or plaster.
  - *Additive Process:* Building up form using pliable materials: modeling with wet clay, wax, paper-mâché, or welding metal.
  - *Armature:* An internal structural skeleton of wire, metal, or wood supporting the weight of wet modeling clay during sculpture construction.
  - *The Cire-Perdue (Lost-Wax) Casting Process:*
    1. A clay core is sculpted and covered with a detailed layer of **beeswax**.
    2. Metal runner channels and air vents are attached.
    3. The wax is covered with successive coats of fine investment clay slurry to form a solid mould.
    4. The mould is baked in a kiln; the wax melts and drains out (**lost wax**), leaving an empty hollow cavity.
    5. Molten bronze or brass is poured into the cavity.
    6. When cooled, the outer clay mould is broken open to reveal the solid bronze sculpture.
- **Ceramics and Pottery:**
  - *Hand-Building Methods:* **Pinch Pot** (pressing thumb into a ball of clay), **Coiling** (stacking rolled clay ropes), **Slab Method** (joining flattened sheets of clay).
  - *Throwing:* Shaping clay symmetrically on a rotating **potter's wheel**.
  - *Stages of Clay Dryness:*
    - **Plastic State:** Wet, soft, moldable.
    - **Leather-Hard State:** Partially dried, firm like cheese; ideal for carving patterns, trimming foot rings, and attaching handles with **slip** (liquid clay glue).
    - **Bone-Dry / Greenware:** Completely air-dried, fragile, ready for the first firing.
    - **Bisque / Biscuit Firing:** First firing in a kiln at $900^\circ - 1,000^\circ\text{C}$ to vitrify clay into porous permanent ceramic.
    - **Glazing & Glaze Firing:** Applying powdered silica and metallic oxides, then refiring at high temperature to form an impervious, glossy glass coating.

## 5. Graphics, Printmaking, and Textiles
- **Typography and Graphic Design:**
  - *Serif Fonts:* Characters having small decorative finishing strokes / feet at the ends of letters (e.g., Times New Roman).
  - *Sans-Serif Fonts:* Clean, modern characters without decorative finishing feet (e.g., Arial, Helvetica).
  - *Calligraphy:* The art of beautiful, expressive hand lettering using broad-edged pens or brushes.
- **Printmaking Methods:**
  - *Relief Printing:* The raised surface carries the ink; the non-image areas are carved away (e.g., **Woodcut, Linocut**).
  - *Intaglio Printing:* The design is incised or etched into metal plates below the surface; ink is forced into grooves (e.g., **Etching, Engraving**).
  - *Serigraphy (Screen Printing):* Stencil printmaking using a fine mesh screen (silk/polyester) stretched on a wooden frame; ink is pressed through open mesh areas using a rubber **squeegee**.
- **Textile Crafts:**
  - *Tie and Dye (Adire Oniko):* Fabric is tied with raffia or string, folded, or pleated before dipping into an indigo dye bath.
  - *Batik:* Fabric is painted or stamped with **melted wax** (resist), dyed in cold water dye, and boiled to remove wax.
  - *Adire Eleko:* Traditional Yoruba paste-resist textile using **cassava starch paste (*lafun*)** painted through stencils or freehand with feathers onto fabric.
  - *Weaving:* Interlacing two sets of threads at right angles: **Warp** (longitudinal lengthwise threads held in tension) and **Weft / Woof** (transverse crosswise threads woven across). Examples: **Aso-Oke** (Yoruba narrow strip loom) and **Akwete** (Igbo broad vertical loom).

# SECTION 3: TRADITIONAL AFRICAN ART HISTORY

## 6. Classical Nigerian Art Traditions
- **The Nok Culture (c. 900 BC – 200 AD):**
  - Oldest terracotta sculptures in Sub-Saharan Africa, discovered near Nok village in Kaduna State.
  - **Key Diagnostic Traits:**
    - Distinct **triangular or oval pierced eyes, pupils, nostrils, and ears**.
    - Flared nostrils, open expressive lips.
    - Elaborate braided, tiered, and beaded coiffures.
    - Broken hollow terracotta figures depicting human heads, monkeys, and elephants.
- **Igbo-Ukwu (9th Century AD):**
  - Located in Anambra State; excavated by British archaeologist **Thurstan Shaw** in 1959.
  - **Key Diagnostic Traits:**
    - High-level metallurgy: sophisticated leaded bronze casting using cire-perdue.
    - Masterpiece: **The Roped Bronze Pot** (a vessel enclosed within a delicate netting of simulated rope).
    - Ornate surface decoration featuring geometric bands, spirals, insects (crickets, flies, beetles), and snakes.
    - Over 165,000 glass beads, ivory tusks, and regalia associated with the sacred **Eze Nri** monarchy.
- **Ile-Ife Art (11th – 15th Century):**
  - Classical Yoruba art centered around the spiritual home of the Ooni.
  - **Key Diagnostic Traits:**
    - Supreme **idealized naturalism / realism** with serene facial composure.
    - Lifelike proportions of the human face.
    - **Delicate parallel vertical striations (scarification lines)** running down the cheeks and forehead.
    - Holes around the hairline, chin, and lower jaw used to attach real hair beards and beaded crowns.
    - Media: Terracotta and brass/copper alloy heads (*cire-perdue*). Famous masterpiece: The **Olokun Head**.
- **Benin Court Art (14th – 19th Century):**
  - Royal court art made exclusively for the palace of the **Oba of Benin** by the hereditary royal brass-casters guild (**Iguneronmwon**).
  - **Key Diagnostic Traits:**
    - Commemorative portrait brass heads of deceased Obas placed on royal ancestral altars.
    - Stylized depiction with large eyes, heavy features, and **beaded high coral collars (*odigba*)** extending from neck up to the lower lip.
    - Retangular bronze relief plaques depicting warriors, Portuguese soldiers, and court ceremonies that once lined wooden pillars of the palace.
    - **The 16th-Century Ivory Pendant Mask of Queen Mother Idia:** Worn at the waist of the Oba; carved with miniature Portuguese heads and mudfish along the tiara; adopted as the official emblem of **FESTAC '77**.
- **Esie Soapstone Carvings (Kwara State):**
  - Discovered in 1933; the largest collection of stone sculptures in Africa (over 800 figures carved from soft **steatite / soapstone**), depicting seated men and women playing instruments and holding weapons.

# SECTION 4: MODERN & CONTEMPORARY NIGERIAN ART

## 7. Pioneers and the Zaria Art Society
- **Pioneers of Modern Nigerian Art:**
  - **Aina Onabolu (1882–1963):** Universally acknowledged as the **"Father of Modern Nigerian Art"**. Self-taught portraitist who proved that Africans could master European perspective, anatomy, and academic realism without white supervision. Successfully lobbied the colonial administration to introduce formal art education into Nigerian secondary schools, becoming the first indigenous art master at King's College, Lagos.
  - **Akinola Lasekan (1916–1972):** Pioneer portrait painter and renowned political cartoonist for Nnamdi Azikiwe's *West African Pilot*, using art as a potent ideological weapon against colonial oppression.
  - **Chief Justus Akeredolu:** Pioneered miniature realistic **thorn carvings** using wild cotton-tree thorns in Nigeria.
- **The Zaria Art Society ("The Zaria Rebels", 1958):**
  - Formed in 1958 by student artists at the Nigerian College of Arts, Science and Technology (now ABU, Zaria) in protest against Eurocentric academic curricula.
  - Key Founders: **Uche Okeke, Bruce Onobrakpeya, Demas Nwoko, Yusuf Grillo, Simon Okeke, Felix Ekeada, and Oseloka Osadebe**.
  - **Core Ideology: "Natural Synthesis":** The conscious, deliberate fusion of the best elements of traditional Nigerian cultural heritage, folklore, and materials with valid Western technical methods, rejecting slavish imitation of European styles.
- **Contemporary Nigerian Masters:**
  - **Ben Enwonwu (1917–1994):** Celebrated painter and sculptor; merged African aesthetics with Western technique. Commissioned by Queen Elizabeth II to sculpt her bronze portrait (1957). Masterpiece: **"Anyanwu" (The Awakening)** at the National Museum, Lagos; painted the iconic *"Tutu"* portrait series.
  - **Bruce Onobrakpeya (b. 1932):** Master printmaker, painter, and sculptor; pioneer of **Plastography** (printmaking using plastic relief surfaces), metal foil relief, and deep etching based on Urhobo folklore and Christian mythology.
  - **Yusuf Grillo (1934–2021):** Master painter; renowned for stylized, elongated figures depicted in cool harmonic palettes of **deep blues and purples**; celebrated for stained glass and mosaic murals.
  - **Demas Nwoko (b. 1935):** Architect, sculptor, and theater designer; pioneer of "New Culture" architecture utilizing local laterite and terracotta bricks.
  - **Uche Okeke (1933–2016):** Master artist who pioneered the modern aesthetic adaptation of traditional Igbo **Uli** body and wall motifs (delicate, lyrical, minimalist linear designs).

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB ART

1. **Nok Art Pierced Eye Traps:** Whenever you see "triangular eyes with pierced or perforated pupils and nostrils", the answer is **Nok Art**.
2. **Ife Facial Striations:** Ife heads are characterized by supreme **naturalism / realism** and parallel **vertical facial striations (grooves)**.
3. **Queen Mother Idia Ivory Mask:** The iconic 16th-century ivory mask adopted as the official emblem of **FESTAC '77** is from **Benin Court Art**, representing Queen Mother **Idia**.
4. **Father of Modern Nigerian Art:** The "Father of Modern Nigerian Art" is **Aina Onabolu**, who introduced formal art education to Nigeria.
5. **Natural Synthesis Ideology:** "Natural Synthesis" was the philosophy of the **Zaria Art Society (1958)**, formulated by artists like **Uche Okeke** and **Bruce Onobrakpeya**.
6. **Igbo-Ukwu Roped Bronze Pot:** The excavated 9th-century bronze casting of a pot encased in a decorative rope net was discovered at **Igbo-Ukwu** by **Thurstan Shaw**.
7. **Impasto Painting Definition:** Applying thick, unthinned paint with visible raised texture using a palette knife or stiff brush is called **Impasto**.
8. **Plastography Invention:** The specialized printmaking technique called **Plastography** was invented by Nigerian master artist **Bruce Onobrakpeya**.
9. **Complementary Color Pairs:** Red & Green, Blue & Orange, Yellow & Purple. Placing complementary colors side by side produces the highest visual contrast.
10. **Bisque Firing:** The very first firing of unglazed bone-dry clay (greenware) in a kiln is called **Bisque (Biscuit) Firing**.

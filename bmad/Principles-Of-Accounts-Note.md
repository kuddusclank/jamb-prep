## Overview & How JAMB Tests Principles of Accounts

In the UTME, Principles of Accounts is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into five main domains:
1. **Conceptual Framework & Double Entry (~8 Questions):** Accounting cycle, accounting concepts and conventions, source documents, books of original entry, classification of accounts, ledger postings, and trial balance.
2. **Financial Statements & Adjustments (~10 Questions):** Trading, Profit & Loss Account (Income Statement), Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position), accruals, prepayments, bad debts, provisions for doubtful debts, and methods of calculating depreciation.
3. **Control Accounts, Correction of Errors & Bank Reconciliation (~8 Questions):** Sales and purchases ledger control accounts, suspense accounts, errors that do and do not affect the trial balance, adjusted cash book, and bank reconciliation statements.
4. **Special Accounts (~10 Questions):** Manufacturing accounts (prime cost, factory overheads), non-profit organizations (receipts & payments, income & expenditure, accumulated fund), partnership accounts (appropriation, goodwill, dissolution), and limited liability company accounts.
5. **Accounting Ratios & Public Sector Accounting (~4 Questions):** Profitability ratios, liquidity ratios, efficiency ratios, sources of government revenue, and the Consolidated Revenue Fund.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Equations in Principles of Accounts:**
> - Fundamental Accounting Equation: $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liabilities} \implies \text{Capital} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}$.
> - Cost of Goods Sold ($\text{COGS}$): $\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Purchases} + \text{Carriage Inwards} - \text{Returns Outwards} - \text{Closing Stock}$.
> - Incomplete Records Profit: $\text{Closing Capital} + \text{Drawings} - \text{Capital Introduced} - \text{Opening Capital}$.
> - Straight-Line Depreciation: $\frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual/Scrap Value}}{\text{Useful Life (Years)}}$.

# SECTION 1: ACCOUNTING CONCEPTS, DOUBLE ENTRY & TRIAL BALANCE

## 1. Accounting Concepts and Conventions
- **Business Entity Concept:** The business enterprise is treated as an independent legal and economic entity, completely separate from its owner(s). The owner's personal transactions must never be mixed with the firm's transactions.
- **Going Concern Concept:** Assumes that the business will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future without the intention or necessity of liquidation.
- **Historical Cost Concept:** Assets are recorded and shown in the balance sheet at their original cost of acquisition, rather than their current estimated market or replacement values.
- **Accrual / Matching Concept:** Revenues and expenses are recognized and matched in the financial period in which they are incurred or earned, regardless of when cash is actually paid or received.
- **Prudence / Conservatism Concept:** Revenue and profits are not anticipated; they are recognized only when realized. However, provision must be made for all known potential losses and liabilities. (Inventory is valued at the **lower of cost and net realizable value**).
- **Consistency Concept:** The same accounting methods, policies, and procedures must be applied consistently from one financial period to another (e.g., using straight-line depreciation continuously).
- **Periodicity Concept:** The economic life of a business is divided into regular artificial time intervals (usually 12 months) for financial reporting.
- **Materiality Concept:** Financial statements should disclose items that are significant enough to influence the decisions of users. Insignificant or trivial items (e.g., a wastebasket or stapler) can be expensed immediately rather than capitalized as non-current assets.
- **Money Measurement Concept:** Only transactions and events capable of being measured and expressed in monetary terms are recorded in the accounting records.
- **Dual Aspect (Duality) Concept:** Every transaction has a two-fold effect: every debit entry must have an equal and corresponding credit entry.

## 2. Double Entry System & Books of Original Entry
- **Rules of Double Entry:**
  - *Personal Accounts (Debtors & Creditors):* **Debit the receiver, Credit the giver**.
  - *Real Accounts (Physical & Intangible Assets):* **Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out**.
  - *Nominal Accounts (Expenses, Incomes, Gains & Losses):* **Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains**.
- **Source Documents & Subsidiary Books (Journals):**
  | Source Document | Book of Original Entry | Nature of Transactions Recorded |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Sales Invoice** | Sales Day Book (Sales Journal) | Credit sales of trading inventory |
  | **Purchases Invoice** | Purchases Day Book (Purchases Journal) | Credit purchases of trading inventory |
  | **Credit Note Issued** | Returns Inwards Book (Sales Returns) | Goods returned by customers |
  | **Debit Note Sent** | Returns Outwards Book (Purchases Returns) | Goods returned to suppliers |
  | **Receipts / Payment Vouchers** | Cash Book | All cash and bank receipts and payments |
  | **Petty Cash Vouchers** | Petty Cash Book | Minor, small-value cash expenses (under the Imprest System) |
  | **Journal Vouchers** | General Journal (Journal Proper) | Non-routine transactions: purchase/sale of non-current assets on credit, opening entries, correction of errors |

- **The Cash Book:**
  - *Two-Column Cash Book:* Contains separate columns for **Cash** and **Bank**.
  - *Three-Column Cash Book:* Adds columns for **Discount Allowed** (Debit side — expense) and **Discount Received** (Credit side — income).
  - *Contra Entry:* A transaction involving both cash and bank accounts simultaneously (e.g., cash deposited into bank or cash withdrawn from bank for office use). Indicated by a letter **"C"** in the folio column; does not require posting to a ledger.
  - *Petty Cash Imprest System:* A fixed cash sum (**Float / Imprest amount**) is allocated to the petty cashier. At the end of the period, the chief cashier reimburses the exact total amount spent, restoring the petty cash balance back to the original float.

---

## 3. The Trial Balance & Correction of Errors
- **Trial Balance:** A schedule of all debit and credit ledger account balances extracted at a specific date to test the arithmetic accuracy of double-entry postings.
- **Errors that DO NOT Affect Trial Balance Agreement (Both totals remain equal):**
  1. *Error of Omission:* A transaction is completely omitted from both books of original entry and the ledgers.
  2. *Error of Commission:* An entry is posted to the correct side of the correct class of account, but to the **wrong person's account** (e.g., debited to Ade's account instead of Ayo's account).
  3. *Error of Principle:* A transaction violates fundamental accounting principles (e.g., treating capital expenditure as revenue expenditure — debiting repairs to the Motor Vehicle asset account).
  4. *Error of Original Entry:* An incorrect initial figure is entered in the journal and posted to both accounts in the ledger (e.g., ₦5,200 recorded and posted as ₦2,500).
  5. *Compensating Error:* An error on the debit side is accidentally offset by an equal error on the credit side.
  6. *Error of Complete Reversal of Entries:* Both accounts receive entries, but on the wrong sides (e.g., payment to a creditor debited to Cash and credited to Creditor).
- **Errors that DO Affect Trial Balance Agreement:**
  - One-sided omission, mathematical miscasting (under-adding or over-adding an account), posting different figures to debit and credit sides, or posting to the same side twice.
  - **Suspense Account:** A temporary holding account opened to absorb the difference in the trial balance totals while investigations are conducted. Once errors are located, they are corrected via the General Journal, and the Suspense Account closes out to zero.

# SECTION 2: BANK RECONCILIATION & FINANCIAL ADJUSTMENTS

## 4. Bank Reconciliation Statements
- **Purpose:** To explain and reconcile discrepancies between the balance shown in the firm's **Cash Book (Bank column)** and the balance shown on the **Bank Statement** issued by the bank at a specific date.
- **Causes of Discrepancies:**
  - *Items in Cash Book but NOT YET on Bank Statement:*
    - **Uncredited Lodgements / Cheques:** Cheques received and entered on the debit side of the cash book, but not yet cleared or credited by the bank.
    - **Unpresented Cheques:** Cheques issued to suppliers and credited in the cash book, but not yet presented at the bank for payment.
  - *Items on Bank Statement but NOT YET in Cash Book:*
    - Bank charges, Commission on Turnover (COT), interest on overdraft.
    - Direct debits and Standing Orders (e.g., insurance premiums paid automatically).
    - Dishonoured cheques (returned unpaid by the bank due to lack of funds).
    - Direct credit transfers / lodgements by customers directly into the bank account.
- **Two-Step Reconciliation Procedure:**
  1. **Update the Cash Book (Adjusted Cash Book):** Enter all items found on the bank statement that were omitted from the cash book (credit charges, debit direct lodgements).
  2. **Prepare Bank Reconciliation Statement:**
     $$\text{Balance as per Adjusted Cash Book}$$
     $$\mathbf{Add:} \text{ Unpresented Cheques}$$
     $$\mathbf{Less:} \text{ Uncredited Lodgements}$$
     $$\mathbf{Equals:} \text{ Balance as per Bank Statement}$$

## 5. Adjustments and Final Accounts of Sole Proprietors
- **Cost of Goods Sold ($\text{COGS}$) & Gross Profit:**
  $$\text{COGS} = \text{Opening Stock} + \text{Purchases} + \text{Carriage Inwards} - \text{Returns Outwards} - \text{Closing Stock}$$
  $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Net Sales (Sales } - \text{Returns Inwards)} - \text{COGS}$$
  *(Carriage Inwards is added to Purchases; Carriage Outwards is an operating selling expense in the Profit & Loss Account).*
- **End-of-Year Adjustments:**
  - *Accrued Expenses:* Expense incurred but unpaid. **Add** to the expense in the P&L Account; show as a **Current Liability** in the Balance Sheet.
  - *Prepaid Expenses:* Expense paid in advance. **Subtract** from the expense in the P&L Account; show as a **Current Asset** in the Balance Sheet.
  - *Accrued Income:* Income earned but unreceived. **Add** to income in P&L Account; show as a **Current Asset**.
  - *Prepaid Income:* Income received in advance. **Subtract** from income in P&L Account; show as a **Current Liability**.
- **Bad Debts and Provisions for Doubtful Debts:**
  - *Bad Debt Written Off:* Irrecoverable debt; debited as an expense in the P&L Account and deducted from Debtors in the Balance Sheet.
  - *Provision for Doubtful Debts:* Estimated percentage of remaining good debtors unlikely to pay:
    $$\text{New Provision} = \text{Percentage} \times (\text{Debtors} - \text{Bad Debts})$$
    - An **increase** in provision is debited as an expense in P&L.
    - A **decrease** in provision is credited as an income/gain in P&L.
    - Total provision is deducted from Debtors in the Balance Sheet.
- **Depreciation Methods for Non-Current Assets:**
  - *Straight-Line (Fixed Installment) Method:*
    $$\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Estimated Residual / Scrap Value}}{\text{Estimated Useful Life in Years}}$$
  - *Reducing (Diminishing) Balance Method:*
    $$\text{Depreciation Charge} = \text{Fixed Percentage} \times \text{Net Book Value (Cost } - \text{Accumulated Depreciation)}$$

# SECTION 3: CONTROL ACCOUNTS, INCOMPLETE RECORDS & SPECIAL ACCOUNTS

## 6. Control Accounts & Single Entry (Incomplete Records)
- **Control Accounts:**
  - **Sales Ledger Control Account (Total Debtors Account):** Summarizes individual debtor accounts in the sales ledger.
    - *Debit side:* Opening debtors, Credit sales, Dishonoured cheques, Interest charged to debtors.
    - *Credit side:* Cash/Bank receipts from debtors, Discount allowed, Returns inwards, Bad debts written off, Set-offs / Contra entries, Closing debtors.
  - **Purchases Ledger Control Account (Total Creditors Account):** Summarizes individual creditor accounts.
- **Single Entry / Incomplete Records Calculations:**
  - *Capital at Start (Statement of Affairs):* $\text{Assets at start} - \text{Liabilities at start}$.
  - *Ascertaining Net Profit for the Year:*
    $$\text{Net Profit} = \text{Closing Capital} + \text{Drawings} - \text{Capital Introduced} - \text{Opening Capital}$$

---

## 7. Partnership Accounts
- **Profit and Loss Appropriation Account:**
  $$\text{Net Profit for the Year}$$
  $$\mathbf{Add:} \text{ Interest on Drawings (Partners pay the firm)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Less:} \text{ Interest on Capital (Firm pays partners)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Less:} \text{ Partners' Salaries / Commissions}$$
  $$\mathbf{Equals:} \text{ Divisible Profit (Shared in agreed Profit-Sharing Ratio)}$$
- **Partners' Current Accounts vs. Capital Accounts:**
  - Under the *Fixed Capital Method*, partners' original capital remains unchanged in the Capital Account; all drawings, salaries, interest, and share of profit are recorded in a separate **Current Account**.
  - A partner's debit balance on the current account indicates they have drawn more money than their entitlements.
- **Goodwill on Admission of a Partner:**
  - Goodwill is the established reputation, customer loyalty, and earning capacity of a firm beyond its tangible assets.
  - When a new partner is admitted, existing goodwill is credited to old partners in their **old profit-sharing ratio**.

---

## 8. Manufacturing Accounts
- Prepared by manufacturing businesses to determine the cost of producing goods rather than buying finished goods.
- **Divisions of Manufacturing Cost:**
  $$\text{Direct Raw Materials Consumed (Opening Raw Materials } + \text{Purchases } - \text{Closing Raw Materials)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Add:} \text{ Direct Factory Wages / Labour}$$
  $$\mathbf{Add:} \text{ Direct Factory Expenses (Royalties)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Equals:} \mathbf{PRIME\ COST}$$
  $$\mathbf{Add:} \text{ Factory Overheads (Indirect wages, factory rent, depreciation of factory plant)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Add:} \text{ Opening Work-in-Progress (WIP)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Less:} \text{ Closing Work-in-Progress (WIP)}$$
  $$\mathbf{Equals:} \mathbf{COST\ OF\ PRODUCTION}\text{ (Transferred to Trading Account)}$$

---

## 9. Non-Profit Making Organizations (Clubs & Societies)
- **Receipts and Payments Account:** A summarized cash book recording all actual cash receipts (debit) and cash payments (credit) during a period, regardless of whether they relate to past, present, or future years. Does not record non-cash items (depreciation, bad debts, accruals).
- **Income and Expenditure Account:** Equivalent to a Profit and Loss Account; prepared on an accrual basis. Records revenue income and revenue expenditure for the current year only.
  - Credit balance represents **Surplus of Income over Expenditure**.
  - Debit balance represents **Deficit of Income over Expenditure**.
- **Accumulated Fund:** Represents the capital of the club:
  $$\text{Accumulated Fund} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$$

# SECTION 4: ACCOUNTING RATIOS & PUBLIC SECTOR ACCOUNTING

## 10. Key Accounting Ratios
- **Profitability Ratios:**
  - Gross Profit Margin: $\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100\%$
  - Net Profit Margin: $\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100\%$
  - Return on Capital Employed (ROCE): $\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Capital Employed}} \times 100\%$
- **Liquidity Ratios:**
  - Current Ratio (Working Capital Ratio):
    $$\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad (\text{Standard Benchmark } = 2:1)$$
  - Quick Ratio (Acid Test):
    $$\text{Acid Test Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Stock}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \quad (\text{Standard Benchmark } = 1:1)$$
- **Efficiency / Activity Ratios:**
  - Rate of Stock Turnover:
    $$\text{Stock Turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold}}{\text{Average Stock}} \quad \text{where } \text{Average Stock} = \frac{\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Closing Stock}}{2}$$

## 11. Public Sector Accounting
- Governed by cash-basis accounting rather than the accrual basis.
- **Consolidated Revenue Fund (CRF):** The main treasury fund of the government into which all public revenues (taxes, oil royalties, customs duties, mining fees) are paid, and from which statutory expenditures (debt service, judicial salaries, statutory recurrent spending) are disbursed under the authority of an **Appropriation Act**.
- **Development Fund:** Capital project financing fund for infrastructure (roads, bridges, schools).

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB PRINCIPLES OF ACCOUNTS

1. **Remember the Imprest Reimbursement Rule:** Under the petty cash imprest system, the reimbursement amount is ALWAYS equal to the **total petty expenses spent during the period**.
2. **Carriage Inwards vs Carriage Outwards:** **Carriage Inwards** is added to Purchases in the Trading Account (increases cost of goods); **Carriage Outwards** is an operating selling expense debited to the Profit & Loss Account.
3. **Discount Column in Cash Book:** Total of Discount Allowed column is debited to Discount Allowed Account in the General Ledger; Total of Discount Received column is credited to Discount Received Account. They are **never balanced against each other**!
4. **Error of Principle Identifier:** Whenever a question mentions buying or repairing a non-current asset and posting it to an expense account (or vice versa), the answer is an **Error of Principle**.
5. **Bank Reconciliation Unpresented Cheque Sign:** When reconciling starting from the Adjusted Cash Book balance, **ADD** Unpresented Cheques and **SUBTRACT** Uncredited Cheques to reach the Bank Statement balance.
6. **Incomplete Records Profit Shortcut:** Add drawings to closing capital, then subtract opening capital and fresh capital. $\text{Profit} = (\text{Closing Capital} + \text{Drawings}) - (\text{Opening Capital} + \text{New Capital})$.
7. **Acid Test Excludes Inventory:** Because stock (inventory) is the least liquid current asset (takes time to sell), subtract it from current assets when computing the Acid Test ratio: $\frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Stock}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$.
8. **Work-in-Progress in Manufacturing:** In the manufacturing account, always **ADD Opening WIP** and **SUBTRACT Closing WIP** to arrive at the Cost of Production.
9. **Partnership Absence of Deed:** Under the 1890 Partnership Act, profits are shared **equally**, and interest on capital is **0%**.
10. **Receipts & Payments is Pure Cash:** Non-cash transactions like depreciation, accruals, and bad debts NEVER appear in a Receipts and Payments account!

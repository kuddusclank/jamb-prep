# JAMB Islamic Religious Studies (IRS) — Comprehensive Study Note for Candidates (Jambites)

**Subject Code:** `IRS` / `IS`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Islamic Religious Studies  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests IRS

In the UTME, Islamic Religious Studies is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into three canonical areas:
1. **The Glorious Qur'an & Hadith Sciences (~15 Questions):** History of revelation and compilation, Makkan vs. Madinan Surahs, Tajweed rules, commentary on prescribed Surahs, Hadith terminology (Sanad, Matn, Isnad), the Six Authentic Compilations (*Sihah al-Sittah*), and An-Nawawi's Forty Hadiths.
2. **Tawhid & Jurisprudence / Fiqh (~15 Questions):** Articles of Faith (*Iman*), dimensions of Tawhid, forms of Shirk, the Five Pillars of Islam, purification (*Taharah*), Salah, Zakah rates and recipients, Sawm regulations, Hajj rites, Islamic family law (marriage, divorce, custody), inheritance shares (*Mawarith*), and ethics.
3. **Islamic History & Islam in West Africa (~10 Questions):** Pre-Islamic Arabia (*Jahiliyyah*), biography of Prophet Muhammad (SAW) in Makkah and Madinah, the Rightly Guided Caliphs (*Khulafa' ar-Rashidun*), the spread of Islam to Kanem-Borno, Hausaland, and the 1804 Sokoto Jihad.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Legal and Quranic Terminology in JAMB IRS:**
> - The Five Legal Classifications (*Al-Ahkam al-Khamsah*): **Fard/Wajib** (obligatory), **Mustahabb/Sunnah** (recommended), **Mubah** (permissible), **Makruh** (disliked), **Haram** (strictly prohibited).
> - Specific inheritance fractions in the Qur'an: $\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{8}, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{6}$.
> - Compilation milestones: First compilation under Caliph **Abu Bakr** (compiled by **Zayd ibn Thabit**); standardization and distribution of copies under Caliph **Uthman ibn Affan**.

# SECTION 1: THE GLORIOUS QUR'AN AND HADITH SCIENCES

## 1. Revelation, Compilation, and Tajweed
- **The Process of Revelation:**
  - Revealed piecemeal over **23 years** (13 years in Makkah, 10 years in Madinah) via the Angel **Jibril (Gabriel)** to Prophet Muhammad (SAW).
  - First revelation: **Surah Al-Alaq (96:1–5)** in the Cave of Hira in 610 AD (*"Iqra' bismi Rabbika alladhi khalaq..."*).
- **Makkan vs. Madinan Surahs:**
  - *Makkan Surahs (86 Surahs):* Revealed before the Hijrah; focus on monotheism (**Tawhid**), destruction of idols, the Day of Judgment, Paradise, Hell, moral rectitude, and stories of past prophets. Short verses, poetic rhythm, frequent use of the vocative *"O mankind!"* (*Ya ayyuhan-nas*).
  - *Madinan Surahs (28 Surahs):* Revealed after the Hijrah; focus on legal rulings, social and economic legislation, civil laws, family law, jihad, hypocrites (*Munafiqun*), and community governance. Long verses, detailed ordinances, frequent use of *"O you who believe!"* (*Ya ayyuhalladhina amanu*).
- **Compilation of the Holy Qur'an:**
  - During Prophet's lifetime: Written on palm branches, flat stones, shoulder bones of camels, and committed to memory by hundreds of companions (**Huffadh**).
  - **First Official Compilation (Abu Bakr's Caliphate):** Triggered by the Battle of Yamamah (632 AD), where over 70 Huffadh were martyred. Umar ibn al-Khattab urged Abu Bakr to compile the Qur'an. Commissioned **Zayd ibn Thabit** (chief scribe). The master manuscript was kept with Abu Bakr, then Umar, and finally deposited with **Hafsah bint Umar** (the Prophet's widow).
  - **Standardization & Distribution (Uthman's Caliphate):** Differences in dialect and recitation among Muslim soldiers in Armenia and Azerbaijan alarmed Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman. Caliph Uthman retrieved Hafsah's copy, instructed Zayd ibn Thabit, Abdullah ibn az-Zubayr, Sa'id ibn al-As, and Abdur-Rahman ibn al-Harith to transcribe authoritative copies in the dialect of **Quraysh** (*Mushaf Uthmani*), distributed them to major Islamic provinces (Makkah, Kufa, Basra, Damascus), and ordered all conflicting private scrolls burned.
- **Rules of Tajweed (Recitation of the Qur'an):**
  - *Rules of Noon Sakinah and Tanween ($\dot{\text{ن}}$ and $\text{\textquotesingle\textquotesingle}$):*
    1. **Izhar (Clarification):** Clear pronunciation without nasalization before the 6 throat letters: $\text{ء, هـ, ع, ح, غ, خ}$ (Hamzah, Ha, 'Ayn, Haa, Ghayn, Khaa).
    2. **Idgham (Assimilation):** Merging noon into the following letter; occurs before the 6 letters of *Yarmaloon* ($\text{ي, ر, م, ل, و, ن}$):
       - With Ghunnah (nasal sound): $\text{ي, ن, م, و}$ (Yanmoo).
       - Without Ghunnah (complete merger): $\text{ل, ر}$.
    3. **Iqlab (Conversion):** Converting Noon Sakinah or Tanween into a hidden $\text{Mim (م)}$ with ghunnah when followed by the letter $\text{Ba (ب)}$.
    4. **Ikhfa' (Concealment):** Light nasalization between Izhar and Idgham before the remaining 15 letters ($\text{ت, ث, ج, د, ذ, ز, س, ش, ص, ض, ط, ظ, ف, ق, ك}$).

## 2. Commentary on Selected Prescribed Surahs
- **Surah Al-Fatihah (The Opening / Umm al-Kitab):** 7 verses; essential pillar of every Salah unit; encapsulates praise of Allah (*Al-Hamd*), His mercy (*Ar-Rahman Ar-Rahim*), sovereignty on Judgment Day (*Maliki Yawmid-Deen*), exclusive worship (*Iyyaka na'budu*), and supplication for the straight path (*As-Sirat al-Mustaqim*).
- **Surah Al-Alaq (The Clot, 96):** Verses 1–5 highlight the supreme value of literacy, knowledge, and writing with the pen (*Al-Qalam*).
- **Surah Al-Ikhlas (Purity of Faith, 112):** Equivalent to one-third of the Qur'an; establishes absolute monotheism: Allah is One (*Ahad*), the Self-Sufficient/Eternal (*As-Samad*), He begets not nor was He begotten (*Lam yalid wa lam yulad*), and there is none comparable to Him.
- **Surah Al-Hujurat (The Chambers, 49):** The ethical charter of Islam: forbids raising voices above the Prophet, verifying news from corrupt persons (*Fasiq*) before acting, making peace between conflicting believers, prohibits mocking, defaming, insulting with nicknames, suspicion (*Dhann*), spying (*Tajassus*), and backbiting (*Ghibah* — compared to eating the flesh of one's dead brother).
- **Surah Luqman (31):** Sage Luqman's parental advice to his son: absolute avoidance of Shirk (greatest injustice), kindness and obedience to parents, establishing Salah, enjoining good and forbidding evil, patience, avoiding arrogance/pride, and walking with humility.

## 3. Sciences of Hadith & Canonical Compilations
- **Terminology:**
  - *Sunnah:* The sayings (*Aqwal*), actions (*Af'al*), silent approvals (*Taqrir*), and physical descriptions of Prophet Muhammad (SAW).
  - *Hadith:* The oral or written narration recording a Sunnah.
  - *Sanad / Isnad:* The unbroken chain of narrators transmitting the Hadith from one authority to another up to the Prophet.
  - *Matn:* The actual text or substance of the Hadith.
- **Classification of Hadith According to Authenticity:**
  1. **Sahih (Sound / Authentic):** Transmitted by an unbroken chain of upright, reliable, highly accurate narrators with retentive memory, free from hidden defects (*'Illah*) or contradiction of stronger texts.
  2. **Hasan (Good):** Meets the criteria of Sahih except that one or more narrators have slightly lesser precision in memory; acceptable for legal rulings.
  3. **Da'if (Weak):** Fails to meet the conditions of Sahih or Hasan due to a broken chain or unreliable narrator; cannot be used as primary proof in Islamic legal rulings.
  4. **Mawdu' (Fabricated):** A forged lie falsely attributed to the Prophet; rejected.
- **The Six Canonical Hadith Compilations (*Sihah al-Sittah*):**
  1. **Sahih al-Bukhari:** Compiled by Imam Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari (194–256 AH); the most authentic book after the Holy Qur'an.
  2. **Sahih Muslim:** Compiled by Imam Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj an-Naysaburi (206–261 AH).
  3. **Sunan Abu Dawud:** Compiled by Imam Abu Dawud Sulayman ibn al-Ash'ath (202–275 AH); focuses heavily on legal traditions (*Ahadith al-Ahkam*).
  4. **Jami' at-Tirmidhi:** Compiled by Imam Abu Isa Muhammad at-Tirmidhi (209–279 AH).
  5. **Sunan an-Nasa'i:** Compiled by Imam Ahmad ibn Shu'ayb an-Nasa'i (215–303 AH).
  6. **Sunan Ibn Majah:** Compiled by Imam Muhammad ibn Yazid Ibn Majah (209–273 AH).

# SECTION 2: TAWHID AND JURISPRUDENCE (FIQH)

## 4. Articles of Faith (Iman) and Tawhid
- **The Three Categories of Tawhid:**
  1. **Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah (Oneness of Lordship):** Believing that Allah alone is the Creator, Provider, Sustainer, and Ruler of the universe without partner.
  2. **Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah / al-Ibadah (Oneness of Worship):** Directing all acts of worship (prayer, supplication, sacrifice, vows, hope, fear) **exclusively to Allah alone**.
  3. **Tawhid al-Asma' was-Sifat (Oneness of Names and Attributes):** Affirming all divine names and attributes that Allah and His Messenger have affirmed for Himself without changing their meaning (*Tahrif*), denying them (*Ta'til*), describing their exact manner (*Takyif*), or comparing them to creation (*Tamthil*).
- **Shirk (Polytheism — The Unforgivable Sin):**
  - *Shirk Akbar (Major Shirk):* Associating partners with Allah in worship, invoking dead saints, making sacrifices to deities; nullifies all good deeds and condemns the person permanently to Hell if they die without repentance.
  - *Shirk Asghar (Minor Shirk):* Does not expel a person from Islam, but is a grave sin; primary form is **Riya'** (showing off in religious worship to seek praise of people).
- **The Six Articles of Faith:** Belief in: 1. Allah, 2. His Angels, 3. His Revealed Books, 4. His Messengers, 5. The Day of Resurrection, 6. Divine Predestination (**Qada' and Qadar**).

---

## 5. Pillars of Islam & Ritual Practices
- **Taharah (Purification):** Prerequisite for Salah.
  - *Wudu (Minor Ablution):* Washing hands, mouth, nose, face, arms to elbows, wiping head/ears, washing feet to ankles.
  - *Ghusl (Major Ritual Bath):* Obligatory after sexual intercourse, emission of semen, menstruation (*Hayd*), and post-natal bleeding (*Nifas*).
  - *Tayammum (Dry Ablution):* Permitted using clean dust/earth when water is unavailable or medically harmful.
- **Salah (The 5 Daily Prayers):** Subh/Fajr (2 raka'ahs), Zuhr (4), Asr (4), Maghrib (3), Isha (4).
  - *Special Prayers:* Jumu'ah (Friday congregational prayer with Khutbah, replacing Zuhr); Salatul Janazah (Funeral prayer, 4 Takbirs, no bowing or prostrations); Salatul Kusuf/Khusuf (Solar/Lunar eclipse); Salatul Istisqa' (Drought prayer for rain); Salatul Tarawih (Night prayer in Ramadan).
- **Zakah (Obligatory Alms):**
  - *Zakat al-Mal:* $2.5\%$ ($\frac{1}{40}$) levied annually on accumulated wealth, cash, and merchandise held for one full lunar year (**Hawl**), provided it meets the minimum threshold (**Nisab** $= 85\text{ grams of pure gold}$ or equivalent).
  - *The 8 Quranic Beneficiaries of Zakah (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60):*
    1. The Poor (*Al-Fuqara'*).
    2. The Needy (*Al-Masakin*).
    3. The Zakah Collectors / Administrators (*Al-'Amilina 'alayha*).
    4. Those whose hearts are to be reconciled to Islam (*Al-Mu'allafati qulubuhum*).
    5. Freeing slaves / captives (*Fir-Riqab*).
    6. Debtors in distress (*Al-Gharimin*).
    7. In the Cause of Allah (*Fi Sabilillah*).
    8. The Stranded Traveler (*Ibn as-Sabil*).
- **Sawm (Fasting):**
  - Fasting the entire month of Ramadan from dawn (*Fajr*) to sunset (*Maghrib*).
  - Exemptions: Sick, travelers, pregnant and nursing women (make up missed days later); permanently ill or aged pay **Fidyah** (feeding one poor person for each day missed).
  - **Kaffarah:** Expiation for intentionally breaking fast by sexual intercourse: freeing a slave, or fasting **60 consecutive days**, or feeding 60 poor persons.
- **Hajj (Pilgrimage to Makkah):**
  - Essential Rites: Entering **Ihram** at designated geographical boundaries (**Miqat**); **Tawaf** (circling the Ka'bah 7 times counter-clockwise); **Sa'y** (running 7 times between hills of Safa and Marwah commemorating Hajar's search for water); **Wuquf at Arafah** on the 9th of Dhul-Hijjah (**The Central Pillar: "Al-Hajj 'Arafah"**); staying at Muzdalifah; **Rami al-Jamarat** (stoning the 3 pillars representing Satan at Mina); sacrifice of animal (**Hady**); and farewell Tawaf (*Tawaf al-Wada'*).

## 6. Islamic Family Law and Mawarith (Inheritance)
- **Marriage (Nikah):**
  - Mandatory conditions: Mutual consent of bride and groom, approval of the bride's guardian (**Wali**), payment of the nuptial gift/dowry (**Mahr**) directly to the bride, and attendance of **two upright Muslim male witnesses**.
  - Prohibitions (*Mahram*): Mother, daughters, sisters, aunts, nieces, foster-mothers (by milk), mother-in-law, stepdaughters.
- **Divorce (Talaq) and Dissolution:**
  - *Talaq:* Repudiation initiated by husband (Talaq Raj'i allows revocation during Iddah; Talaq Ba'in is irrevocable).
  - *Khul':* Dissolution requested by wife by mutual agreement upon returning the Mahr.
  - *Faskh:* Judicial annulment granted by a Sharia Court on grounds of neglect, cruelty, impotence, or abandonment.
  - *Iddah (Waiting Period):* Divorced menstruating woman $= 3$ menstrual cycles; divorced non-menstruating woman $= 3$ lunar months; pregnant woman $=$ until delivery; **widow whose husband died $= 4\text{ months and } 10\text{ days}$**.
- **The Science of Inheritance (*Mawarith*):**
  - Prerequisites before distribution: Pay funeral expenses $\to$ settle debts $\to$ execute valid will (**Wasiyyah**, limited to a maximum of **$\frac{1}{3}$ of the estate** and cannot be given to a legal heir).
  - *Fixed Quranic Shares (*Fara'id*):*
    - **Husband:** Receives **$\frac{1}{2}$** if wife leaves no children; receives **$\frac{1}{4}$** if wife leaves children.
    - **Wife:** Receives **$\frac{1}{4}$** if husband leaves no children; receives **$\frac{1}{8}$** if husband leaves children (shared equally if multiple wives).
    - **Single Daughter:** Receives **$\frac{1}{2}$** if sole child; **$\frac{2}{3}$** shared equally if two or more daughters without sons.
    - **Son and Daughter together:** The male receives **twice the share of the female** ($2:1$).
    - **Mother and Father:** Receive **$\frac{1}{6}$** each if deceased leaves children.

# SECTION 3: ISLAMIC HISTORY AND CIVILIZATION

## 7. Biography of Prophet Muhammad (SAW)
- **Makkan Period (570 – 622 AD):**
  - Born in Makkah in the **Year of the Elephant (570 AD)**, father Abdullah died before his birth; mother Aminah died when he was 6; grandfather Abdul-Muttalib cared for him, then uncle Abu Talib.
  - Known as **Al-Amin** (The Trustworthy) and **As-Sadiq** (The Truthful); participated in *Hilf al-Fudul* (League of the Virtuous); married **Khadijah bint Khuwaylid** at age 25.
  - Received prophethood at age 40 in Cave of Hira; secret preaching for 3 years, then open preaching on Mount Safa; boycott in the valley of Abu Talib (*Shi'b Abi Talib*).
  - The Year of Sorrow (*'Am al-Huzn*, 619 AD): Death of Khadijah and Abu Talib; journey to Ta'if where he was stoned; the Night Journey and Ascension (**Al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj**), where the 5 daily prayers were ordained.
  - Pledges of Al-Aqabah with Muslims of Yathrib; **The Hijrah (622 AD):** Migration to Madinah with Abu Bakr, marking Year 1 of the Islamic Calendar (AH).
- **Madinan Period (622 – 632 AD):**
  - Established the brotherhood between the Emigrants (**Muhajirun**) and Helpers (**Ansar**); drafted the **Constitution of Madinah** (*Sahifat al-Madinah*).
  - Major Military Encounters:
    - **Battle of Badr (17th Ramadan, 2 AH / 624 AD):** 313 ill-equipped Muslims defeated 1,000 well-armed Quraysh warriors.
    - **Battle of Uhud (3 AH / 625 AD):** Initial victory compromised when Muslim archers abandoned their posts on Mount Rumah for booty; 70 Muslims martyred, including Hamzah ibn Abdul-Muttalib.
    - **Battle of the Trench / Khandaq (5 AH / 627 AD):** Muslims defended Madinah by digging a deep trench suggested by **Salman al-Farsi**.
    - **Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (6 AH / 628 AD):** 10-year truce with Makkah; permitted peaceful expansion of Islam.
    - **Conquest of Makkah (*Fath Makkah*, 8 AH / 630 AD):** Bloodless conquest; 360 idols around the Ka'bah smashed; general amnesty granted.
  - The Farewell Pilgrimage (*Hajjat al-Wada'*, 10 AH): Farewell Sermon on Mount Arafah proclaiming human equality and brotherhood; passed away in Madinah in 632 AD (11 AH).

---

## 8. The Rightly Guided Caliphs and Islam in West Africa
- **The Khulafa' ar-Rashidun:**
  1. **Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (632–634 AD):** Defeated apostate movements in the **Riddah Wars**; suppressed false prophets (Musaylimah al-Kadhdhab); initiated the first compilation of the Qur'an.
  2. **Umar ibn al-Khattab (634–644 AD):** Rapid expansion of Islamic state defeating Persian and Byzantine empires; conquered Jerusalem, Syria, Egypt, and Iraq; established the Hijri calendar, regular police, state treasury (*Bayt al-Mal*), and judicial department.
  3. **Uthman ibn Affan (644–656 AD):** Standardized and published the canonical Uthmanic Qur'an; expanded naval power in the Mediterranean.
  4. **Ali ibn Abi Talib (656–661 AD):** Governed during internal strife; faced challenges at the Battle of the Camel and Battle of Siffin; assassinated by the Kharijite Abdur-Rahman ibn Muljam.
- **Islam in Nigeria:**
  - Introduced to **Kanem-Borno** in the 11th century via trans-Saharan trade; Mai Hume Jilme (1086 AD) made Islam the state religion.
  - Introduced to **Hausaland (Kano and Katsina)** in the 14th century by Wangarawa traders and clerics from Mali during the reign of Sarki Ali Yaji of Kano (1349–1385).
  - **The 1804 Sokoto Jihad:** Led by **Shaykh Usman Dan Fodio**; purified Islamic practice, abolished un-Islamic taxes, established the Sokoto Caliphate, and revitalized Islamic scholarship across Nigeria.

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB IRS

1. **Uthmanic Standardization Reason:** Caliph Uthman standardized the Qur'anic text to eliminate differences in dialect and pronunciation among Muslims of non-Arab provinces.
2. **First Collector of the Qur'an:** **Zayd ibn Thabit** was commissioned by Caliph Abu Bakr at the urging of Umar to compile the first complete manuscript of the Qur'an.
3. **Iddah Duration for Widows:** A widow whose husband has died observes an Iddah of **4 months and 10 days** (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:234).
4. **Wife's Inheritance Share:** A wife inherits **$\frac{1}{4}$** of her deceased husband's estate if there are no children, but **$\frac{1}{8}$** if the husband left surviving children.
5. **The Essence of Hajj:** The Prophet declared: **"Al-Hajj 'Arafah"** (Hajj is standing at Arafah on the 9th of Dhul-Hijjah). Missing Arafah invalidates the entire Hajj.
6. **Zakah Rate on Cash and Gold:** The rate of Zakah on cash and savings is **$2.5\%$ ($\frac{1}{40}$)**, provided it meets the Nisab and has been held for one complete lunar year (**Hawl**).
7. **Number of Takbirs in Janazah:** The Janazah (funeral) prayer consists of **4 Takbirs** and has **no bowing (Ruku') or prostrations (Sujud)**.
8. **Salman al-Farsi's Tactical Innovation:** The defensive trench in the Battle of Khandaq was suggested by the Persian companion **Salman al-Farsi**.
9. **Riya' is Minor Shirk:** Showing off in religious devotion to win human praise is **Riya'** (*Shirk Asghar*).
10. **Maximum Bequest in a Will:** In Islamic inheritance law, a person cannot bequeath more than **$\frac{1}{3}$** of their net estate to non-heirs in a will (*Wasiyyah*).

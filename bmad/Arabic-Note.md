**Subject Code:** `ARA`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for Arabic  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests Arabic

In the UTME, Arabic is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major segments:
1. **Comprehension & Translation (*al-Fahm wa at-Tarjamah*) (~10 Questions):** Reading unseen Modern Standard Arabic (*al-Fusha*) prose excerpts, answering textual questions, and accurate translation between Arabic and English.
2. **Grammar & Syntax (*an-Nahw*) (~14 Questions):** Parts of speech, nominal vs. verbal sentences, case endings (*al-I'rab*), modifiers (*Kana* and *Inna* families), prepositions, pronouns, and number-gender agreement (*al-'Adad wa al-Ma'dud*).
3. **Morphology (*as-Sarf*) (~8 Questions):** The triliteral root system, the 10 verb measures/forms (*Awzan al-Fi'l*), active and passive participles (*Ism al-Fa'il* and *Ism al-Maf'ul*), verbal nouns (*al-Masdar*), broken plurals (*Jam' at-Taksir*), and the diminutive (*at-Tasghir*).
4. **Literature & Appreciation (*al-Adab*) (~8 Questions):** Major literary periods (Pre-Islamic *Jahiliyyah*, Early Islamic, Umayyad, Abbasid, and Modern), famous poets and writers (Imru' al-Qays, Hassan ibn Thabit, al-Mutanabbi, Ahmad Shawqi, Naguib Mahfouz), and poetic genres.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Grammatical Traps in JAMB Arabic:**
> - Governance of *Inna* vs. *Kana*:
>   - **إِنَّ (Inna) and its sisters:** Make the subject **Mansub** (Accusative with Fathah) and keep the predicate **Marfu'** (Nominative with Dammah).
>   - **كَانَ (Kana) and its sisters:** Keep the subject **Marfu'** (Nominative with Dammah) and make the predicate **Mansub** (Accusative with Fathah).
> - Numbers 3 to 10 Rule: The numeral takes the **OPPOSITE GENDER** of the singular countable noun (*al-Ma'dud*), and the countable noun must be **Plural Majrur** (Genitive).

# SECTION 1: GRAMMAR AND SYNTAX (AN-NAHW)

## 1. Sentence Structures & Components
- **Parts of Speech (*Aqsam al-Kalam*):**
  - **Al-Ism (Noun / Substantive):** Indicates a person, animal, place, or concept without reference to time (e.g., كِتَابٌ, رَجُلٌ). Recognized by Tanween, Alif-Lam ($\text{ال}$), or following a preposition.
  - **Al-Fi'l (Verb):** Indicates an action tied to time: Past (*al-Madi*), Present/Future (*al-Mudari'*), and Imperative (*al-Amr*).
  - **Al-Harf (Particle):** Word with no complete independent meaning except when joined with a noun or verb (e.g., فِي, مِنْ, عَلَى, إِلَى).
- **The Nominal Sentence (*al-Jumla al-Ismiyyah*):**
  - A sentence that begins with a noun.
  - Composed of two essential parts:
    1. **Al-Mubtada' (The Subject):** The definite noun about which information is given; naturally **Marfu' (مَرْفُوع)** with Dammah.
    2. **Al-Khabar (The Predicate):** The word or phrase that completes the meaning; naturally **Marfu' (مَرْفُوع)** with Dammah.
    $$\text{Example: } \text{الطَّالِبُ مُجْتَهِدٌ} \quad (\text{"The student is hardworking"})$$
- **The Verbal Sentence (*al-Jumla al-Fi'liyyah*):**
  - A sentence that begins with a verb.
  - Standard constituent order: **Verb (*Fi'l*) $\to$ Subject Doer (*Fa'il*) $\to$ Direct Object (*Maf'ul bihi*)**.
  - The **Fa'il (Subject)** is ALWAYS **Marfu'** (Nominative).
  - The **Maf'ul bihi (Object)** is ALWAYS **Mansub** (Accusative with Fathah).
    $$\text{Example: } \text{كَتَبَ التِّلْمِيذُ الرِّسَالَةَ} \quad (\text{"The pupil wrote the letter"})$$

---

## 2. Modifiers: Inna and Kana Families (An-Nawasikh)
- **Kana and its Sisters (*Kana wa Akhwatuha* — Verbs of Becoming/State):**
  - Enter a nominal sentence and modify the case endings:
    $$\text{Ismu Kana} \implies \mathbf{Marfu'}\text{ (Dammah)} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Khabaru Kana} \implies \mathbf{Mansub}\text{ (Fathah)}$$
  - *Members of the family:* كَانَ (was), أَصْبَحَ (became in the morning), أَمْسَى (became in the evening), ظَلَّ (remained/continued), بَاتَ (spent the night), صَارَ (became/transformed), لَيْسَ (is not/negation), مَازَالَ (still/continues).
    $$\text{Example: } \text{كَانَ الجَوُّ بَارِدًا} \quad (\text{"The weather was cold"})$$
- **Inna and its Sisters (*Inna wa Akhwatuha* — Particles of Emphasis):**
  - Enter a nominal sentence and reverse the effect of *Kana*:
    $$\text{Ismu Inna} \implies \mathbf{Mansub}\text{ (Fathah)} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Khabaru Inna} \implies \mathbf{Marfu'}\text{ (Dammah)}$$
  - *Members of the family:* إِنَّ (verily/certainly), أَنَّ (that), كَأَنَّ (as if / resembling), لَكِنَّ (but/however), لَيْتَ (if only / wishful longing), لَعَلَّ (perhaps / hopeful expectation).
    $$\text{Example: } \text{إِنَّ اللهَ غَفُورٌ} \quad (\text{"Verily, Allah is Forgiving"})$$

## 3. The Four Grammatical Cases (Al-I'rab)
| Case | Arabic Term | Primary Marker (Singular) | Dual Marker (*al-Muthanna*) | Sound Masc. Plural (*Jam' Mudhakkar*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | **Ar-Raf' (الرَّفْع)** | **Dammah ($\text{ُ}$)** | **انِ** (*-ani*) | **ونَ** (*-una*) |
| **Accusative** | **An-Nasb (النَّصْب)** | **Fathah ($\text{َ}$)** | **يْنِ** (*-ayni*) | **ينَ** (*-ina*) |
| **Genitive** | **Al-Jarr (الجَرّ)** *(Nouns only)* | **Kasrah ($\text{ِ}$)** | **يْنِ** (*-ayni*) | **ينَ** (*-ina*) |
| **Jussive** | **Al-Jazm (الجَزْم)** *(Verbs only)* | **Sukun ($\text{ْ}$)** | Deletion of Nun | Deletion of Nun |

- **Key Jarr Triggers:** Noun preceded by a preposition (*Harf Jarr*: فِي, مِنْ, إِلَى, عَنْ, عَلَى, بِـ, لِـ, كَـ) OR functioning as the second noun in a possessive construct (**Mudaf Ilayhi**, e.g., كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ).
- **Sound Feminine Plural Exception:** Takes **Kasrah** instead of Fathah in the Accusative case (e.g., رَأَيْتُ الطَّالِبَاتِ).

---

## 4. Number Systems & Countables (Al-'Adad wa al-Ma'dud)
- **Numbers 1 and 2:** Agree with the noun in gender, follow the noun as adjectives (e.g., رَجُلٌ وَاحِدٌ, اِمْرَأَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ).
- **Numbers 3 to 10 (The Gender Polarity Rule):**
  - The numeral takes the **OPPOSITE GENDER** of the singular form of the noun counted.
  - The counted noun (*al-Ma'dud*) is in the **Plural Genitive (Majrur)**:
    - *For Masculine Nouns (e.g., كِتَابٌ):* Use the feminine numeral (with Ta Marbutah):
      $$\mathbf{ثَلَاثَةُ\ كُتُبٍ} \quad (\text{Three books})$$
    - *For Feminine Nouns (e.g., غُرْفَةٌ):* Use the masculine numeral (without Ta Marbutah):
      $$\mathbf{ثَلَاثُ\ غُرَفٍ} \quad (\text{Three rooms})$$
- **Numbers 11 to 99:** The counted noun is ALWAYS **Singular Accusative (Mansub)**:
  $$\text{Example: } \text{إِحْدَى عَشْرَةَ كَوْكَبًا} \quad (\text{"Eleven planets"})$$

# SECTION 2: MORPHOLOGY (AS-SARF)

## 5. Triliteral Roots and Derived Verb Forms (Awzan al-Fi'l)
- Most Arabic words derive from a **3-consonant root (ف - ع - ل)**:
  - **Form I (فَعَلَ - Fa'ala):** Basic root action (e.g., كَتَبَ - wrote).
  - **Form II (فَعَّلَ - Fa''ala):** Causative or intensive action (e.g., عَلَّمَ - taught).
  - **Form III (فَاعَلَ - Fa'ala):** Mutual or reciprocal action (e.g., جَالَسَ - sat with).
  - **Form IV (أَفْعَلَ - Af'ala):** Transitive / causative (e.g., أَخْرَجَ - expelled / took out).
  - **Form V (تَفَعَّلَ - Tafa''ala):** Reflexive of Form II (e.g., تَعَلَّمَ - learned).
  - **Form VI (تَفَاعَلَ - Tafa'ala):** Mutual interaction between parties (e.g., تَعَاوَنَ - cooperated).
  - **Form VII (اِنْفَعَلَ - Infa'ala):** Passive/reflexive (e.g., اِنْكَسَرَ - broke / got broken).
  - **Form VIII (اِفْتَعَلَ - Ifta'ala):** Participatory / intensive (e.g., اِجْتَمَعَ - gathered / assembled).
  - **Form IX (اِفْعَلَّ - If'alla):** Colors and physical defects (e.g., اِحْمَرَّ - turned red / blushed).
  - **Form X (اِسْتَفْعَلَ - Istaf'ala):** Seeking, requesting, or considering (e.g., اِسْتَغْفَرَ - sought forgiveness).

## 6. Derived Nouns, Participles, and Broken Plurals
- **Ism al-Fa'il (Active Participle / The Doer):**
  - Form I pattern: **فَاعِلٌ (Fa'il)** (e.g., كَاتِبٌ = writer, عَالِمٌ = scholar, جَالِسٌ = sitting).
  - Derived forms: Prefix $\text{مُـ}$ (*Mu-*) with Kasrah on penultimate letter (e.g., مُعَلِّمٌ = teacher, مُسْلِمٌ = Muslim).
- **Ism al-Maf'ul (Passive Participle / The Receiver of Action):**
  - Form I pattern: **مَفْعُولٌ (Maf'ul)** (e.g., مَكْتُوبٌ = written, مَعْلُومٌ = known).
  - Derived forms: Prefix $\text{مُـ}$ (*Mu-*) with Fathah on penultimate letter (e.g., مُعَلَّمٌ = taught).
- **Al-Masdar (Verbal Noun / Gerund):**
  - Name of the abstract action (e.g., كِتَابَةٌ = writing, خُرُوجٌ = exiting, اِسْتِغْفَارٌ = seeking pardon).
- **Jam' at-Taksir (Broken Plural):**
  - Irregular plurals formed by altering the vowel pattern of the singular noun:
    - كِتَابٌ $\to$ كُتُبٌ (Books)
    - مَدْرَسَةٌ $\to$ مَدَارِسُ (Schools)
    - قَلَمٌ $\to$ أَقْلَامٌ (Pens)
    - مَسْجِدٌ $\to$ مَسَاجِدُ (Mosques)
- **At-Tasghir (The Diminutive):**
  - Expresses smallness, affection, or contempt. Standard Form I pattern is **فُعَيْلٌ (Fu'ayl)**:
    - كَلْبٌ (dog) $\to$ كُلَيْبٌ (small puppy)
    - رَجُلٌ (man) $\to$ رُجَيْلٌ (little man)
    - قَلَمٌ (pen) $\to$ قُلَيْمٌ (small pencil)

# SECTION 3: OUTLINE OF ARABIC LITERATURE (AL-ADAB)

## 7. Major Literary Epochs and Masterpieces
- **1. Pre-Islamic Era (*al-Asr al-Jahili*, c. 500 – 622 AD):**
  - Celebrated for oral poetry characterized by strict meter, monorhyme, desert imagery, and tribal valor.
  - **Al-Mu'allaqat (The Seven Suspended Odes):** Golden masterpieces written in gold ink and traditionally hung on the walls of the Ka'bah. Prominent poets:
    - **Imru' al-Qays:** The "Wandering King", master of love elegies (*Qifa nabki...*).
    - **'Antarah ibn Shaddad:** The valiant warrior-poet celebrated for heroic epics and chivalry (*Furusiyyah*).
    - **Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma:** Known as the poet of peace and wisdom.
- **2. Early Islamic & Umayyad Era (622 – 750 AD):**
  - Language elevated by the inimitable eloquence of the Holy Qur'an (*I'jaz al-Qur'an*).
  - **Hassan ibn Thabit:** Officially titled the **"Poet of the Prophet"** (*Sha'ir an-Nabi*), defended Islam against Quraysh satires.
  - Umayyad court poets famous for poetic duel satire (*Naqa'id*): **Al-Farazdaq, Jarir, and al-Akhtal**.
- **3. The Abbasid Era (The Golden Age, 750 – 1258 AD):**
  - Blossomed in Baghdad; cosmopolitan, philosophical, scientific, and literary sophistication.
  - **Abu Nuwas:** Master of wine and urban hedonistic poetry.
  - **Al-Mutanabbi (Abu at-Tayyib):** Universally acknowledged as the greatest classical Arabic poet; celebrated for pride, philosophical mastery, and panegyrics for Sayf ad-Dawlah.
  - **Al-Jahiz:** Master of classical Arabic prose; author of *Kitab al-Hayawan* (The Book of Animals) and *Kitab al-Bukhala* (The Book of Misers).
  - **Al-Hariri:** Master of the *Maqamat* (picaresque rhymed prose tales).
- **4. The Modern Period (*an-Nahdah* — The Renaissance):**
  - **Ahmad Shawqi (1868–1932):** Egyptian neoclassical poet, crowned **"Prince of Poets"** (*Amir ash-Shu'ara'*).
  - **Hafiz Ibrahim:** The "Poet of the Nile".
  - **The Mahjar (Emigrant) Poets:** Arabic poets who emigrated to North and South America, establishing the Pen League (*ar-Rabitah al-Qalamiyyah*) led by **Kahlil Gibran** (*The Prophet*) and Mikha'il Nu'aymah.
  - **Naguib Mahfouz (1911–2006):** Egyptian novelist; won the **Nobel Prize in Literature in 1988** for his monumental *Cairo Trilogy*.

# SECTION 4: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB ARABIC

1. **Inna vs Kana Inversion:** *Inna* makes the subject **Mansub** (Fathah) and predicate **Marfu'** (Dammah); *Kana* makes the subject **Marfu'** (Dammah) and predicate **Mansub** (Fathah).
2. **Numbers 3 to 10 Opposite Gender Rule:** For numbers 3–10, if the countable noun is masculine, choose the numeral with **Ta Marbutah** ($\text{ة}$); if feminine, choose the numeral without it!
3. **Prince of Poets Title:** The official "Prince of Poets" (*Amir ash-Shu'ara'*) in modern Arabic literature is **Ahmad Shawqi**.
4. **Poet of the Prophet:** The companion appointed as the official poet of Prophet Muhammad (SAW) was **Hassan ibn Thabit**.
5. **Form X Verb Meaning:** A Form X verb on the pattern **اِسْتَفْعَلَ (Istaf'ala)** denotes *requesting, seeking, or asking* for something (e.g., *Istaghfara* = asked for forgiveness).
6. **Sound Feminine Plural Accusative Exception:** In the Accusative case (*Nasb*), the sound feminine plural ending in $\text{ات}$ takes a **Kasrah**, NEVER a Fathah.
7. **Jussive Case (*Jazm*) Restriction:** The Jussive case applies **strictly to imperfect verbs (*al-Fi'l al-Mudari'*)**, never to nouns.
8. **Genitive Case (*Jarr*) Restriction:** The Genitive case applies **strictly to nouns**, never to verbs.
9. **Arab Nobel Laureate in Literature:** The first and only Arab author to win the Nobel Prize in Literature is Egyptian novelist **Naguib Mahfouz** (1988).
10. **Diminutive Pattern:** The standard morphological pattern for the diminutive (*at-Tasghir*) is **فُعَيْلٌ (Fu'ayl)**, turning *Rajul* into *Rujayl*.

# JAMB History — Comprehensive Study Note for Candidates (Jambites)

**Subject Code:** `HIS`  
**Total Questions in UTME:** 40 Questions  
**Time Target:** 25 – 27 Minutes  
**Maximum Scaled Score:** 100 Marks  
**Authoritative Reference:** Official JAMB IBASS Syllabus for History  

---

## Overview & How JAMB Tests History

In the UTME, History is tested via **40 multiple-choice questions** grouped into four major chronological segments:
1. **Nigeria up to 1800 (~10 Questions):** Early archaeological civilizations (Nok, Ife, Benin, Igbo-Ukwu), Kanem-Borno, the Hausa States (Bayajidda legend), the Old Oyo Empire, Nupe, Jukun, and the Trans-Saharan Trade.
2. **19th-Century Transformations (~10 Questions):** The 1804 Sokoto Islamic Jihad (Usman Dan Fodio), Kanem-Borno under al-Kanemi, the Yoruba Civil Wars (Owu and Kiriji wars), European explorers (Mungo Park, Lander Brothers), and Christian missionary enterprises.
3. **Colonial Rule, Indirect Rule & Nationalism (1900–1960) (~12 Questions):** British military conquests, the 1914 Amalgamation by Lord Lugard, Indirect Rule across Northern, Western, and Eastern regions, early nationalist movements, political parties (NNDP, NYM, NCNC, AG, NPC, NEPU), and constitutional milestones.
4. **Post-Independence Politics & Wider Africa (~8 Questions):** The First Republic, military coups of 1966, the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970), the oil boom, the Berlin Conference (1884–1885), the Scramble for Africa, Apartheid in South Africa, and regional bodies (AU, ECOWAS).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Chronologies in JAMB History:**
> - **1804:** Outbreak of the Sokoto Jihad led by Shaykh Usman Dan Fodio.
> - **1830:** The Lander Brothers (Richard and John) discover the mouth of the River Niger emptying into the Bight of Benin.
> - **1851 / 1861:** British bombardment of Lagos (1851) and formal annexation of Lagos as a Crown Colony under King Dosunmu (1861).
> - **1884–1885:** Berlin West Africa Conference partitions Africa among European imperial powers.
> - **1886 / 1893:** Peace treaty ending the 16-year Kiriji / Ekiti-Parapo War in Yorubaland.
> - **January 1, 1914:** Lord Frederick Lugard amalgamates the Northern and Southern Protectorates with the Colony of Lagos.
> - **May 30, 1967 – January 15, 1970:** The Nigerian Civil War.

# SECTION 1: NIGERIA UP TO 1800 (EARLY CIVILIZATIONS)

## 1. Early Archaeological Cultures in Nigeria
- **The Nok Culture (c. 900 BC – 200 AD):**
  - Located in the Nok valley of Kaduna State, spreading across Jos Plateau and Benue valley.
  - Discovered in 1928 by tin miners and excavated scientifically by British archaeologist **Bernard Fagg** in 1943.
  - Recognized for producing the **oldest terracotta sculptures in Sub-Saharan Africa**, distinguished by pierced triangular/oval eyes, flaring nostrils, and elaborate hairstyles.
  - Proved early knowledge of **iron smelting** in West Africa independent of external civilizations.
- **Ile-Ife:**
  - Spiritual and ancestral cradle of the Yoruba people; tradition attributes its founding to **Oduduwa**.
  - Renowned for naturalistic bronze/brass and terracotta portrait heads produced via the **cire-perdue (lost-wax) casting technique**, depicting divine kings (**Ooni**) and royal consorts with facial scarifications.
- **The Benin Kingdom:**
  - Founded by the Ogiso dynasty (Sky Kings); later transformed when Prince Oranmiyan of Ife arrived and established the Oba dynasty, fathering **Oba Eweka I**.
  - **Oba Ewuare the Great (15th Century):** Expanded the empire, fortified the city with monumental defensive earthworks (Benin City walls and moats), and established royal guilds.
  - Famous royal brass-casting guild (**Iguneronmwon**) produced ceremonial plaques, commemorative heads, and the famous **Ivory Mask of Queen Mother Idia** (adopted as the official symbol of the Second World Black and African Festival of Arts and Culture — **FESTAC '77**).
  - Destroyed during the British Punitive Expedition of February 1897 under Admiral Harry Rawson; Oba Ovonramwen was exiled to Calabar, and thousands of royal bronze artifacts were plundered.
- **Igbo-Ukwu (9th Century AD):**
  - Located in Anambra State; accidentally discovered in 1938 by **Isaiah Anozie** while digging a water cistern; excavated by British archaeologist **Thurstan Shaw** in 1959.
  - Unearthed hundreds of leaded bronze castings decorated with intricate floral, geometric, and filigree motifs (e.g., the bronze roped pot), alongside over 165,000 glass trade beads and a sacred burial chamber of an **Eze Nri** (priest-king of the ancient Nri Kingdom).

## 2. Northern Kingdoms and the Trans-Saharan Trade
- **Kanem-Borno Empire:**
  - Founded around Lake Chad by the nomadic Kanuri under the **Sayfawa Dynasty** (one of the longest-ruling dynasties in world history, c. 850 – 1846 AD).
  - Initial capital at **Njimi** (east of Lake Chad); relocated to **Birni Ngazargamu** in the late 15th century by Mai Ali Gaji.
  - **Mai Idris Alooma (1571–1603):** Brought Kanem-Borno to its Golden Age; instituted Islamic legal reforms, introduced Turkish military instructors, imported muskets/firearms from the Ottoman Empire, and organized a formidable camel cavalry.
- **The Hausa States (Hausa Bakwai & Banza Bakwai):**
  - **The Bayajidda Legend:** Prince Abuyazidu (Bayajidda) fled Baghdad, arrived in Daura, killed the sacred snake *Sarki* that prevented the townsfolk from drawing water, and married the Queen of Daura (**Daurama**).
  - *Hausa Bakwai (The 7 Legitimate States):* Daura, Kano, Katsina, Zaria (Zazzau), Gobir, Rano, and Biram.
  - *Banza Bakwai (The 7 Illegitimate / Bastard States):* Kebbi, Zamfara, Nupe, Gwari, Yauri, Ilorin, and Kwararafa.
  - Specializations: Kano and Katsina (commerce and dye-pits), Zaria (warriors and slave supply), Gobir (northern defensive bastion against desert nomads).
- **The Trans-Saharan Caravan Trade:**
  - Network of trade routes connecting North Africa and the Mediterranean coast with the West African forest and savanna regions across the Sahara Desert.
  - *Goods Exported from West Africa:* Gold, slaves, ivory, ostrich feathers, dyed leather (Moroccan leather), and **kola nuts**.
  - *Goods Imported into West Africa:* Rock salt, cowrie shells (currency), horses, firearms, textiles, brassware, and glass beads.
  - Major Northern Nigerian termini: **Kano, Katsina, and Kukawa**.

# SECTION 2: 19TH-CENTURY TRANSFORMATIONS IN NIGERIA

## 3. The 1804 Sokoto Islamic Jihad
- **Leader:** **Shaykh Usman Dan Fodio** (1754–1817), a learned Fulani Islamic scholar and preacher of the Toronkawa clan, assisted by his brother **Abdullahi Dan Fodio** and his son **Muhammad Bello**.
- **Underlying Causes:**
  - *Religious:* Corruption and syncretism of local Hausa rulers who mixed Islamic practices with traditional pagan animism (*shirk*).
  - *Social and Political:* Oppression and corruption of the Habe rulers of Gobir (Sarki Nafata and Sarki Yunfa), arbitrary justice, and moral decadence.
  - *Economic:* Imposition of heavy, un-Islamic taxes (**Jangali** on cattle, market tolls, extortionate land taxes) which infuriated Fulani pastoralists and peasant Hausa farmers (**Talakawa**).
- **Outbreak & Spread:**
  - Sarki Yunfa attempted to assassinate Usman Dan Fodio and attacked his community at Degel.
  - Usman Dan Fodio performed **Hijra** (flight) from Degel to Gudu on February 21, 1804; his followers declared him **Amir al-Mu'minin** (Commander of the Faithful).
  - Decisive victory at the **Battle of Tabkin Kwatto (June 1804)** against the royal army of Gobir.
- **Establishment & Structure of the Sokoto Caliphate:**
  - Replaced corrupt Hausa Habe rulers with Fulani Emirs across Katsina, Kano, Zaria, Adamawa, Bauchi, Nupe, and Ilorin.
  - Divided administratively in 1812:
    - **Western Sector (Gwandu):** Administered by brother **Abdullahi Dan Fodio**.
    - **Eastern Sector (Sokoto):** Administered by son **Muhammad Bello** (who became the second Sultan of Sokoto).
- **Kanem-Borno's Defense:**
  - Borno successfully repelled the Fulani jihadist invasions through the military and religious leadership of **Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi**, an Islamic scholar from Kanem who challenged Dan Fodio's religious justifications in written theological letters. Al-Kanemi displaced the ancient Sayfawa dynasty and established the **Shehu Dynasty** with its new capital at **Kukawa**.

## 4. The Yoruba Civil Wars and the Fall of Old Oyo
- **Collapse of the Old Oyo Empire:**
  - Internal constitutional crises between the **Alaafin (Aole)** and his **Bashorun**; imperial army overextended.
  - **Afonja's Rebellion (1817):** Afonja, the Are-Ona-Kakanfo based at **Ilorin**, revolted against Alaafin Aole, allied with Fulani Muslim elements led by **Shehu Alimi**, and broke away from Oyo. Alimi's followers later assassinated Afonja, absorbing Ilorin into the Sokoto Caliphate as a Fulani emirate under Emir Abdulsalami.
  - Fulani cavalry sacked the imperial capital **Oyo-Ile (Katunga)** around 1835; Alaafin Atiba founded a new capital at present-day Oyo.
- **The Yoruba Wars of the 19th Century:**
  - *The Owu War (1821–1826):* Sparked by a dispute at the Apomu market; Allied forces of Ife and Ijebu laid a 5-year siege on fortified **Owu**, completely leveling it. Generated hundreds of thousands of refugees, triggering massive population shifts.
  - *Rise of New Military Centers:*
    - **Ibadan:** Founded in 1829 as a joint military camp of Oyo, Ife, and Ijebu warriors; evolved into a republican military superpower based on individual merit and valor rather than hereditary monarchy.
    - **Abeokuta:** Founded in 1830 by Egba refugees led by **Sodeke** under the protective outcrop of Olumo Rock.
    - **Ijaye:** Led by military general **Kurunmi**, champion of conservative Oyo tradition.
  - *The 16-Year Kiriji / Ekiti-Parapo War (1877–1893):*
    - The longest civil war in Yoruba history.
    - Triggered by Ibadan's oppressive imperial rule, extortionate tax collectors (**Ajeles**), and military arrogance.
    - The eastern Yoruba states formed the **Ekiti-Parapo Grand Alliance** led by **Fabunmi of Imesi-Ile** and Commander-in-Chief **Ogedengbe of Ilesha**, fighting Ibadan troops under **Latosa**.
    - Ended through British diplomatic and military intervention with the **Peace Treaty of 1886** and final pacification of Yorubaland in **1893**.

## 5. European Exploration and Christian Missions
- **Exploration of the River Niger:**
  - **Mungo Park:** Scottish explorer; dispatched by the African Association. In his first journey (1795–1797), he reached the Niger at Segou and proved it flows **eastward**. In his second journey (1805), he drowned with his companions at the **Bussa Rapids** in modern Niger State.
  - **Hugh Clapperton, Dixon Denham, and Walter Oudney (1822–1824):** Crossed Sahara; discovered Lake Chad; Clapperton visited Kano, Sokoto, and Sultan Muhammad Bello.
  - **The Lander Brothers (Richard and John Lander, 1830):** Solved the long-standing geographic mystery of the Niger's termination; sailed down the river from Bussa and proved it empties into the Atlantic Ocean through the vast maze of creeks in the **Niger Delta (Oil Rivers)**.
- **Christian Missionary Endeavors:**
  - Led by freed African returnees from Sierra Leone (Saro) and European societies: Church Missionary Society (CMS), Roman Catholic Mission (RCM), Methodists, and Presbyterians.
  - **Bishop Samuel Ajayi Crowther (CMS):** Captured as a boy from Osogun in Yorubaland, rescued by the British naval squadron, educated in Sierra Leone and England. Accompanied the 1841 Niger Expedition; consecrated the **first African Anglican Bishop** in 1864 at Canterbury Cathedral; **translated the Holy Bible and Anglican Book of Common Prayer into the Yoruba language**.
  - **Mary Slessor (Presbyterian Church of Scotland):** Arrived in Calabar in 1876; successfully campaigned against the barbaric traditional practice of **killing twin babies** and human sacrifices among the Efik and Ibibio peoples.

# SECTION 3: COLONIAL RULE, NATIONALISM & INDEPENDENCE (1900–1960)

## 6. British Conquest and the 1914 Amalgamation
- **British Territorial Acquisition:**
  - 1851: Bombardment of Lagos (deposed King Kosoko, installed King Akitoye).
  - 1861: Annexation of Lagos as a British Crown Colony by Treaty signed with King Dosunmu.
  - 1885: British declared the **Oil Rivers Protectorate** (later Niger Coast Protectorate) following the Berlin Conference.
  - Subjugation of Indigenous Resistance:
    - **King Jaja of Opobo:** Resisted British commercial monopoly in palm oil; tricked aboard HMS *Goshawk* by Consul Harry Johnston in 1887 and exiled to Saint Vincent in the West Indies.
    - **Nana Olomu of Itsekiri:** Resisted British intrusion; British bombarded his heavily defended capital at Ebrohimi in 1894; exiled to the Gold Coast.
    - **The 1897 British Punitive Expedition on Benin:** Oba Ovonramwen exiled to Calabar.
    - **Conquest of Northern Nigeria (1900–1903):** Led by Sir Frederick Lugard; British forces took Kano and Sokoto; Sultan Attahiru I was martyred at the Battle of Burmi (1903).
- **The Amalgamation of January 1, 1914:**
  - **Sir Frederick Lugard** unified the **Protectorate of Northern Nigeria** and the **Protectorate of Southern Nigeria** (along with the Colony of Lagos) into a single political entity: **The Colony and Protectorate of Nigeria**.
  - *Primary Motive:* Financial expediency. The Northern Protectorate operated under constant budget deficits subsidized by imperial grants, while the Southern Protectorate enjoyed vast customs revenue surpluses from maritime trade and spirits imports. Lugard amalgamated both to use the Southern financial surplus to balance Northern deficits and fund railway construction.

## 7. The Nationalist Movement & Political Parties
- **Pioneering Nationalism (Herbert Macaulay Era):**
  - **Herbert Macaulay (1864–1946):** Grandson of Bishop Ajayi Crowther; surveyor, civil engineer, journalist, and politician; widely revered as the **"Father of Nigerian Nationalism"**.
  - Founded the **Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP)** in 1923 following the introduction of the elective principle under the Clifford Constitution; dominated the 3 elected seats for Lagos from 1923 to 1938.
- **The Nigerian Youth Movement (NYM, 1934):**
  - Formed by energetic educated youths: **Ernest Ikoli, Dr. J.C. Vaughan, Kofo Abayomi, H.O. Davies**, joined later by **Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe** (1937) and **Chief Obafemi Awolowo**.
  - First genuine pan-Nigerian nationalist movement with branches across the nation; broke the NNDP's monopoly by winning all Lagos seats in the 1938 Legislative Council elections. Disintegrated in 1941 due to ethnic factionalism during the vacant legislative council seat contest between Ernest Ikoli and Samuel Akinsanya.
- **The Tripartite Regional Parties (1950s):**
  - **NCNC (National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons):** Founded in 1944 with Herbert Macaulay as President and Nnamdi Azikiwe as General Secretary. Mainstream party in the Eastern Region and parts of the Western Region.
  - **Action Group (AG):** Founded in 1951 by **Chief Obafemi Awolowo** as the political wing of the Yoruba cultural organization, *Egbe Omo Oduduwa*. Slogan: "Life More Abundant"; introduced **free universal primary education**, modern healthcare, and Africa's first television station (WNTV, 1959) in the Western Region.
  - **Northern People's Congress (NPC):** Evolved from the cultural group *Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa*; dominated by the Northern traditional aristocracy; led by **Sir Ahmadu Bello (Sardauna of Sokoto)** and **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**. Slogan: "One North, One People, Irrespective of Religion, Rank or Tribe".
  - **NEPU (Northern Elements Progressive Union):** Radical, populist opposition party in the North founded in 1950 by **Mallam Aminu Kano**; championed the rights of the commoners (*Talakawa*) against feudal aristocratic exploitation.

# SECTION 4: POST-INDEPENDENCE NIGERIA & WIDER AFRICA

## 8. Post-Independence Politics & The Civil War (1960–1970)
- **The First Republic (1960–1966):** Prime Minister was Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa (NPC); ceremonial President was Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe (NCNC).
- **The First Military Coup (January 15, 1966):**
  - Executed by young military officers led by **Major Chukwuma Kaduna Nzeogwu** and Major Emmanuel Ifeajuna.
  - Assassinated Prime Minister Balewa, Northern Premier Ahmadu Bello, Western Premier Ladoke Akintola, and Federal Finance Minister Festus Okotie-Eboh.
  - **Major-General J.T.U. Aguiyi-Ironsi** assumed power; issued **Unification Decree No. 34** in May 1966 abolishing the federal system; sparked northern anti-Ironsi riots.
- **The Counter-Coup (July 29, 1966) & Road to War:**
  - Ironsi was assassinated at Ibadan by northern counter-coup plotters; **Lt. Col. Yakubu Gowon** became Head of State and restored federalism.
  - Severe anti-Igbo pogroms in the North prompted millions of Easterners to flee to their homeland.
  - **The Aburi Accord (January 1967):** Meeting held in Aburi, Ghana, hosted by General Joseph Ankrah, between Gowon and Eastern Region Military Governor **Lt. Col. Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu** to preserve peace. Differing interpretations of the agreement led to deadlock.
  - Gowon restructured Nigeria into **12 states on May 27, 1967** to break regional hegemony.
  - On **May 30, 1967**, Ojukwu formally proclaimed the Eastern Region as the independent **Republic of Biafra**.
- **The Nigerian Civil War (July 6, 1967 – January 15, 1970):**
  - Fighting broke out at Garkem on July 6, 1967.
  - Federal troops enforced a complete naval and economic blockade around Biafra, leading to catastrophic mass starvation and kwashiorkor.
  - Biafra surrendered unconditionally on **January 15, 1970** when Major-General Philip Effiong delivered the instrument of surrender to General Gowon at Dodan Barracks, Lagos.
  - Gowon declared: **"No victor, no vanquished"**, launching the national policy of the **3Rs (Reconciliation, Rehabilitation, and Reconstruction)**.

## 9. Wider Africa and Global Affairs
- **The Scramble for Africa & Berlin Conference (1884–1885):**
  - Convened by German Chancellor **Otto von Bismarck** in Berlin to avoid military conflict among European powers over African territory.
  - Enacted the **Doctrine of Effective Occupation** (a European power had to formally occupy and demonstrate administrative control over an African territory before claiming sovereignty). Ignored indigenous ethnic boundaries, dividing ethnic groups across artificial colonial frontiers.
- **The Apartheid Regime in South Africa (1948–1994):**
  - Institutionalized racial segregation introduced by the Afrikaner-dominated National Party in 1948, forcing non-white South Africans into impoverished reserves (**Bantustans / Homelands**).
  - Tragic milestones: **Sharpeville Massacre (1960)**, Soweto Youth Uprising (1976).
  - Anti-Apartheid heroes: **Nelson Mandela, Oliver Tambo, Walter Sisulu, Steve Biko** (founder of Black Consciousness Movement), and Archbishop Desmond Tutu.
  - Nigeria's Leading Role: Despite geographic distance, Nigeria was officially designated a **"Frontline State"**; boycotted the 1976 Montreal Olympics over New Zealand's rugby tour of South Africa, nationalized assets of Barclays Bank and British Petroleum (BP) to pressure Britain, and established the South Africa Relief Fund (SARF).
  - Mandela was released in February 1990 after 27 years imprisonment; universal non-racial democratic elections held on **April 27, 1994** elected Nelson Mandela as South Africa's first black President.

# SECTION 5: 10 GOLDEN STRATEGIES FOR JAMB HISTORY

1. **Bernard Fagg and the Nok Culture:** Whenever a question asks who excavated or scientifically recognized the **Nok Culture**, the answer is **Bernard Fagg** (1943).
2. **First African Anglican Bishop:** **Bishop Samuel Ajayi Crowther** was consecrated as the first African bishop in 1864 and translated the Bible into Yoruba.
3. **Thurstan Shaw and Igbo-Ukwu:** The archaeological excavations of the 9th-century bronze roped pot and Eze Nri artifacts at Igbo-Ukwu were carried out by **Thurstan Shaw**.
4. **Motive for the 1914 Amalgamation:** Lord Lugard amalgamated Nigeria in 1914 strictly for **financial/economic convenience**—using Southern customs revenue surpluses to subsidize Northern administrative deficits.
5. **Herbert Macaulay's Moniker:** Herbert Macaulay is universally recognized as the **"Father of Nigerian Nationalism"** and founded the first political party in Nigeria (NNDP, 1923).
6. **Kiriji War Duration and Treaty:** The Kiriji / Ekiti-Parapo War lasted for **16 years (1877–1893)** and was resolved by the Peace Treaty of 1886/1893.
7. **Biafra Surrender Date:** The Nigerian Civil War officially ended on **January 15, 1970**, when General Philip Effiong surrendered to General Yakubu Gowon.
8. **Otto von Bismarck and Berlin:** The 1884–1885 Berlin West Africa Conference that partitioned Africa was convened by German Chancellor **Otto von Bismarck**.
9. **FESTAC '77 Symbol:** The official emblem of the 1977 Second World Black and African Festival of Arts and Culture (FESTAC) was the **16th-century Ivory Mask of Queen Mother Idia of Benin**.
10. **Sokoto Jihad 1804 Battle:** The decisive initial battle of the 1804 Sokoto Jihad was the **Battle of Tabkin Kwatto** (June 1804).
